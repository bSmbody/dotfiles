# Hydragen: High-Throughput LLM Inference with Shared Prefixes

## What this paper does
An exact attention implementation for batches whose sequences share prefixes. Attention is split into attention over the shared prefix and over each sequence's unique suffix, combined exactly through their log-sum-exp (softmax denominator) values. Queries from all sequences are batched against the single copy of the prefix KV, turning many matrix-vector products into one matrix-matrix product on tensor cores and removing redundant prefix reads that paged attention still performs. Generalizes to tree-shaped sharing by decomposing at every tree node. Up to 32x end-to-end throughput over vLLM (CodeLlama-13b) and >16x attention speedup.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it compute attention over a shared prefix plus unique suffixes, and why is it faster?
Attention over a concatenation K1||K2 is recovered from the two partial attentions and their LSEs [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 5]. Since all sequences' queries see identical prefix keys/values, they are merged into one attention call with many queries, raising arithmetic intensity [Section 3.2]. Paged attention avoids redundant prefix storage "but not redundant prefix reads" [Section 1].
### Goal: Does it handle tree-structured / multi-level sharing?
Yes: decompose at every vertex of the sharing tree and batch the queries of all descendants against each node's KV [Section 3.3]. Two-level sharing on APPS (128 samples per problem) cut evaluation time by 18% at equal batch and a further 45% from the larger batch the saved memory allows [Section 4.4, Fig 6].
### Goal: When does it help most, and what are its limits?
Largest gains when batch size is large, shared prefixes are long and unique suffixes are short; it does not optimize suffix attention, and gains vanish when decoding is bound by weight reads (small batch, short sequences) [Sections 3.4, 5]. The prototype needs the user to specify where sharing occurs; dynamic discovery of overlaps inside a continuous-batching server is left to future work [Section 5].
