# MathArena: Evaluating LLMs on Uncontaminated Math Competitions

## What this paper does
MathArena evaluates LLMs on math competitions as soon as they are released, so that the problems cannot be in training data. It covers final-answer competitions (AIME, HMMT February, BRUMO, CMIMC; 30 problems each for AIME and HMMT Feb.) and proof-based ones (USAMO, IMO, Putnam), plus recent Project Euler problems. Each model is sampled 4 times per problem and scored by pass@1, with confidence intervals for accuracy and paired permutation tests for rank. It compares 2024 against 2025 versions of the same competitions against human-quantile lines to detect contamination.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Is AIME 2024 contaminated, and is HMMT a cleaner alternative?
Yes, by its analysis. On AIME, most models lie 10-20% above the human-quantile line when 2024 is plotted against 2025, "suggesting inflated performance on AIME 2024 due to data contamination", with QwQ-Preview-32B nearly 60% above. "In contrast, the discrepancy is much smaller for HMMT, indicating more trustworthy results", which the authors attribute to HMMT being less prominent [full text, Section 4.1 "Data contamination of past competitions", Fig. 4]. Even new competitions leak: 8 AIME 2025 problems and 1 HMMT 2025 problem were found online in similar form, mostly easier ones [same section; App. B].
### Goal: How large are these competitions, and how does it handle variance on so few problems?
AIME and HMMT February each have 30 problems [full text, Section 3.1, Table 1]. Accuracy CIs treat each answer as a Bernoulli trial with variance p(1-p)/N, rank CIs come from a paired permutation test [Section 3.4 "Variance estimation"; App. C]. Repeated runs (100 samples per problem, 25 four-sample estimates) give sharp score distributions [Section 4.1 "Repeating runs"]. AIME, HMMT and CMIMC rankings correlate above 80% Spearman [Section 4.1 "Cross-competition correlation"].


### Goal: Which other final-answer competitions does it include, and how many problems does each have?
Table 1 lists the final-answer competitions AIME (February, 30 problems), HMMT February (30), BRUMO (April, 30) and CMIMC (May, 40), plus the proof-based USAMO (6) and IMO (6) [full text, Section 3.1, Table 1]. All 2025 editions were evaluated, 162 problems in total across seven competitions including Project Euler [Section 4, "Setup"].
