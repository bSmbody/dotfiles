# Quality Recovery for Quantized KV Caches via Low-Rank Attention Adaptation

## What this paper does
The paper keeps a KV quantizer fixed (affine 4-bit, KIVI K2V2, KVarN K4V2, 2-bit affine) and distills the floating-cache model into low-rank updates of the Q/K/V projections. The student runs with the physically packed incremental quantized cache during training, which makes it quantization-aware fine-tuning of the attention projections for a given cache format. The adapters can be merged densely or kept as an auxiliary branch.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Headline numbers?
4-bit affine-cache adapters recover 54.24% ± 2.47% of the held-out perplexity gap on TinyLlama-1.1B and 75.96% ± 4.04% on Gemma-4-12B. On NF4 Llama-3.1-8B they recover 60.42% under KIVI K2V2 and 37.61% under KVarN K4V2 (one run each). Gemma RULER 4K/8K subset: 42.80 to 48.33 (floating 46.15) (Abstract).
### Goal: Does it recover retrieval at 2 bits?
No. In the affine 2-bit stress test, adaptation recovers only 11-12 of 180 NIAH cases versus 170/180 for floating (Section 6). It is evidence that KV-QAT-style adaptation recovers perplexity but not long-range retrieval at 2 bits.
### Goal: Limitations?
Small models, mostly perplexity. Several results are single-seed. No reasoning or long-generation evaluation. The NF4 path keeps an auxiliary branch with extra MACs (Section 6).
