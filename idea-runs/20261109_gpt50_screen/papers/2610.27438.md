# MLIPS: Multi-Layer Indexer Pool Sharing

## What this paper does
Training-free inference method for production learned sparse attention (DeepSeek-V4-Flash CSA, MiniMax-M3, GLM-5.1). A producer layer's indexer publishes a top-M candidate pool much larger than the attention budget K, and later consumer layers re-score and select their own top-K inside that pool, which avoids a full-prefix index scan per layer. Implemented in vLLM for DeepSeek-V4-Flash, it gives 1.14-1.40x speedups with -1.49 to +0.29 point accuracy changes. An exploratory Top-G construction studies shared working sets across query groups.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Where do a selector's missed choices sit: inside a wider rank-ordered pool, and are they shared across nearby queries?
Mostly inside a wider pool by rank. With the consumer's own full-search top-K as reference, the producer's top-M pool (M = 16,384 entries for DSV4-Flash and GLM-5.1, 128 blocks for MiniMax-M3) retains 89-94% of the consumer's choices below 200K query positions. The producer's final top-K retains only 31-65%. Across up-to-1M captures the figures are 72-85% pool coverage vs 23-56% final overlap, with coverage declining at later positions (0.90 → 0.76 → 0.66 across 0-200K / 200-500K / 500K-1M on RULER-1M) [full text, Section 3]. Nearby queries share candidates: a fixed pool retains 84.6% (DSV4-Flash) and 91.8% (MiniMax-M3) of the full-search choices one query later, and 78.5% / 89.7% three queries later [Section 3, "Nearby queries share candidates too"]. A shared Top-G pool covers 82.4% of the next layer's selections for the smallest window, falling to 52.9% at a 65,536-position window [Section 6]. These are count-based index recalls on sampled prefill activations, not attention mass and not task accuracy [Section 3, Appendix C]. The paper says nothing about whether misses cluster in key *position*. Its locality is by rank within a pool and by query offset.
