# Low-Discrepancy Dither for Quantized Recurrent State Caches

## What this paper does
Studies the rounding rule for low-precision recurrent-state caches in Mamba and hybrid models (130M-1.4B). Shows round-to-nearest discards small updates (write erasure), so its error grows linearly; stochastic rounding grows like sqrt(T); a deterministic golden-ratio Weyl dither keeps the counting error at O(log T). Weyl beats stochastic rounding across formats and long decoding horizons at no extra cost.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What error-persistence model does its allocation use, and does it include delta-rule erasure and cross-step error correlation?
Per-entry scalar recurrence h_t = a_t h_{t-1} + w_t with time constant tau = 1/(1-a); 13-23% of entries have tau >= 100 [full text, Section 2]. Lemma 1: under RTN, an update smaller than half a step is lost entirely; Proposition 1: accumulated error equals the discrepancy of the threshold sequence, i.e. rounding errors across steps are correlated, not independent [Section 3]. The theory covers scalar decay only, no delta-rule term [Section 3 "What the theory does not cover"].
### Goal: When is rounding/quantization committed or precision changed (timing), and is that schedule fixed or input-adaptive?
Rounds at every write in the main decode regime; with rounding every 8th write the Weyl advantage mostly disappears on hybrids [Section 6.5 "Periodic checkpointing"]. Limitations: small models, teacher-forced KL, no task-accuracy differences detected [Section 8].

### Goal: Does this work (or what it cites) apply error feedback / noise shaping to KV caches, and why did it not use noise shaping for state caches?
No. It studies only the rounding THRESHOLD (rtn vs stochastic vs Weyl dither) for recurrent-state caches of Mamba/hybrid models [full text, Section 1]. Its related work states that noise shaping / Sigma-Delta "are the classical answer to quantization inside a feedback loop, but they need the previous rounding error to be stored, which doubles the size of a state cache; at equal storage only the choice of threshold remains" [Section 7, "Stochastic and dithered rounding"]. For attention models it says KV-cache quantization is the analogous question "where errors are not fed back" [Section 7, "Quantizing state-space models"]. It also reports that Nemotron 3 found casting the Mamba state to FP16 made generations up to 37% longer, i.e. rounding bias inflating output length [Section 1], and that a block Hadamard rotation before INT4 interacts badly with Weyl dither [Section 6.5].
