# Quantization Inflates Reasoning: Token Inflation as a Hidden Cost of Low-Bit Reasoning Models

## What this paper does
A measurement study of weight-only PTQ (RTN, GPTQ, AWQ, HQQ, ParoQuant; INT4/INT3, group 128) on Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking. It defines the CoT Token Inflation Ratio (CTIR), shows PTQ often preserves accuracy while lengthening CoT, links inflation to more and more repetitive steps, quantifies the serving cost in vLLM, and compares mitigations (repetition penalty, concise prompts, calibration data, QAT via DiscQuant). It studies weights only; KV cache is not quantized.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is CTIR defined and what magnitudes are observed, and does any part concern the KV cache?
CTIR(Mq;D) = (mean reasoning-token length of quantized model − that of BF16) / BF16 length × 100%, averaged over reasoning benchmarks [full text, Section 5, CTIR equation]. INT4 gives mild but measurable inflation; INT3 is large: on Qwen3-4B AWQ-INT3 has an average CTIR of 276.1% and HQQ-INT3 292.1%; on Qwen3-30B, AWQ-INT3 32.8% and HQQ-INT3 60.1%. GPTQ inflates less than AWQ/HQQ [full text, Section 5, Table 2]. Correctness-conditioned analysis: inflation is stronger on BF16-correct problems, so it is not only failed problems getting longer [full text, Appendix E]. The study is weight-only by design ("we focus on weight-only PTQ"); KV and activation quantization are only cited from Liu et al. (2504.04823) [full text, Section 3 and Section 2.1]. So no KV-side CTIR exists here.

### Goal: What evaluation protocol is used (decoding, sample counts, budgets) and how is serving cost measured?
Greedy decoding (temperature 0) in vLLM; 100 random questions per benchmark (AIME25 all 30); AIME25, GSM8K, GPQA-D, BBH, LCB, MBPP plus HellaSwag/PIQA/MMLU-Pro [full text, Section 3]. Repetitiveness = fraction of CoT steps whose Qwen3-Embedding-4B cosine similarity to another step exceeds τ [full text, Section 6, Rep equation]. Serving: Qwen3-30B-A3B on one H100, 1K prompt, batch 64, CoT budget varied: a 1.1× CTIR raises p90 time-to-first-visible-token from 1050 s to 1400 s and lowers throughput from 0.055 to 0.047 req/s [full text, Section 6]. Agentic BFCLv4: GPTQ-INT4 adds 13.3% CoT tokens on Qwen3-4B with almost no change in tool calls [full text, Section 5].

### Goal: Which mitigations work?
Repetition penalty (1.1–1.4) and concise prompts trade accuracy for length (e.g., "no restate" cuts INT4 MBPP tokens 30% but accuracy 90→81) [full text, Section 7.1, Table 4]. Reasoning-trace calibration data (Math-CoT) vs Wikitext moves INT3 GPTQ from 28% to 65% accuracy and 30K to 12K tokens [full text, Section 7.2]. QAT (DiscQuant) reaches 68% at 9.5K tokens vs BF16 73% at 8.5K, the only Pareto improvement [full text, Section 7.3, Fig. 5].
