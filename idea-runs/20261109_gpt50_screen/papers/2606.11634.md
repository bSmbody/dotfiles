# Architecture-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning Makes Sliding-Window Attention Competitive in Math Reasoning

## What this paper does
SWARR converts a pretrained self-attention model (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B; Qwen3-4B-Base in the appendix) to pure sliding-window attention in every layer (2K/4K/8K/12K windows, no global layer), recovers it by SFT on 42B math tokens, and then runs on-policy RL (CISPO) under the SWA constraint with 64K generation. SFT leaves a clear gap to SA; RL closes most of it (SWA8k matches SA within 0.4 pts at 1.5B; SWA12k-4B within 1 pt). The central claim is that on-policy RL changes the conclusion one would draw from conversion+SFT alone about an architecture's viability for math reasoning.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it choose which layers keep full attention, or use on-policy signals for allocation?
No. Every layer is converted to SWA with the same window; there is no full-attention layer and no allocation search [full text, Section 3.1, Section 4.1]. Hybrid variants with global tokens are named as future work [Limitations]. On-policy training is used only to adapt weights (RL with CISPO) [Section 3.2].

### Goal: What does it show about how much long-range access long-CoT math needs after on-policy training?
After RL, SWA8k-1.5B is within -0.4 pts of SA at matched steps, SWA4k within +0.1 at matched time, SWA2k still -6.3/-2.5 [Section 4.3]. At 4B, SWA12k-RL 87.1 vs SA-RL 88.1 [Appendix A.2.3, Table 8]. Under matched length distributions RL-trained SWA trajectories are more local than SA's, which the authors read as "long-range dependencies are not strictly necessary for correct reasoning" — the policy learns to rely on local context [Section 5.1.2, Table 3]. So on-policy training can make the model route around missing long-range access, which shrinks residual failures that any architecture choice would fix.

### Goal: What scope limits does it state?
Math reasoning only, 1.5B and 4B scale, pure SWA without global tokens [Limitations].
