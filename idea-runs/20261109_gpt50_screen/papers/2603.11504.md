# LongFlow: Efficient KV Cache Compression for Reasoning Models

## What this paper does
A per-step decode-time eviction method for long-output reasoning. It approximates the next-step eviction impact by the current step (adjacent queries are similar) and ignores the softmax-denominator change, giving score ||α_t^i v^i||_1 computed as a free by-product of the attention pass; eviction is fused into a custom Triton FlashAttention kernel with a static memory layout. Evaluated on R1-Distill-Llama-8B and Qwen3 0.6B–8B against H2O, VATP, R-KV.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What evaluation protocol is used (output cap, budgets, datasets)?
Generation length set to 16,000 tokens; KV budget 3,200 or 2,400 tokens; MATH-500, AMC-23, AIME-24/25, GPQA, Minerva, OlympiadBench, GSM8K; baselines compress every 128 steps, LongFlow every step [full text, Section 4.1]. The claimed "80% compression" is 3.2K budget relative to the 16K cap, not to actual lengths [full text, Section 4.2]. No length-inflation, loop or cap-hit statistics are reported.

### Goal: What accuracy is reported vs. baselines?
Average degradation vs vanilla as low as 0.08% (R1-Llama-8B) and ~1.3% (Qwen3-8B) at 3.2K; R-KV "generally achieves the highest average accuracy"; LongFlow is on par with or better than H2O/VATP and degrades less at 2.4K [full text, Section 4.2, Table 1].

### Goal: How is throughput measured, and what is claimed?
Qwen3-1.7B, one A100-40GB, input 512, output 16K, budget 3.2K, batch size increased until OOM: LongFlow 11.8× FullKV throughput and ~4× other compression methods, with less fragmentation allowing larger max batch [full text, Section 4.4, Fig. 3]. This is a max-batch throughput comparison with a small model, not cost per correct answer. Limitations: depends on query stability; decode-only [full text, Limitations].
