# GrowPage: On-Demand KV Budgeting for Efficient LLM Reasoning Serving

## What this paper does
Treats per-request KV capacity as a runtime resource in a PagedAttention server (nano-vLLM, following Zipage). It keeps short- and long-timescale summaries of normalized pre-RoPE queries, updated in-kernel; at each capacity boundary it estimates attention demand and chooses "Compress & Hold" (compact within existing pages) or "Grow by One Page" (append a page from the free pool), falling back to compression under global memory pressure and to the scheduler's preemption otherwise. Evaluated on R1-Distill-Llama-8B and Qwen3-8B over GSM8K, MATH500, AMC23, AIME24, LCB, against FullKV (vLLM, nano-vLLM), MorphKV, R-KV, G-KV and Zipage.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Why are fixed budgets inadequate for reasoning serving?
The minimum budget B* at which a request is reliably correct (correct at all larger tested budgets, aggregated over repeated generations) spans the whole tested range on AIME24, AMC23 and LCB, and a single request's attention sparsity alternates between concentrated and diffuse regimes during decoding [full text, Section 2, Figs. 2–3].

### Goal: What protocol and metrics are used?
Pass@1 (32 generations per question for AMC23/AIME24, fewer for larger sets) and decoding throughput TPS, plus total inference time and TPOT in the appendix; all serving baselines share the same GPU memory budget, and eviction research baselines (MorphKV, R-KV, G-KV) run at their largest executable batch [full text, Section 5.1; Appendix F.1, G.2].

### Goal: What gains are reported, and how do they compare with fixed-budget paged compression?
Average TPS +64.3% (R1-Llama-8B) and +60.2% (Qwen3-8B) over full KV at comparable pass@1; MorphKV/R-KV/G-KV reduce memory but without serving co-design give limited throughput [full text, Section 5.2, Table 1]. Mixed GSM8K+AMC23+AIME24 workload: GrowPage 84.9% at 2213 tok/s, mean budget 3442, 121 resident requests vs Zipage-4096 83.7%, 1690 tok/s, 100 requests; Zipage-8192 85.1% at ~1.9× lower throughput [full text, Section 5.2, Fig. 5]. Throughput is in tokens/s, so any length inflation would look like a gain; per-correct cost is not reported.
