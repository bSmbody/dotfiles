# Yet another fast multipole method without multipoles — Pseudo-particle multipole method

## What this paper does
Makino (astrophysical N-body). In tree codes and FMM, the far field of a clump of particles is normally carried as multipole-expansion coefficients, whose translation formulae are complex and which special-purpose N-body hardware (GRAPE) cannot evaluate, because it only computes point-particle interactions. The paper instead represents the clump's multipole expansion up to order p by a small set of pseudo-particles at fixed positions on a ring (2D) or sphere (3D), with only their masses free; the masses are a linear transform of the physical masses, reproduce the expansion exactly to order p, and can be built hierarchically from child pseudo-particles (M2M). Accuracy matches classical FMM of the same order.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism, i.e. how are pseudo-particles placed and weighted so that they reproduce a clump's far field?
Fix 2p+1 pseudo-particle positions equispaced on a ring of radius r around the clump centre and let only their masses vary; the masses are a discrete inverse Fourier transform of the multipole coefficients alpha_k = sum_i m_i (z_i/a)^k, so the ring reproduces the multipole expansion exactly up to order p, and the far potential is evaluated as an ordinary sum of particle-particle potentials [full text, Section 3.1, Eqs 7-11]. Arbitrary positions plus masses would need fewer particles (3K degrees of freedom) but require solving a nonlinear system of cost at least O(p^3), with no guarantee a solution exists; fixing positions turns it into a linear O(p^2) or O(p log p) computation [Section 3.1]. The masses can be computed directly from the physical masses without forming coefficients (Eq 12), i.e. the fold is a fixed linear map of the sources [Section 3.1, Eq 12]. 3D is the same with spherical harmonics on a sphere [Section 3.2].

### Goal: Why is it useful there, i.e. what constraint does it get around?
GRAPE-type special-purpose machines only "evaluate and accumulate the gravitational interaction between particles", so previously only monopole (effectively dipole) tree codes could run on them; with pseudo-particles, high-order expansions become point particles and run on the same hardware unchanged [Section 5.2]. Also simplifies the M2L step relative to Anderson's method [Section 5.1].

### Goal: How are coarser cells built from finer ones (M2M), and what does accuracy depend on?
Parent pseudo-particles are computed by treating the child nodes' pseudo-particles as source particles in Eq 12, so the hierarchy needs no coefficient algebra [Section 3.1, M2M paragraph]. The expansion converges only outside the clump radius (|z|>a), the usual FMM well-separatedness condition [Section 3.1, Eq 5-6]. Ring radius is a trade-off: a finite ring has spurious higher-order moments (want r small), but masses grow like r^-p and round-off explodes if r is too small; r = 0.75a works and error is insensitive to r unless very high accuracy is needed [Section 4]. Errors achieve the theoretical order, slightly above direct multipole evaluation [Section 4, Fig 2]. Fewer particles are possible for low orders (monopole 1, dipole 1 at the centre of mass, quadrupole 4) if positions are also chosen [Section 5.3].
