# Understanding and Mitigating Numerical Sources of Nondeterminism in LLM Inference

## What this paper does
Shows that changing batch size, GPU count or GPU type changes the outputs of LLMs even under greedy decoding with a fixed seed. The root cause is non-associative floating-point arithmetic at limited precision: BF16 rounding differences flip near-tied top-1/top-2 tokens, and in reasoning models the flip cascades into a different chain of thought. It quantifies accuracy and length variance across 12 configurations for BF16/FP16/FP32 and proposes LayerCast (BF16 weights, FP32 compute) for near-deterministic inference.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How often and how early do long reasoning traces diverge under rounding-level perturbations, and why?
Under BF16 greedy decoding, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B on MATH500 diverges across runtime configurations on "over 90% of examples", and "divergence frequently occurs early in generation"; under FP32 only 2.2% diverge, and later [full text, Section 3.2, Fig 5]. Configuration changes alone produce up to 9% std in AIME'24 accuracy and up to 9,000 tokens difference in response length [Section 3.2, Tables 3-4]. The mechanism: "the token probability differences between the top two competing tokens are often minimal" for reasoning models, so small numerical fluctuations flip the argmax [Section 3.2, Fig 3; Section 3.4 summary: "the fundamental cause of nondeterministic outputs is the small gap between competing logits"]. Random sampling is affected too: precision adds variance to Pass@1 across configurations [Section 3.3, Table 5].
