# KV Cache is 1 Bit Per Channel: Efficient Large Language Model Inference with Coupled Quantization

## What this paper does
Coupled Quantization (CQ) jointly vector-quantizes groups of c contiguous channels of each key/value embedding with a learned (optionally Fisher-weighted) k-means codebook per channel group, exploiting inter-channel mutual information. CQ-8c8b gives 1 bit per channel for both K and V. Evaluated by perplexity (WikiText-2, C4, context 2-8K) and zero-shot accuracy on LLaMA/LLaMA-2/Mistral 7-13B.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does "coupled" mean in CQ: are rounding decisions coupled across tokens, or are channels jointly encoded?
Channels, not tokens. "Channels of a key or value activation embedding are divided into equally sized, non-overlapping groups of contiguous channels ... jointly quantized and share a single quantization code", each group mapped to the nearest centroid in L2 [full text, Section 3.2]. Centroids are learned offline on calibration data [Section 3.2.1]. Each token is encoded independently and deterministically (nearest centroid); there is no randomized or cross-token coupled rounding.
### Goal: How does 1-bit KV (both K and V) fare?
At 1 bit per FPN (CQ-8c8b) WikiText-2 perplexity is 8.09 vs FP16 5.68 on LLaMA-7b, 6.55 vs 4.57 on LLaMA-2-13b, 7.25 vs 4.76 on Mistral-7b; at 1.25 bits (CQ-8c10b) 6.78 / 5.47 / 5.90. KVQuant-1b is 321-4709 or NaN, KVQuant-1b-1% 9.9-13.8 [Section 4.1, Table 1]. Bit counts exclude centroids, scales and zero points [Section 4]. So 1-bit KV is usable but clearly lossy, and only perplexity at ≤8K is measured. In a 2026 long-CoT test, CQ-8c10b on Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 drops AIME25 from 74.4 to 14.4 [2610.03027, Table 2].
