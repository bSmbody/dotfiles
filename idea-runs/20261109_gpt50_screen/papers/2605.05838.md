# MDN: Parallelizing Stepwise Momentum for Delta Linear Attention

## What this paper does
Adds a stepwise momentum state M_t to gated delta-rule linear attention (M_t = mu_t M_{t-1} - eta_t k_t (v_t - alpha_t S_{t-1}^T k_t)^T, S_t = alpha_t S_{t-1} - beta_t M_t) and derives an exact chunkwise-parallel algorithm for it by decoupling the recursive coefficients, so training keeps strict causality instead of blockwise momentum. Analyzes momentum as a second-order system with complex eigenvalues and constrains gates for stability. Momentum DeltaNet is trained at 400M and 1.3B.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is the momentum update made chunkwise-parallel, and what property does it rely on?
Eliminating M gives a second-order recurrence S_t = (a I - b k k^T) S_{t-1} - g S_{t-2} + b k v^T [Table 1], still linear in the states with coefficients depending only on gates and keys. The chunk form keeps two states (S, M) across chunks; within a chunk a lower-triangular inverse T = Tril(I + (P K^T ⊙ Γ^-))^{-1} produces U, Y, Z and corrected values Ṽ = U - Y S + Z M [Section 3.1 "Chunkwise Algorithm" / Eq 14-18]. Backward recomputation of both states is too costly, so Ṽ is materialized [Section 3.1, "Practical Considerations"].

### Goal: What does it say about prior momentum / nonlinear TTT parallelization and its own efficiency limits?
Prior non-linear RNNs (TTT, LMM/Titans, LaCT) use blockwise momentum updates for hardware utilization, which introduces intra-block non-causality and a training–inference mismatch that worsens with block size; sliding-window schemes (Atlas) truncate history [Section 1, Figure 1]. MDN's own training throughput is lower than Comba and GDN because of the dual-state computation, comparable to Mamba2/KDA [Section 4.3, "Efficiency Analysis"].
