# State Soup: In-Context Skill Learning, Retrieval and Mixing

## What this paper does
Short workshop study on Mamba-2.8B treating recurrent states as task vectors: states from in-context-learning prompts form a retrievable skill library, and states can be linearly mixed (mean, or "A-decay mixing" that uses the cumulative transition product) to improve few-shot performance and next-token loss.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What state-mixing rules are used, and how well do mixed states work?
Mean-mixing 32/k states each from k examples: mixtures of 4-shot states are on par with or slightly better than processing all 32 shots at once; 1-shot mixtures are poor [full text, Section 2.2, Fig 2]. Mixing a "counting" state with an "English-French" state made the model predict the next number in French, but needed a weighted mean and is not quantified [Section 2.2]. A-decay mixing f(x_1..x_t) = f(x_{k+1}..x_t) + (prod A) f(x_1..x_k) is exact for one layer and uses the product the parallel scan already computes; on 100-token chunks it beats mean-mixing and the c2-only state but stays worse than sequential processing in the stacked model [Section 2.3, Fig 3]. Only diagonal Mamba transitions; no delta-rule erasure.
