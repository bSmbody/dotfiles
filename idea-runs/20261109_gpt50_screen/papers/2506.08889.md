# SeerAttention-R: Sparse Attention Adaptation for Long Reasoning

## What this paper does
A learned block-sparse attention for decoding of reasoning models. A lightweight AttnGate (pooled K + linear, with shared sparsity per GQA group) is self-distilled to mimic block-wise attention maps of the frozen model (OpenR1-Math, 800 steps, ~12 GPU-hours for 8B). At decode, a K-compression cache (1/128 of KV at block 64) predicts which KV blocks to load via top-k token budget or threshold. A TileLang block-sparse flash-decoding kernel is provided. KV is kept in full (no memory saving); savings are bandwidth/compute.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What evaluation protocol (models, budgets, samples, max length) is used?
Qwen3-4B/8B/14B and R1-Distill-Qwen-14B on AIME24, AIME25, MATH-500, GPQA-D; max output 32,768 tokens (shorter than Qwen3's official 38,912); pass@1 averaged over 64 samples for AIME, 8 for MATH-500, 16 for GPQA; baselines full attention and Quest at block 64 with all layers sparse [full text, Section 4.1]. Token budgets 2K–8K for AIME and 1K–6K for MATH/GPQA [full text, Section 4.3].

### Goal: Does inaccurate sparse attention inflate generation length?
Yes. Qwen3-8B AIME24, full attention 74.5% at 15.1K tokens. Quest at 2K/4K/6K/8K budgets: 13.3/44.2/52.5/59.6% with 30.0/22.9/19.6/17.2K tokens. SeerAttention-R: 56.6/72.3/74.2/75.1% with 19.8/16.3/15.3/15.1K [full text, Section 5.4, Table 1]. The authors call the effect "universal across different post-training efficiency optimizations" and cite quantization as the same phenomenon [full text, Section 5.4].

### Goal: What speedups are shown and is there end-to-end serving?
Oracle block sparsity is lossless at 2K budget. SeerAttention-R is near-lossless at a 4K budget on AIME24 and 2K on MATH/GPQA, whereas Quest is not lossless even at 8K [full text, Sections 4.2–4.3]. The kernel reaches up to ~9× over FA3 at 0.9 sparsity for bs=16, long sequences; ~6× at bs=4, 32K [full text, Section 4.4, Fig. 6]. End-to-end serving integration (vLLM/SGLang, PagedAttention) is explicitly left to future work [full text, Section 6.1].
