# NAPX: A Polynomial Time Approximation Scheme for the Noah's Ark Problem

## What this paper does
Gives the first (1 − epsilon)-approximation for the general Noah's Ark Problem: choose which taxa to protect under a budget to maximize expected phylogenetic diversity. Each taxon has a survival probability a_i that rises to b_i at cost c_i. It reviews which special cases are solved exactly (greedy or dynamic programming), explains why the general case lacks optimal substructure, and gives a pseudo-polynomial dynamic program over clades whose cost depends on the budget B and the tree height h.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What is the budgeted objective and how does it treat shared information and partial survival?
E(PD|S) = sum over edges e of lambda(e) × (1 − prod_{i in C_e ∩ S}(1 − b_i) × prod_{j in C_e − S}(1 − a_j)), maximized subject to sum_{s in S} c_s <= B [full text, Section 1.2, Eq 2]. "The probability that an edge survives is equivalent to the probability that at least one leaf below it survives." Protection raises survival only partially (a_i to b_i), so the objective is budgeted, partial and set-level. Per-species utility values "can be incorporated into the branch lengths without altering the problem" [Section 1.2].

### Goal: Which special cases are solved by greedy, and why is the general case hard?
0→1 NAP with unit costs (pick B leaves of maximum PD) is solved by greedy. So are 0→c_i→1 NAP on ultrametric trees and (1 − x_i)→(1 − kappa x_i) NAP with unit costs on general trees [Section 1.3]. The general problem generalizes Knapsack and is NP-hard [Section 1.1]. Pardi and Goldman's clade DP solves the 0→c_i→1 case in O(nB^2). For a_i→b_i it fails: a budget split that maximizes a clade's E(PD) need not maximize the clade's survival probability, and the global optimum can need the latter. In Fig 1, the locally best pick in the y/z clade is not part of the optimal solution [Section 1.3].

### Goal: What does NAPX cost and what limits does it name?
O(n B^2 h^2 (log n + log 1/epsilon)^2 / log^2(1 − epsilon)). It is polynomial only when B is polynomial in n, and it degrades for large budgets or tall trees. Expected running time is much lower than the worst case [Sections 2.1, 2.3, 3].
