# Short window attention enables long-term memorization (SWAX)

## What this paper does
Studies hybrids that interleave sliding-window attention (SWA) with xLSTM (mLSTM) layers, 1:1, pretrained at 1.4B and 7B. Finds that longer windows hurt long-context retrieval because the model over-relies on SWA and under-trains the recurrent memory. Proposes stochastic window training (sample a 128 or 2048 window per batch, anneal to the long window for the last 10%), which keeps the long-context recall of short windows and the short-context quality of long windows. Also tested with Gated DeltaNet in place of xLSTM and on local-global (SWA + full attention) models.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is trained, and how is attention reach restricted during training?
Whole hybrid models are pretrained from scratch. For each batch the SWA window is 128 with probability p (0.5 at 1.4B, 0.75 at 7B) or 2048 otherwise; short windows are no longer sampled for the last 10% of training [full text, Section 3; Section 4.4 "Stochastic window size"; Appendix B]. The goal is stated as "reducing over-reliance on the SWA layers for long-context recall", applied to the attention layer rather than the memory layer as in Memory Mosaic [Section 3].

### Goal: Does restricting attention make the recurrent layers carry long-range recall, and is this framed as dropout?
Yes. Stochastically trained models match the short-window model on RULER NIAH and are "drastically better" than the 2048-window hybrid at 1.4B and 7B; if allowed the long window throughout training the model "will under-utilize the Linear Attention Layers for the long-term recall task". The authors call it "a form of dropout ... on the attention mechanism" [Section 4.4 "Long-context performance of the stochastic training", Figures 6-7]. The benefit carries over to GDN as the recurrent layer [Section 4.5, Table 4].

### Goal: Is it applied to pretrained hybrids, to full-attention KV eviction, or as a fine-tune?
No. It is a pretraining recipe over window sizes; there is no eviction policy, no fine-tune of an existing hybrid, and no decode-time cache management [Sections 3, 4.1, 5].
