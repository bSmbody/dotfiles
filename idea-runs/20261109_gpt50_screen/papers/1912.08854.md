# A Theory of Trotter Error

## What this paper does
Analyses the error of product formulas (Lie-Trotter, Suzuki), which approximate the evolution generated by a sum of operators H = sum_gamma H_gamma by applying the individual evolutions one after another in some order. It proves error bounds that scale with nested commutators of the summands rather than their norms, so the error is small whenever the summands commute or nearly commute, gives a host of improved quantum-simulation and quantum Monte Carlo algorithms, and shows the asymptotic bound does not depend on how the summands are ordered.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What controls the error of applying several operator evolutions sequentially in some order instead of jointly?
For H = A + B the Lie-Trotter product e^{tB} e^{tA} differs from e^{t(A+B)} by an additive error that is an integral of e^{(t-tau)H}[e^{tau B}, A]e^{tau A}, and after one application of the fundamental theorem of calculus the multiplicative error is bounded by O(||[B,A]|| t^2) [full text, Section 3.1, Eqs 26-31]. Generally, for a p-th order formula the error is O(alpha_comm t^{p+1}) with alpha_comm the sum of norms of (p+1)-fold nested commutators of the summands [Section 1.3, Theorem; Section 3.4, Theorem 11]. "Product formulas ... can perform significantly better when the operator summands commute or nearly commute" [Section 1.1].
### Goal: Does the result depend on the order in which the pieces are applied, and is the controlling quantity cheap to evaluate?
"Although the definition of a specific product formula depends on the ordering of operator summands, our asymptotic bound does not", so the speedups hold irrespective of ordering, though ordering matters for tight constants and local observables [Section 1.3]. The commutator expression "is succinct and easy to evaluate" and is computed explicitly for several Hamiltonian classes [Section 1.3; Section 4]. Prior BCH-truncation analyses were loose because higher-order terms can dominate at fixed t [Section 1.2].
