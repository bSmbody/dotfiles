# MiniMax-M1: Scaling Test-Time Compute Efficiently with Lightning Attention

## What this paper does
Technical report of MiniMax-M1 (456B total / 45.9B active MoE), continued-pretrained from MiniMax-Text-01 on 7.5T tokens, then SFT and large-scale RL with the CISPO algorithm. Its attention is a hybrid: one softmax-attention block after every seven lightning-attention (I/O-aware linear attention, TransNormer-style) blocks. It supports 1M input and 40K/80K generation. Released openly with vLLM and Transformers support. The report focuses on RL scaling and on the problems the hybrid architecture causes in RL (train/inference precision mismatch).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What hybrid structure does M1 use and what inference cost advantage is claimed at long generation?
"A transformer block with softmax attention follows every seven transnormer blocks with lightning attention" (7:1) [full text, Section 1]. Versus DeepSeek-R1, M1 "consumes less than 50% of the FLOPs at a generation length of 64K tokens, and approximately 25% of the FLOPs at a length of 100K tokens" [Section 1, Figure 1 right]. These are theoretical FLOP comparisons, not measured latency or KV bytes. Full RL run: 3 weeks on 512 H800 [Section 1]. Since 1 of 8 layers keeps full softmax KV, KV memory and decode KV reads still grow linearly with length in those layers.

### Goal: What inference-side or numerical problems did the hybrid cause, by the authors' account?
Training-mode vs inference-mode token probabilities diverged (correlation ~0.9x), which "prevented reward growth"; "this issue did not appear in smaller, dense models with softmax attention". Layer-wise analysis traced it to high-magnitude activations in the LM head; FP32 LM head raised correlation to ~0.99x [Section 3.2, Figure 3]. Also: optimizer sensitivity (gradients 1e-18 to 1e-5, set beta2=0.95, eps=1e-15), pathological repetitive long generations handled by truncating after 3,000 consecutive tokens with p>0.99 [Section 3.2], and pattern collapse ("latter portions of generated sequences degraded into incoherent or garbled text") during length extension to 80K, attributed to negative-sample gradient imbalance [Section 5].

### Goal: What limitations do the authors state?
Compared with DeepSeek-R1-0528, M1 "lags in mathematical and coding competitions" though it is comparable or better on tool use and long context [Section 1]. The conclusion is forward-looking only (agents, workflows); no explicit architecture limitation is stated [Section 7].
