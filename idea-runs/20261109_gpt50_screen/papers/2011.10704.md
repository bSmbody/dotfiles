# Neural Group Testing to Accelerate Deep Learning

## What this paper does
Applies classical group testing to neural-network inference: a group of M samples is tested in one forward pass, negative groups are ruled out, positive ones retested. Three designs for multi-sample networks (pixel averaging; feature-space max-pool merge after the first ~20% of layers; hierarchical tree merge), three schedules (two-round, multi-round adaptive, one-round non-adaptive double pooling). On ImageNet-based firearm moderation (0.1% prevalence) the best config (multi-round + tree merge, M=16) cuts MACs by >73%.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are samples pooled, and does pooling need training or degrade recall?
Design 1 averages pixels (no modification) and loses recall; Designs 2/3 merge features by max-pool after T=0.2L layers, initialised from the individual net and fine-tuned on positive/negative groups; these reach 100% recall [full text, Sections II-A, III-B, III-C1]. Savings come from the shared trunk after the merge point having fixed cost regardless of M.
### Goal: Which group-testing schedule actually saves compute?
Adaptive multi-round testing is needed: two-round with M=16 gives 46% relative compute vs 26% for multi-round; non-adaptive one-round (double pooling) is offered for latency [full text, Sections II-B, III-C2]. Savings are largest at low prevalence [Section III-C3].
