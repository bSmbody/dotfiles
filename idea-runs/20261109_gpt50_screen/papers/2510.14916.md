# Efficient and Robust Carathéodory-Steinitz Pruning of Positive Discrete Measures

## What this paper does
Numerical-analysis paper on compressing a positive quadrature rule (M nodes) to at most N nodes (N = dimension of the function space) with positive weights that preserve all N moments, per Carathéodory's theorem. It gives a streaming Carathéodory-Steinitz pruning (SCSP/GSCSP) that updates a QR factorization with Givens rotations so each pruning iteration costs O(N^2) and storage depends only on N, proves stability under total-variation perturbations, and compares with NNLS and LP. Applied to cut-cell DG quadrature.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it provide, under what conditions, and is it randomized/unbiased?
Moment conditions are written as V^T w = eta with V in R^{M x N}; if M > N, V^T has a nontrivial kernel vector n, and moving w along n until one weight hits zero removes a node while preserving moments and positivity; repeat until <= N nodes remain [full text, Section 2.3]. It is deterministic pruning; there is no randomization and no E[w] = p (unbiasedness) property. The naive kernel-vector step is O(N^3) per iteration; GSCSP downdates the QR with Givens rotations to O(N^2) per iteration [Section 3.2], for linear total runtime in M at fixed N [Section 5.1 / Fig 1]; NNLS was empirically faster in their tests [Section 5.1]. The iterations are sequential over removed nodes.
