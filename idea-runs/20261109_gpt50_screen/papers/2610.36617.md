# Balanced State Subspaces for Mamba-2 State Compression

## What this paper does
BASS is a post-training Mamba-2 state compressor. It estimates controllability and observability Gramians of each head's state from one calibration pass, computes a balanced-truncation rotation, and applies it exactly through Mamba-2's scalar-identity transition, keeping the top-r balanced directions. HARP allocates ranks per layer from the Hankel spectrum and protects retrieval-sensitive layers. It halves the state at +0.3 WikiText-2 PPL on average across 130M-2.7B versus +1.6 for GHOST.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Is the kept subspace fixed or input-dependent, and what does it say about raw state energy as an importance signal?
Fixed: Gramians are averaged over calibration data and one rotation and rank per layer is applied to every input [full text, Sections 5.1-5.3]. Raw state energy is a poor importance signal: per-head states are almost rank-one (median effective rank 1.16-1.28; the top direction holds 89-93% of energy), and that direction is bias-like, aligned with the layer's mean write direction B (|cos| 0.88-0.95), while individual writes vary (consecutive B_t cosine 0.51-0.54). Content the output reads has small energy and lies off the native coordinates. The best r native coordinates keep only 0.69-0.72 of the balanced subspace's row mass at r=64 [full text, Section 4, Findings 1-2]. Useful rank varies across layers (up to 12.8) but little across heads in a layer [full text, Section 4, Finding 3].
### Goal: What limitations does it report?
At 25% retention the cost is +20 to +25% PPL. Calibration source and length shift the 50% cost by up to 1.5 points. Long-context recall, hybrids and OOD suites are untested. Decode gives 1.5-2.2x throughput at large batch but loses 6-10% at batch 1 in an unoptimised loop. Extending to Mamba-1 (no rotation invariance) and Mamba-3 is future work [full text, Limitations].
