# KVFetch: Temporal Prefetching for the Missing Half of KV Cache Compression

## What this paper does
Argues that score-based eviction, summary compensation and offload-and-recall all decide what to keep or recall by content relevance to the current query, i.e. only associative addressing. Under compression, verbatim copying of identifiers or code breaks midway ("sequential forgetting") because low-semantic continuation tokens are evicted. KVFetch is a training-free plug-in: rare-token spans are demoted to a 4-bit position-indexed cold tier, a monotone read pointer over attention peaks detects active copying, and positional successors are swapped into hot slots. On RULER-16K it lifts verbatim copying from 0.8 to 78.4 at iso-budget.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
Host compressor's content score for the hot tier; admission to the cold tier by low unigram-frequency rank runs (capped at 5% of L) [full text, Section 3.4]; recall triggered when the attention peak advances monotonically in position (>=2 advances in 8 steps) and the k=8 positional successors are swapped in [Sections 3.5-3.6, Eq 2-4]. Cold-tier entries are recoverable; "nothing is permanently lost" among hot+cold [Section 3.6].

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Tokens such as hex digits or rare identifiers "receive uniformly low importance scores at prefill because the verbatim demand that will eventually consume them does not yet exist" [Section 3.4]. Sequential forgetting "resists better scoring, larger budgets, summary compensation, and dynamic re-scoring" and accounts for 86% of the per-task gap to full KV [Section 3.2]. Boundary: "KVFetch repairs continuation, not localization" — if the host cannot localize the target (Mistral-7B), no trajectory forms; it does not fix multi-needle selection errors [Section 4.5].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Training-free; recall depends on a hand-designed positional trigger that requires the host's content score to keep the span's head tokens as anchors [Section 3.5].
