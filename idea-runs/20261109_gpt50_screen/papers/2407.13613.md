# Revisiting Randomization with the Cube Method

## What this paper does
Econometrics paper that brings Deville and Tillé's (2004) cube method from survey sampling to RCT assignment. The cube method draws a random 0/1 vector with given inclusion probabilities pi while (approximately) satisfying linear balancing equations on covariates, via a discrete martingale random walk on the polytope [0,1]^n intersect (pi + ker A) (flight phase), then a landing phase for the last <= q units. The paper derives balancing approximation bounds and asymptotic normality showing variance reduction when outcomes are linear in balanced covariates.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Is "martingale elimination producing weights with E[w]=p and exact linear moment constraints" an existing technique, and what are its variance properties and costs?
Yes: "the cube method is a discrete martingale that moves in (at most) n steps from the interior point pi(0) to ... a vertex of C", each step a random direction in ker(A) with E[pi(t+1)|pi(t)] = pi(t), fixing one unit per step; balancing constraints sum Z_i s_i/pi_i = sum Z_i hold exactly through the flight phase, with a landing phase (LP or constraint suppression) for the last r <= q units, so exact balance can fail [full text, Section 3]. Under a linear outcome model, HT variance drops to the residual variance after regressing on the balanced covariates (Prop 3), i.e. the gain is that of a regression/control-variate adjustment on Z [Section 4.2 / Prop 3]. Steps are sequential over n units.
