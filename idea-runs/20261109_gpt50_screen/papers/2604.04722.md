# Don't Waste Bits! Adaptive KV-Cache Quantization for Lightweight On-Device LLMs

## What this paper does
A learned token-level KV precision controller for on-device LLMs: for each decoded token, a small MLP takes three saliency features -- next-token predictive entropy, token rarity (smoothed self-information of token frequency), and final-layer attention variance averaged over heads -- and picks a K/V precision class from {2, 4, 8, FP16}, trained to trade expected latency against accuracy. Motivated by a Huffman / source-coding analogy. Evaluated on SmolLM-135M/360M/1.7B on commonsense benchmarks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it use attention entropy/concentration or uncertainty to pick KV precision?
Yes, heuristically: predictive entropy H_t of the next-token distribution and attention variance V_t (a "coarse indicator" of sharpness) are inputs to the learned controller [full text, Sec 3.5 Eq 14-16]. There is no derivation linking concentration to K or V error, K and V receive the same class, and the decision is per written token (precision at write time), not per head or per read.

### Goal: Scale of evidence?
Small models only (SmolLM up to 1.7B) on commonsense tasks; e.g. SmolLM-360M on HellaSwag: 17.75% lower ms/token than static KV quantization, +7.60 accuracy points, within 0.30 of FP16 [Sec 4.1; abstract]. No long-context or long-CoT evaluation.
