# RAQ-MLA: Role-Aware Quantization for Multi-Head Latent Attention Caches

## What this paper does
Training-free quantization of the MLA cache (compressed latent c^KV, d_c=512, and decoupled positional key k^R, d_R=64). It analyzes the cache and finds the latent mainly acts as the value while the positional key dominates the attention score. It then fits one invertible transform per cached tensor against the error that tensor delivers to the attention output (attention-weighted value error for the latent, score perturbation for the positional key), folds the inverses into W^UK/W^UV and the query, and reaches about 2.5 bits per element with near-lossless reasoning/coding on four MLA models.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How much of the attention score, and of score-side quantization error, comes from the latent (content) path versus the positional key in a real MLA model?
On GLM-4.7-Flash, pooled over 47 layers, the positional term carries 92.5% of the logit attribution and the content term 7.5%; the k^R outlier channels (a tenth of its 64) carry 63.8% alone [full text, Section 3, Fig 3a, Appendix D]. Under INT4 (latent per token, k^R per channel), the content key's error in the attention weights is 1.9% versus 17.5% for the positional key; at the attention output the latent's error is 1.7% through the content key but 8.3% through the value, while the positional key's reaches 14.7% [full text, Section 3, Fig 3b]. Conclusion: "Within the latent, then, the value readout carries most of its error through to the output, while the attention score is dominated by k^R." This is measured on one model family (GLM) only.
### Goal: How are value-path errors correlated across tokens?
Under the deployed INT2 quantizer, value-readout errors are nearly uncorrelated across tokens (mean off-diagonal |rho| 0.044), though summed off-diagonal terms still give 14.2% of value-path distortion at layer 23 [full text, Appendix D, Fig 9].
### Goal: Does RAQ-MLA give any sound error bound or runtime certificate?
No. It is an offline transform-fitting quantizer with empirical evaluation; Theorem 4.1 concerns the fitting objective, not a runtime certificate [full text, Sections 4.4-4.5].
