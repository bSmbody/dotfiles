# Lasso Screening Rules via Dual Polytope Projection

## What this paper does
Develops safe screening rules (DPP, EDPP) that discard Lasso features certified to have zero coefficients before solving, using the fact that the dual optimum is the projection of y/lambda onto a convex polytope. Nonexpansiveness of projection bounds how far the dual optimum can move when lambda changes, so the dual at a new lambda lies in a ball around the known dual at an old lambda; a feature is discarded if the maximum of its linear test over that ball stays below the activation threshold. A sequential version reuses the exact solution at lambda_k to screen at lambda_{k+1}; extended to group Lasso; speeds solvers by orders of magnitude.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does the sequential rule certify inactivity at a new parameter from the old solution, and what makes it safe?
Theorem 2 bounds the dual movement: ||theta*(lambda) - theta*(lambda0)|| <= |1/lambda - 1/lambda0| ||y||, so theta*(lambda) lies in a ball B(c, rho) centered at the known dual [full text, Section 2.2, Eq 11-12]. Theorem 3: the sup of |x_i^T theta| over the ball equals |x_i^T c| + rho ||x_i|| (center value plus radius times feature norm), so feature i is certified inactive if that stays below 1 [Section 2.2, Eq 13-14]. Corollary 5 (sequential DPP) applies this along a decreasing lambda grid, each step centered at the previous exact solution [Section 2.2]. Safety comes from the rigorous ball containment; heuristic "strong rules" assume nonexpansiveness without proof and must check KKT violations and repair [Section 1].

### Goal: What does the rule need to store and compute per feature, and what governs how many features it can discard?
Per feature only the inner product with the previous dual (a by-product of the previous solve) and the feature norm; the test is one comparison [Section 2.2, Corollary 5]. Discard power depends on the ball radius: small parameter moves give small radii and aggressive screening; EDPP tightens the ball using projections of rays and firm nonexpansiveness to detect far more inactive features [Section 2.3].
