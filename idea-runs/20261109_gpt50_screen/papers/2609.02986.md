# Modern Transformers Are Implicit Hybrids: From Functional Differentiation to Principled Hybrid Architecture Design

## What this paper does
Analyzes heads of RoPE Transformers (Qwen3 series, Llama3.1) with two intervention metrics (RFIS, RPD) and finds a two-type taxonomy, retrieval heads vs positional heads, separated by a mid-low RoPE frequency band (GPBand). From this it derives design principles (local positional modeling, position-independent global retrieval, head-granular and layer-specific allocation) and instantiates a head-wise hybrid (HwH: NoPE full-attention heads + GDN heads within each layer) pretrained from scratch at small scale (380M), evaluated on LM, NIAH retrieval and length extrapolation.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it decide where full attention goes in a hybrid, from what signal, and does it use the model's own long generations or long-CoT reasoning?
Allocation is a fixed design rule derived from offline head analysis of pretrained Transformers, not a per-model search: HwH-std uses GDN only in the first and last layers, NoPE-FA:GDN = 1:3 (by Q/K/V dimension) in the middle half and 1:7 elsewhere [full text, Section 4.1]. The layer-specific rule is validated by ablations (uniform 1:3 vs removing FA in first/last layers vs 1:7 outside the middle) on commonsense averages and NIAH-Single-2 extrapolation at 380M [Section 4.4]. Models are pretrained from scratch, not converted; there is no distillation, no on-policy signal, no per-failure localization, and no long chain-of-thought reasoning evaluation [Sections 4.1-4.4].
