# Stochastic Sparse Attention for Memory-Bound Inference

## What this paper does
SANTA replaces the decode-time softmax-V product with an unbiased Monte Carlo estimate: sample S indices from each head's post-softmax distribution, gather those V rows and average them (no multiplies). S2ANTA adds stratified and systematic (single random offset) sampling to cut variance; GPU kernels (S2ANTA-prop with global sync, S2ANTA-flash with per-tile sampling) give up to 1.5x attention-kernel and 1.25x end-to-end decode speedup on Llama-3.1-8B at 32k. Keys are still read in full; a separate Bernoulli qK^T method sparsifies key features.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does SANTA sample per head independently, and how does it handle GQA V sharing?
Yes. Sampling is defined per query/head: S indices i.i.d. (or stratified/systematic with one offset U per query) from that head's A_q [full text, Section 2 / Eq 4; Section 2.1 Construction]; statistics are measured "per head" [App. N.1]. Under GQA the V traffic is the union over the group's samples; the authors bound it: at S=128, 32k context, "even accounting for the worst-case union across Llama 8B's four-query GQA group, this is <=1.56% of value rows" [Section 3.4]; worst case up to 12.5% in another setting [Section 4.3]. No cross-head coupling of samples is proposed; offsets are not stated to be shared across heads.
### Goal: What fraction of decode bytes does SANTA remove, i.e. what is left for a cross-head scheme to save?
Only the V stage is sparsified; computing p_h requires every key, so K traffic stays ~100% [Section 1, Section 2.2, App. E Table 10]. For keys under GQA, per-head Bernoulli feature sampling blows up to the group union (67.5% per head -> 97.9% per group), so they use a mean group query to share key access [Section 5.1, App. C.2] — a group-sharing device, but on the K side and biased by construction.

# Stochastic Sparse Attention for Memory-Bound Inference (SANTA)

## What this paper does
Training-free decode-time attention that computes the exact post-softmax distribution p (full key scan), then samples S << n_k indices from p and averages the gathered V rows, giving an unbiased estimate of pV with variance ~1/S. Variance-reduced variants S^2ANTA use stratified or systematic sampling of the CDF. CUDA kernels give ~1.5x decode-step attention-kernel speedup over FlashInfer/FlashDecoding at 32K (RTX 6000 Ada, Llama-3.1-8B shapes) and ~1.25x end-to-end batched decode. A separate Bernoulli qK^T estimator sparsifies key features. ICML 2026.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does SANTA already sample V rows from the exact attention distribution p (full key scan) with unbiasedness and variance reduction?
Yes. It "treat[s] A as a categorical distribution over keys and sample[s] S indices i.i.d.", fetching only those V rows: o_hat = (1/S) sum V_{i_s} [full text, Section 2 / Eq 4]; unbiased with variance scaling 1/S [Appendix A.1-A.2]. S^2ANTA-strat/sys split the CDF into S equal-mass strata, one draw per stratum (sys: one shared offset) [Section 2.1]; only strat has a proven variance-reduction guarantee [Section 2.1, App. A.5]. No value-moment constraint or control variate is used.

### Goal: What speedups and where do savings come from?
Savings are on the value stage only: "S/n_k reduction in post-softmax additions and reads to rows of V" [Section 2.2]; keys are still all read (SANTA++ notes SANTA's full-key scan is 50% of KV access [2609.35629, Section 3]). Accuracy-matching operating points S=128 (prop) and S=2048 (flash) give ~1.50x kernel speedup at 32K [Section 3.4 / Fig 4] and up to 1.25x end-to-end decode latency at batch 6, 30K prompt, 2048 output tokens [Section 3.5 / Table 3]. Reasoning tests are GSM8K/MMLU with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, not long-CoT [Section 4]; top-k is competitive on short-context GSM8K [Section 6, App. H].
