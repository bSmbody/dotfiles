# Smooth Model Predictive Path Integral Control without Smoothing

## What this paper does
Proposes Smooth MPPI (SMPPI). It lifts the control input to its derivative inside Model Predictive Path Integral control, so that sampled control sequences come out smooth without an external smoothing filter. The information-theoretic derivation of MPPI is kept. The paper evaluates on an inverted-pendulum swing-up and on aggressive autonomous driving with a learned neural-network vehicle dynamics model, and reviews the standard MPPI update in its background section.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What is the core MPPI mechanism: how are sampled rollouts combined into the next control, and why do all samples contribute?
MPPI samples K noisy control sequences around the current mean sequence U, v_t = u_t + eps_t, and rolls each out through the (possibly learned, non-differentiable) dynamics. The new control is the importance-weighted average of all sampled perturbations, u_t <- u_t + sum_k w(E^k) eps_t^k, with w proportional to exp(-(C(V^k) - beta)/lambda) [full text, Section II, Eqs 8-10]. The weights come from the free-energy/KL derivation: the optimal control distribution q* is proportional to exp(-S/lambda) p, and importance sampling estimates its mean from samples of the current distribution [Section II, Eqs 3-7]. No sample is discarded by a hard rule. Every rollout enters the update with a soft, exponentiated weight, and beta (the minimum cost) is subtracted only for numerical stability [Section II].

### Goal: What makes MPPI practical: parallel sampling, receding horizon, warm start?
MPPI "has benefitted greatly from recent advances in GPUs, as a large number of samples can be computed in parallel to achieve better real-time performance" [full text, Section I]. Its "key property ... is to draw samples upon the previously optimized control sequence", that is, a warm start from the last plan [Section I]. Rollouts run over a finite horizon T and are re-optimised at every control step. The dynamics used for rollouts can be a learned neural network: the driving experiments train one [Section V-B].

### Goal: What are MPPI's stated weaknesses?
Sampling makes the commands chatter, which burdens the actuators [Section I, III-A]. Warm-starting from the previous sequence can stop the sequence from converging quickly when the environment changes fast [Section I]. Reducing the sampling variance smooths actions but stops MPPI from responding to rapid changes, while increasing it causes chattering [Section III-B]. External smoothing filters can break optimality or constraints [Section III-A].
