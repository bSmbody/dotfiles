# An Empirical Study of Example Forgetting during Deep Neural Network Learning

## What this paper does
Defines a forgetting event for a training example as a transition from correctly to incorrectly classified between consecutive SGD updates, and an unforgettable example as one that, once learned, is never forgotten. It shows that forgetting statistics are stable across seeds and architectures, that forgettable examples act like support vectors, and that unforgettable examples can be removed from the training set with little loss: 30-35% of CIFAR-10, 50% of permuted MNIST and 80% of MNIST.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism (forgetting events / unforgettable examples) and why does it identify examples a learner can do without?
Forgetting is tracked per example along the learner's own SGD trajectory: acc_i^t > acc_i^{t+1} is a forgetting event; "unforgettable" means learned at some t* and correct for all k >= t* [full text, Section 3]. The ordering is stable (Pearson 89.2% seed to seed, 97.6% between 5-seed groups) and not a chance effect: shuffled-gradient updates produce at most about two chance forgetting events [Section 4]. In a two-partition continual setup, training on forgettable examples barely hurts the unforgettable ones, which "suggests that examples that have been forgotten at least once may be able to 'support' those that have never been forgotten" [Section 4.1, Figure 4].

### Goal: What evidence shows that removing unforgettable examples is safe, and what assumption does that rely on?
Removing examples in increasing order of forgetting count keeps generalization: up to 30% of CIFAR-10 with comparable accuracy, 35% with <0.2% degradation, while random removal drops accuracy quickly [Section 5, Figure 5 left, Figure 6]. The assumption is that what the learner itself retains stably throughout its own updates carries little information that is not already represented, while what it repeatedly loses is the informative "support-vector" part [Section 5]. Caveat: the most-forgotten tail includes outliers and noise that hurt performance [Section 5, Figure 5 right]. The ordering transfers across architectures (ResNet18 to WideResNet) [Section 6].
