# PICASO: Permutation-Invariant Context Composition with State Space Models

## What this paper does
ICLR 2025. For SSMs (Mamba-style, diagonal transitions), maps a database of retrieved contexts to precomputed states and composes several of them into one initial state that approximates processing the concatenated contexts. CASO chains states with the decay of later contexts; PICASO averages CASO over all (S_n) or cyclic (C_n) orderings, computed in polynomial/linear time via elementary symmetric polynomials when transitions commute. Zero-shot it approaches concatenation (WikiText, MSMARCO) at 5.4x speed; fine-tuning through the composition (BPTC/BP2C) closes the gap.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does PICASO compose states from several contexts, and what does it assume about the transitions?
CASO: x(u) = x(u_n) + sum_{i<n} A(u_n)...A(u_{i+1}) x(u_i) [full text, Section 3.3, Prop 1, Eq 3], exact for one layer, approximate for stacked layers. PICASO averages CASO over permutations; the efficient forms require that "the matrices A(u_i) commute (e.g., are diagonal)" (Prop 2) or are invertible (Prop 3, cyclic) [Section 3.3]. Non-commuting (DeltaNet/GDN Householder) transitions are not treated; extension to attention hybrids is left as future work [Section 7].
### Goal: Does PICASO give an order-sensitivity (commutator-like) quantity, and are composed states in-distribution?
Prop 4 bounds the distance between the two orderings of two contexts by ||(I-A(u'))x(u)||^2 + ||(I-A(u))x(u')||^2, i.e. order sensitivity vanishes as decay approaches identity, and CASO states lie closer together than independent states, which they credit for why averaging works ("approximate linear structure of the state space is only valid locally") [Section 4, Prop 4, Fig 2]. Composed states are "out-of-distribution for the model, since these states do not arise from any sequence of input tokens"; zero-shot PICASO lags concatenation (WikiText 10.1% vs 11.1% improvement over baseline) and fine-tuning is needed to match it [Section 6.3.1-6.3.2].
