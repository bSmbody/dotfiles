# LayerBoost: Layer-Aware Attention Reduction for Efficient LLMs

## What this paper does
Converts Qwen3-4B (36 layers) into a hybrid with three layer types: kept softmax attention, sliding-window attention (window 64), and attention removed (identity). Layers are assigned by a per-layer sensitivity analysis (remove one layer's attention, measure the drop on commonsense benchmarks) followed by a budget-constrained keep/revert search on downstream benchmarks, then a short "healing" distillation (40M Dolma-3 tokens, CE + attention-map KL). Reports small degradation on commonsense/MMLU and serving speedups in vLLM.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal decides which layers keep full attention, is it computed on the model's own generations, and how is the budget set?
Sensitivity S_l = E(M) - E(M_{-l}), where M_{-l} replaces layer l's attention by identity and E is the average of PIQA, WinoGrande, ARC-e, ARC-c [full text, Section 3.2 / Eq 3; Appendix A.1 / Eq 8]. A constrained search then modifies layers progressively, keeping a modification only if downstream scores do not drop significantly [Section 3.2]. The full-attention budget is fixed by hand at 33% (12 of 36 layers) [Section 4.1]. The signal is short multiple-choice benchmark accuracy on the pretrained model before any training, not long generations; the Limitations section says benchmark-driven sensitivity may not capture long-form reasoning [Section 5]. No long-CoT benchmark is evaluated.

# LayerBoost: Layer-Aware Attention Reduction for Efficient LLMs

## What this paper does
LayerBoost scores each layer of a pretrained Transformer by the drop in average commonsense benchmark accuracy (PIQA, WinoGrande, ARC) when its attention is replaced by identity, then under a fixed full-attention budget keeps softmax attention in high-sensitivity layers, uses a 64-token sliding window in moderate ones and removes attention in low ones, accepting modifications via a progressive accept/revert search on benchmarks, followed by a 10M-token distillation "healing" phase.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it choose which layers keep full attention, and on what signal?
Single-layer ablation (attention to identity) scored by short commonsense multiple-choice benchmark accuracy of the unmodified pretrained model [full text, Section 3.2 / Eq 3; Appendix A.1, Eq 8]; then a budgeted progressive accept/revert search over configurations evaluated on downstream benchmarks [Section 3.2]. The full-attention budget is fixed by hand [Section 3.2]. No student rollouts, no on-policy stage, and no per-example localization.

### Goal: Does it evaluate long-form reasoning, and what limitations does it state?
It states that benchmark-driven sensitivity "may not fully capture all behaviors, especially for out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., long-form reasoning ...)", that configurations are model-specific, and that the heuristic has no optimality guarantee [Section 5].
