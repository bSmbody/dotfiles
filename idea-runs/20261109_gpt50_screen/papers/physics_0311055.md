# Improved neighbor list algorithm in molecular simulations using cell decomposition and data sorting method

## What this paper does
Molecular-dynamics paper on avoiding O(N^2) pair-distance evaluation. Reviews the Verlet neighbor table (each atom keeps a list of atoms within cutoff plus a "skin", rebuilt periodically or when displacements exceed a threshold) and cell linked lists, then combines them: cell decomposition to build lists in O(N), partial updating of the list only for cells whose atoms moved too far (dirty flags), and data sorting for cache locality. Reports 2-3x speedups over conventional algorithms for small and large systems.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the Verlet skin mechanism, what guarantees correctness between rebuilds, and what is the trade-off?
The cutoff sphere r_cut is surrounded by a skin r_s; atom j enters i's list if r_ij <= r_cut + r_s. The skin "should be large enough, so that between two times of neighbor list reconstruction no atom can penetrate through the skin into the cutoff sphere of another atom if it is not in the neighbor list" [full text, Section 2.1]. Lists are updated "periodically in a fixed interval, or automatically when the displacements of some atoms are larger than a certain value" [Section 2.1]. Force evaluation then costs O(N * N_NL) instead of O(N^2); the list holds only a few extra atoms (those in the skin shell). Trade-off: with more mobile atoms, either the skin or the rebuild frequency must grow [Section 2.1].

### Goal: How does partial updating avoid full rebuilds when only some particles move?
Each cell carries a "dirty flag"; every timestep accumulated displacements are checked per cell, and if the sum of the two largest displacements exceeds r_s, the flag of that cell and its neighbors is set and only those lists are rebuilt [Section 2.3.2]. This lets the skin be small (efficient force evaluation) without frequent global rebuilds, at the cost of storing per-atom lists separately [Section 2.3.2].
