# WakeKV: Reactive, Reversible KV Residency for Heads That Change Their Minds

## What this paper does
Measures that most attention heads change which positions they read during generation (63-85% have at least one drift event) across needle retrieval, long CoT and multi-turn recall with 1.5B-8B models, so one-time head classification is wrong. Finds that cheap signals cannot predict head "wake-ups" well enough to prefetch. WakeKV keeps an LRU GPU-resident page set per head and demotes evicted pages to a CPU reservoir holding full uncompressed state, fetching them back on demand. Improves miss rate over frozen classification, destructive eviction, SnapKV, R-KV and ReasonAlloc at matched budget.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
Per-head recency (LRU) of reads over resident pages; least-recently-used page demoted to CPU reservoir at full precision; a later want of a non-resident page is a miss that is fetched back over PCIe [full text, Section 3]. Fully reversible.

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Four candidate wake-up signals (page-overlap change, windowed-median drift, entropy trend, needle-mass delta) reach only precision 0.08-0.20 at recall 0.41-0.70; a just-generated-token marker reaches F1 0.53-0.62 on CoT but nothing survives on NIAH/multi-turn; wake-ups are bursty and a coarse trigger catches 54% at a 2%-of-steps rate. "Neither cheap proactive route proved reliable enough to prefetch" [Section 2.2]. Limitations: simulator counts misses not stall time; narrow real-system result (one model, one A30) [Section 6].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Keeps the full state of every page (GPU or CPU), so total KV memory is not reduced, only GPU residency; relies on demand being observable to trigger a fetch.
