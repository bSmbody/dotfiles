# It's All Connected: A Journey Through Test-Time Memorization, Attentional Bias, Retention, and Online Optimization

## What this paper does
Miras recasts sequence models (Transformers, Titans, modern linear RNNs) as associative memories that optimise an internal "attentional bias" objective, and recasts forgetting as a retention regulariser. A model is specified by four choices: memory structure, attentional bias, retention gate and memory learning algorithm. The paper proposes new attentional biases (l_p, Huber) and new retention gates (f-divergence/KL on a simplex, elastic net, l_q, Bregman), and instantiates them as the Moneta, Yaad and Memora models.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Do any of the proposed retention gates weight retention by how the memory has been read (queries), or by a key-space importance?
No. The retention gates are a local premetric D_t(W, W_{t-1}) plus a global regulariser G_t(W) [full text, Sections 3.2-3.3]. The proposed variants are elementwise or norm-based: KL/f-divergence on a scaled simplex giving a softmax-of-log update [Section 5.2, Variant 1 / Eq 18-21], elastic net giving soft thresholding plus multiplicative decay [Variant 2-3 / Eq 22-23], l_q norms [Variant 4], and separable Bregman divergences [Variant 5]. All of them are built from the current state and the current pair's gradient. None uses a statistic of past queries or a matrix-valued (key-space) weighting. The framework does let a quadratic retention premetric take any metric, which is where a read-weighted one would fit.
