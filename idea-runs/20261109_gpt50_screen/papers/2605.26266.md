# Quantized Keys Steal Attention: Bias Correction for KV-Cache Compression in Video Diffusion

## What this paper does
Shows that zero-mean key-quantization noise delta_i = q.eps_i/sqrt(d) inflates each quantized token's softmax mass by E[e^delta] >= 1 (the "Jensen bias"), so quantized cached keys steal attention from unquantized current-chunk keys. It removes the bias in expectation by subtracting b_i = log E[e^delta_i] from each cached score; under the uniform rounding-noise model this has a closed form (sum of log sinh(a)/a) and a second-order Taylor form b_i ~ (1/24d) sum_c q_c^2 Delta_{i,c}^2 = sigma_i^2/2, computed from the existing step sizes and the query, with no extra storage. It is evaluated at INT2 on three AR video diffusion models, plus a small LLM partial-prefill NLL diagnostic.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What exactly is the correction, and is it the "exp(q.mu + q^T Sigma q / 2)" cell-variance correction?
Yes. The model is k_hat = k + eps with eps_c ~ U(-Delta/2, Delta/2), independent across channels (Sec 4.1, Eq 5-7). Correction is b_i = log E[e^{delta_i}] (Eq 10), exact form Eq 12, Taylor form Eq 13. Sec 4.2 notes the Taylor term equals sigma_i^2/2 by the cumulant generating function (Eq 14), so it holds for any zero-mean noise with known score variance. This is exactly the within-cell second-moment logit correction exp(q.mu + q^T Sigma q/2) with a diagonal Sigma = Delta^2/12. Grouped per-token and QuaRot (Hq) variants are given (Eq 15, App F); a per-channel variant is in App E. No extra memory; the overhead is negligible next to QK^T (Sec 4.3, App B).
### Goal: Does it treat V, or the K-V joint cell?
No. Only key scores are corrected; values are used as their dequantized reconstruction (Sec 4, App D pseudocode). There is no joint (K,V) codebook and no E[e^{qK} V | C] term.
### Goal: Does it apply to single-token LLM decoding / long CoT?
It is not tested there. The LLM evidence is teacher-forced NLL on LongBench-Pro prompts under chunked partial prefill (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-32B; chunks 128-8192), where the correction reduces INT2's NLL degradation (Sec 5.4, App O). The authors state that "standard single-token decoding offers less headroom for the correction because many cached tokens compete with only one unquantized current token", and that the gain is limited when attention is concentrated on few cached tokens (Sec 6, Limitations). There is no generation, reasoning benchmark or throughput result.
