# Retrieval-Aware Distillation for Transformer-SSM Hybrids

## What this paper does
Converts a pretrained Transformer (Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen-family below 3B) into a Transformer-SSM hybrid that keeps only the attention heads implementing Gather-and-Aggregate retrieval and replaces all other heads with DiscreteMamba2 heads, then distills with a modified MOHAWK pipeline. Keeping about 2% of heads (10-20) recovers over 95% of teacher performance on retrieval-heavy tasks, versus 25-50% of heads for periodic placements, and lets the SSM state shrink by up to 8x.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are the retained attention components selected (single vs joint ablation, teacher vs student, teacher-forced vs on-policy), and is the budget data-driven?
Each teacher head is ablated individually (output masked to zero) on a synthetic key-value retrieval task, and heads are ranked by the accuracy drop; the top-k are kept, with k a user-chosen budget [full text, Sections 4.1-4.2]. Selection is single-component, on the teacher, before distillation, on a synthetic teacher-forced probe, with no joint or interaction test and no on-policy signal. After distillation, ablating the student's heads shows 11 of the 20 kept heads remain critical for retrieval [Appendix A.3.2].

### Goal: Does it evaluate long generation or reasoning, and what limitations does it state?
No long chain-of-thought or reasoning-rollout evaluation; models are below 3B. The Conclusion lists as limitations that the synthetic KV probe may miss components needed by other retrieval settings such as multi-hop retrieval, and that scale transfer is untested [Section 6].
