# DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression

## What this paper does
Technical report of an open-weight multimodal MoE (552B backbone, 16B active in decode, 8B in prefill via a Causal Encoder-Decoder) with 1M context. KV compression is built into the architecture: Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2) shares main KV and indexer K across layers (Full / Reindex / Reuse modes) with a hierarchical sparse indexer, and the main KV cache is stored in FP4 (E2M1, one E4M3 scale per 16 channels) trained by QAT during post-training, while SWA KV stays FP8. Global KV is 890 bytes/token, about 1/4 of DeepSeek-V4-Flash; SWA Bounded Replay cuts the persistent (SSD/host) KV to about 1/8.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is the KV cache quantized, and what accuracy cost is stated?
FP4 main KV via QAT, quantized after RoPE; non-RoPE and RoPE parts share the format; NVFP4-like without the second-level global scale, justified by the RMSNorm-bounded latent magnitude (<= ~22.6); "We retain FP8 for the SWA KV cache due to its sensitivity to quantization" [full text, Section 2.4.4]. MXFP4 is used for indexer Q/K for hardware portability "despite the higher accuracy of alternative formats" [Section 2.4.4]. Accuracy cost: "FP4 global KV caches during training with only marginal performance degradation" — no separate ablation number found [Section 1].
### Goal: What offloading / persistent-cache design is used?
Global KV kept on SSD for >=72 h; SWA KV moved to a short-TTL host-DRAM pool; on SWA miss, Encoder SWA Bounded Replay recomputes only n_win tokens instead of L x n_win, accepting approximate states; decoder SWA is likewise replayed approximately and simulated in post-training [Sections 3.2.1-3.2.2].
### Goal: What limitations do the authors state, and what is released?
"Potential selection errors in CSA2 and approximate state reconstruction in SWA Bounded Replay may still cause capability degradation in untested boundary cases"; they will stress-test "sparse retrieval over long contexts and SWA state reconstruction at cache-resumption boundaries" [Section 6]. Weights at https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash [abstract]; kernels named as FlashMLA, DeepGEMM, TileKernels, DeepSelect [Section 3.2]. No post-hoc KV quantization of this model is studied.

# DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression

## What this paper does
Technical report of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (552B backbone, 1M context, multimodal). It replaces V4's CSA/HCA hybrid with pure Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2): cross-layer sharing of main KV and indexer K with three static per-layer modes (Full, Reindex, Reuse), a Hierarchical Sparse Indexer whose first Full layer builds a candidate pool for later Reindex layers, FP4 (E2M1 + E4M3 scale per 16 channels) main KV via QAT, and a Causal Encoder-Decoder (decoder global KV projected from the last encoder layer; 8B active in prefill, 16B in decode). Deployment adds SWA Bounded Replay so SWA KV need not be persisted.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the CSA2 structure and what still grows with context per decoded token?
Modes: Full computes main KV, indexer K and fresh top-K; Reindex reuses a previous layer's main KV + indexer K but rescoring with its own indexer Q; Reuse reuses both KV and top-K indices; every layer keeps its own query and SWA KV [full text, Section 2.3.1]. Config: encoder 2 SWA layers + 18 CSA2 layers (m=2) in 3 groups of 1 Full + 5 Reuse; decoder 20 CSA2 layers (m=1) in 5 groups of 4 (first group Full+3 Reuse, others Reindex+3 Reuse); 32 indexer heads x 128 dims, top-k 512, n_win 128; hierarchical pool up to 2,048 blocks x 8 = 16,384 candidates [Section 4.2.1]. The authors state the remaining linear term: "the remaining indexers still score the full causally visible context. For extremely long contexts, this cost remains a major computational bottleneck"; with the hierarchical pool, deeper indexers become constant per query but "the first Full Mode layer still scans the entire causally visible range" [Section 2.3.2]. Decode FLOPs rise only 1/4 from 4K to 1M [Section 1, Figure 2].

### Goal: What KV byte numbers are reported?
Global KV (always in HBM) is 890 bytes per token, about 1/4 of V4-Flash; persistent KV (SSD/host) about 1/8 of V4-Flash with SWA Bounded Replay [Abstract; Section 6]. FP4 main KV nearly halves storage relative to V4's FP8; SWA KV kept in FP8 "due to its sensitivity to quantization" [Section 2.4.4]. Sparse attention trained from scratch at 64K with no dense warmup [Section 1].

### Goal: What approximations and limitations do the authors state?
SWA Bounded Replay replays only the last n_win tokens instead of L x n_win, so prefix-hit states are "approximate" and outputs "are not mathematically identical across positions", reported as negligible quality impact, with replay simulated in post-training [Section 3.2.2]. Limitations: "Potential selection errors in CSA2 and approximate state reconstruction in SWA Bounded Replay may still cause capability degradation in untested boundary cases"; future stress tests target "sparse retrieval over long contexts and SWA state reconstruction at cache-resumption boundaries" [Section 6]. It cites IndexCache, YOIO and HySparse as layer-dimension compression prior work [Section 2.3].
