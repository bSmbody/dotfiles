# Multiverse: Your Language Models Secretly Decide How to Parallelize and Merge Generation

## What this paper does
Multiverse fine-tunes a reasoning model to generate in a MapReduce structure. On <Parallel>/<Outline>, the model decomposes the task (Map), decodes branches in parallel (Process), and merges them (Reduce). Multiverse Attention, following APE, starts every branch at the same position index with no cross-branch attention; at Reduce the branch KVs are concatenated and position resumes at the max branch position. Multiverse Engine, built on SGLang with radix-cache prefix sharing, runs the branches as batch members and merges their KV without copying. The model is Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct after 3 hours of SFT on the 1K-example Multiverse-1K set. Code: https://github.com/Multiverse4FM/Multiverse [abstract footnote].

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What accuracy and parallelism are achieved on long reasoning?
At 32K context [Section 6.2, Table 2], Multiverse-32B vs an Autoregressive-32B baseline trained on the same data: AIME24 53.8 vs 54.6, AIME25 45.8 vs 45.0, MATH500 91.8 vs 92.8, GPQA-D 60.7 vs 61.6. The degree of parallelism (generated tokens / sequential length) is only 1.15-1.18.

### Goal: What wall-clock speedup, and how does it vary with length and batch?
At bs=1 with 8K/16K/32K generations, most samples fall in the 1.0-1.3 parallelism range and average 18.5% speedup. Highly parallel examples reach up to 2.1x [Section 7, Fig 15]. At a fixed 4K output, speedup "scales linearly with the degree of parallelism" for batch 1 to 128, because decoding stays memory-bound [Section 7, Fig 16].

### Goal: Limitations and KV implications?
[Section 9]: application outside LLM reasoning is unexplored; training is SFT-only, and RL to encourage more parallelism is future work. In the observed data parallelism is low (≈1.15x), so the gain is bounded. Parallel paths share the prefix KV, but each path adds its own KV, and the merged sequence keeps all branch KVs. The total KV is therefore not reduced; only sequential latency is [Section 5.3, by construction].
