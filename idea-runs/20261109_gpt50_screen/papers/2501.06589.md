# Ladder-Residual: Parallelism-Aware Architecture for Accelerating Large Model Inference with Communication Overlapping

## What this paper does
ICML 2025. An architecture change that lets the tensor-parallel AllReduce of block i overlap with block i+1: block i+1 takes x_{i-1} as input instead of x_i, so each block's output reaches the next block one block late. Ladder models are trained from scratch at 1B/3B, and the upper half of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is retrofitted and then lightly retrained. A 70B Ladder model gets about 30% token/s speedup at TP=8.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the delayed-signal mechanism and what does it buy?
x_i^* = h_i(x_{i-2}); x_i = AllReduce(x_i^*) + x_{i-1}. The residual stream still carries every block's output, one block late [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 2]. On Llama-70B at batch 1, TP=8, decode latency improves 23.7% (P2P=1) / 37.7% (P2P=0) and token/s 30.8% / 59.9% [Table 2].
### Goal: What happens when a pretrained model is made delay-tolerant without retraining, and how much retraining recovers it?
Applying Ladder to layers 16-32 of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct zero-shot drops the average from 56.11 to 41.65, and GSM8K from 84.99 to 10.54 ("a huge performance drop mainly on generative tasks"). SFT on 3B tokens for 2 epochs recovers 57.61 (GSM8K 86.81). Adapting 20 layers recovers less (53.86, GSM8K 76.19). Touching the lower layers "can destroy knowledge that is hard to recover without large-scale retraining" [Section 4.2, Table 4].
