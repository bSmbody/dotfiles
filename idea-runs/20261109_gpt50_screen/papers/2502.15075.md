# Quantize What Counts: More for Keys, Less for Values

## What this paper does
The paper argues, from weight-norm training dynamics and a norm-based quantization error bound, that key projections have larger Frobenius/spectral norms than value projections, so under a fixed memory budget giving keys more bits than values (e.g. K4V2) strictly reduces quantization error. Validated on Llama-3, Mistral and other families with short-context benchmarks; integrates with rotation methods.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is its K-vs-V allocation principle, and does it depend on attention distribution?
Purely energy-based: E||K||_F^2 > E||V||_F^2 because ||W^K|| > ||W^V||, and uniform b-bit MSE is Theta(||M||_F^2 2^-2b), so bits should follow energy [full text, Sec 3.1-3.2]. It ignores how attention weights reshape K and V error (no softmax, no concentration, no per-head variation) and the claim is a global K-over-V preference, not per head.

### Goal: Scope of evidence?
All evaluations use a maximum context of 2,000 tokens [Limitations]; no long-CoT or long-context results. Key-favored allocations (e.g. 4-bit K, 2-bit V) retain up to 98.3% of uniform-allocation accuracy while saving memory [abstract]. Note that later long-CoT papers disagree in part: KV-COBRA finds V-heavy splits better in its SVD setting, while PN-Mix and MixKVQ agree K is more sensitive at 2 bits.
