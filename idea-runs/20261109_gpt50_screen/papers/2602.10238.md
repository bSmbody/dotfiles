# Learning to Evict from Key-Value Cache

## What this paper does
Introduces KV Policy (KVP): one lightweight 2-layer MLP agent per KV head (112 agents for Qwen2.5-7B) that ranks cached tokens by predicted future utility using only key, value and position — no queries or attention scores at inference. Agents are trained offline with REINFORCE/RLOO on precomputed traces, using a reward equal to the negative normalized area under the cost-vs-budget curve, where cost is the future attention mass of evicted tokens. Because the reward is additive over tokens, one ranking serves all budgets. Outperforms heuristics on RULER (to 128K) and OASST2.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
Score f(x_i) = MLP(k_i, v_i, pos_i) per head, with a Plackett-Luce/Gumbel-sort stochastic ranking during training [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 3.2-3.3]; at inference the trailing entries are evicted to meet the budget (first 4 and last 16 always retained) [Appendix A.2]. Eviction is permanent; the paper says heuristic policies lead to "an irrecoverable loss of critical information" [Section 1].

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Argues heuristics are backward-looking ("they assume that what was important before will remain important") [Section 1]. Limitations: uniform budget across heads/layers; "cross-head dependencies are not modeled"; the reward uses "future attention as a proxy for task-level utility rather than a direct downstream signal" [Section 5, Limitations]. Supervised ranking losses fail because per-head importance is heavy-tailed and dominated by sinks [Section 4.2, Appendix A.5].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Explicitly assumes per-token additivity: the per-budget cost is the sum over evicted tokens of their future attention [Eq 3.4], and nestedness of optimal sets "holds in our setting because the reward is additive over tokens", which is what makes eviction equivalent to ranking [Section 3, Proposition 1]. Agents are per-head and trained independently [Section 3.2, Appendix A.6]. Training is fully offline on static precomputed traces with "no additional LLM inference"; future tokens are fixed [Section 3.2, Algorithm 1].

# Learning to Evict from Key-Value Cache

## What this paper does
KVP learns KV-cache eviction for softmax-attention LLMs as a ranking problem. A small per-KV-head MLP scores each cached token from its key, value and position only; a Plackett-Luce policy sampled with Gumbel-sort is trained offline with REINFORCE (RLOO baseline) on precomputed Q/K/V traces. The reward is the negated normalised area under the cost-versus-budget curve, where cost is the future attention mass of evicted tokens. It beats heuristic baselines on RULER up to 128K and OASST2.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is learned, from what signal, and does the learned policy's own eviction change the state it later sees?
The scorer f(k_i, v_i, pos_i) gives one budget-agnostic ranking, which works because nested optimal sets imply a total order (Proposition 1) [full text, Section 3, Prop 1, Section 3.1]. The supervision is future attention from the next f tokens of the static training trace, so training needs no LLM inference [full text, Sections 3.1.1, 3.2]. Training is offline over fixed traces. Cached entries are immutable: KVP's scores depend only on static token features, so already-cached tokens keep valid scores as the sequence grows, and the method does not model how evictions alter later inputs [full text, Sections 3.2, 5]. Budgets are uniform across heads and layers. Cross-head dependencies are not modelled, and joint optimisation "would require online LLM inference" [full text, Section 5].
