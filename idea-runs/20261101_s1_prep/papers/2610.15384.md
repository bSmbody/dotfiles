# E8 Lattice Key Quantization for Low-Bit KV-Cache Compression

## What this paper does
The paper vector-quantizes keys with the E8 lattice (8-dim blocks, the densest 8-D sphere packing) at a 2-bit key payload, with scalar 2-bit values (k2v2). Table-based asymmetric scoring computes q·k from a precomputed query lookup table without dequantizing the codebook, so decode throughput matches scalar kernels. The claim is that structured VQ exploits correlation across adjacent dimensions and achieves higher SQNR than scalar quantization at equal rate.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Headline numbers?
E8 k2v2 gives about 6x KV compression, about 20% smaller than scalar k3v2, with near-baseline long-context perplexity. It beats scalar k3v2 in 80% of model-dataset-context perplexity settings and beats KIVI k2v2 everywhere in the perplexity suite (Abstract, Section 4). Single-sequence decode throughput on an A100 is at parity with fp16, KIVI k2v2 and scalar k3v2 within ±0.2 tok/s (Section 4.5, Table 3).
### Goal: Long-CoT relevance and limitations?
Evaluation is perplexity-centric over five LLM families. No AIME or long-generation evaluation was found. Throughput is measured only for a single sequence on an A100 path; paged-attention runtimes and other accelerators are future work (Section 4.5). The quality evaluations use dense reconstruction for batched teacher forcing (Section 3).
