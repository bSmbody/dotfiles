# Sparrow: Sparse Rollout for Stable and Efficient Long-context RL of Large Language Models

## What this paper does
Studies block-top-k sparse attention used only for RL rollouts of Qwen3 thinking models (1.7B-14B, up to ~40K generation), with dense-attention training. It shows that instability is driven by the lower tail of per-token actor-policy mismatch, not its mean. It schedules sparsity to hold the 5-percentile acceptance rate near a threshold (~0.86), giving 2.0-2.4x rollout speedup. It also adds DistillSparse, a LoRA used only during rollouts and distilled on-policy toward the dense log-probs that dense training already computes.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are sparse-attention errors distributed over query tokens in long CoT?
They are highly skewed. Under the same sparsity and length, "the dominant majority of tokens see their per-token acceptance rate above 0.999, perfectly aligned with the dense policy", even at a KV budget of 512, and only a minor portion of tokens behave poorly [full text, Section 3.1; Figure 1(b)]. The mean acceptance rate barely correlates with length (r ≈ -0.1), while the 5-percentile tail does (r = -0.55) and governs collapse [Section 3.1-3.2]. The method is block top-k with page size 16 (Quest-style) [Appendix D].

### Goal: Does this setting have a dense teacher available, and at what cost?
Yes. Training is dense, so dense log-probs on the sparse rollouts are computed anyway. DistillSparse reuses them as the distillation target "without significant additional cost" [Section 4.5, Algorithm 1]. Measured overhead: the dense policy update is ~10% of the step and sparse distillation adds ~55% to it, for a 1.72x end-to-end speedup [Appendix D]. So in sparse-rollout/dense-training RL, the exact dense signal is essentially free, and a sparse-only selector-feedback method has no edge there.
