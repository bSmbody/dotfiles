# KV-Rescue: Recovering Reasoning Language Model KV Eviction Loss via Stepwise Interleaving

## What this paper does
Argues that accuracy lost to KV eviction is an information gap rather than a capability gap: an evicted 7B model and a full-cache 1.5B model make complementary errors, and an oracle choice between them recovers 79% of the eviction loss. KV-Rescue interleaves reasoning steps from the evicted base model and a small full-context helper under PRM-guided stepwise best-of-N, and prunes degenerate base candidates online with entropy and gzip-ratio detectors. Recovers 87% of the accuracy lost at budget 64 on five math benchmarks with Qwen2.5-Math 7B/72B.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
It does not change the eviction rule; it uses existing scores (R-KV, SnapKV) with TopB retention, noting that "once an entry is evicted, subsequent decoding steps cannot access it without recomputation or retrieval" [full text, Preliminaries, Eq 1]. Recovery comes from a separate full-context small model, not from the cache.

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Aggressive eviction causes trajectory-level degeneration: incoherent high-entropy text or confident repetition loops that run to the length limit [Preliminaries, Reasoning Model Degeneration, Fig 2-3]. The helper supplies 17-34% of committed steps, rising from 17% at budget 512 to 33% at budget 64 as more history is lost; a typical failure is the evicted base "loses track of the requested quantity" [Discussion, Fig 6-7].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Training-free; assumes the eviction loss is informational and can be compensated by a second model that keeps full context. Not a method for choosing what to keep.
