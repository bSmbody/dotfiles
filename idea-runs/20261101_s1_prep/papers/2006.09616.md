# Dynamic Tensor Rematerialization

## What this paper does
DTR is an online, greedy checkpointing runtime for training under a memory budget. When memory runs out it evicts resident tensors chosen by a heuristic, and when an evicted tensor is accessed it rematerializes it by replaying the operator that produced it, recursively if that operator's inputs were evicted too. The proposed heuristic evicts the tensor that is stalest, largest and cheapest to recompute, where the recompute cost counts the whole chain of already-evicted tensors linked to it. A union-find approximation makes this cheap. In simulation, chain-aware heuristics reach lower budgets with fewer rematerializations than LRU, size or local-cost rules.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How does DTR value a resident tensor for eviction, and what metadata does it use?
Each tensor t carries staleness s(t) (time since last access), memory m(t) and cost c_0(t), "the time required to compute t from its parent tensor(s)" [full text, Section 2]. The heuristic evicts the tensor minimizing h_DTR(t) = c(t) / [m(t) s(t)], with projected cost c(t) = c_0(t) + sum over t' in e*(t) of c_0(t'). The evicted neighborhood e*(t) is "the set of evicted tensors that would either need to be rematerialized to recompute t or would need t to be resident to be recomputed" [Section 2; Appendix C.2, "Evicted neighborhood"]. Including both directions "penalizes creating long chains of evicted tensors". So a tensor's value depends on which of its ancestors and descendants are already gone: a cheap tensor becomes expensive once its operands have been evicted. Constants cannot be evicted, since no operation rematerializes them [Section 2].

### Goal: Does tracking the evicted neighborhood matter, and how is it approximated cheaply?
Yes. "Heuristics incorporating more information about chain rematerializations (h_DTR, h_DTR^eq, and h_MSPS) can operate on lower budgets and perform fewer rematerializations than heuristics using less information", including h_DTR^local, which uses c_0 only [Section 4.2, Fig 2]. The cheap variant h_DTR^eq drops edge directions and tracks connected components of evicted tensors in a union-find structure with a running cost sum. Eviction unions a tensor with its evicted neighbors. Rematerialization only subtracts its cost and moves it to an empty set, which leaves "phantom dependencies" [Section 4.1; Appendix C.2, "Relaxed (Union-Find) evicted neighborhood"]. It "closely matches" h_DTR with up to two orders of magnitude fewer metadata accesses [Section 4.2].

### Goal: What assumptions does DTR rely on?
Operators are pure, deterministic functions of their arguments; tensors are constants or operator outputs [Section 2]. Rematerialization is exact replay. Banishing (permanently freeing) a tensor makes its children non-rematerializable, which DTR treats as a trade-off separate from eviction [Section 2, "Deallocation"]. If all inputs and outputs of one operation cannot fit, rematerialization fails, so a budget floor exists [Section 2].
