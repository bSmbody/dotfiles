# Kitty: Accurate and Efficient 2-bit KV Cache Quantization with Dynamic Channel-wise Precision Boost

## What this paper does
Builds on KIVI (per-channel K, per-token V, FP16 residual) with two additions: keep the first 32 tokens in full precision, and boost a small fraction (12.5% "Kitty", 25% "Kitty-Pro") of key channels, chosen at runtime by mean magnitude, to INT4 while the rest stay INT2. A page-centric layout decomposes each quantized page into a dense INT2 part plus a sparse INT4 part so that dequantization stays coalesced; a Triton prototype engine runs it.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What accuracy at what effective bits, on which reasoning benchmarks and output caps?
Motivation: KV4 ≈ FP16, while KV2 loses on average −15.23 (Qwen3-8B) and −10.15 (LLaMA3-8B) [full text, Section 2.2, Table 1]. Main evals use lm-eval-harness few-shot CoT (GSM8K 8-shot, MATH-Algebra 4-shot, GPQA 5-shot, HumanEval) with max 4,096 new tokens; AIME24/25 with max 32,768; T=0.6; 3–10 repeats [full text, Section 5.1]. Kitty beats KIVI-K2V2* by 5.17 average points on Qwen3-8B; Kitty-Pro is near FP16 on Qwen3-14B and LLaMA3.3-70B [full text, Section 5.2, Table 3]. On AIME at 32K, KIVI-K2V2 loses ~13 points; Kitty narrows the gap to 3–4 points [full text, Section 5.3, Table 4]. Effective bits ≈2.25 (12.5% boost) / 2.5 (25%) on K, plus residual and sink overhead. No generation-length statistics are given.

### Goal: What serving/system evidence is reported?
Qwen3-8B end-to-end in the authors' prototype engine, fixed max sequence 8,192, batch raised until OOM; baselines are HuggingFace dynamic/static FP16 caches and HF KIVI-INT4. Kitty-Pro allows 8× batch and 2.1–4.1× throughput over FP16 [full text, Section 5.5, Fig. 5]. No vLLM/SGLang comparison, no FP8-KV baseline, 8K length cap, and the authors note CUDA-level work would improve it further [full text, Section 5.5].
