# Hold Onto That Thought: Assessing KV Cache Compression On Reasoning

## What this paper does
A benchmark study of decode-enabled KV eviction (H2O, SnapKV-D, R-KV, KNorm, StreamingLLM, PyramidKV) on long-reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH500 and others) for reasoning models (R1-Distill Llama-8B and Qwen-7B, Nemotron-Nano-8B) and a non-reasoning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Heavy-hitter methods (H2O, SnapKV-D) dominate for reasoning models. Low budgets lengthen reasoning traces, sometimes without termination.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: When eviction drops needed tokens in a reasoning trace, does the output get longer (re-derivation) or does it fail in another way?
Both, in a degraded form. "Lower budgets are capable of triggering longer reasoning traces" [full text, Section 4.5, Figure 4; Appendix A.1, Table 7]. The authors attribute this to "a higher likelihood of critical token eviction, resulting in longer reasoning" [Section 4.7]. The worked example they show is "long circular babble" that "never produces an answer": repeated re-statements of the problem's definitions with "Wait" and "Hold on" [Section 4.7; Appendix A.2]. So the model does try to rebuild lost context in new tokens, but the example is a dead-end loop, not a clean re-derivation from surviving operands. The paper does not measure whether re-derivation succeeds or how its cost depends on what survives.

### Goal: Which tokens matter for reasoning-model eviction, per this study?
Critical tokens (names, quantities, key entities) persist with high accumulated attention through decoding. H2O and SnapKV-D keep them at higher rates, which the authors use to explain why heavy-hitter methods win for reasoning models [Section 4.7; Appendix A.4, Table 8].
