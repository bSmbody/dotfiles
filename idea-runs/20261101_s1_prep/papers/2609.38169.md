# STEPQuant: When and Where Errors Matter in Delta-Rule Recurrent State Quantization

## What this paper does
Spatial-temporal post-training quantization for delta-rule recurrent states (Qwen3.8-27B, Kimi-Linear-48B). Temporal part: lifetime-aware bit allocation that weights per-unit reconstruction distortion by how long errors persist under the retention gate, plus FP16 "pivot" units. Spatial part: key-row-aware dual-axis (row and column) scale fitting. Close to FP32 at a nominal 6-bit budget, with SGLang kernels.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is precision/capacity allocated across state units, and is the allocation fixed or input-dependent?
Bits b_u per unit (a head in Qwen GDN, a key row in KDA) minimize sum_u L_u d_u(b_u) under a budget, with lifetime weight L_u = sum_j exp(2 j l_u) from the mean log retention over calibration tokens [full text, Section 3.2, Eqs 6-8]. "The pivot selection and bit allocation are determined during offline calibration and fixed across requests"; the packed layout is fixed with no per-token allocation [full text, Sections 3.2, 4.4].

### Goal: What error-persistence model does its allocation use, and does it include delta-rule erasure and cross-step error correlation?
Proposition 1 gives E_t = (I - beta_t k_t k_t^T) D_t E_{t-1} + eps_t and notes the delta update reduces error along the current key while "errors in directions rarely aligned with subsequent keys depend mainly on gate decay" [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 5]. The allocation then uses "gate retention alone": L_u = sum_j exp(2 j l_u) with l_u the mean log retention, minimizing sum_u L_u d_u(b_u) [Section 3.2, Eqs 6-8], i.e. decay-only and a sum of per-step squared errors (no cross-step terms). Its own Appendix B.1 shows the delta feedback matters a lot: removing it (exact-read oracle) raises summed output error 26.82x (INT6) and 18.65x (INT8) [Appendix B.1]. Gate half-life still correlates with accumulated INT6 error at Spearman ~0.80 [Section 3.1; Appendix B.5].
### Goal: How does it treat the attention-layer KV cache versus the recurrent state, and is the byte budget shared between them?
Recurrent state only; the bit budget b-bar is over state units [Eq 8]. Attention KV is outside the method.
