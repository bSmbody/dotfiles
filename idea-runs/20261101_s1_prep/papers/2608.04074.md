# Spend Bits Where Queries Look: KV Cache Vector Quantization with Attention-Preserving Transforms (NOVA-KV)

## What this paper does
Frames 2-bit KV quantization as transform coding where distortion is the error in attention products. Derives closed-form, non-orthogonal key transform U with UU^T = c·M_q (M_q = Q^T Q from calibration) so query-weighted logit error becomes plain MSE in the transform domain, then applies MSE-optimal VQ (8-bit index per group of 4 coefficients) to K; V stays INT2 with scale/zero. Proves equal-volume coefficient partitions let equal-size codebooks reach the variable-rate optimum, meeting fixed-width paged layouts. Called "NovaKV" in TaSQ, which uses it as a post-RoPE VQ baseline.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the mechanism and what is the true bit rate?
Key transform from M_q-weighted KLT (non-orthogonal; generalized Parseval) [full text, Sec 3.1, Thm 1]; equal-volume grouping for fixed-rate VQ [Sec 4, Thm 2]. At 128K: K = 2 BPE index + 0.125 per-token BF16 scale; V INT2 = 2.25; average 2.1875, plus BF16 sink(64)+recent(256) band 0.034 → 2.22 BPE; OSCAR is 2.28 BPE [Sec 5, "Bit rate"].
### Goal: How does it do on long-context retrieval and reasoning vs OSCAR/QuaRot/TurboQuant?
RULER-NIAH under chunked prefill (quantize-on-write): Qwen3-8B 128K NOVA-KV 75.4 vs OSCAR 25.3, QuaRot 0.0, BF16 83.4. On GPT-OSS-20B (sliding+full attention MoE) QuaRot 0 and OSCAR ~0 at every length; NOVA-KV 89.6 @8K, 54.0 @128K (BF16 95.8/80.4). Reasoning (GPQA-D, HumanEval, LCB v6, AIME25, MATH500, 5 samples): no detectable drop on Qwen models (McNemar p=0.17/0.24); GPT-OSS-20B mean 72.4 vs BF16 76.5, OSCAR 13.9, TurboQuant(3.25 BPE) 37.4 [Sec 5, "Long context", "Accuracy comparisons", Tables 2-3]. Note the chunked-prefill harness lowers prior methods relative to their papers.
### Goal: What serving / ablation evidence exists?
SGLang decode throughput 1.6–3.4× BF16 at batch 1–4 (30–90K input), within a few % of OSCAR; at 90K BF16 cannot admit batch 16 [Sec 5 "Throughput", Fig 4]. Ablation: NOVA transform with scalar quant collapses to 0; VQ under OSCAR basis gives 62.7@128K; both needed for 75.4 [Table 4]. No per-correct-answer cost or output-length measurement reported.

# Spend Bits Where Queries Look: KV Cache Vector Quantization with Attention-Preserving Transforms (NOVA-KV)

## What this paper does
NOVA-KV treats KV quantization as transform coding in which distortion is measured as error in the attention products. Under a high-resolution model it derives closed-form optimal key and value transforms from calibration statistics. The optimal key transform is non-orthogonal and satisfies a generalized Parseval relation, so attention-aware distortion becomes MSE in the transform domain. MSE-optimal vector quantizers (one 8-bit index per group of 4 coefficients) are then applied to the transformed keys. Equal-volume grouping lets equal-size codebooks reach the variable-rate optimum under a fixed-width layout. Values stay INT2. It is implemented in SGLang.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Bit rate and format?
Keys: 8-bit codebook index per 4 entries (2 BPE) plus per-token BF16 scale. Values: INT2 with scale and zero-point (2.25). With the BF16 sink+recent band (64, 256), the total is 2.22 BPE, versus OSCAR 2.28 (Section 5, Bit rate). Transforms and codebooks are stored per model.
### Goal: Headline numbers on reasoning and long context?
Table 3 (5 samples): Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 AIME25: BF16 70.0, OSCAR 63.3, NOVA-KV 67.3, TurboQuant(3.25 bits) 2.2, QuaRot 0.0; mean drop -1.6 (NOVA) vs -2.6 (OSCAR). GPT-OSS-20B (hybrid sliding-window, MoE): NOVA-KV mean 72.4 vs BF16 76.5, while OSCAR collapses (mean 13.9, AIME25 0.0). RULER NIAH under chunked prefill: Qwen3-8B at 128K: BF16 83.4, NOVA-KV 75.4, OSCAR 25.3, QuaRot 0.0 (Section 5, Long context).
### Goal: Throughput / serving?
SGLang decode throughput is 1.6-3.4x BF16 at batch 1-4 for 30-90K inputs, within a few percent of OSCAR; it is only 1.1-1.5x on GPT-OSS-20B because sliding-window layers dominate. At 90K, BF16 cannot run batch 16 but the 2-bit arms can (Section 5, Throughput). No comparison against an FP8/FP4 KV pool.
### Goal: Limitations / caveats?
Calibration-dependent transforms. The transform collapses under scalar quantization (it compacts rather than flattens energy), so it needs VQ (Table 4). The baselines were reimplemented under chunked prefill and may score lower than in their original papers. No output-length analysis was found in the main text.
