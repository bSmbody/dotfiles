# Rethinking the Role of Efficient Attention in Hybrid Architectures

## What this paper does
A scaling-law and mechanism study of hybrids pretrained from scratch (S1-S5, up to 0.66B, up to 1000N tokens, 16K context) that combine full attention with SWA, Lightning attention, Mamba-2 or Gated DeltaNet. It fits loss and log(LongPPL) scaling curves, restricts receptive fields at inference, probes layers and traces retrieval-head gradients, then runs small design studies: full-to-efficient ratio (1:1 vs 1:3), layer-wise vs head-wise mixing, and NoPE on full-attention layers.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it study the full-attention ratio or placement, and what does it find?
Only the ratio, with fixed interleaving: 1:3 gives the same validation loss as 1:1; for log(LongPPL) the sparser model is worse at small scale but the gap closes as model size grows, "suggesting that full-attention density can be safely reduced once enough full-attention layers are available" [full text, Section 6.1, Figure 6]. Head-wise mixing gives no final advantage over layer-wise, only slower convergence [Section 6.2]. There is no placement search and no per-layer selection.

### Goal: Does the hybrid design still matter after sufficient training?
Mostly not for final capability: hybrids with different efficient-attention modules converge to similar loss and log(LongPPL) as training grows; the design mainly changes how fast long-context ability emerges [Section 4.3, Figure 2]. Restricting full attention to ~2K tokens sharply hurts LongPPL while restricting GDN/SWA barely matters, so long-range information is carried by full attention [Section 5.1, Figure 3].

### Goal: Does it evaluate long chain-of-thought reasoning, conversion, or on-policy training?
No. Models are pretrained from scratch; evaluation is LongPPL, RULER, LongBench and 19 short benchmarks; no distillation, conversion, reasoning-model or on-policy stage [Sections 4.1, 6.3; Limitations].
