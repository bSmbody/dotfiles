# PN-Mix: Pair-Norm Guided Mixed-Precision Key Cache Quantization for Efficient Long-Output LLM Inference

## What this paper does
PN-Mix gives higher precision to post-RoPE key channel pairs with large query-key pair-norm product sigma_{h,i} (mean ||q_i|| over GQA query heads times mean ||k_i||), computed once at prefill and reused across long decoding; sinks (first 32 tokens) and a residual buffer stay FP16; values are uniform low-bit. Derived from a logit perturbation decomposition at RoPE channel-pair level. Evaluated on reasoning/code benchmarks and AIME24/25 with R1-distill and Qwen3 models up to 32K-token outputs, vs ChanMix and Kitty, at about 2.26 average key bits; Triton kernels on one A100.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does it find about K vs V sensitivity on long CoT?
Quantizing only K or only V to 2.25 bits on R1-Llama-8B and Qwen3-8B up to 32K tokens on AIME: V-only degrades less than K-only even without sink protection; with first 32 tokens in FP16, V-only approaches FP16 while K-only still degrades [full text, Sec 4.1 Table 8]. Hence they refine only K. This is a model-level average result; no per-head or per-concentration breakdown.

### Goal: Is allocation static or online, and does importance transfer from prefill to decode?
Static after prefill. Prefill-based pair-norm selection has the highest Jaccard overlap with decode-time salient pairs among criteria tested, overlap does not decrease with decoding progress, and 97-99% of importance sits in the top 50% of pairs [Sec 4.3]. Note this is channel-level saliency (query-energy driven), which is stable, in contrast to token-level importance (RECAP finds token repair importance does not transfer).

### Goal: Results?
At 2.26 average key bits it matches or beats ChanMix/Kitty at 2.32-2.39 bits and degrades gracefully to 1.5 bits [abstract]; highest mean AIME accuracy among non-FP16 methods in 5 of 6 model-benchmark settings [Sec 6.2]. Efficiency only on single A100 [Limitations].

# PN-Mix: Pair-Norm Guided Mixed-Precision Key Cache Quantization for Efficient Long-Output LLM Inference

## What this paper does
PN-Mix is a mixed-precision key-cache quantizer for RoPE models. A logit-perturbation analysis at the RoPE channel-pair level shows that key quantization error matters in proportion to the query-key pair-norm product. During prefill, PN-Mix selects salient RoPE channel pairs once (scored with query and key statistics) and reuses that selection throughout decoding. Salient pairs are stored at high precision (e.g. 3 bits) and the rest at low precision (e.g. 1 bit) in post-RoPE paged layouts. Values use 2-bit quantization. It is EMNLP 2026 work, the closest prior to the "per-channel precision during long output" direction, and it targets long-output (reasoning) inference explicitly.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Headline numbers on reasoning models?
Table 3 (AIME, mean ± std): Qwen3-8B AIME24/AIME25 with bV=2: FP16 76.33 / 67.00; KIVI-2.25 56.33 / 51.33; ChanMix 2.32 bits 60.66 / 57.00; Kitty 2.39 bits 71.33 / 62.00; PN-Mix(1) 2.26 bits 74.67 / 64.00; PN-Mix(2) 2.29 bits 74.33 / 65.33. PolarQuant at 2.25 bits gives 0. PN-Mix is best among the quantized methods in 5 of 6 model-benchmark settings (Section 6.2). The Pareto sweep degrades gracefully down to 1.5 key bits (Section 6.3).
### Goal: Efficiency?
Triton kernel with bH=3, bL=1, bV=2. On an A100-40GB with Qwen3-8B, it supports L_gen=32K at B=32 and B=110 at 8K (8x longer and 5.5x larger than FP16), and reaches 2.37x FP16's maximum per-GPU throughput. FP16 is faster at equal batch when it fits (Section 6.4, App. E).
### Goal: Limitations?
Saliency is fixed at prefill (a static channel set during decode). The method is tied to RoPE pairs. Efficiency is measured only on one A100 with a Triton implementation (Limitations). No generated-length or loop analysis was found. The comparison is against FP16, not against FP8 KV.
