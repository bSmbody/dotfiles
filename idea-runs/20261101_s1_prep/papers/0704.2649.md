# Hedging our bets: the expected contribution of species to future phylogenetic diversity

## What this paper does
Defines the HED index: a species' expected marginal contribution to future phylogenetic diversity (PD) when every other species goes extinct independently with its own probability. PD of a surviving set is the total length of tree edges that connect it to the root, so an edge is kept if any descendant survives. The paper gives a closed-form, polynomial-time formula, compares the resulting ranking with the Shapley index on prosimian primates, and derives HEDGE scores that fold in the focal species' own extinction risk, one for actions that save species and one for actions that cause extinctions.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How is a species' value defined when relatives share branches, and how is it computed?
Delta_PD(S,i) = PD(S ∪ {i}) − PD(S). The HED index psi_i is its expectation over the random surviving set of the other species, with independent extinctions eps_j [full text, Section II, Eq 1]. For rooted trees, Theorem III.1 gives psi_i = sum over the edges e_r on i's path to the root of l(e_r) × prod_{j in C(e_r) − {i}} eps_j. Each shared edge thus counts in full only to the extent that all other carriers below it are likely to be lost. If all relatives surely survive, psi_i is just the pendant edge. If they are threatened, psi_i is "heightened" [Section II, III.1]. The computation is polynomial, not a sum over subsets [Section III].

### Goal: Why does it favor isolated or under-protected species, and what evidence shows the ranking changes?
In the Fig 1 example, D ranks first because it shares its edge only with E, which has high extinction risk, while A shares its path with C, which is safe, so A ranks last [Section III.2]. On prosimians, the Aye-Aye (an isolated lineage) ranks first. The two Arctocebus species fall from 12th (Shapley) to 26th (HED) "because neither is severely threatened, the chances are good that their common path will persist" [Section IV]. Rankings correlate strongly with pendant-edge length (0.98) but depart from it where relatives are endangered [Section IV].

### Goal: How is the focal species' own risk incorporated, and which score fits actions that cause extinctions?
Theorem IV.1: psi'_i = eps_i psi_i is the gain in expected PD from conditioning on i surviving, and psi''_i = (1 − eps_i) psi_i is the loss from conditioning on i going extinct; they sum to psi_i [Section IV.1]. psi' is meant for actions that save species, psi'' for actions that might cause extinctions ("such as building a dam"). Setting eps to 0 or 1 for chosen species and recomputing shows how others' rankings shift [Section IV.1].

### Goal: What assumptions does the index rely on?
Independent (not identical) extinctions [Section II]. Edge lengths represent unique feature diversity and need not be clock-like [Section I]. The index measures expected contribution to future PD and, unlike Shapley, does not split existing PD among present species [Section III.3].
