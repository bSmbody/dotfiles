# RDKV: Rate-Distortion Bit Allocation for Joint Eviction and Quantization of the KV Cache

## What this paper does
Casts KV compression as a rate-distortion problem in which eviction (0 bits) and quantization are two ends of one allocation curve. Per layer-head, V-cache tokens get a weight from the total-variation change in attention when the token is removed (w_t = Σ_τ a_{τ,t}); K-cache channels get weights from Q/K column norms. Reverse water-filling (Lagrangian MCKP over bit set {0,2,4,8,16}, with calibrated per-bit distortion tables) assigns each token (V) or channel (K) a width. Allocation runs once after prefill; a "TriZone" packed layout serves the mixed-bit cache. Evaluated on long-input benchmarks, not long generation.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the allocation objective and solver?
ΔD = Σ_u w_u σ_u 2^{-b_u} s.t. Σ b_u ≤ B; optimum b* = [log2(ln2·w_u σ_u/λ)]_+, so units below the water level are evicted [full text, Sec 3.1, Eq 5, Thm 3.3]. Discrete widths are solved as MCKP by bisection on λ in O(U|B|) [Eq 6]. K is allocated per channel and V per token, per head [Sec 3.2, Fig 2].
### Goal: Is it decode-time / applicable to long CoT?
No. Compression happens once after prefill and is never re-evaluated during decoding; the authors list decode-phase extension as future work [App K]. Spend Bytes on Breadth (2610.05685) runs RDKV's decode mode only approximately byte-matched and modifies its quantizer zero-point.
### Goal: What results and efficiency are reported?
Baselines SnapKV, AdaKV, ThinK(+SnapKV), SnapKV+ZipCache under matched budgets n∈{64..1024}·L on Llama-3.1-8B (plus Mistral-7B, Qwen3-4B, Llama2-13B, Qwen2.5-72B in App D) [Sec 4]. LongBench avg 49.47 at 1024L, within 0.35 of FullKV; +2.7 over best baseline at 64L [Sec 4.1, Table 1]. On A100, decode latency flat at ~18 ms vs FullKV 82 ms at 128K (4.5×), peak memory 30.5 GB vs 58.1 GB; RULER at 128K 67.0 vs AdaKV 58.4 [Sec 4.2, Fig 5]. App E includes a comparison with HqeKV.

# RDKV: Rate-Distortion Bit Allocation for Joint Eviction and Quantization of the KV Cache

## What this paper does
RDKV casts KV compression as one rate-distortion bit allocation where eviction is the zero-rate end: V tokens get weight w_t = sum_tau a_{tau,t} (cumulative attention), K channels get w_c = ||Q[:,c]|| ||K[:,c]|| / sqrt d; reverse water-filling (Lagrangian MCKP over {0,2,4,8,16} bits with calibrated empirical distortion) assigns bits per token (V) and per channel (K) per head, once after prefill. Packed "TriZone" decode kernel. Evaluated on LongBench, RULER, InfiniteBench, NIAH across five LLMs; 128K-256K contexts on A100.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are V-token and K-channel precisions decided, and is the V weight linear or quadratic in attention?
Linear: from the TV distance of evicting token t, ||a - a_hat||_TV = a_{tau,t}, summed over queries gives w_t = sum_tau a_{tau,t} [full text, Sec 3.1 Prop 3.1]. K channel weight from rank-one logit deviation spectral norm [Prop 3.2]. Optimal b_u* = [log2(ln2 w_u sigma_u / lambda)]_+, with a phase transition to eviction (b=0) below the water level [Thm 3.3]. So V bits ∝ log(cumulative attention mass), not the p^2 law of ReadKV/RECAP and not head-level concentration; per-head K/V budgets are not co-optimized against attention shape.

### Goal: Is it online and does it target long CoT?
No: "applied once after the prefilling stage"; streaming re-budgeting during decoding is named as future work [abstract; Sec 5]. Benchmarks are long-input (LongBench/RULER/InfiniteBench), not long generation. On LongBench 97.81% of full-cache accuracy at 2.48% retention; 4.5x decode speedup and 1.9x peak-memory reduction at 128K vs FlashAttention-2 [abstract].
