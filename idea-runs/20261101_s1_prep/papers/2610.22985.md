# Prism Attention: Proposal-Refined Index Sharing Mechanism for Efficient LLMs Inference

## What this paper does
Inference-time cross-layer index sharing for token-level sparse decoding. Instead of reusing an anchor layer's exact top-kappa indices in later layers, an anchor (proposal) layer exports an expanded top-(rho*kappa) candidate pool, built from low-bit quantized keys. Each refined layer re-selects its own top-kappa inside that pool. Evaluated on reasoning (AIME24, GPQA, MATH500) and long-context tasks with Qwen3, R1-distill-Llama, GLM-4.7-Flash and GLM-5.2. NeurIPS 2026.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Where do the salient tokens that a reused selection misses sit relative to that selection?
Close to its boundary. Reused indices drift from the layer-wise optimum, but "the resulting salient token shift is highly localized rather than arbitrary". Newly needed tokens beyond the proposal top-128 "are mostly captured by the expanded proposal region between the original and expanded boundaries rather than scattered across the full context" [full text, Section 3, Figures 2-3]. Expanding the proposal from rho = 1 to 4 lifts accuracy from 59.22 to 64.43, with smaller returns at rho = 8 [Section 5.4]. The pattern holds across model families, 4K-16K lengths and task types, though its magnitude varies by task [Appendix B]. Caveat: the "selector" here is another layer's attention ranking, not a trained indexer, and nothing is trained.
