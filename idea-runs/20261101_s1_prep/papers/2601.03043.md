# Lil: Less is Less When Applying Post-Training Sparse-Attention Algorithms in Long-Decode Stage

## What this paper does
Shows that post-training sparse attention / eviction in the decode stage (H2O, StreamingLLM-Sink, InfLLM, Quest) lengthens reasoning outputs, so end-to-end time and memory residency can rise ("Less is Less"). Uses LZ77 (gzip) compression ratio of the generated text as an information measure, showing the extra tokens are mostly repeats. Proposes Guardian, an early-stopping rule: every f=250 decode steps compress prompt+output; if the compressed size grew by fewer than t=20 bytes, stop.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How large is the length inflation under sparse attention/eviction and how is it diagnosed?
On first 200 cases of GSM8K, MATH500, AIME with DeepScaleR-1.5B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B and Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, outputs stay longer than full attention by up to 90% across budgets; small budgets make models "generate indefinitely due to lost context"; larger budgets produce the answer early and then keep verifying and forget it [full text, Section 3, Figs. 1, 5]. LZ77 compression ratio is lower (more redundant) under sparse attention despite longer outputs [full text, Section 4, Fig. 2]. Incorrect runs are truncated at 2× the full-attention length to avoid overstating effects [full text, Section 6.2, Table 2].

### Goal: What does Guardian achieve, and at what cost?
Up to 90% token savings with <2% accuracy drop, sometimes improving accuracy when the right answer was produced early [full text, Section 6.2, Table 1]. Most savings come from terminating incorrect runaway generations; LZ77 on 128K tokens costs ~34 ms, about one decode step, amortized over f=250 [full text, Section 6.2]. Implemented in HuggingFace on one A100-80GB; no serving-engine throughput or per-correct cost is reported [full text, Section 6.1].
