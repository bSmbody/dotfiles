# How Linear Attention Remembers

## What this paper does
A causal-intervention study of pretrained GLA-340M, GDN-340M, GDN-1.3B and hybrid (GDN + full attention, Qwen3.5-4B/9B) checkpoints on controlled associative recall. It patches recurrent writes, pre-query states and query-time head outputs to trace how a fact is written, retained and read; varies memory load, fact-to-query distance and the alignment of later write keys with the target's write key; and compares pure recurrent with hybrid models. It proposes no new update rule.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: In pretrained linear-attention models, is recall loss driven more by passive decay over distance or by interference from other stored/later-written associations?
By interference. At fixed sequence length, raising active associations from K=1 to K=16 cuts recall by roughly 30-40 points in GLA-340M and GDN-340M, while stretching fact-to-query distance from 128 to 1024 tokens at K=4 gives "only a modest decline" [full text, Section 4.2 / Figure 7a-b]. Aligning a later distractor's write key with the target's write-key direction (norm preserved) lowers the target margin by 0.27-0.44 logits, while orthogonalising it or aligning a control component has little mean effect [Section 4.2 / Figure 7c]. At K=32 recall is 47-60% (340M) and 69% (GDN-1.3B) [Section 4.2 / Figure 8]. The tested distances are short (≤1024 tokens).
### Goal: Are reads of stored facts concentrated in specific query-time pathways?
Yes. Swapping a selected head's pre-query state, or patching only its query-time output, shifts the answer comparably toward the donor, and restoring the clean output after a state swap removes the effect, i.e. "a concentrated query-time read pathway" mediates recall [Section 3.2 / Figure 4]. Four facts can coexist selectively (61-89% targeted transfer, 0.4-1.4% non-target breaks) but with cross-fact causal coupling rather than independent slots [Section 3.3 / Figure 5].
### Goal: In hybrids, which memory carries recall?
The full-attention KV. In a near-matched GDN hybrid (8 of 24 blocks full attention), replacing the recurrent/local state recovers <1% of the donor effect and replacing KV recovers >99%; in Qwen3.5-4B, 0.2-3.6% vs 96-100% [Section 4.1 / Figure 6]. The authors read this as a boundary of the recurrent mechanism, not proof that recurrent layers are idle, and suggest mechanisms that "selectively gate, refresh, or partition recurrent writes" as future work [Section 6].


### Goal: In the write-overlap intervention, how strong is the alignment, which update rules show the load and overlap damage, and does the delta rule's erase add or remove interference?
The intervention aligns a later distractor's write key *with the target direction while preserving its norm and leaving the remaining update unchanged*, so the distractor is written at the target's own key direction, not at a partial overlap. That alignment lowers the target margin by 0.27-0.44 logits "across tested loads". Orthogonalising it, or aligning a control component, has "little mean effect" [full text, Section 4.2 / Figure 7c]. The text does not say per model which checkpoints Figure 7c covers. The load effect (K=1 to 16 costs 30-40 points) holds in both GLA-340M, which has no delta erase, and GDN-340M [Section 4.2 / Figure 7a]. Collateral damage differs by rule. Both GDN models stay below a 2% non-target break rate through K=32, while GLA-340M exceeds 5% from low load onward. The authors explain this through the update rule: GLA "allow[s] overlapping contributions to remain superposed", whereas GDN's delta correction "may suppress some accumulated cross-talk and help preserve selectivity under load" [Section 4.2 / Figure 8]. So the paper locates interference in superposed additive writes and credits the delta erase with reducing it. It does not show the erase term as the binding channel.
