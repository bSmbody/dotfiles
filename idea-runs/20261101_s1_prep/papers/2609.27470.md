# DeltaS: Reading the Gated Linear Attention State for KV Cache Eviction in Streaming Video

## What this paper does
Query-agnostic, training-free KV eviction for the full-attention layers of a 3:1 GDN hybrid video LLM (Qwen3.5-9B) in streaming video. Each one-second chunk is scored once, when it is prefilled, by its normalized recurrent-state change ("state drift", mean over GDN layers of ||S_after - S_before||_F / ||S_before||_F). Chunks with higher drift are retained, inside temporal buckets, plus sink tokens and a 1,024-token recent window. It beats query-agnostic bounded-memory baselines by 2.1 points on average across six long-video benchmarks at an 8,192-token budget.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What criterion does DeltaS use to decide which attention KV entries to keep, and does it credit an entry against what the GDN state still holds at eviction time?
The score is write-time novelty: state drift over the chunk, motivated by the delta rule writing "only the residual between the incoming representation and what can already be retrieved from the current state", so drift reflects "how much information it brings beyond what the state already holds" [full text, Sections 1, 3.2, Eqs 1-3]. Higher drift means **retain** in the attention cache [Section 3.3, Algorithm 1]. Scores are computed once per chunk and "later eviction steps reuse the scores assigned when each chunk was processed" [Section 3.3]. So the entries most strongly absorbed by the state are the ones kept exactly; there is no term for whether later writes have erased (or preserved) that chunk's content in the state, and no complementarity logic. One shared ranking serves all attention layers.

### Goal: What does its controlled comparison show about state-based signals, and what are its scope and limits?
With budget, sinks and a 1,024-token window fixed (12.5% of M=8,192 reserved for recent tokens), state drift beats SnapKV, L2-norm, KeyDiff, TaR, VaN and HOLA's beta||e|| applied to the frozen backbone (e.g., LVBench 46.0 vs 44.9 for beta||e|| and 44.0 for VaN) [Section 4.3, Table 2]. Token-level beta||e|| costs 206.1 ms per eviction event vs 6.4 ms for DeltaS [Section 4.5]. Limitations: single backbone, relies on the gated delta rule, chunk-level granularity [Section 6]. Scope is streaming video with question/answer KV discarded after each answer [Section 3.3]; there is no decode-time eviction of generated tokens and no long-CoT evaluation.


### Goal: How long do the hybrid's GDN heads retain a written contribution?
Retention horizons are multi-scale. Some heads keep earlier state contributions for only a few seconds, while "after 60 seconds, 14.1% of heads still retain at least half of that contribution". The per-head ranking of horizons is nearly identical across three benchmarks (rho ≈ 0.998) [full text, Section 3.1, Figure 2]. In this backbone the GDN states total a constant 48 MiB, and the attention KV grows by 32 KiB per token [Section 3.1].
