# GateSight: Recurrent Memory Anticipates Attention Memory for Query-Agnostic KV Eviction in Hybrid LLMs

## What this paper does
For GDN-attention hybrids (Qwen3.8-27B: 48 GDN + 16 full-attention layers; Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with sparse attention), it compresses the KV cache of the retained attention layers once after prefill, query-agnostically. Per-(layer, KV head) MLPs read native GDN signals (write gate beta, log-decay g, value norm) of the six preceding GDN layers plus age features and predict each entry's future utility; per-layer budgets are proportional to the mean attention output gate. Implemented in vLLM with physical compaction; at 10% cache it beats SnapKV, TriAttention, KeyDiff and KVzip on most SCBench/RULER-4096 settings.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it define and estimate which KV entries of the full-attention layers to keep, and does the criterion account for the recurrent layers?
Utility U(i) = max over sampled continuations of the norm of the exact leave-one-out change of that attention layer's (ungated) output when entry i is removed, "with the queries, the other entries and the rest of the forward pass held fixed" [full text, Section 2 "Future KV utility", Eq 2]. Continuations are M=4 samples of up to 128 tokens. Eviction "removes position i from the softmax ... and the GDN states are left untouched" [Section 2 "Attention KV cache"]. So the recurrent layers are used only as an input feature source for predicting a layer-local attention-output distortion (Eq 3, Section 4.1), not as a path that could compensate for the evicted entry. Correlations between GDN signals and U are modest (+0.09 to +0.23; slow-head effective write up to +0.33) [Section 3.2].

### Goal: When is the eviction decision made, and does it handle tokens generated during decoding?
Scoring runs once when prefill ends; the compacted cache is reused for later questions [Section 4.3; Appendix G]. The estimator is trained on ~1K-token generic documents [Section 4.1]. Evaluation is SCBench multi-query and RULER-4096; no method or experiment addresses KV growth from long generated outputs (no decode-time eviction is described) [Sections 4.3, 5.1].


### Goal: Do the GDN signals that predict attention utility indicate that the two memories hold the same tokens (duplication) or complementary ones?
They indicate duplication. Every GDN summary correlates **positively** with the layer-local utility U(i) (+0.09 to +0.23). The effective write beta||v|| restricted to slow heads (median half-life >= 1,000 tokens) reaches +0.19 to +0.33 from the third attention layer on, and its top decile hits the top utility decile at 1.82x chance: "the entries that the slow heads ... write strongly tend to be the ones the attention layers will need" [full text, Section 3.2, Figure 2]. The utility label itself is the exact leave-one-out change of one attention layer's ungated output "with the queries, the other entries and the rest of the forward pass held fixed", measured on 4 sampled continuations of up to 128 tokens [Section 2 "Future KV utility", Eq 2]. A label of this kind cannot reward evicting an entry whose content a slow GDN head still holds, so a scorer trained on it learns to keep exactly the entries the slow heads already absorbed.
