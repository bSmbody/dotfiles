# Lethe: Layer- and Time-Adaptive KV Cache Pruning for Reasoning-Intensive LLM Serving

## What this paper does
Decode-time token eviction for reasoning models (AAAI 2026). Spatial part: per layer, token importance = attention summed over batch, heads and queries; tokens are sorted and segmented, and the layer's budget is cut at the first segment where attention drops sharply (layerwise sparsity-aware budgets). Temporal part, RASR: a decayed accumulated-attention score s_t = γ s_{t−1} + Σ attention, combined with age; tokens under a dynamic threshold are evicted periodically, sparing recent and boundary tokens. Eviction only, no quantization.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the budget allocation and eviction rule?
Layer budget from attention-drop segmentation of s^(l) (GQA keys repeated) [full text, Sec "Spatial...", Eqs 2-3]; RASR score Eq 5 with decay γ, periodic ranking with age, recent/boundary protection [Sec "Temporal..."]. Hyperparameters sparse_ratio=400, recent_ratio=0.3 [Ablation]. Allocation is per layer (not per head) and token-count based, not precision.
### Goal: What accuracy results and baselines?
R1-Distill Qwen-7B/32B, Llama-8B/70B on MATH500 and an 8-subject MMLU subset; baselines FullKV, H2O, StreamingLLM, PyramidKV. MATH500 Qwen-7B 85.4 vs FullKV 86.4 (H2O 67.2, StreamingLLM 65.8); Llama-70B 88.1 vs 88.7, PyramidKV −7.9 [Sec "Accuracy Preservation", Table 1]. No AIME, no output-length or loop-rate measurement; generation lengths tested 1.5K–20K.
### Goal: Efficiency?
A100: throughput Qwen-7B batch 16 193.3 vs 111.0 tok/s FullKV, batch 32 245.0 vs OOM (≈2.56× peak claim); 20–40% lower latency at long lengths; Llama-70B memory plateaus ~800 MB after 6K tokens vs 36 GB+ [Sec "Inference Efficiency", Tables 2-3, Fig 4]. HF-style implementation, not a paged serving engine.
