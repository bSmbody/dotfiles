# MpFA: Hardware-Efficient Train-Free QK4V8 FlashAttention Kernels on Blackwell GPUs

## What this paper does
MpFA is a training-free FlashAttention kernel for Blackwell (B200). It uses NVFP4 for QK^T and FP8 for PV, and stores K in NVFP4 and V in FP8, so the kernel consumes a low-bit KV cache without dequantization (KV bytes per token = 1/2.56 of BF16). The authors find that fully FP4 attention fails to deliver end-to-end speedups because of non-matmul costs (online quantization after softmax, data movement, contention on the softmax path). They add rank-one smoothing compensation as an extra Tensor Core MMA, plus an asynchronous pipeline, tensor-memory reuse and adaptive prefill/decode partitioning.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Headline systems numbers?
Prefill is 1.31-1.33x over BF16 FA4 and 1.15-1.17x over FP8 FlashInfer at 16K-128K. The decode kernel is 2.08x on average over split-KV BF16 FA4 at 8K-128K. End-to-end output throughput is 2.81x over BF16 FA4, but only 1.06x over FP8 FlashInfer at 32K-128K (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-14B) (Introduction, Section 5).
### Goal: Accuracy?
Rank-one compensation recovers 62.5% of the accuracy loss on average across ten model-task pairs, with about 2% kernel overhead. The residual loss concentrates on GSM8K and RULER-QA; direct NVFP4+FP8 loses 0.108 on Llama-3.1-8B GSM8K (Section 5, around line 1133). No long-CoT evaluation.
### Goal: Relevance?
It sets the current hardware-native FP4/FP8 KV deployment floor on Blackwell. Its end-to-end gain over FP8 KV is only 1.06x, consistent with F7 (UltraQuant): FP4-class KV beats FP8 mainly through capacity, not kernel speed.
