# Gated Attention for Large Language Models: Non-linearity, Sparsity, and Attention-Sink-Free

## What this paper does
A systematic study (30+ variants, 15B-A2.54B MoE and 1.7B dense models, up to 3.5T tokens) of gating inside softmax attention. The winning variant is a head-specific, query-dependent sigmoid gate applied elementwise (or headwise) to the SDPA output before the output projection (position G1): Y' = Y * sigmoid(X W_theta) with X the pre-norm hidden state. It improves perplexity and benchmarks, stabilizes training, removes attention sinks and massive activations, and improves YaRN length extrapolation. Code and models released [full text, Abstract].

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the gate, and what inference overhead does it add?
Eq 5: Y' = Y ⊙ σ(X W_θ), studied at positions after Q/K/V projections (G2-G4), after SDPA (G1), and after the concatenated output (G5); headwise vs elementwise, head-specific vs shared, multiplicative vs additive [full text, Section 2.2]. "Since the parameters and flops introduced by the gating are small, the wall-time latency introduced by gating is less than 2%" [Section 3.1]. Headwise G1 gating adds <2M parameters to the 15A2B MoE [Section 3.2.1]. The gate does not change KV cache size: it acts on the attention output, so no KV bytes are added.

### Goal: What does gating do to attention sinks and activations, which matter for KV eviction and quantization?
Baseline 1.7B sends on average 46.7% of attention to the first token across layers; with the gate 4.8%; layer 21: 83% -> 4% [Section 1, Figure 2]. Head-specific query-dependent G1 gating "largely reduces the attention score allocated to the first token and decreases massive activations"; head-shared gating or G2-only reduces massive activations but not the first-token sink [Section 4.3, Table 4]. Massive activations originate in early-layer FFN outputs [Section 4.3].

### Goal: What long-context results and limitations are stated?
RULER after YaRN extension from 32K to 128K: baseline 37.51 (64K) / 31.65 (128K) vs gated 66.60 / 58.82; in the original 32K range the baseline drops by up to 41.56 points and the gated model by at most 7.87 [Section 4.4, Table 5]. Limitations: implications of non-linearity on attention dynamics "remain under-explored"; no rigorous theory for how sinks affect length generalization [Limitations]. The paper does not test KV-cache compression or quantization on gated models.
