# MagicDec: Breaking the Latency-Throughput Tradeoff for Long Context Generation with Speculative Decoding

## What this paper does
MagicDec argues that, for long sequences, decoding stays memory-bound on KV loading even at large batch. Speculative decoding (SD) can therefore raise both throughput and latency once the sequence passes a "critical sequence length". Below that length the usual large-batch penalty of SD applies. The draft is the target model itself, running self-speculation over a compressed KV cache (StreamingLLM or SnapKV), with verification against the full cache. A bottleneck-aware cost model picks the draft (self-spec vs small model), the KV budget and gamma for a given batch size and sequence length. Evaluation covers LLaMA-3.1-8B/70B, LLaMA-2-7B-32K, Qwen2.5 and Mistral on PG-19.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What are the headline speedups and in which setting?
Up to 2.51x for LLaMA-3.1-8B at large batch on 8xH100 [Section 1, Section 5]. Setup [Section 5.1]: PG-19 prompts with long prefill (up to 32K-100K), but each request generates only 96 tokens, greedy, StreamingLLM draft budget of 256/512. Other models reach up to 2.06x (Mistral-7B), 1.89x (Qwen2.5-7B) and 1.51x (Qwen2.5-32B) [Section 5.3, A.5]. SnapKV drafting with budget 2049 beats StreamingLLM with budget 512 in every case, because it has the higher acceptance rate [Section 5.2].

### Goal: How does speedup change with batch size and sequence length?
On 8xA100, SD gives a speedup once the sequence exceeds ~4000 tokens, and above that point the speedup grows with batch size [Section 5.1, Fig 6]. SD loses only when the batch is large and the sequence short. The critical length depends on hardware: H100 > A100 > L40, because of the FLOPS-to-bandwidth ratio. It also depends on GQA, since non-GQA LLaMA-2-7B-32K at 8K gains more than GQA LLaMA-3.1-8B at 32K [Section 5.1, Table 1]. At short sequences or small batch, a small draft model with compressed KV beats self-speculation. Self-spec wins only when both batch and length are large [Section 5.3, Fig 7].

### Goal: Is it evaluated on long generation (long CoT), and what limitations are stated?
No. All decoding experiments generate 96 tokens after a long prompt [Section 5.1]. Long generations of 16K-128K, where the KV cache grows during decoding and the draft cache must be updated, are not tested. Stated limitations [Section 6]: only decode is optimized and prefill is left untouched; gains are larger on high-end GPUs; offloading and distributed settings are left to future work.
