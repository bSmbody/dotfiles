# The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images

## What this paper does
A causal audit (EMNLP 2026 Findings) of crop-and-zoom tool use in six models (DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, Thyme, Mini-o3, Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen3-VL-8B) on five fine-grained perception benchmarks, at three levels: policy (tool vs direct inference, same weights), trajectory (replace every returned crop with a random same-shape crop from elsewhere in the image), and step (counterfactual per-call Visual Evidence Gain, VEG). Identifies two failure modes: Calling Without Looking and Looking Without Planning.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does visual tool-use improve accuracy over direct inference, and does it vary by model?
Policy-level ATE "varies sharply across models: essentially null on DeepEyes, modest on Pixel Reasoner / Qwen3-VL-4B / Thyme, and largest on Mini-o3 and Qwen3-VL-8B (e.g., +21.3 pp on VisualProbe)" [full text, Section 3.3.1].

### Goal: Do returned crops causally matter?
Random-crop corruption: near-zero effect for DeepEyes and Thyme; modest for Pixel Reasoner; large negative for Mini-o3 and both Qwen3-VL models, partly because their reasoning notices the inconsistency and re-calls until the budget is exhausted (force-answer control shows a residual evidence effect) [Section 3.3.2]. Step-level: DeepEyes VEG ≈ 0 everywhere, almost always a single call; for Qwen3-VL-8B non-saturated calls carry large signal and over-extension calls appear early (k=2,3) [Section 3.3.3, Fig 5].

### Goal: What is the paper's interpretation?
Gains are concentrated in a calibrated minority of trajectories; ignoring crops is a policy-calibration problem, hypothesized to come from outcome-only RL ("RL-trap") [Section 5]. Nothing in the paper attributes crop-ignoring to the old low-resolution view remaining in context.
