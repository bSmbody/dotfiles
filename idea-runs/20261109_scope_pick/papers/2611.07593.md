# TACT-KV: Tri-Axis Cosine Transform for Compressing Volumetric KV Caches in Medical VLMs

## What this paper does
Calibration-free visual-KV compression: reshape the visual KV of each layer into its (slice/frame, H, W) grid, apply a separable 3D DCT-II, allocate a per-layer bit budget across frequency bins by reverse water-filling, and quantize with fixed scalar codebooks; text KV stays bf16. A tile-fused inverse path inside FlashAttention avoids materializing the cache. Lowest 1-bit flip rate on 11/12 medical VLM-dataset pairs; transfers to Video-MME on four general VLMs; up to 8x larger decoding batch at equal HBM.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How much accuracy does low-bit uniform quantization of visual KV lose on a general VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) on video?
Video-MME (no subtitles), Qwen3-VL-8B bf16 = 72.33. 2-bit: KIVI 71.30, TACT-KV 71.00, VidKV 71.67, AKVQ-VL 71.37; 1-bit: TACT-KV 67.56 while KIVI collapses to 3.44 [full text, Section 3.5, Table 5]. So plain 2-bit visual-KV quantization (8x smaller than bf16) costs about 1 point there, with everything kept in HBM.
### Goal: What does it gain in serving terms, and what are its limits?
Up to 8x larger max decoding batch at equal HBM, with fused dequantization in the attention kernel [Section 3.6, Table 6; Section 4]. Limits: compresses only the visual part, so savings depend on visual tokens dominating; video gains are empirical [Section 4, Limitation]. The visual-KV spatial correlation it exploits (neighboring tokens strongly correlated) is the same spatial locality C2 relies on [Section 1, Fig 1].
