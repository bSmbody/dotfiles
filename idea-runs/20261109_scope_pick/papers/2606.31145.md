# SeKV: Resolution-Adaptive KV Cache with Hierarchical Semantic Memory for Long-Context LLM Inference

## What this paper does
Text-only. SeKV splits the context at high-surprisal anchors into spans (~120 tokens). Each span keeps on GPU a routing summary and a coarse entry (surprisal-weighted mean key and value); low-rank pre-RoPE K/V factors live on CPU. At each decoding step, trained per-head routers pick spans to reconstruct; reconstructed spans enter attention at token resolution, all other spans as a single coarse entry with log|S| added to its score, all in one softmax ("lossy but non-evicting"). Frozen LLM, <0.1% params. At 10% GPU KV budget beats fixed-resolution methods; trails ShadowKV/RetroInfer by ~1.8 points with 3x less transfer.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does SeKV implement the coarse-resident / fine-on-demand computation with a log-count bias?
Yes, exactly in text: "every other span as its coarse entry (k̄_S, v̄_S) with log|S| added to its score so that one entry competes in proportion to the tokens it stands for (Mohtashami and Jaggi 2023). Because all entries share one softmax, zooming in refines a span's contribution, and no span leaves attention" [full text, Section 3.2 Mixed-resolution attention]. Fine detail is on CPU as low-rank factors, fetched asynchronously [Section 3.1-3.2].
### Goal: How well does training-free selection by coarse keys work?
Worse: "replacing the trained router with training-free routing in the original key space costs 2.8 to 3.8 points" (training-free = score spans by q·k̄_S) [Section 4.4, Table 3]. NIAH failures: 43% reconstruction, 38% routing misses, 19% budget cuts; "only 25-29% of heads expand the needle span; the other heads attend to its coarse entry" [Appendix F, Table 13].
### Goal: How do transfers stay manageable, and how is RoPE handled?
"spans expanded at the previous step stay on GPU and are reused, which avoids most transfers" [Section 3.2]; 3x less H2D data per step than ShadowKV [Section 1]. Keys are factorized pre-RoPE and RoPE is applied after reconstruction at original positions [Section 3.1].
### Goal: Does segmentation matter (fixed chunks vs content-aware)?
Fixed-length chunks of equal mean length cost 2.4-2.9 points; dropping exact anchors costs 3.0-3.9 [Section 4.4, Table 3]. The resident floor (anchors, summaries, coarse entries, window) is ~2.4% of full KV at 128K [Section 4.5].
