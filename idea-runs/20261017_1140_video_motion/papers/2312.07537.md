# FreeInit: Bridging Initialization Gap in Video Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
Observes that the forward diffusion schedule fails to fully corrupt the spatio-temporal low-frequency band of real videos, so training-time terminal latents keep correlated low-frequency content while inference starts from i.i.d. Gaussian noise. FreeInit closes this gap iteratively: sample a full video with DDIM, re-diffuse the result to z_T with the same noise, keep the low-frequency band of that z_T (3D FFT, Gaussian low-pass, D0=0.25) and replace the high band with fresh Gaussian noise, then sample again. Evaluated on AnimateDiff, ModelScope and VideoCrafter (UCF-101, MSR-VTT prompts) with DINO consistency, FVD, VBench motion smoothness and dynamic degree.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does FreeInit use to shape the coarse (low-frequency) spatio-temporal structure that early denoising commits to, and at what sampling cost?
The low-frequency SNR of training-time terminal latents is above 0 dB, and replacing everything but 20% of the low-frequency band of a re-noised real video with random noise leaves the generated result essentially unchanged, so the low-frequency part of the initial noise dominates what is generated [full text, Section 3.2, Fig. 4-5]. FreeInit therefore edits only that band: z_T = sqrt(a_T)·DDIM(eps) + sqrt(1-a_T)·eps, then z'_T = IFFT3D(FFT3D(z_T)·H + FFT3D(eta)·(1-H)) [full text, Section 4, Eq. 8-11]. The low-frequency target is the model's own previous output, so no external or prompt-derived motion target is involved. Each iteration is a full sampling pass: metrics use 4 extra iterations, and 2-3 are recommended in practice because gains saturate [full text, Section 5.1; Appendix G]. A coarse-to-fine schedule with fewer DDIM steps in early iterations is offered as mitigation [full text, Appendix G, Eq. A6].

### Goal: What does FreeInit report about its effect on motion amount (dynamic degree), and what failure modes does it list for moving content?
Dynamic degree drops for every base model: AnimateDiff 97.03→75.30, ModelScope 80.54→68.61, VideoCrafter 92.62→83.27. The authors read this as moving closer to the UCF real-video reference of 76.83 [full text, Section 5.3, Table 2]. The cut-off frequency trades motion against consistency: a larger D0 raises consistency and smoothness but "harms the dynamic degree" (D0=0.125: DD 89.52; D0=0.25: DD 75.24) [full text, Appendix C, Table A2]. Listed failure: small, fast-moving objects (water drops) vanish, because iterative low-frequency refinement "tends to guide the generation towards the more stable low-frequency subjects", and the strengthened low-frequency semantics take control away from the text condition [full text, Appendix G, Fig. A7].
