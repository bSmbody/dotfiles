# Video Models Reason Early: Exploiting Plan Commitment for Maze Solving

## What this paper does
Uses mazes (Frozen Lake, VR-Bench) solved by image+text-to-video models (Wan2.2-14B, HunyuanVideo-1.5) to study when the agent's motion path forms during denoising. Decoded x̂0 predictions show "early plan commitment": the route is fixed within the first few steps and later steps refine appearance. It proposes Early Planning Beam Search: partially denoise many seeds for τ steps, score the decoded x̂0 with a task-specific trajectory verifier, and fully denoise only the top-K. It also proposes ChEaP, which chains generations for long-horizon mazes.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How early does the coarse motion trajectory commit during denoising, and can re-noising the same seed change it?
Trajectory convergence is the cosine similarity between motion-energy maps of the decoded x̂0 at step t and of the final video. It reaches 93% at step 5 of 40 on 4×4 mazes for Wan2.2-14B and is nearly perfect by step 10 [full text, Section 4.3, Fig. 3]. Both models "commit to a trajectory within the first 10-15% of their denoising schedules", which the authors read as "a structural property of video diffusion" [full text, Appendix 0.C.1]. Re-noising at a step and continuing (refinement) gives "at most 25% trajectory diversity" from the same seed, versus "68% diversity between different seeds". Hence "the route is largely encoded in the initial noise sample" [full text, Section 4.4, Fig. 4].

### Goal: How does early beam search exploit commitment, and what does it require?
EPBS evaluates N = ⌊(B − KT)/τ⌋ + K seeds: τ=5, K=1, B=400 gives 73 candidates versus 10 for best-of-N. Each candidate's decoded step-τ x̂0 is scored, and only the top-K are finished [full text, Section 5.1, Alg. 1]. The verifier "requires ... privileged information: the locations of the agent, the goal, and obstacle cells", and tracks the agent across frames [full text, Section 5.1]. EPBS "matches best-of-N accuracy with 3.3× fewer NFEs" on Wan2.2-14B [full text, Section 5.2]. The method depends on a task-specific checker of what the motion should be; open-domain text prompts provide none.
