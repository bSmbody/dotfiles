# Applying Guidance in a Limited Interval Improves Sample and Distribution Quality in Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
NeurIPS 2024. Shows that classifier-free guidance acts differently at different noise levels in image diffusion. At high noise it drastically reduces variation and pulls samples toward "a handful of template images" per prompt. At middle noise it sharpens features. At low noise it is largely unnecessary. The method keeps the guidance weight but applies it only inside a middle noise interval (σ_lo, σ_hi] and uses the plain conditional prediction elsewhere. This improves ImageNet-512 FID from 1.81 to 1.40 with EDM2. It also preserves composition diversity and reduces saturation on SD-XL, and saves compute. The interval is chosen per model, by FID or by visual inspection.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: Why is guidance harmful at high noise levels, and what does it do to the composition and diversity of samples?
In a 1D toy with w = 6, guidance at all noise levels makes trajectories "drift quite far from the smoothed data distribution" early on, because the unconditional branch repels the guided trajectories. The result is a "catastrophic mode drop". Disabling guidance at high σ restores both modes, and disabling it at low σ has little effect [full text, Section 3, Fig. 2]. In full-scale models, raising w with constant guidance changes "the composition of the image ... drastically, towards some limited set of per-class 'templates'", and saturates colours [Section 4.3, Fig. 6, Fig. 9]. Lowering σ_hi, i.e. switching guidance off at high noise, changes composition, from "simplified image layouts" (high σ_hi) to "unnecessary complexity" (low σ_hi), and reduces saturation [Section 4.3, Fig. 8]. With the interval, raising w adds detail while "keeping the color palette and overall composition unchanged" [Fig. 7]. So the early, high-noise part of guidance is what collapses layout onto a few templates and saturates colour. Detail is added by mid-range guidance.

### Goal: How is the interval chosen, and does the paper consider video or motion?
The rule is w(σ) = w inside (σ_lo, σ_hi] and 1 elsewhere, with the bounds rounded to step boundaries [Section 3.1 / Eq 5–6]. For EDM2 the interval is tuned by FID. For SD-XL it was "chosen by visual inspection": (0.28, 5.42], i.e. 50% of the steps [Section 4.3]. The interval is a per-model hyperparameter, and the authors suggest exposing it as one [Abstract]. The method is time-only: one gain for the whole sample at each step. All experiments are on images. The paper does not study video, frames or motion.
