# CFG-Zero*: Improved Classifier-Free Guidance for Flow Matching Models

## What this paper does
A training-free CFG variant for flow-matching models. Analysis on Gaussian mixtures shows that, when the learned flow is inaccurate, CFG steers samples onto wrong trajectories, and in the first step the guided velocity is worse than a zero velocity. Two fixes follow. An "optimized scale" s* = ⟨v_c, v_u⟩/‖v_u‖² rescales the unconditional velocity inside CFG. "Zero-init" zeroes the solver's velocity for the first few steps (one step by default). The method is evaluated on Lumina-Next, SD3, SD3.5 and Flux, and on Wan2.1 text-to-video with VBench, with no extra model evaluations.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What does zero-init change in the early steps, why, and what is reported for Wan2.1 text-to-video?
Zero-init sets the velocity to zero for the first K solver steps (K = 1 by default). For an underfitted model, the error of both conditional and CFG velocities at t = 0 exceeds that of an all-zero velocity [full text, Section 4.2 / Eq 12; Section 3 algorithm comment "K equals to 1 by default"]. It thus removes the whole first update: conditional, unconditional and guidance alike. It does not single out any component. On Wan2.1-1.3B, the paper says CFG-Zero* raises the VBench Total Score, Aesthetic Quality (+2.57), Imaging Quality (+2.73), Motion Smoothness (+0.92) and Spatial Relationship (+1.09), and lowers Temporal Style (−0.77) [Section 5.2]. The VBench table's numbers, dynamic degree included, did not survive text extraction, so this digest cannot report CFG-Zero*'s own dynamic-degree figure. The motivation is error reduction and fidelity, not motion [Section 1, Section 4].
