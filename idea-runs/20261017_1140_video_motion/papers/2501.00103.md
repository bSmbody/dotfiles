# LTX-Video: Realtime Video Latent Diffusion

## What this paper does
LTX-Video is an open text- and image-to-video latent diffusion transformer with about 1.9B parameters. It is built around a very high-compression Video-VAE: 32×32×8 pixels per token, 128 channels, 1:192 compression, with patchifying moved into the VAE. The transformer runs full spatiotemporal self-attention, uses RMSNorm, QK-normalisation and RoPE, and takes text through cross-attention. It is trained with rectified flow. The VAE decoder also performs the final denoising step in pixel space. The model generates 121 frames at 768×512 in about 2 s on an H100 with 20 steps.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What are LTX-Video's prediction target, positional encoding, latent geometry and speed, and what does that imply for a guidance rule that splits the clean-video estimate into spatial bands and a temporal mean?
- **Prediction target.** Rectified flow with z_t = (1−t)z_0 + tε and velocity target v = ε − z_0, sampled by Euler steps z_{t−Δt} = z_t − Δt·v [full text, Section 2.5.1]. A clean-latent estimate is therefore linear in the velocity.
- **Positional encoding.** RoPE replaces absolute embeddings. Coordinates are fractional and normalised to a maximum resolution and duration (pixels and seconds), and frequencies are spaced exponentially [Section 2.2.1, Fig. 7–8; Section 4.3.2]. Like Wan, it therefore has a relative positional bias across frames.
- **Latent geometry.** Each token covers 32×32 pixels and 8 frames [Abstract; Section 2.1]. The latent grid is much coarser than Wan's (8×8 spatial VAE plus 2×2 patchify), so a "coarsest spatial band" in latent space spans a larger pixel area, and fewer latent frames exist.
- **Speed.** 2 s for 121 frames at 768×512 with 20 steps on an H100 [Section 1].
- **Model size.** The model-comparison table lists LTX-Video at 1.9B [Section 2.2 table].
- **Stated limitations.** Sensitivity to prompt formulation, and support for clips of only up to about ten seconds [Section 5].
