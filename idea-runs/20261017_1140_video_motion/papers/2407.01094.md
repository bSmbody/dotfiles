# Evaluation of Text-to-Video Generation Models: A Dynamics Perspective

## What this paper does
DEVIL is an evaluation protocol centred on video dynamics. It provides about 800 prompts, each labelled with a dynamics grade from 1 (static) to 5 (very high), balanced across grades. It defines dynamics scores at three temporal granularities: inter-frame (optical-flow strength, SSIM-based structural change, perceptual-hash change), inter-segment (patch-level and global aperiodicity) and video level (temporal entropy, temporal semantic diversity). These are fused into one human-aligned dynamics score. From that score it derives three model-level metrics: dynamics range, dynamics controllability and dynamics-based quality. It reports over 90% Pearson correlation with human ratings.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does DEVIL measure whether the amount of motion matches what each prompt asks for, and why must quality metrics be read jointly with dynamics?
- **Prompts.** Each prompt has a grade G ∈ {1..5}: static, low, medium, high, very high. Grades were first assigned by GPT-4 and then refined by six annotators. 800 prompts were sampled evenly across grades, and each grade covers 19 object and 4 scene categories [full text, Section 3.3]. VBench and EvalCrafter prompts are "heavily skewed towards lower dynamic content" [Section 3.3, Fig. 3].
- **Dynamics controllability.** D_control is the fraction of prompt pairs with different grades whose generated-video dynamics scores are ranked the same way as their grades [Section 3.2 / Eq 2]. It directly tests "more motion only where the prompt asks for it".
- **Dynamics range.** D_range is the 99th minus the 1st percentile of dynamics scores [Eq 1].
- **Overall dynamics score.** S averages three linear-regression fits to human ratings, one per granularity [Section 3.5 / Eq 12–15].
- **Quality versus dynamics.** Per-video dynamics correlates negatively with subject consistency (Pearson −88.9), background consistency (−79.4), motion smoothness (−64.0) and naturalness (−51.8). Models therefore "cheat" these metrics by generating low-dynamics video [Section 3.2, Table 2].
- **Dynamics-based quality.** D_quality averages a composite quality over 12 equal dynamics bins, which compares quality at matched dynamics [Eq 3].
- **Naturalness.** Judged by Gemini-1.5 Pro on five levels [Appendix E].

So a consistency drop that comes with more motion has to be judged at matched dynamics, not raw.
