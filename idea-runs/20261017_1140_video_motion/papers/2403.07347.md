# Frequency Decoupling for Motion Magnification via Multi-Level Isomorphic Architecture

## What this paper does
FD4MM (CVPR 2024) is a learning-based video motion magnification method that splits frame features into spatial low- and high-frequency parts. The inter-frame difference of the deepest low-frequency features is treated as the motion field and is the only thing magnified; multi-level high-frequency details are kept unmagnified and recoupled afterwards by a learned mixer to avoid ringing. Sparse high/low-pass filters and a contrastive regulariser reduce noise and undesired magnification. It reports better SSIM/LPIPS than prior learned magnifiers at lower FLOPs.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: Which spatial-frequency band does motion magnification act on, and why is that band chosen?
FD4MM magnifies only the low-frequency band. The magnified frame is approximately the sum of unmagnified multi-level high-frequency details plus α times the inter-frame difference of the deep low-frequency features times the spatial gradient of the image, I_m ≈ ΣH_i + α·ΔL_d·∂f/∂x [full text, Section 3.1 / Eq 1]. The authors observe that "low-frequency features exhibit a centrally clustered spectral distribution with more stable spatial structures" and choose "the stable low-frequency features to model the motion field, and high-frequency features with more energy to preserve spatially detailed features, such as appearance clues" [Section 1, Fig. 2]. The low band is obtained by a dilated (smoothing) convolution and the high band as the residual, like wavelet detail coefficients [Section 3.2 / Eq 2].

### Goal: What goes wrong when high-frequency detail or noise is magnified together with motion?
Subtle motion "is susceptible to confusion with inevitable photography noise", which yields "amplified noise, undesired motion and distortion" [Section 1]. Hand-crafted Eulerian filters "are prone to ringing artifacts and require the selection of different hyperparameters" per scene [Section 1]. Incomplete separation of texture from shape "may inadvertently introduce undesired motions" [Section 1]. FD4MM therefore keeps high-frequency details out of the magnifier and recouples them with a mixer that "allows the magnified low-frequency to guide the high-frequency details ... to avoid ringing artifacts" [Section 3.1, Fig. 4]; adding the mixer gives the largest single ablation gain (SSIM 0.8521 → 0.9104) [Section 4.3, Table 4].
