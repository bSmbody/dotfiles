# FreqPrior: Improving Video Diffusion Models with Frequency Filtering Gaussian Noise

## What this paper does
A noise prior for video diffusion that keeps FreeInit's low-frequency refinement but fixes its variance decay. It partially samples to an intermediate step, re-diffuses to T, and refines the noise with a variance-preserving frequency filter. The low band (filter M) comes from a mix of the re-diffused latent and fresh noise; the high band (filter sqrt(1−M²)) comes from fresh Gaussian noise. Real and imaginary parts of two noise sets are combined so the refined prior stays close to standard Gaussian. Evaluated on ModelScope, AnimateDiff and VideoCrafter with VBench, reporting higher quality, semantic and total scores than FreeInit at about 23% less time.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What noise-prior mechanism does FreqPrior use to shape low-frequency structure before denoising, and at what cost?
Algorithm: repeat n times — partially sample z_T to z_t, re-diffuse to z_noise, then NoiseRefine. Refinement mixes z_noise with fresh noise by angle θ, low-passes with M, adds fresh high frequencies with sqrt(1−M²), and takes Re/Im combinations of two sets [full text, Section 3.2, Eq. 2-5, Alg. 1]. "FreeInit and our method require two additional samplings to acquire the noise prior". Partial sampling saves about 23% versus FreeInit or Gaussian noise at triple steps [full text, Section 4.2]. As with FreeInit, the low-frequency content comes from the model's own partial sample, with no prompt-specific motion target.

### Goal: What does FreqPrior say about motion dynamics and its own limitations?
Lowering the initial-noise standard deviation from 1 to 0.96 produces "a progressive loss of details alongside a reduction in motion dynamics". FreeInit's variance decay is blamed for its "excessive smoothness, limited motion dynamics" [full text, Section 1, Fig. 1]. Limitations: it "can occasionally result in unnatural smoothness that does not align with the laws of physics", and it may change the content layout relative to the original noise [full text, Appendix G]. Dynamic degree is reported only inside the aggregate VBench quality score, not separately [full text, Section 4.1; Appendix E].
