# Alias-Free Generative Adversarial Networks

## What this paper does
StyleGAN3 (NeurIPS 2021). Observes that StyleGAN2 synthesis depends on absolute pixel coordinates: fine detail stays "glued" to screen coordinates instead of moving with object surfaces ("texture sticking"), visible in latent interpolations. Traces it to unintended positional references reaching intermediate layers (image borders, per-pixel noise inputs, positional encodings, and above all aliasing from upsampling filters and pointwise nonlinearities). Redesigns the generator with a continuous-signal interpretation (Fourier-feature input, margins, Kaiser-windowed sinc resampling, filtered nonlinearities, no noise inputs) so it becomes translation- and rotation-equivariant at sub-pixel scale, with FID matching StyleGAN2.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What is texture sticking, what causes it, and why does the network lock content to the reference rather than ignore it?
Texture sticking is fine detail that "appears to be fixed in pixel coordinates" while the coarse object moves, "breaking the illusion of a solid and coherent object moving in space" [full text, Section 1]. The network "can partially bypass the ideal hierarchical construction by drawing on unintentional positional references available to the intermediate layers through image borders, per-pixel noise inputs and positional encodings, and aliasing" [Section 1]. It does not ignore such references: it "has the means and motivation to amplify even the slightest amount of aliasing and combining it over multiple scales allows it to build a basis for texture motifs that are fixed in screen coordinates" [Section 1]. A pressure against change has the same effect: path-length regularization "penalizes image changes upon latent space walk and thus encourages texture sticking" [Section 4, Ablations].

### Goal: What is the fix principle, and how is sticking diagnosed?
Principle: eliminate the unwanted side information so that "the exact sub-pixel position of each feature is exclusively inherited from the underlying coarse features" [Section 1; Section 3.1]. Per-pixel noise inputs are removed because they are "strongly at odds" with that goal, and re-enabling them "compromises equivariances significantly" [Section 3.1; Section 4, Ablations]. Borders are handled with a margin because padding "is known to leak absolute image coordinates" [Section 3.2, config e]. Diagnostics: (i) average the images generated from a small latent neighbourhood; "the intended result is uniformly blurry because all details should move together", and sticking shows as unwanted sharpness; (ii) stack a fixed column of pixels over an interpolation; sticking gives horizontal streaks instead of a time-varying field [Figure 1 caption].

### Goal: What does removing the references cost, and what conditions does the fix rely on?
At equal training, the boundary/upsampling fix alone costs 16% FID "probably because we started to constrain what the feature maps can contain"; the full redesign recovers FID [Section 3.2, config e; Abstract]. Once references are gone, the network invents its own coordinate systems on object surfaces (phase-like feature maps) [Section 4, Internal representations]. The fix assumes position should be inherited from coarse features; for pixel-grid-aligned data (jagged cartoons, letterboxing) awareness of the grid may be desirable [Section 5]. The paper concerns GAN generators trained from scratch; it says attention layers and tokenizing transformers may be "at odds with equivariance" and leaves them open [Section 5]. It does not study diffusion models, guidance, or sampling-time interventions.
