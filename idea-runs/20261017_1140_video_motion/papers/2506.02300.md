# Through a Steerable Lens: Magnifying Neural Network Interpretability via Phase-Based Extrapolation

## What this paper does
An interpretability method that treats a classifier's input gradient toward a target class as an "infinitesimal motion" and magnifies it, by analogy to phase-based video motion magnification. The image is mapped into a complex steerable pyramid (or Fourier) domain, the gradient is computed there, and a single gradient direction is linearly extrapolated, mostly in the phase component with amplitude held fixed. Reconstructing the extrapolated coefficients yields morph sequences that move and deform image structure toward the target class, shown on synthetic disks, MNIST, colour MNIST and FER2013.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How does it carry motion magnification over to amplifying a model-derived difference direction, and what assumption makes that transfer work?
The paper draws "a conceptual parallel to motion magnification: the gradient of a neural network with respect to an input image is analogous to subtle motion—an infinitesimal perturbation", and amplifies "a single local tangent vector" by linear extrapolation instead of integrating a path [full text, Section 1]. Extrapolation is S_k = S_0 + kαg in a transformed domain [Section 3.1 / Eq 2]. A gradient step both scales amplitude and shifts phase [Section 3.3 / Eq 6–9]; the method keeps only the phase shift, extrapolating phase linearly with amplitude fixed [Section 3.3 / Eq 10–11]. The working assumption is that the representation is localized and disentangled enough that the model's response is "locally smoother or more linear" there, so a fixed direction remains meaningful over a finite extrapolation distance; the authors list this fixed-direction assumption as a limitation [Section 5].

### Goal: What happens when the same difference direction is extrapolated in a representation that does not separate position from contrast?
"Naive pixel-space gradient ascent ... often adds noise or globally brightens/darkens", and Fourier-domain extrapolation "tends to produce more global shifts or blurring" [Section 4.2 discussion, Appendix C]. The authors attribute the difference to phase encoding "structural information and position, while amplitude encodes contrast or energy", so phase-focused extrapolation produces "deformations or structural edits rather than simple brightening or blurring" [Section 5]. On the synthetic Arcade dataset, phase extrapolation moves a disk in the target direction [Section 4.1, Fig. 2].
