# VideoJAM: Joint Appearance-Motion Representations for Enhanced Motion Generation in Video Models

## What this paper does
VideoJAM argues that the pixel-reconstruction objective biases video diffusion models toward appearance over motion. Its fine-tuning framework has the model predict, from one joint latent, both the video and an optical-flow motion representation. At inference, "Inner-Guidance" uses the model's own noisy motion prediction to steer generation. It fine-tunes DiT-4B and DiT-30B and introduces VideoJAM-bench, 128 motion-heavy prompts, evaluated with VBench metrics and human 2AFC.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is VideoJAM-bench, how is it evaluated, and what does the paper show about appearance bias and the dynamic-degree/smoothness trade-off?
- **The benchmark.** 128 prompts "designed to be diverse, encompassing simple motions (e.g., walking), complex human movements (e.g., gymnastics), rotational motions (e.g., spinning balls), and physics-based actions" [full text, Appendix F].
- **Evaluation protocol.** VBench metrics are aggregated into appearance and motion groups, with one shared seed per prompt for every model. A human Two-alternative Forced Choice (2AFC) compares quality, motion and text alignment, with each comparison rated by 5 users [Section 5.2].
- **Appearance bias.** A vanilla DiT-4B's loss is "nearly invariant to frame shuffling until step 60", and steps t ≤ 60 decide coarse motion. This supports the claim that the objective "favor[s] appearance over motion" [Section 3, Fig. 3; Appendix B].
- **Dynamics/smoothness trade-off.** CogVideoX-2B has the highest dynamic degree and the lowest motion smoothness among small models ("abundant motion ... yet incoherent"). The DiT-4B base has the best smoothness and the worst dynamic degree ("very subtle movements") [Appendix D].

So dynamic degree and motion smoothness have to be reported together, and a 2AFC with several raters is the accepted human protocol.
