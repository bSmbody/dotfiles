# Why Do Video Diffusion Models Violate Physics? Unveiling the Flaws in Attention Mechanisms

## What this paper does
An interpretability study of "motion planning" in Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (50 steps), using falling-and-bouncing-ball prompts. Cross-attention to the object token shows several candidate regions per frame that converge to one trajectory within the first ~5 steps. A small set of heads drives this, found by attribution patching. Self-attention analysis shows that RoPE's spatial decay makes each region attend to the same spatial location in other frames ("spatial anchoring"). Frames that settle early therefore pull other frames' candidates to their position, overriding physically plausible ones. The paper scales the RoPE height/width frequencies by λ<1 during the first 5 steps, training-free or with LoRA fine-tuning concentrated on early timesteps, and evaluates on VideoPhy.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: When and how does the coarse motion trajectory form in early denoising?
In the first 5 denoising steps, the head-averaged cross-attention to the object token goes from noise (T1), to several highlighted regions along the motion path (T3), to the final position (T5), and shows a clear trajectory by T7. Attention entropy and trajectory-support quality both show a turning point around step 5 [full text, Section 4.1, Fig. 2-3]. Training uses a sampler concentrated on "the initial 10% of denoising timesteps", where motion planning "predominantly occurs" [full text, Appendix H.1.4].

### Goal: What mechanism makes early commitment produce static or physically wrong motion?
Early on, "multiple candidate regions compete unstably", and a few frames fix the object position first. Through RoPE's spatial anchoring (attention favours small Δp^h, Δp^w across frames), an early-stabilised region in frame 10 raises the confidence of spatially adjacent candidates in other frames. That overrides the physically plausible candidate and yields "the failure mode where the basketball remains stationary in the air" [full text, Section 5, Fig. 8-9]. The other failures in Fig. 1 (mid-air bouncing, anti-gravity floating, sudden freezing) "share this mechanism" [full text, Section 1; Section 5].

### Goal: What does the training-free fix require, and what are its limitations?
The fix multiplies the RoPE rotation angle on the h/w axes by λ<1 during the first 5 steps [full text, Section 6.1, Eq. 3]. Training-free use "indeed improves the motion trajectories, but requires manual tuning of λ^{h/w}", so the main results use LoRA fine-tuning on 80K WISA videos [full text, Section 6.2-6.3]. λ=0.75 was picked by sweeping 0.55-0.90 on hand-selected cases [full text, Appendix H.1.3]. A learnable per-head, timestep-conditioned λ failed: about half the heads went above 1, half below, with no gain. The authors blame the flow-matching loss, in which motion (small frame-to-frame displacement) is "a minor proportion" of what is predicted [full text, Appendix H.1.3]. On VideoPhy, the training-free version scores SA/PC 57.43/39.94 versus base 53.64/29.45. The gains concentrate on the solid-* subsets with large-magnitude motion [full text, Table 1; Section 6.3].


### Goal: Does the analysis include classifier-free guidance, and what exactly does spatial anchoring reward across frames?
The analysis uses the conditional branch only: "Since the model uses classifier-free guidance (CFG) for generation, we study the conditional branch by default" [full text, Section 4 opening]. Sampling itself uses standard CFG, u_CFG = u(C⁻) + s_CFG(u(C⁺) − u(C⁻)) [Appendix B, Wan2.1-T2V architecture]. How the CFG extrapolation interacts with anchoring is therefore not studied. Anchoring: self-attention from region k of one frame "concentrates within region k of other frames"; the RoPE query–key product Re[Σ_a q^a k^a* e^{iΔp^a θ}] decays with Δp^h, Δp^w, so a region favours spatially close regions in every frame [Section 5, Fig. 6, Fig. 16]. The competition is measured by mutual consistency (MC), a head-averaged, normalised cross-frame self-attention intensity between candidate regions [Section 5]. In steps 1–5 several candidates per frame "compete unstably with no clear advantage" (winner–loser gap oscillating around 0, Fig. 19); a frame that stabilises early (frame 10, MC 0.63 at step 3) "steadily raises confidence of spatially adjacent regions in other frames", which flips frame 14's winner from K1 (0.21) to K3 (0.33) and leaves "the basketball ... stationary in the air" [Section 5, Fig. 8–9].

### Goal: Why does the training-free RoPE fix need LoRA, and how is its strength chosen?
The fix rescales the h/w rotation angles of every query and key by λ<1 during the first 5 steps, a change to positional encoding the pretrained network never saw [Section 6.1, Eq. 3; Section 6.2]. Training-free, it "indeed improves the motion trajectories, but requires manual tuning of λ^{h/w}. Therefore, we adopt fine-tuning to enable the model to gradually adapt to λ^{h/w}" [Section 6.3]. λ = 0.75 was picked by sweeping 0.55–0.90 on hand-picked cases [Appendix H.1.3]. The fine-tuned sampler must concentrate on early steps (p_early = 0.9); too high hurts aesthetics, too low distorts trajectories [Appendix H.1.4]. A learnable per-head, timestep-conditioned λ split about half the heads above 1 and half below, with no gain, which the authors attribute to motion being "a minor proportion of the entire frame information" in the flow-matching loss [Appendix H.1.3]. The λ scale is global: the same for every head, token and prompt.



### Goal: How is the training-free RoPE-λ result on VideoPhy configured, and how stable is the training-free version?
On VideoPhy, λ^{h/w} = 0.70 is used "for both training-free and training-based settings", applied only in the first 5 of 50 steps [full text, Appendix H.2 Evaluation details; Section 6.2]. Table 1 gives SA/PC 53.64/29.45 for the base model, 57.43/39.94 for the training-free modified RoPE, and 64.72/41.98 for LoRA + modified RoPE. The metrics are semantic adherence and physical commonsense, not motion amount. Gains concentrate on the solid-* subsets, which hold larger-magnitude motion [Section 6.3, Table 1]. In the quick basketball check, λ was hand-tuned per seed in [0.50, 0.90]: seeds 8, 20, 23 and 29 used 0.55, 0.60, 0.85 and 0.50. The training-free output "often suffers from instability in terms of motion trajectories and object permanence" [Appendix H.3.1, Fig. 23 caption]. Because the method "cannot scale in practice", the authors fine-tune [Appendix H.2].

### Goal: Do the cross-attention heads that write the most condition-induced (conditional minus unconditional) content into the object region decide the trajectory?
No, not on the case tested. Head contribution is scored by attribution patching in steps 1–5. The metric is the dot product, inside the object region, of the post-intervention conditional velocity with Δu^clean = u^cond − u^uncond. The authors pick Δu^clean because it "eliminates general information" and keeps the condition-induced increment [Section 4.2 / Eq 2; Appendix E.1]. Type (2) heads have a large contribution (> 0.5) but no trajectory pattern. Zero-ablating them, or Type (1) without layers 0–1, at all steps leaves the shape and trajectory unchanged. Ablating both types changes only the object's size, so their impact "focuses on static content such as the appearance of the object and the background, rather than motion planning. Even if the contribution is large, it does not mean that their real impact on the trajectory is large" [Section 4.2, Fig. 4(b,c); Appendix E.2]. Ablating Type (1) heads in layers 0–1 removes all displacement ("initialization of motion"). Ablating the rarer Type (3) heads, which converge to the trajectory early, collapses the trajectory [Section 4.2, Fig. 4(a,e)]. The test uses one successful seed (26) of the basketball prompt, with spot checks on other seeds [Appendix E.3]. It does not ask whether static content can cause a stuck trajectory in a failing case.


### Goal: Which sampler settings (steps, timestep shift, CFG scale, solver) does the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B analysis use, and what fraction of the noise-to-data path do steps 1–5 cover?
Appendix B, Table 3 lists the inference settings: 50 steps, FlowUniPC solver, sample shift 5.0 ("flow-matching timestep schedule shift"), CFG scale s_CFG = 5.0 (default), 81×480×832 output, latent 16×21×60×104, DiT token grid 21×30×52 [full text, Appendix B, Table 3]. Guided velocity is u(C⁻) + s_CFG(u(C⁺) − u(C⁻)) [Appendix B, Wan2.1-T2V architecture]. The paper does not state the per-step noise levels. With the standard shifted schedule σ' = sσ/(1 + (s − 1)σ) and s = 5, step 5 of 50 (σ = 0.9) gives σ' ≈ 0.978. That is my own computation, not the paper's: the window in which the paper places trajectory competition covers only about 2% of the interpolation from noise to data, at very low SNR.


### Goal: How are the "winner" candidate and mutual consistency defined, and is the self-attention anchoring evidence aggregated over many prompts and seeds or drawn from case studies?
The winner is defined post hoc from the finished video. The anchor regions are "the candidates at step 49, layer 27", one region per frame. For each early candidate, the anchor distance d_k is measured from that frame's final-step region, and "the winner k⁺ = argmin_k d_k is the candidate closest to the reference area in frame f_i" [full text, Section 5]. So which candidate "won" in each frame is read off the final trajectory. If the object ends up moving, the per-frame winners lie at different positions by construction. Mutual consistency is MC(Ω_i,k) = (1/f) Σ_j max_k_j C̄(Ω_i→Ω_j)·C̄(Ω_j→Ω_i), the head-averaged, normalised cross-frame self-attention between candidate regions [Section 5]. It was validated by its correlation with anchor distance, which strengthens over steps [Appendix G, Fig. 18]. The evidence for anchoring is qualitative plus case studies. The self-attention map "focuses on the same position of different frames" [Section 5, Fig. 6, Fig. 16]. The causal story that an early-settled frame pulls other frames' winners is one failed basketball case (seed 20, frames 10 and 14) [Section 5, Fig. 8–9], with seed 26 as a second visualisation [Appendix G, Fig. 17]. The main analysis prompt is a basketball falling onto a wooden floor [Section 4 opening]. Generality is checked only for the cross-attention head classification ("other cases and random seeds", e.g. a sliding cube) [Section 4.2; Appendix E.3, Fig. 14–15]. There is no multi-prompt statistic linking anchoring or MC to stationary outcomes, and no motion-amount metric. VideoPhy results use one seed (42) per prompt [Appendix H.2].
