# SpecLoR: Spectral Lookahead Rectification for Motion-Coherent Text-to-Video Generation

## What this paper does
A plug-and-play sampler for flow-matching T2V (Wan2.2-A14B, 40-step UniPC; also HunyuanVideo-1.5). In an early "lookahead window" (steps 2-5) it projects z_t to a clean estimate z_{t,0} = z_t − t·v and takes a 3D spatio-temporal FFT. It clips the amplitude spectrum toward a natural-video power-law envelope (μ_ref, σ_ref, precomputed from about 2,000 real videos) or adds a residual from a small trained text-conditioned adapter. The phase is kept untouched. The result is inverted and re-noised to t with the initial noise (deterministic) or fresh noise (stochastic), costing 4 extra NFEs. Reports gains in VideoScore2 physical consistency and visual quality on Dynamic-Bench and VideoJAM-Bench over FlowMo and SRVS.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does SpecLoR modify during the early lookahead window, and what does it leave untouched?
It modifies only the 3D amplitude spectrum of the lookahead clean latent. A_tgt = clip(A_curr, γ·μ_ref, μ_ref + kσ_ref) with γ=0.6 and k=3, and A_rect = A_curr + λ(A_tgt − A_curr) with λ=0.5. The phase is "strictly locked" before inverse FFT and re-noising [full text, Section 4.1-4.4, Eq. 1-5]. The window is steps 2-5 of 40. Moving it to steps 10-13 or 14-17 does worse than the unguided baseline because "by step 6 and beyond ... drift has already permanently corrupted the geometric phase" [full text, Appendix C.1, Table S1]. The authors state that phase "inherently encodes spatiotemporal displacement". Rectifying phase with global statistics instead broke the motion structure, e.g. fast actions became disjointed slow motion [full text, Section 5.3, Fig. 6].

### Goal: Is the rectification target prompt-specific, and what does SpecLoR report about motion amount and failure modes?
SpecLoR-Zero's target is a universal, prompt-agnostic envelope that is bound to a fixed latent shape and must be re-extracted if resolution changes [full text, Section 4.3; Section 6]. SpecLoR-Adapter is a separately trained 2-block text-conditioned DiT that predicts a residual amplitude, trained on natural videos with the base model frozen [full text, Section 4.3, Eq. 4; Appendix B.3]. Effect on motion amount varies. On Dynamic-Bench, dynamic degree is 0.916 for Zero-deterministic, 0.850 for Zero-stochastic, 0.875 for Adapter-deterministic and 0.800 for Adapter-stochastic. The authors call this a "fundamental consistency-dynamism trade-off" in which stochastic re-noising "inherently dampens high-frequency motion variance" [full text, Section 5.3, Table 2]. On MovieGen-Bench, dynamic degree rises slightly, 0.754→0.782 [full text, Appendix C.3, Table S2]. Failure modes: because the early phase is locked, non-rigid or chaotic dynamics get trapped (an ink drop stays a rigid sphere; Jell-O wobble bleeds into the bowl). The stated cause: "if initial phase predictions are severely corrupted during the earliest ODE steps, locking the phase might trap extreme non-rigid transformations into unnatural states" [full text, Section 6; Appendix C.2].
