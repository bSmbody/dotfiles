# Characterizing Motion Encoding in Video Diffusion Timesteps

## What this paper does
Quantifies the heuristic that early timesteps carry motion and later ones appearance. For 76 TGVE videos it DDIM-inverts the video, then resamples with a subject-edited prompt only inside a timestep range [τ_end, τ_start], keeping the original prompt elsewhere. Appearance editing is measured as CLIP-score change and motion preservation as Lucas-Kanade optical-flow cosine similarity. Sweeping all ranges on ModelScope, Latte and CogVideoX reveals an early motion-dominant and a later appearance-dominant regime, with a boundary near τ≈700-900 of 1000. It then restricts one-shot motion-customisation LoRA training and inference to the motion-dominant timesteps.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Where is the motion-dominant regime of the denoising trajectory, and how consistent is it across architectures?
With τ_end fixed at 0, making τ_start larger (re-conditioning earlier) sharply lowers optical-flow similarity to the source video, while τ_end hardly matters for motion. The authors conclude that "motion signals are dominantly encoded in early denoising timesteps" [full text, Section 3.4, Fig. 2]. The motion/appearance threshold "varies across specific models" but "is consistently around [700,900]" of 1000 timesteps. That holds for a U-Net with separate spatial/temporal attention (ModelScope), a transformer with separate attentions (Latte), and a transformer with unified 3D attention (CogVideoX) [full text, Section 1; Section 3.3-3.4].

### Goal: Does the paper offer a sampling-time mechanism for prompt-appropriate motion when no reference video exists?
No. The probe and its application both start from a reference video: DDIM inversion of a source video for the analysis [full text, Section 3.2], and LoRA fine-tuning on a single reference video restricted to early timesteps for motion customisation [full text, Section 4.1-4.2]. It localises where motion is encoded but provides no way to decide what motion should form from a text prompt alone.
