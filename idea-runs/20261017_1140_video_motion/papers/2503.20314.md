# Wan: Open and Advanced Large-Scale Video Generative Models

## What this paper does
The technical report for the open Wan suite: a 3D causal VAE (Wan-VAE) and flow-matching diffusion transformers at 1.3B and 14B parameters, with T2V, I2V, editing, personalisation, camera control and real-time extensions. It describes the data pipeline, including motion-quality tiers, the DiT design (umT5 cross-attention for text, shared adaLN MLP), the training schedule, inference acceleration, and benchmark and human evaluations. In those evaluations Wan-14B leads, and Wan-1.3B is competitive while needing only 8.19 GB VRAM.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What are Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B's prediction target, latent geometry and inference footprint, and what does its training data imply about static versus moving content?
- **Prediction target.** Rectified-flow matching, x_t = t·x_1 + (1−t)·x_0, with velocity target v = x_1 − x_0 and an MSE loss [full text, Section 4.2.2 / Eq 1–3]. The clean-video estimate is therefore a linear function of the velocity at each step.
- **Latent geometry.** Wan-VAE compresses (1+T)×H×W×3 video to [1+T/4, H/8, W/8] with 16 channels [Section 4.1.1]. Patchify is a (1,2,2) 3D convolution, giving L = (1+T/4)×H/16×W/16 tokens [Section 4.2.1]. Text enters through cross-attention [Section 4.2.1].
- **Inference footprint.** The 1.3B model supports 480p and needs 8.19 GB VRAM, which fits consumer GPUs [Abstract; Section 1]. Sampling uses "typically around 50" steps [Section 4.4]. The paper gives no per-video time on an RTX 4090 for the unaccelerated 1.3B model.
- **Training-data composition.** Pre-training data is sorted into motion tiers. "Static videos" (interviews) are kept for quality. "Camera-driven motion" with minimal subject motion gets "substantially lower sampling priority", and post-training selects videos with simple and complex movement [Section 3.1–3.2]. The paper does not quantify how static the 1.3B model's outputs are.
- **Positional encoding.** The extracted text does not describe the DiT's positional encoding. The RoPE details used in this run come from [2609.23658].
