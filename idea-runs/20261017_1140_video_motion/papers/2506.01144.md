# FlowMo: Variance-Based Flow Guidance for Coherent Motion in Video Generation

## What this paper does
Training-free guidance for flow-matching text-to-video models (Wan2.1-1.3B, CogVideoX-5B). At each of the first 12 of 50 denoising steps, it takes the CFG velocity prediction and computes an appearance-debiased temporal signal: the L1 distance between consecutive latent frames. It then takes the per-patch variance of that signal over time, averages over channels, and uses the maximum over spatial patches as the loss. One gradient step (Adam) on the input latent z_t lowers this loss, and the step is re-predicted. It reports better VBench motion smoothness and final score and a human-preference gain in motion coherence on VideoJAM-bench.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Where in the denoising trajectory does FlowMo locate the formation of coarse motion, and what signal does it compute there?
On Wan2.1-1.3B, the clean-latent estimate x̄1 = z_t − σ_t·u shows coarse spatial structure in steps 0-4, while "motion is determined at steps 5-8, and refined in later steps". The variance gap between human-rated coherent and incoherent videos becomes visible from about step 5 [full text, Section 3.2, Fig. 2-3, Eq. 4]. The signal is Δu_f = |u_{f+1} − u_f| on the CFG velocity, σ² = variance over frames per patch and channel, s = channel mean, and L = max_{w,h} s. The update is z_t ← z_t − η∇L (Adam, η=0.005), after which both CFG branches are re-run [full text, Section 3.3, Alg. 1, Eq. 2-3, 7-9]. It is applied in the first 12 timesteps "since these are responsible for coarse motion and structure". Applying it at all steps creates artifacts, and replacing max with mean weakens it because "most patches are static" [full text, Section 4 Implementation; Section 4.3].

### Goal: Does FlowMo's objective encode how much motion the prompt asks for, and what is its effect on dynamic degree and cost?
No. The loss only penalises temporal variance of frame-to-frame change in the worst patch [full text, Eq. 3, 8]. Uniform or zero frame differences minimise it, and nothing in it depends on the motion the prompt implies. Dynamic degree drops: Wan2.1-1.3B 83.21→81.96, CogVideoX-5B 65.29→63.92. The authors say "some decrease to the dynamic degree is expected", because removed artifacts were contributing spurious motion [full text, Section 4.2, Table 1; Appendix E]. Runtime goes from 99.27 s to 234.30 s (×2.39) because of backpropagation through the DiT. Experiments ran on two 80 GB H100s. Stated bound: it "cannot synthesize motion types the model has not learned to represent" [full text, Section 4 Implementation; Section 4.4].
