# Video Diffusion Models are Training-free Motion Interpreter and Controller (MOFT)

## What this paper does
NeurIPS 2024. It extracts "motion features" (MOFT) from intermediate features of pretrained video diffusion models (AnimateDiff U-Net and others). The temporal mean over frames is subtracted from the features ("content correlation removal"), and the channels whose PCA weights track panning direction are kept ("motion channel filter"). MOFT then drives training-free motion control and point-drag editing by gradient steps on the latent, which match masked MOFT to a reference MOFT taken from a real video or synthesised from channel statistics.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does MOFT treat the temporal mean of a video tensor as content and the residual as motion, and on which tensor?
Yes. Following VideoFusion's shared-plus-residual noise, it hypothesises that "we can filter out the content correlation by eliminating similar information across frames", X_norm = X − (1/F) Σ_i X_i. The shared part is "content correlation information ... semantic content and appearance", and the residual "primarily capture[s] motion information" [full text, Section 3.1 / Eq 1]. PCA shows that raw features do not separate by panning direction while mean-removed features do [Section 3.1, Fig. 2]. The operation acts on intermediate network features (U-Net upper block 1), not on the CFG difference or the sampler state [Section 3.3 / Eq 2].

### Goal: Does it use the decomposition to change sampling, and does it touch classifier-free guidance or withhold a frame-shared component?
It changes sampling only through latent optimisation, z ← z − η∇L with L = masked ‖M − M^r‖ against a reference MOFT [Section 4.1 / Eq 3, Alg. 1]. A motion target is required (a reference video or a synthesised channel trend). It does not modify the CFG combination rule, and nothing is withheld or rescaled in the guidance. MOFT is reported to be informative at early denoising steps, earlier than DIFT [Section 4.1, Fig. 6].
