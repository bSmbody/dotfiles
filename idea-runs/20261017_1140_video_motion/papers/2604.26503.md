# Delta Score Matters! Spatial Adaptive Multi Guidance in Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
Argues from a Tweedie/differential-geometry view that CFG is a tangential linear extrapolation whose uniform global scale pushes high-energy regions off the curved data manifold. SAMG replaces the scalar CFG weight with a per-pixel map: guidance energy ‖Δε‖² is normalised to [0,1] over spatial positions at each step and mapped linearly to a scale between ω_max (low energy) and ω_min (high energy). Zero extra cost; evaluated on SD1.5/SDXL/SD3.5 and on T2V with CogVideoX-2B and ModelScope (ChronoMagic-Bench-150).

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use to change motion amplitude or motion quality relative to standard classifier-free guidance?
SAMG turns the CFG scale into a pointwise field. It computes pixel-wise guidance energy E_t(x) = ‖Δε‖², normalises it with min/max "performed spatially across the current timestep" [full text, Section 3.3 / Eq 11], and sets Ω(x) = ω_max − Ê_t(x)(ω_max − ω_min), so high-energy (edge/texture) positions get ω_min and smooth regions get ω_max; the output is ε_u + Ω ⊙ Δε [Eq 12]. The map is computed per position with no spatial smoothing [Section 4.4]. Nothing in the rule distinguishes the temporal axis from the spatial ones or separates frame-shared from frame-varying content; the motivation is over-saturation and structural collapse [Section 1].

### Goal: What does it report about its effect on dynamic degree / flow magnitude, and about how the guidance scale interacts with motion?
For video it reports CLIP SIM, MTScore, frame LPIPS/SSIM and CHScore-Flow as a motion-smoothness measure (CogVideoX-2B 60.15 vs 59.93; ModelScope 72.10 vs 70.47) [Section 4.3]. It states that high global scales cause "temporal inconsistencies in videos" [Abstract, Section 1]. It reports no dynamic degree or flow-magnitude result, so the effect of its adaptive scale on motion amplitude is not measured.

### Goal: What method assumption limits it for controlling motion amplitude under CFG (target signal, per-model or per-prompt tuning, cost, applicability)?
The scale field is driven only by local guidance energy, a proxy for manifold-deviation risk (Assumption 1, bounded curvature) [Section 3.3]; it has no motion signal and no temporal decomposition, so it can modulate how hard CFG pushes at a location but not whether the push changes inter-frame motion or static appearance. Limitations reported are resolution/overlap failures in the compressed latent [Appendix D]; bounds [ω_min, ω_max] are hand-set [Section 4.4].
