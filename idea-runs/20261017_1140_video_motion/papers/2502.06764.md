# History-Guided Video Diffusion

## What this paper does
ICML 2025. Introduces the Diffusion Forcing Transformer (DFoT), a video diffusion model trained with independent per-frame noise levels so that it can condition on any subset of history frames, and History Guidance (HG), a family of CFG-style guidance rules over that history. HG-v is plain CFG with history vs. masked history; HG-t composes scores conditioned on different history subsequences; HG-f ("fractional") guides with history frames that are only partially noised, which keeps their low-frequency content. Evaluated on Kinetics-600, RealEstate10K, robot imitation and very long rollouts; HG-f is reported to fix the static videos that strong vanilla history guidance produces.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: Does its "guidance across time and frequency" split a guidance difference into frame-shared and inter-frame (temporal) components with separate gains, and does it apply to text CFG in a pretrained T2V model?
No. Eq 5 is a weighted sum of conditional-minus-unconditional score differences, each conditioned on a different history subset H_i and history noise level k_{H_i}, with weights ω_i [full text, Section 5 / Eq 5]. "Time" means which history subsequence is conditioned on; "frequency" means how noisy the history frames are, and Appendix A.2 shows that conditioning on a noised input acts as a shrinkage that attenuates weak (for natural images, high) frequencies [Appendix A.2, Proposition A.7]. HG-f is ∇log p(x|x_H) + ω[∇log p(x|x_H^{k_H}) − ∇log p(x)] [Section 5 / Eq 6]: one ω per difference, and no decomposition along the generated video's time axis. It requires DFoT's per-frame-noise training to evaluate history-conditioned scores, so it does not apply to text-conditioned CFG in an off-the-shelf T2V model [Section 4, Section 5].

### Goal: What does it report about guidance strength versus motion dynamics, and how does restricting the guided information to low frequencies change it?
Strong vanilla history guidance (ω ≥ 3) makes "overly static videos with minimal motion", because guidance toward history consistency has the trivial solution of copying the most recent history frame [Section 5, "Frequency Axis"; Section 6.3]. Fig. 5's caption states that "vanilla history guidance trades off dynamics·diversity for quality·consistency". Guiding with fractionally masked history (k_H = 0.3, 0.6) leaves high-frequency detail and fast motion unconstrained by guidance; it "consistently increases dynamics while maintaining quality" and lowers Kinetics-600 FVD from 181.6 to 170.4 [Section 6.3, Fig. 5–6]. So in this setting, strong guidance toward detailed appearance drives the sample static, and removing the detailed band from what is guided restores motion.
