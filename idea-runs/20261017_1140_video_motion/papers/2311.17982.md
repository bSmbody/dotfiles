# VBench: Comprehensive Benchmark Suite for Video Generative Models

## What this paper does
VBench (CVPR 2024) splits video-generation quality into 16 disentangled dimensions. Temporal quality covers subject consistency, background consistency, temporal flickering, motion smoothness and dynamic degree. Frame-wise quality covers aesthetic and imaging quality. Video-condition consistency covers object class, multiple objects, human action, colour, spatial relationship, scene, appearance style, temporal style and overall consistency. Each dimension has its own prompt suite of about 100 prompts and a specialised automatic evaluator, such as DINO, CLIP, RAFT, a frame-interpolation prior, MUSIQ or ViCLIP. Human preference annotations validate each dimension.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are dynamic degree, motion smoothness, subject/background consistency, imaging quality and overall consistency computed, and which prompts do they use?
- **Dynamic degree.** RAFT estimates flow between consecutive frames. The mean of the largest 5% of flow magnitudes decides whether the video is static, and the score is the fraction of non-static videos [full text, Appendix G.1 "Dynamic Degree"]. It is binary per video. Its prompt suite is the Subject Consistency suite, which contains motion descriptions [Appendix H.1].
- **Subject consistency.** The mean, over frames, of the DINO cosine similarity to the first frame and to the previous frame [Appendix G.1, Eq 1].
- **Background consistency.** The same formula with CLIP image features [Eq 2].
- **Motion smoothness.** Drop the odd frames, re-interpolate them with a video frame-interpolation model, and score the MAE to the true frames [Appendix G.1].
- **Imaging quality.** MUSIQ trained on SPAQ, averaged over frames [Appendix G.1].
- **Overall consistency.** ViCLIP video–text similarity [Section 3.1.2].
- **Temporal flickering.** MAE between consecutive frames on prompts with a static-scene prefix [Appendix G.1, Eq 3–4; Appendix H.1].
- **Prompt suites.** About 100 prompts per dimension [Section 3.2; Appendix H.1]. The Subject Consistency suite (19 COCO movable categories with actions) is reused for both motion smoothness and dynamic degree [Appendix H.1].

The paper also states that a completely static video scores well on the consistency dimensions, which is why dynamic degree exists, and it notes a trade-off between temporal consistency and dynamic degree [Section 3.1.1; Section 5].
