# RAFT: Recurrent All-Pairs Field Transforms for Optical Flow

## What this paper does
RAFT (ECCV 2020) is a deep optical-flow network with three parts: a per-pixel feature and context encoder, a multi-scale 4D all-pairs correlation volume, and a convolutional-GRU update operator. The update operator iteratively refines a single high-resolution flow field by looking up the correlation volumes. At publication it had state-of-the-art accuracy on Sintel and KITTI, strong cross-dataset generalisation, and was efficient.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does RAFT estimate, and is it cheap and robust enough to score flow magnitude on thousands of generated videos?
RAFT estimates dense per-pixel displacement between two frames. It maintains one flow field at full resolution rather than a coarse-to-fine cascade, which the authors say avoids missing "small fast-moving objects" [full text, Section 1]. It runs at 10 fps on 1088×436 video on a GTX 1080Ti, and at 550 ms per frame on 1080p with 12 iterations [Section 1; Section 4.5]. Sintel final-pass EPE is 2.855 px and KITTI F1-all is 5.10% [Abstract]. So per-frame-pair flow on 480p generated clips costs well under a second on a modern GPU, and thousands of videos can be scored within a GPU-day. The paper does not discuss generated video. Its known failure cases are fast motion, occlusion, motion blur and textureless surfaces [Section 1], so flow on blurry or flickering generated frames can be noisy. VBench builds its dynamic degree on RAFT [2311.17982].
