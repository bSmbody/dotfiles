# HPSv3: Towards Wide-Spectrum Human Preference Score

## What this paper does
HPSv3 (ICCV 2025) is a text-to-image human-preference model. It is trained on HPDv3, which holds 1.08M text–image pairs and 1.17M pairwise comparisons, drawn both from current generators and from real photographs of low to high quality. The model is a Qwen2-VL backbone with an MLP head, trained with an uncertainty-aware ranking loss. The paper also proposes Chain-of-Human-Preference (CoHP), which uses HPSv3 to pick the best image at each refinement step.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does HPSv3 score, on what inputs, and what are its limits as a per-frame quality guard for video?
HPSv3 maps an (image, prompt) pair to a scalar preference score. Qwen2-VL extracts joint image–text features and an MLP produces the output, r = f_φ(E_θ(x, c)) [full text, Section 4.1 / Eq 1]. It is trained on pairwise comparisons with an uncertainty-aware ranking loss [Section 4.1]. It is image-only: it sees no temporal information. As a video guard it can therefore only be applied per frame (for example, averaged over sampled frames), where it measures per-frame visual quality and prompt fit and is blind to motion. The authors list a "unified scoring metric" that gives no breakdown into colour fidelity, sharpness or clarity, plus a prompt-distribution bias toward JourneyDB-style prompts [Section 10, Limitation]. So oversaturation or sharpness changes caused by guidance would show only through the holistic score.
