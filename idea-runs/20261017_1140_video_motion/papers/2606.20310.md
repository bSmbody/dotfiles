# Through the PRISM: Preference Representation in Intermediate States of Video Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
A latent video reward model. A frozen video DiT (CogVideoX-2B, Wan2.1-1.3B or Wan2.1-14B) processes a noisy latent z_t and the prompt through its first 12 blocks. A learnable query cross-attends to the visual and text tokens, and an MLP outputs a timestep-conditioned reward; about 21.8M parameters are trained. Training uses 26,391 human preference pairs, annotated on visual quality, text alignment and motion quality and kept only when all three agree. Because it scores noisy latents, it enables early-step best-of-N (N=5) that cuts sampling cost by up to 10× while beating pixel-space VLM reward models on VBench.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How early in denoising can PRISM discriminate candidates, and what does it detect about motion?
Selecting at step 1 cuts normalised time to 10% (CogVideoX-2B) and 19% (Wan2.1-1.3B), but on CogVideoX-2B step-1 selection is "close to the Random baseline". Selecting slightly later beats the strongest pixel-space baseline, and the larger PRISM (Wan2.1-14B) already beats all baselines at step 1 [full text, Section IV-D2, Fig. 6]. Qualitatively, the pixel-space baselines tend "to select near-static videos with minimal dynamic content", while PRISM picks up articulated motion [full text, Section IV-C2, Fig. 5]. The reward is a single overall preference scalar, not a prompt-specific estimate of motion amount.

### Goal: What does PRISM require, and what can it not do?
It needs a reward head trained on a large human-labelled preference set built from many generators [full text, Section IV-B]. It is tied to a VAE latent space: an evaluator for a different VAE requires decode and re-encode [full text, Section V]. Used for best-of-N it only selects among N=5 independent seeds and does not alter a seed's committed motion. At N=5, the random-selection baseline already moves VBench by about 0.3 points [full text, Section IV-A; IV-C2].
