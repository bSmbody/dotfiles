# Improving Motion in Image-to-Video Models via Adaptive Low-Pass Guidance

## What this paper does
Shows that image-to-video (I2V) models are markedly less dynamic than their text-to-video siblings: Wan2.1 dynamic degree is 39.4 for T2V and 32.1 for I2V on matched first frames. It traces this to premature exposure to the conditioning image's high-frequency details in the first denoising steps, which lock the sampler onto a static "shortcut" trajectory. ALG low-pass filters (downsamples) the conditioning-image latent inside the CFG conditional and unconditional terms during the early steps. A third, unconditional term keeps the original image for fidelity. On Wan2.1/2.2 and LTX-Video I2V, ALG raises VBench dynamic degree by about 33% on average with near-unchanged quality metrics.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does ALG identify as the cause of suppressed motion at early denoising steps, and what evidence supports it?
Hypothesis: "over-exposure to high-frequency elements during early generation stages ... disrupts the coarse-to-fine nature of the generative process", so the input image's fine details lock in early and confine the trajectory, yielding static video [full text, Section 3.1]. Evidence has three parts. (i) Dynamic degree drops 18.6% from T2V to I2V while other VBench quality metrics barely change [full text, Table 1]. (ii) Low-pass filtering the input image raises dynamic degree monotonically with filter strength, at a fidelity cost [full text, Fig. 3]. (iii) PCA of intermediate I2V features shows them locking onto the input's fine details after a single denoising step (t=0.02 of 50 steps), and low-pass filtering prevents this [full text, Section 3.1, Fig. 2].

### Goal: What is ALG's mechanism and cost, and is it applicable when no conditioning image exists (pure T2V)?
v_ALG = v(x_t, x_init, ∅) + w·(v(x_t, x_init^(t), c) − v(x_t, x_init^(t), ∅)), with x_init^(t) a low-pass filtered image latent whose strength κ(t) is a step function: strong before t_trans, zero after. The equation uses three velocity evaluations per early step instead of CFG's two [full text, Section 3.2, Eq. 2-4]. Filtering only until t_trans=0.06 already gives +32% dynamic degree, and κ*=1.6 gives +29% DD for −0.5% VBench-QS [full text, Section 5, Fig. 5]. Results: Wan2.2 31.7→39.0, Wan2.1 28.9→39.4, LTX-Video 15.5→21.5 [full text, Table 2]. The lever is the frequency content of an external conditioning image, which pure T2V does not have. The method also specifies no motion target: it removes a static attractor rather than saying what motion should form.


### Goal: What mechanism does it use to change motion amplitude or motion quality relative to standard classifier-free guidance?
ALG keeps the I2V CFG form v(x_t, x_init, ∅) + w(v(x_t, x_init, c) − v(x_t, x_init, ∅)) [full text, Section 2 / Eq 1] but replaces the conditioning image x_init by a low-pass filtered version (bilinear down/up-sampling or Gaussian blur) whose strength decays with the timestep, returning to the original image after a transition time t_trans [Section 3.2]. The diagnosis: intermediate DiT features lock onto fine details of the input image after a single denoising step, which "confines the trajectory" and yields static video; low-pass filtering delays this [Section 3.1, Fig 2]. Motion is thus increased by changing the condition fed to both CFG branches early, not by changing the guidance combination.

### Goal: What does it report about its effect on dynamic degree / flow magnitude, and about how the guidance scale interacts with motion?
Wan 2.1 I2V has 18.6% lower dynamic degree than T2V from the same first frames [Section 3.1, Table 1]. Static low-pass filtering raises dynamic degree monotonically with filter strength but lowers aesthetic quality [Fig 3]. ALG raises dynamic degree Wan 2.2 31.7 → 39.0, Wan 2.1 28.9 → 39.4, LTX-Video 15.5 → 21.5 with similar quality scores [Section 3.2, Table 2]. A transition at t_trans = 0.06 already gives +32% dynamic degree; increasing initial filter strength gives diminishing gains [Section 5]. Motion-augmented prompts help both CFG and ALG [Table 4]. The paper does not vary the CFG scale itself.

### Goal: What method assumption limits it for controlling motion amplitude under CFG (target signal, per-model or per-prompt tuning, cost, applicability)?
The mechanism requires an external image condition whose frequency content can be filtered, so it does not apply to pure text-to-video sampling where no such condition exists [Section 2 problem setup, Section 3.2]. The amount of motion is controlled open-loop by two hyperparameters (initial filter strength κ*, transition time t_trans) [Section 5, Fig 5]; there is no measurement of the motion in the current prediction and no prompt-dependent target.


### Goal: Which band of the conditioning image does ALG keep under full guidance in the early window, and does the paper locate the static shortcut in coarse content or in fine detail?
It keeps the coarse band and withholds fine detail. The paper attributes suppressed motion to "premature exposure to high-frequency details in the input image", which makes the model "lock onto these fine details ... preventing large-scale, coarse motions from developing" [full text, Abstract; Section 1]. Its hypothesis is "over-exposure to high-frequency elements during early generation stages, which disrupts the coarse-to-fine nature of the generative process" [Section 3.1]. ALG is built "to use only low-frequency information at the early stage" [Section 3.2]. The default filter is bilinear down/up-sampling by a factor of 2.5 [Section 4, setup], so the image's coarse layout stays in both CFG branches at the full guidance weight. Dynamic degree rises monotonically with filter strength, at an aesthetic-quality cost [Section 3.1, Fig. 3], with diminishing gains in κ* [Section 5, Fig. 5b]. Down-sampling helps more than Gaussian blur, which the authors attribute to more aggressive removal of fine detail [Section 5, Fig. 5c]. The paper never withholds the coarse content while keeping the detail, and its explanation of the shortcut is entirely about high-frequency content.
