# Training-free Guidance in Text-to-Video Generation via Multimodal Planning and Structured Noise Initialization (Video-MSG)

## What this paper does
Builds an explicit motion plan and injects it through the initial noise. GPT-4o writes a background description. A T2I model (FLUX) plus an I2V model animate the background. RAM and Grounding-DINO detect background objects, and GPT-4o then outputs per-frame foreground bounding boxes. A T2I-generated object, cut out with SAM, is pasted along those boxes to form a "video sketch". The sketch is VAE-encoded, forward-noised to t_inv = α·T, and denoised by the T2V backbone (VideoCrafter2 or CogVideoX-5B), with α chosen by an LLM. Improves T2V-CompBench motion binding, spatial relations and numeracy.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does Video-MSG specify a motion target and inject it into early denoising?
The target is a pixel-space sketch: a static object image translated and resized along LLM-planned bounding boxes over an animated background [full text, Section 3.1-3.2]. Injection is SDEdit-style. The sketch latent is forward-diffused to t_inv = α·T, z_{t_inv} = sqrt(α_t) z_0 + sqrt(1−α_t) ε, and denoising starts there [full text, Section 3.3]. The full pipeline uses GPT-4o, a T2I model, an I2V model, RAM, Grounding-DINO and SAM. The authors contrast it with LVD, whose backprop-based attention energy guidance "could not fit ... even on an A100 (80GB)" with CogVideoX-5B [full text, Section 4.2].

### Goal: What trade-offs and failures does the noise-inversion ratio expose, and which motion types does it not improve?
Lower α means stronger trajectory control. On VideoCrafter2, motion binding goes 0.2233 (no inversion) → 0.2793 (α=0.8) → 0.3980 (α=0.5) [full text, Section 4.3, Table 2]. But "a lower α can lead to less natural generations" (the boy's motion is less natural at α=0.7). At α=0.9 "the egg movement is small and does not follow the trajectory" [full text, Section 4.4]. Video-MSG "did not improve the scores in dynamic attribute binding ... and object action and interaction". The authors attribute this to such changes being "difficult to guide solely with bounding boxes" [full text, Section 4.2].
