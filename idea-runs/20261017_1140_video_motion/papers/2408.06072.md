# CogVideoX: Text-to-Video Diffusion Models with An Expert Transformer

## What this paper does
CogVideoX (ICLR 2025) is an open text-to-video diffusion transformer family at 2B and 5B parameters. It has four main components:
- a 3D causal VAE with 8×8×4 compression;
- an "expert transformer" that runs full 3D attention over concatenated text and video tokens, with separate expert adaptive LayerNorms for each modality;
- progressive multi-resolution training with frame packing;
- explicit uniform timestep sampling.

The diffusion setting is v-prediction with zero terminal SNR. The paper reports automated metrics and a human evaluation against open and closed models.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What are CogVideoX-2B's prediction target, positional encoding, resolution/length and inference cost, and how do they differ from the 5B model?
- **Prediction target.** v-prediction with zero SNR on the LDM noise schedule, not flow matching [full text, Section 3 opening, line "we adopt v-prediction ... and zero SNR"]. A clean-video (x̂0) estimate is therefore available at every step through the usual v-to-x0 conversion.
- **Positional encoding.** The 5B and 2B models differ here. The paper introduces 3D-RoPE, which applies 1D RoPE independently to x, y and t on 3/8, 3/8 and 2/8 of the channels [Section 2.2, "3D-RoPE"]. It shows RoPE converging faster than sinusoidal absolute embeddings [Section 4.1, Fig. 10a]. But the hyperparameter table lists "Position Encoding: sinusoidal" for CogVideoX-2b and "RoPE" for CogVideo-5b [Appendix A, Table 6]. So CogVideoX-2B uses absolute sinusoidal positions and has no RoPE distance decay.
- **Resolution and inference cost.** 2B at 480×720, 6 s: 49 s and 18 GB on an H800 with 50 inference steps. 5B at the same setting: 113 s and 26 GB [Appendix A, Table 7].
- **Architecture size.** 2B has 30 layers, 32 heads and hidden size 1920. 5B has 42 layers, 48 heads and hidden size 3072 [Table 6].
- **Attention.** Full 3D attention is chosen over 2D+1D because separated attention "makes it challenging to maintain the consistency of large-movement objects" [Section 2.2, "3D Full Attention", Fig. 5].
