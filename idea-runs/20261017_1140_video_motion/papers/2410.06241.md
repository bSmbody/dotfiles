# ByTheWay: Boost Your Text-to-Video Generation Model to Higher Quality in a Training-free Way

## What this paper does
Training-free edit of temporal attention maps in U-Net T2V models (AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter2). Temporal Self-Guidance blends the temporal attention map of the first up-block into later blocks to reduce cross-block disparity (fewer structural artifacts); Fourier-based Motion Enhancement scales the high-frequency components (along the softmax axis) of temporal attention maps by β > 1, which provably raises map "energy", correlated with RAFT motion magnitude. Applied during the first 20% of steps under ordinary CFG; raises VBench dynamic degree at no extra cost.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use to change motion amplitude or motion quality relative to standard classifier-free guidance?
It does not touch the CFG combination; it edits the model's internal temporal attention. Energy E of a temporal attention map [full text, Section 4.2.1 / Eq 4] correlates positively with RAFT flow magnitude [Fig 2c]. The map is FFT-decomposed along the softmax axis, its high-frequency band is multiplied by β and inverted (Eq 5–6); Theorem 1 keeps the softmax property and Theorem 2 shows β > 1 raises energy [Section 4.2.2]. Temporal Self-Guidance blends up_blocks.1 maps into later blocks with ratio α [Section 4.1 / Eq 3]. β is chosen as max{β0, sqrt((E1 − E2^L)/E2^H)} so that post-enhancement energy is at least the original energy [Section 4.3 / Eq 7]. Operations run in the first 20% of DDIM steps with CFG [Section 5.1].

### Goal: What does it report about its effect on dynamic degree / flow magnitude, and about how the guidance scale interacts with motion?
VBench dynamic degree rises AnimateDiff 0.4073 → 0.5245 and VideoCrafter2 0.4086 → 0.5547, while FreeInit lowers it to 0.2941 [Section 5.3 / Table 2]. Keeping only low-frequency attention components gives near-static video; only high-frequency components gives abundant but flickering motion [Section 4.2.2, Fig 5]. Too large β gives motion artifacts; too large α loses motion [Section 5.5]. No study of the CFG scale's effect on motion.

### Goal: What method assumption limits it for controlling motion amplitude under CFG (target signal, per-model or per-prompt tuning, cost, applicability)?
The amount of motion is set by user-chosen β0, α, τ that differ strongly across backbones (AnimateDiff α=0.6, β=1.5; VideoCrafter2 α=0.1, β=10); the authors note defaults "may not be universally optimal for different backbones, since different base models exhibit variations in their motion preferences" [Section 5.1]. The only adaptive element (Eq 7) references the model's own unenhanced energy, not a prompt-implied motion level. The method presupposes separate per-block temporal attention maps of a factorized U-Net [Section 3.2, Section 4.1]; its upper bound is the backbone's sampling distribution [Section 11 Limitation].
