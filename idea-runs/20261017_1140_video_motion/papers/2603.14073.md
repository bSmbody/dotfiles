# MotionCFG: Boosting Motion Dynamics via Stochastic Concept Perturbation

## What this paper does
Training-free CFG variant for T2V (Wan2.1-1.3B/14B, CogVideoX-5B). An LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) marks motion tokens in the prompt; at each early step their text embeddings are perturbed with fresh Gaussian noise to form a "motion-degraded" negative condition, and the sampler extrapolates away from it (anchored on the conditional prediction) for the first τ fraction of steps, then reverts to ordinary negative-prompt CFG. Interpreted as a CFG++-style manifold-constrained repulsion. Reports large gains on DEVIL/FLOW dynamics metrics at ~1 s extra cost over CFG, and an extension to object counting.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use to change motion amplitude or motion quality relative to standard classifier-free guidance?
It replaces the null/negative anchor of CFG during early steps. Motion-token embeddings are perturbed, c_pert[i] = c[i] + σ_c n_t for i in I_motion, with fresh noise n_t per step [full text, Section 3.2 / Eq 2]. The guided prediction is ε(c) + ω(ε(c) − ε(c_pert)) (c-anchored form) for the first τ fraction of steps, then standard CFG with a negative prompt [Section 3.2 / Eq 4, Algorithm 1]. Section 3.3 derives Eq 4 as one gradient step pushing the Tweedie estimate x̂0(c) away from x̂0(c_pert) (CFG++-style). The guidance difference is still multiplied by one scalar ω over the whole spatiotemporal latent; what changes is only which condition it is contrasted against. Motion tokens come from an LLM via in-context prompting (or manual choice) [Section 3.2, Appendix B.1].

### Goal: What does it report about its effect on dynamic degree / flow magnitude, and about how the guidance scale interacts with motion?
The paper states CFG is "motion-agnostic" and, with static-dominated training data, its uniform extrapolation "tends to reinforce the dominant static modes" [Section 1, Section 3.1]; replacing the null prompt with "static, still picture" gives only marginal gains (Table 3: DEVIL 0.3083 null vs 0.3069 negative-prompted). On Wan2.1-14B MotionCFG roughly doubles FLOW (6.06 vs 2.95) at comparable X-CLIP [Section 4.1]. Perturbing non-motion tokens also raises dynamics (DEVIL 0.3320) but drops X-CLIP by 0.35, so the contrast direction mixes content change with motion [Table 3]. σ_c raises FLOW from 4.02 to 6.40 [Table 4]; but lengthening the window τ from 0.05 to 0.30 lowers FLOW 1.80 → 1.11 and X-CLIP 22.94 → 21.94 [Table 5], i.e. the response to the schedule is non-monotone. σ_c = 3.0 causes over-saturation and black silhouettes [Appendix B.3]. Versus FlowMo it is stronger on dynamics at 1.0× instead of ~1.4–1.5× time [Appendix C.1, Table 10].

### Goal: What method assumption limits it for controlling motion amplitude under CFG (target signal, per-model or per-prompt tuning, cost, applicability)?
(1) No target: the negative anchor is a random perturbation of the motion-token embedding, so the method pushes away from a "motion-ambiguous" neighbourhood but never measures how much motion x̂0 has or should have; the amount is set only by (σ_c, τ, ω) [Section 3.2, Tables 4–5]. (2) The intervention window is hand-set; the authors list "adaptive scheduling methods that automatically identify the optimal intervention window" as future work and note that different attributes need attribute-specific scheduling [Section 4.3, Section 5 Limitations]. (3) It depends on an external LLM for token identification [Section 5 Limitations]. (4) A single scalar ω multiplies the full difference ε(c) − ε(c_pert), so appearance changes caused by the perturbation are amplified together with motion (Table 3 non-motion result, Table 5 X-CLIP drop). Cost is 2 NFE/step, like CFG [Appendix B.2].


### Goal: Does early-window guidance amplification transfer across backbones without schedule changes, and how much do the dynamics of plain negative-prompt CFG move across its hyperparameter sweep?
It does not transfer unchanged. On CogVideoX, applying MotionCFG from the first step "exhibits severe artifacts": the Tweedie estimate at t=T "is dominated by dataset-specific biases (e.g., POND5 watermarks)", and amplifying there "may amplify these artifacts instead of enhancing dynamics". The fix is a delayed schedule in which the first steps use plain conditional prediction (ω_std = 1.0) before MotionCFG is applied [full text, Appendix A]. Wan2.1 does not need the delay [Appendix A]. The baseline is CFG with a "static, still picture"-style negative prompt, and all methods share ω_std = 6.0 [Section 4, Baselines; Appendix A]. That baseline "often produces nearly static frames" [Section 4.1]. In the text-fidelity vs. dynamics trade-off plot, where each point is a hyperparameter setting, "Baseline and Interval Guidance cluster in a narrow range, indicating limited controllability over dynamics" [Section 4.1, Fig. 4].


### Goal: How do samplers that weaken early or overall guidance (Interval Guidance, CADS) change motion in T2V compared with negative-prompt CFG, at the same base scale?
Both are run as baselines with the same ω_std = 6.0 as negative-prompt CFG [full text, Section 4 Baselines; Appendix A]. Interval Guidance (IG, Kynkäänniemi et al.) applies guidance only inside a noise interval; the interval used is not stated in the sections read. CADS anneals noise into the condition. On the Gemini motion prompts [Table 1]:
- Wan2.1-1.3B: baseline FLOW 4.10, DEVIL 0.3069, CLIPScore 0.2535; IG FLOW 3.76, DEVIL 0.3147, CLIPScore 0.2392; CADS FLOW 2.22, DEVIL 0.1973.
- Wan2.1-14B: baseline FLOW 2.95, DEVIL 0.2324; IG FLOW 2.65, DEVIL 0.2732; CADS FLOW 2.41, DEVIL 0.2410.
- CogVideoX-5B: baseline FLOW 2.65, DEVIL 0.2415; IG FLOW 2.96, DEVIL 0.2955; CADS FLOW 2.93, DEVIL 0.2884.
The text summarises: "CADS tends to suppress motion (lower FLOW than baseline in most settings), and Interval Guidance offers moderate gains but at the cost of reduced CLIPScore" [Section 4.1]. Qualitatively both "introduce artifacts such as over-saturation or subject morphing" [Section 4.1, Fig. 3]. In the hyperparameter sweep, "Baseline and Interval Guidance cluster in a narrow range, indicating limited controllability over dynamics" [Section 4.1, Fig. 4]. So on Wan2.1, switching guidance off outside an interval lowers pixel-level motion (FLOW) and frame-wise text alignment, with a small rise in the composite DEVIL score; weakening the condition early (CADS) lowers motion. Neither separates the frame-shared from the inter-frame part of the guidance.
