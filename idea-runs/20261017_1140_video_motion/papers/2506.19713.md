# Guidance in the Frequency Domain Enables High-Fidelity Sampling at Low CFG Scales

## What this paper does
Frequency-decoupled guidance (FDG) for image diffusion. The CFG update is passed through a linear invertible frequency transform (a Laplacian pyramid in practice), and its low- and high-spatial-frequency components receive separate guidance scales, w_low < w_high. Analysis shows that low-frequency guidance drives global structure, prompt alignment, oversaturation and diversity loss, while high-frequency guidance mainly adds detail. FDG improves FID/recall over CFG on class- and text-conditional image models at essentially no extra cost, and relates guidance-interval schedules to the changing low/high norm ratio of the guidance signal over sampling steps.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How does FDG decompose the classifier-free guidance update and assign separate gains, and in what prediction space?
Using a linear invertible transform F (Laplacian pyramid or wavelets), CFG is rewritten as F⁻¹[F(D_u) + w(F(D_c) − F(D_u))], so each band is guided independently [full text, Section 4 / Eq 3–6]. FDG replaces the single w with w_low for the low band and w_high for the high band [Section 4]. Predictions are converted to x_0 (denoised) prediction before decomposition [Appendix D, algorithm comment "We assume all model predictions are converted to x_0 prediction"]. Laplacian pyramids are used in all main experiments [Section 4, Implementation details]. It "introduces no significant computational overhead" [Section 4].

### Goal: What does each frequency component of the guidance control, and what failure does a uniform scale cause?
Low-frequency guidance "primarily govern[s] the global structure and condition alignment", and excessive low-frequency guidance is "the main cause of the reduced diversity" and "the primary cause of oversaturation"; high-frequency guidance "enhances quality while preserving diversity" [Section 1; Section 5.2, Fig. 5]. Prompt alignment is driven mainly by the low band [Section 5.3, Fig. 6]. A uniform scale therefore gives blurry/incoherent outputs at low w and oversaturated, low-diversity outputs at high w [Section 4]. The low-band norm of the guidance signal dominates early (high-noise) steps and decreases over sampling while the high-band norm grows [Section 5.4, Fig. 7].

### Goal: Does FDG consider video, a temporal axis, or motion?
No. All experiments are on images; the decomposition is purely spatial (2D Laplacian pyramid / wavelet), and the text does not discuss temporal frequencies, frames, or motion beyond citing video diffusion as an application area of diffusion models [Section 2; grep of full text]. It also does not separate a frame-shared component from frame-varying components.
