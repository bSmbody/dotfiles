# LatSearch: Latent Reward-Guided Search for Faster Inference-Time Scaling in Video Diffusion

## What this paper does
Trains a latent reward model: a transformer over patchified noisy latents, a step embedding and the prompt, predicting visual quality, motion quality and text alignment at any timestep. Video-level reward labels from external verifiers are transferred to intermediate latents by scaling them with the cosine similarity between z_t and the final z0. Training combines regression and preference losses. At inference it runs an SMC-like search: candidates start from perturbed copies of a base noise, and at scheduled steps they are resampled multinomially by softmax reward with duplicate seeds pruned. Reports VBench-2.0 gains and up to 79% runtime reduction against other inference-time scaling methods.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does LatSearch obtain a reward for intermediate (early) latents, and what supervision does it need?
Targets are r̃_t = s_t·r with s_t = ½(1 + cos(z_t, z0)). Here r = (r^VQ, r^MQ, r^TA) comes from external verifiers or human annotation of the decoded final video [full text, Section 3.2, Eq. 6-9]. The model is trained with regression plus pairwise preference losses [full text, Eq. 11-14]. The authors list the similarity weighting as "an approximation of true semantic contribution" and say more principled temporal credit assignment is needed [full text, Section 6].

### Goal: How does the search act on the motion a trajectory has committed to?
Candidates are z_T^(i) = sqrt(1−η²) z_T^(0) + η ε_i, each evolved independently by the sampler. At scoring steps, seeds are resampled by softmax reward and duplicates are pruned [full text, Section 3.3, Eq. 15-17]. It therefore chooses among trajectories but never changes the coarse motion inside one. It also requires the trained latent reward model.
