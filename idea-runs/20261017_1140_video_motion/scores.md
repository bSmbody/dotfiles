### C1 verdict

**Verdict**: `revise(focus=coherence)`

**Evidence**
- [2603.14073] **Core coherence problem.** Under plain CFG, the inter-frame part of the guidance difference already gets the full gain. In MotionCFG that gain is ω_std = 6 with a "static, still picture" negative prompt. Even so, the baseline "often produces nearly static frames", and across its hyperparameter sweep its dynamics "cluster in a narrow range, indicating limited controllability over dynamics" (Section 4.1, Fig. 4). The candidate argues that "most of any extra gain goes to appearance". That is a statement about energy share. In a linear split, the gain on the inter-frame part does not depend on what the frame-shared part receives. C1 can therefore only beat uniform CFG through a mechanism it does not name. Either (a) a high frame-shared gain actively suppresses motion in the later nonlinear denoising steps (early appearance lock-in), or (b) the inter-frame band only helps at gains far above the point where appearance saturates.
- [2502.06764], [2506.08456] Both papers support mechanism (a), and the candidate should build on them. In History Guidance, strong guidance makes videos static because copying the history is a trivial solution. Guiding only the low-frequency history restores motion, and Kinetics-600 FVD improves from 181.6 to 170.4. In ALG, early exposure to fine image detail locks image-to-video sampling into a static shortcut, and low-passing that input raises dynamic degree by about 33%. If (a) is the real cause, the useful lever is a lower frame-shared gain early. Raising the inter-frame "magnification" gain would be secondary, and the paper would need the ablation that separates the two: w_s lowered with w_m = w, against w_m raised with w_s = w.
- [2506.01144] FlowMo finds that T2V predictions are appearance-dominated, which means the inter-frame part of the difference is small in energy. Under mechanism (b), w_m would then have to be large. The claim that "only a moderate extra gain on one band is needed" is unsupported.
- [1804.02684] **Source grounding is mostly correct but stretched.** The candidate states correctly the "anchor + α × difference" form, the split between unmagnified texture and magnified shape, and the band-pass and moderate-factor conditions. But C1 splits raw VAE-latent values additively into a temporal mean and deviations. That is the first-order-Taylor (intensity) variant of Wu et al., which this paper calls prone to noise and excessive blurring, and "degraded ... when large motions or drifts occur". It is not the shape/texture separation C1 cites as justification. For a dog that actually crosses the frame, or for a camera pan, the moving object's appearance sits in the inter-frame part and would be boosted along with the motion. The mapping "temporal mean = texture" holds only while displacement is small relative to the spatial scale. The candidate restricts the split to coarse scales and the early window, which partly covers this. Magnification also maps δ to (1+α)δ: it scales motion that already exists and cannot change its kind. In the motivating failure, it would most likely amplify the in-place gait and the sliding background rather than make the dog cross the beach. The intuition's claim that it "magnifies the motion the prompt asks for" overreaches.
- [2506.02300], [2403.07347] The candidate's own sources predict cross-fading as the default outcome, not a tail risk. Extrapolating a difference in a basis that does not encode position brightens, blurs or shifts globally instead of moving structure. FD4MM gets a "stable motion field" only from *learned* deep low-frequency features plus a learned mixer that recouples high-frequency detail; C1 has neither. For C1 to work, later denoising steps must turn a ghosted early x̂0 into one displaced object rather than two. The candidate needs this assumption but does not state it.
- [2610.14153] **Novelty and baselines.** TrajCFG (October 2026, concurrent) already allocates the CFG gain non-uniformly across T2V latent tokens at zero extra NFE. It raises Wan2.1-1.3B VBench dynamic degree from 65.28 to 70.83, above CFG-Zero (68.90). It gives one gain per token over a flattened axis and has no temporal split, so C1's mean/inter-frame split is still distinct. But the gap's premise that CFG applies "one gain over the whole latent" is now out of date, the candidate does not mention TrajCFG, and it is the zero-cost baseline C1 must beat.
- [2506.19713] FDG's rule is written for any linear invertible transform. C1 is that rule with a temporal transform (mean vs. residual) and a motion goal. Searches found no method that splits the T2V guidance difference into frame-shared and inter-frame bands with separate gains: "motion magnification diffusion", "frequency-decoupled guidance", "temporal frequency classifier-free guidance video diffusion", "frame difference classifier-free guidance", "token-wise guidance video", "temporal low-pass guidance", "appearance motion decoupled guidance video", and recency passes since 2025-10-17 on "classifier-free guidance video", "guidance video diffusion" and "motion video diffusion training-free". So the novelty gap is real but thin. Reviewers will read it as "FDG along time" unless the causal claim in the first bullet is made sharp and then confirmed.
- [2603.14073] **Feasibility overclaim.** C1 claims it "transfers unchanged to CogVideoX-2B and LTX-Video". MotionCFG had to delay its early-window boost on CogVideoX, because the first Tweedie estimates there are dominated by dataset biases such as POND5 watermarks, while Wan2.1 needed no delay (Appendix A). What the early window contains depends on the backbone, so per-model window setting should be expected. Cost and compute are otherwise fine: plain CFG cost (2 NFE per step) on Wan2.1-1.3B, on one 24 GB GPU, fits easily within 40 GPU-days.
- **Contribution type:** passes. C1 is a sampling rule, i.e. a methodological contribution.

**Revision asks** (for the innovator): State which mechanism C1 relies on, (a) relief from early appearance lock-in or (b) inter-frame gain above the appearance ceiling, and ground it in [2502.06764] / [2506.08456]. Define the split so it stays in the small-displacement regime (coarsest spatial band, early window), or move it into a representation that encodes position. Say plainly that C1 boosts the amount of motion, not its kind. Position C1 against TrajCFG [2610.14153]. Drop the "transfers unchanged" claim.

**Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Under plain CFG the inter-frame part of the guidance difference already gets the full gain, and sweeping that gain barely changes dynamics [2603.14073]; so C1's "reallocate the gain" story works only if lowering the frame-shared gain relieves an early appearance lock-in, or if the motion band tolerates gains far above the appearance ceiling, and the candidate names neither, while its additive latent split is the very basis its own sources say cross-fades instead of moving [1804.02684, 2506.02300].


### C2 verdict

**Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`

**Evidence**
- [2502.06764] **The source's band is inverted.** History Guidance traces stasis to vanilla guidance on the full-band history, whose trivial solution is to copy the last frame. Its fix keeps the low band under guidance: fractional masking "retains only low-frequency information ..., allowing high-frequency details (e.g., fine textures and fast motions) to remain unconstrained by guidance", and consistency "is mainly associated with low-frequency details" (Section 5, Eq 6). At the same ω, this "consistently increases dynamics" (Section 6.3, Fig. 5b). C2 casts its coarsest-band frame-shared part as the "copyable content" and withholds it, while finer bands, including their frame-shared part, keep full CFG. That is the reverse of the source's split. In the source, strong guidance on coarse shared content goes together with motion.
- [2506.08456] **Same inversion in ALG.** ALG blames "premature exposure to high-frequency details", which "disrupts the coarse-to-fine nature" of generation (Abstract, Section 3.1), and is designed "to use only low-frequency information at the early stage" (Section 3.2). Its default 2.5× down-sampling keeps the image's coarse layout at full guidance (Section 4). C2's intuition calls the frame-shared CFG part "a sharp conditioning image". But in the window C2 acts in, that part is coarse: the low band dominates early guidance [2506.19713, Fig. 7], and C2 itself restricts the split to the coarsest band. ALG's mechanism, premature detail breaking coarse-to-fine, therefore predicts no lock-in from a coarse early push. Calling that push "the T2V counterpart" of ALG's image maps it onto the part ALG keeps, not the part ALG removes.
- [2609.23658] **The lever's real in-domain support is RoPE spatial anchoring, and C2 uses it only as a side condition.** Positions that settle early pull other frames' candidates onto the same location ("the basketball remains stationary"). A frame-identical coarse term puts the same layout evidence at the same position in every frame, which is exactly the stationary configuration that anchoring favours. This argument, not History Guidance or ALG, has to carry the mapping. Anchoring is the model's own bias, so withholding the push can only stop feeding it, which is C2's stated main risk.
- [2404.07724], [2506.19713] **Thin delta over FDG, and a missing arm.** In Interval Guidance, early guidance harms layout diversity and saturation in images, not motion. FDG shows that skipping early guidance removes mostly low-band guidance (Section 5.4, Fig. 7), and that the low band is the primary driver of prompt alignment (Section 5.3). C2 therefore equals "FDG with w_low = 1 in the early window", except that it keeps the coarse inter-frame part at w. By C2's own appearance-dominance premise, that retained part is small. C2's deciding comparison has three arms: shared part withheld, all guidance withheld, inter-frame part raised. It lacks the arm that withholds the whole coarse band early, which is the only one that separates C2's temporal split from FDG's spatial one. Expect an alignment cost like Interval Guidance's on Wan2.1-1.3B: CLIPScore 0.2535 → 0.2392 [2603.14073, Table 1].
- [2610.14153], [2503.18886] **Misattributed evidence.** The dynamic-degree rise from 65.28 to 68.90 on Wan2.1-1.3B belongs to the full CFG-Zero*. TrajCFG describes that method as "within-step projection with suppression of initial updates" (Section 2), and its optimized scale acts at every step. The rise is not evidence that zeroing the first update helps, so the "mixed evidence" paragraph must drop it or qualify it.
- [2603.14073] **The key assumption is stated and testable but has little support, and the evidence pulls against it.** Across hyperparameters, the CFG baseline (ω_std = 6) and Interval Guidance "cluster in a narrow range" of dynamics (Section 4.1, Fig. 4). The problem statement also says more guidance gives "sharper but no more dynamic", not less dynamic. If the frame-shared push drove lock-in, raising ω should reduce motion unless the inter-frame push exactly offsets it. C2's cancellation account can be tested with its free per-step energy split, but nothing read shows that guidance strength lowers motion in T2V.
- [2607.26706] **Novelty search.** TPD gates the CFG update per frame but targets late events. It uses an LLM and a third branch, and notes that "scaling an update does not redirect it". So it is not a duplicate. Searches found no method that withholds the frame-shared part of a T2V CFG difference: "classifier-free guidance static video motion suppression", "temporal mean guidance video diffusion", "frame-shared guidance", "spatiotemporal guidance video diffusion", "frequency guidance video diffusion", "temporal frequency guidance", "static component guidance video", "inter-frame guidance diffusion", "temporal residual guidance video", "guidance appearance lock-in", "image-to-video static shortcut guidance", "conditional image leakage", plus recency passes since 2025-10-17 on "classifier-free guidance video", "guidance text-to-video", "video diffusion static motion", "CFG early steps flow matching" and "temporal guidance video generation". Positioning against ALG, Interval Guidance, CFG-Zero*, TrajCFG and TPD is accurate. The delta is real but thin.
- **What C2 fixes from C1:** it names mechanism (a). Its only change from CFG is a frame-identical term, so it cannot ghost. It claims amount, not kind, of motion. It drops the claim of unchanged transfer and sets windows per backbone, which is right given CogVideoX's watermark-dominated early estimates [2603.14073, Appendix A].
- **Feasibility and contribution type:** both pass. C2 costs 2 NFE per step and runs Wan2.1-1.3B on one 24 GB GPU, well inside 40 GPU-days, and it is a sampling rule.

**Revision asks** (for the innovator):
- Rebuild the mapping so it no longer contradicts its sources. Either:
  - (i) carry the lever on RoPE anchoring [2609.23658] and early layout commitment [2404.07724, 2506.19713], treating the frame-identical coarse push as same-position evidence in every frame. History Guidance and ALG then become analogies, and C2 must state that both keep coarse shared guidance and explain why T2V differs: there the coarse shared part is an additive term, identical in every frame, rather than a conditioning input. Or:
  - (ii) follow History Guidance and ALG literally and withhold the fine-band frame-shared part.

**Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: C2 borrows its lock-in principle from History Guidance and ALG but inverts their frequency condition: both keep the coarse shared content under full guidance and withhold only its high-frequency detail, which is what restores motion [2502.06764, 2506.08456]. Its own sources therefore do not support withholding the coarse frame-shared part. Unless RoPE anchoring [2609.23658] carries the argument, C2 reduces to FDG's early low-band withholding [2506.19713] plus a small inter-frame remainder.

**Evidence**
- [2609.23658] **The in-domain mechanism is stated correctly.** RoPE's decay in Δp^h and Δp^w makes a region in one frame attend to the same region in other frames.
