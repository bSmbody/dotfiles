# R3 (experimentalist): review of C3, "Unsticking the Subject" (UnStick-CFG), CVPR 2027

*Note on the literature check: during this review the arXiv API returned HTTP 429 (rate-limited), so `lit.py` fell back to arxiv.org web search. That search ANDs every term and sorts by date. I ran 17 mechanism and task queries (13 without a date limit, 4 with `--since 2025-10-18`) and screened the hits. For the core related work I relied on the run's existing digests of the cited papers, and I read one new candidate in full ([2606.06361]). Retrieval of older work ranked by relevance was weaker than usual, so a specialist (R1) should double-check the overlap section.*

## Summary
UnStick-CFG changes classifier-free guidance for text-to-video DiTs. For the first ~5 of 50 steps it splits the guidance difference D = x̂_c − x̂_∅ (in clean-estimate space) into four parts: spatially coarse vs. fine, crossed with frame-mean vs. frame-varying. It applies gain 1 instead of w to the frame-identical coarse cell only, keeps gain w elsewhere, and costs the same 2 NFE per step as CFG. The claimed insight is this: CFG's extrapolated frame-identical layout term is a screen-fixed positional reference. Through RoPE spatial anchoring, it biases the early competition between trajectory candidates toward a subject that stays at the same screen coordinates. The predicted evidence, all directional and at matched NFE:
- more RAFT flow and dynamic degree on Wan2.1-1.3B than both CFG and a whole-coarse-band control (FDG along time);
- lower mutual consistency (MC) of same-position candidates in the attention probe;
- sub-additivity with RoPE-λ;
- a larger gain on RoPE backbones than on sinusoidal CogVideoX-2B;
- flow that rises monotonically with w;
- gains concentrated on high-dynamics prompts;
- alignment and identity guards no worse than CFG.

## Strengths
- **A clean intervention.** It is a two-line change that reduces exactly to CFG and adds no NFE. Because the change Δ = −(w−g_s)·M L D is identical in every frame, the guided estimate's inter-frame differences equal CFG's at every step. Any motion effect must therefore come from how the network responds to the change, which is an unusually clean causal handle for a sampler paper.
- **Unusually good falsification structure.** Every pillar comes with a kill condition, and a day-1–4 pilot carries an explicit stop rule ("if prediction (i) fails here, stop and report the negative result").
- **The right first-order controls.** FDG along time (the whole coarse band at gain 1) and guidance switched off in the window both target the obvious alternative explanation, "it's just weaker early low-band guidance" [2404.07724][2506.19713].
- **The evaluation knows the dynamics–consistency trap.** It uses DEVIL's D_control and dynamics-binned quality, checks static prompts (grades 1–2) for spurious motion, and reads guard metrics at matched dynamics [2407.01094][2311.17982]. Most motion papers skip this.
- **The camera-split prompt set** (static camera vs. tracking shot) is a thoughtful way to separate screen-space sticking from legitimate tracking.
- **The budget arithmetic checks out.** 10 arms × ~410 prompts × ~4 min ≈ 11.4 GPU-days. The total of ~23 GPU-days leaves real reserve under the 40 GPU-day cap. The per-clip time is flagged as an assumption to be measured on day 1.
- **The attention probe extends the source analysis.** It moves the MC analysis of [2609.23658] from the conditional branch to the guided sampler, which that paper explicitly did not study.

## Weaknesses
- **[major] W1. The causal tests do not isolate the claimed mechanism; each has a generic alternative reading.** The authors say the method's delta over FDG is small, so the paper's contribution is the causal account. Even if every prediction holds at threshold, each mechanism pillar is confounded:
  - *(i) The pilot is selected on the outcome.* The 50 pilot seeds are the lowest-flow CFG outputs out of ~600, roughly the bottom 8%. Any arm that perturbs the early trajectory will raise flow on these seeds through regression to the mean, so the gain over CFG on this set is not evidence. Only the UnStick vs. FDG-along-time contrast is partly protected, and FDG along time is not norm-matched: it removes strictly more guidance energy.
  - *(ii) MC and winner location are probed only for CFG vs. UnStick.* Any perturbation that flips the winning candidate will also change MC and the winner's location. Without the same probe on a norm-matched perturbation, (ii) shows correlation, not that anchoring is the mechanism.
  - *(iii) Sub-additivity with RoPE-λ is expected from saturation alone.* Dynamic degree is a bounded share of binary per-video labels, both methods act on the same near-static seeds, and two motion boosters rarely add. The kill condition (additive or super-additive) is unlikely whatever the mechanism, so a "pass" carries little information unless it is calibrated against an unrelated booster paired with RoPE-λ.
  - *(iv) The positional-encoding contrast rests on one sinusoidal model.* CogVideoX-2B differs from the RoPE models in size, data and window. It is also already the most dynamic small model ("abundant motion ... yet incoherent" [2502.02492]). A smaller gain there follows from ceiling and headroom alone, so (iv) can be confirmed even if RoPE plays no role.

  The design lacks the one test that would separate the causal account from "any early perturbation frees near-static seeds": a two-sided dose-response. In particular, it never tests g_s > w (e.g. g_s = 2w−1, which adds exactly the perturbation UnStick removes) or a norm-matched random frame-identical coarse perturbation, on unselected seeds.
- **[major] W2. The thresholds are not quantitative, so "at threshold" means a barely detectable effect, and the study is underpowered to detect one.**
  - *Predictions are directional only.* Every prediction is phrased as "higher", "lowers", "within a small margin" or "monotone". No minimum effect size is stated.
  - *The main study has one seed per prompt.* That contradicts the "seed-bootstrap confidence intervals" in kill condition (i); only prompts can be bootstrapped.
  - *Dynamic-degree differences are hard to resolve.* With ~410 paired prompts and binary per-video dynamic degree, the SE of a paired dynamic-degree difference is about 1.9 points if ~15% of pairs are discordant (my estimate). A difference below ~5 points is therefore not reliably detectable.
  - *The bar is TrajCFG's gain.* TrajCFG, a baseline in this very study, reports +5.55 dynamic degree on Wan2.1-1.3B at zero extra NFE (65.28 → 70.83) [2610.14153]. A threshold-level UnStick result may tie or trail it.
  - *The human study is too small.* It has 50 pairs × 3 raters, only against CFG. With raters correlated within a pair, the effective n is ≈ 50, and a 60/40 preference gives a two-sided p ≈ 0.20. It cannot support the non-inferiority claim on identity ("not worse than CFG"); a non-significant difference is not non-inferiority.
  - *No correction for multiplicity.* Nine predictions × ~10 arms × ~15 metrics are tested with no stated primary endpoint or multiplicity correction.
- **[major] W3. The headline metrics do not measure the claimed phenomenon.** The motivating failure is "the dog trots in place ... while the sand slides behind it". By construction, that video already counts as non-static under VBench dynamic degree (top-5% RAFT flow) and has non-trivial mean RAFT flow from background and limb motion [2311.17982].
  - *Where it is measured, it is measured narrowly.* Subject screen-space travel, the quantity the thesis is about, appears only on the new 80-prompt camera-split set.
  - *The mask comes from the generator itself.* It is taken from the generator's own late-step cross-attention, which the method itself perturbs.
  - *What a skeptic will conclude.* Flow and dynamic-degree gains on VBench, VideoJAM-bench and DEVIL can come from extra background or camera motion or flicker, not from unsticking.
- **[major] W4. Two predictions pull against each other, and this shrinks the key contrast.**
  - *Premise (viii) says the withheld cell is large.* It predicts that the frame-identical coarse cell M L D holds the largest share ρ_k of guidance energy in steps 1–5.
  - *Then UnStick and FDG along time are near-twins.* If ρ_k is large, UnStick (which removes (w−1)·M L D) is close in removed energy both to FDG along time (which removes (w−1)·L D) and to guidance switched off in the window. Prediction (i) then asks for a qualitatively different outcome that hinges on the small residual (I−M) L D.
  - *Guard (ix) is under the same tension.* FDG's analysis puts prompt alignment mainly in the low band [2506.19713]. A large ρ_k therefore also makes "alignment within a small margin of CFG and above the guidance-off arm" harder to achieve.
  - *Consequence.* The discriminating contrast is a small-norm difference measured on a high-variance outcome, which needs far more than 50 pilot seeds and one seed per prompt.
- **[major] W5. Baseline tuning is not on equal footing.**
  - *UnStick gets a tuning loop.* Its window, J and g_s are effectively chosen on the Wan pilot, and the proposal does not say the ~200 pilot prompts are disjoint from the ~410 evaluation prompts.
  - *RoPE-λ is run at a single value.* It uses λ = 0.70, although its authors say training-free use "requires manual tuning", hand-tuned seeds needing values from 0.50 to 0.85 [2609.23658].
  - *TrajCFG is moved off its configuration.* It was configured for 40 steps on Wan2.1-1.3B [2610.14153] and is run here at 50.
  - *MotionCFG responds non-monotonically to its window τ* [2603.14073]. A single setting can land on the wrong side of that curve.
  - *What a fair comparison needs.* Each method's dynamics vs. text-alignment and quality curve, swept over its main knob with matched tuning effort.
- **[major] W6. The backbones are dated and small for a 2027 venue.**
  - *What is covered.* Wan2.1-1.3B as the primary model, plus CogVideoX-2B/5B and LTX-Video, all from 2024–25.
  - *The failure persists at scale.* MotionCFG reports lower base FLOW on Wan2.1-14B (2.95) than on 1.3B (4.10) [2603.14073].
  - *A cheap upgrade is available.* On Wan2.2-5B, TrajCFG reports base dynamic degree 55.56, so there is more headroom than on Wan2.1-1.3B [2610.14153]. The constraints allow offloaded inference, so a 100-prompt × 2-arm run on Wan2.2-5B or an offloaded 14B model is affordable. Without it, reviewers will ask whether the effect survives scale.
- **[minor] W7. The window is set in step indices, not noise levels.** Under a shifted flow schedule (I compute σ ≈ 1.00 → 0.98 for steps 1–5 of 50 at shift 5; I believe that is the public Wan2.1 sampler's default, but I have not verified this in a read paper), the window covers ~2% of the σ range. Two consequences follow:
  - *Transfer.* The setting will not carry over to other step counts or shifts unchanged; TrajCFG, for example, uses 40 steps.
  - *Dose.* The cumulative change to x_t is a tiny fraction of (w−1)·‖M L D‖. Report the σ span of the window and define the window in σ.
- **[minor] W8. The mechanism probe is under-specified for open-domain prompts, and its schedule is optimistic.**
  - *The source method is narrow.* [2609.23658] found its heads by attribution patching and extracted candidate regions for falling-ball prompts.
  - *This probe has gaps.* It does not say how candidates are extracted for arbitrary subjects or which heads it uses. "Winner away from the earliest-committed frame's position" is not defined operationally.
  - *The cost may be underestimated.* Self-attention over ~33K tokens per layer makes on-the-fly region aggregation an engineering task, which the 0.5 GPU-days and the day-1–4 window may not cover.
- **[minor] W9. A possible negative-prompt confound.** The CFG baseline uses a negative prompt, and on Wan the reference baseline uses a "static, still picture"-style negative [2603.14073]. Part of M L D may therefore be the frame-identical "anti-static" push. Withholding it could change motion for reasons unrelated to anchoring. Also run the arms with an empty negative prompt.
- **[minor] W10. Guard margins are unspecified.** "Within a small margin" (TA, ViCLIP) needs a pre-stated non-inferiority margin.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- **[2506.19713] FDG.** It separates low- and high-band guidance gains under a general linear-invertible-transform rule, in images. UnStick-CFG is FDG's rule with a temporal-mean transform composed in and gated to an early window; the authors concede that the method delta is small. This does not kill novelty for a paper framed around a mechanism. It does make the paper depend entirely on the causal evidence, which is where W1, W2 and W4 bite.
- **[2605.19398] DyMoS.** In I2V, it relieves early motion suppression caused by frame-shared content by reducing attention to the reference frame, in the first λ fraction of steps and in the conditional branch only. It shares the general principle that early frame-shared content suppresses motion. It does not kill novelty: DyMoS needs a reference frame, works through attention rather than the CFG rule, and is position-independent.
- **[2609.23658] RoPE spatial anchoring.** It supplies the mechanism, the window, the MC probe and the RoPE-λ baseline. Its own test of the condition-induced static content found that ablating it changed object size, not trajectory, on one successful seed. That is direct prior evidence against the load-bearing premise, though on a non-failing case. It does not kill novelty: it never studied the CFG combination.
- **[2610.14153] TrajCFG.** A concurrent zero-NFE CFG variant with per-token gains. It raises Wan2.1-1.3B dynamic degree by 5.55 points. The mechanism is different (per-token gains, no temporal split), so it does not kill novelty, but it sets the effect-size bar that a threshold-level UnStick result must clear.
- **[2603.14073] MotionCFG.** An early-window CFG modification aimed at motion on the same model, with a single scalar gain and an LLM-chosen anchor. Same niche, different mechanism; it does not kill novelty.
- **[2404.07724] Interval Guidance and [2503.18886] CFG-Zero\*.** Both weaken early guidance as a whole. UnStick is a component-restricted version of the same move; novelty depends on showing that the restriction matters (W1, W4).
- **[2502.06764] History Guidance.** Restricting the guided content to low frequencies restores dynamics. The banding is the opposite of UnStick's, and the guidance is on history, not text, so there is no novelty overlap. It is useful context for the "HG-literal" ablation.
- **[2606.06361] PhaseLock (read this review).** In I2V, during the first half of the steps, it pushes the state's inter-frame latent differences toward a 2-step motion prior. It leaves CFG unchanged and keeps the first frame fixed. It does not overlap: it uses no decomposition of the guidance difference and does not address pure T2V.
- **Screened, no digest.** My recency screening (2025-10 to 2026-10) surfaced no work that splits the T2V guidance difference into frame-mean and frame-varying parts. "Temporal Specification Guidance for Text-to-Video Generation" (temporal-logic guidance), "Temporal State Transport in Video Generation" (spectral transport) and GEARS (test-time scaling with candidate recycling) were screened by biblio and abstract only, not read. I make no overlap claim about them in either direction.

## Missing baselines or experiments
1. **A two-sided, norm-matched causal control on unselected seeds:**
   - g_s ∈ {1, (1+w)/2, w, (3w−1)/2, 2w−1}, i.e. extending the dose-response past CFG, where the anchoring account predicts more stasis;
   - a random frame-identical coarse perturbation matched in per-step norm and spectrum;
   - the complementary single cell (I−M) L D at gain 1.

   Run all of these through the MC and winner-location probe too.
2. **An energy-matched scalar early-guidance reduction.** Choose w_early per step so that the removed energy equals ‖(w−1)·M L D‖. This separates "which component" from "how much guidance is removed".
3. **The full 2×2 single-cell ablation.** Each of the four cells alone at gain 1, plus the existing whole-coarse-band and whole-frame-mean variants.
4. **Statistical protocol.**
   - Evaluate on ≥3 seeds per prompt on at least a 150-prompt subset, never selected on the CFG outcome.
   - Use continuous paired RAFT flow (log scale) as the pre-registered primary endpoint, with a stated minimum effect.
   - Apply a Holm correction across the secondary predictions.
   - Report the pilot's selected-seed gains only as diagnostics.
5. **A subject-centric motion metric on every benchmark.** Use an external detector, segmenter and point tracker rather than the generator's own cross-attention. Report screen-space subject travel and the ratio of subject to background flow, so that background or camera motion and flicker cannot inflate the headline.
6. **Tuning parity.**
   - Sweep each baseline's main knob: RoPE-λ ∈ {0.6, 0.7, 0.8}, TrajCFG s_max and native 40-step configuration, MotionCFG σ_c and τ, and CFG w.
   - Report dynamics vs. TA/VQ Pareto fronts.
   - Tune UnStick's window, J and g_s on a prompt split disjoint from the evaluation prompts.
7. **Calibrate the interaction test (iii).** Run an unrelated booster paired with RoPE-λ (TrajCFG + RoPE-λ or MotionCFG + RoPE-λ), measured on log flow rather than dynamic degree. Without this reference, "sub-additive" does not discriminate between mechanisms.
8. **A within-model positional-encoding contrast to complement (iv).** Use a RoPE-λ dose-response on Wan2.1-1.3B, i.e. UnStick's gain as anchoring is progressively weakened, and normalise cross-backbone gains by baseline headroom.
9. **A current ≥5B backbone on a subset.** For example Wan2.2-5B, or an offloaded Wan2.1-14B on 100 prompts × {CFG, UnStick, FDG along time}.
10. **A better-powered human study.**
    - Compare against the strongest baseline (TrajCFG) and against FDG along time, not only CFG.
    - Use 5 raters per pair, as in VideoJAM's protocol [2502.02492].
    - Include a power analysis and a pre-stated non-inferiority margin for identity breaks.
11. **The σ span of the window on each backbone,** and a version of the window defined in σ.
12. **Empty-negative-prompt versions** of CFG and UnStick (W9).

## Questions for the authors
1. What minimum effect does each prediction correspond to? For example, what relative gain in RAFT flow on the motion prompts, and what dynamic-degree gain over CFG and over TrajCFG, would you consider a success?
2. How will you separate UnStick's gain on the 50 lowest-flow pilot seeds from regression to the mean? Would a sign-flipped perturbation (g_s = 2w−1) also raise flow on those seeds?
3. What value of ρ_k do you expect in steps 1–5? If ρ_k ≥ 0.5, how much of the removed energy separates UnStick from FDG along time, and what sample size does prediction (i) need?
4. Are the ~200 pilot prompts disjoint from VBench, VideoJAM-bench, DEVIL and the camera-split set, and are the window, J and g_s chosen on the pilot?
5. In the probe, how are candidate regions and heads chosen for arbitrary subjects, and how exactly is "winner away from the earliest-committed frame's position" computed?
6. What σ interval do steps 1–5 cover under Wan's default time shift, and under the CogVideoX and LTX schedules?
7. Your design argues that raising the frame-varying gain above w barely changes dynamics [2603.14073]. Why, then, should flow rise monotonically with w under UnStick, where only the frame-varying and fine gains grow with w?
8. Will TrajCFG and RoPE-λ be run at their native configurations (40 steps; per-seed or swept λ), or only at the single settings listed?

## Rating
- rating: 3 (weak reject)
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.25. The load-bearing prediction is (i): UnStick beats both CFG and FDG along time on flow and dynamic degree on unselected Wan2.1-1.3B prompts. Several things count against it:
  - the change is frame-identical and acts over a ~2% σ span;
  - the conditional branch keeps its own shared coarse layout at gain 1;
  - [2609.23658]'s ablation of condition-induced content changed size, not trajectory;
  - switching guidance off early lowered FLOW on this model [2603.14073];
  - if ρ_k is large, the contrast with FDG along time rides on a small residual.
- one_point_fix: Make the primary endpoint a pre-registered, two-sided, norm-matched dose-response on unselected seeds (≥3 seeds per prompt), with a stated minimum effect on paired RAFT flow and subject-tracker travel:
  - g_s from 1 up to 2w−1;
  - a random frame-identical coarse perturbation of matched norm;
  - the complementary (I−M) L D cell.

  This replaces the selected-seed pilot comparison. If stasis increases monotonically with g_s past w and the random control does nothing, the causal account stands without having to rest on the confounded tests (ii) to (iv).
