# ConSA: Controllable Sparsity in Hybrid Attention via Learnable Allocation

## What this paper does
ConSA learns which attention units (layers or KV heads) of a pretrained Transformer keep full attention and which become sliding-window attention. Each unit gets a hard-concrete L0 mask choosing FA vs SWA output; an augmented Lagrangian enforces a user-given target sparsity rho. Stage 1 jointly trains masks and weights on the LM loss (1B tokens); Stage 2 binarizes masks and continues pretraining (100B tokens). Evaluated on self-pretrained 0.6B/1.7B models on MMLU, commonsense, LogiQA and short generation; learned allocations beat interleaved rules, head-wise beats layer-wise, and FA concentrates in contiguous middle-layer blocks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What objective and data drive the allocation, and is it on the model's own generations?
The autoregressive LM loss on pretraining text, under the sparsity constraint (Eq 6) [full text, Section 4.1, Section 4.3, Section 5.1 "Training Pipeline"]. It is teacher-forced on corpus text, not on the model's own rollouts, and the masks are learned jointly with all weights.

### Goal: How is the budget set and what long-generation evaluation is reported?
The budget rho is user-specified (0.25/0.5/0.75 swept) and enforced by the Lagrangian [Section 4.1, Section 5.5]. Evaluation is MMLU, LogiQA, CSQA, PIQA, SIQA, ARC, HellaSwag, WebQA-CN and two Chinese generation tasks [Section 5.1 "Evaluation"]; no long chain-of-thought or long-context retrieval benchmark. The mixer is SWA, not a linear/recurrent layer.

### Goal: What does it find about where full attention should go, relative to interleaving?
Learned layer-wise masks put SWA in the bottom layers (first layer always SWA) and FA in contiguous middle blocks, unlike even interleaving; head-wise SWA ratios follow a W shape across depth [Section 5.5]. An ablation finds unconstrained scalar gates with post-hoc ranking fail to differentiate units [Appendix C].
