# Graph-Enhanced Policy Optimization in LLM Agent Training

## What this paper does
GEPO builds an online state-transition graph for multi-step LLM agents by merging observations whose Sentence-BERT cosine exceeds a threshold. It scores nodes by betweenness centrality times similarity to the task prompt. That criticality sets a dynamic discount for the trajectory-level return, and it multiplicatively rescales the trajectory advantage at each step by (1 + beta * criticality of the successor).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does GEPO use the graph for credit, and does it pool or reweight shared outcome evidence?
The graph changes credit only through centrality. gamma'_t = clip(gamma_base + w_gamma * tanh(Delta I), 0, 0.999) shapes the return [full text, Section 3.3 Eq 3-5], and A_t = A_traj * (1 + beta * I~(s_{t+1})) redistributes it [Section 3.4 Eq 6]. It does not pool returns across states that share a node.

### Goal: What does its theory say about variance?
Appendix B.2 shows sign preservation (c_t >= 1). Propositions 1-2 show that the redistribution concentrates variance at critical steps. Under batch standardization and a decoupling approximation, the total per-step variance is conserved, not reduced [full text, Appendix B.2-B.3]. There is no covariance-aware estimator.
