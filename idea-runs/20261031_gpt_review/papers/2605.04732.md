# Using Common Random Numbers for Simulation-based Planning with Rollouts

## What this paper does
Studies when common random numbers (CRN) reduce the variance of the estimated value difference between two policies in an MDP. It shows the obvious "same random MDP for both" estimator can have higher variance than independent simulation, proposes a depth-dependent estimator that shares randomness only after the depth where the policies agree and proves it never has higher variance than independent sampling, and applies seeded rollouts to annuity payouts and Ludo.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Why does sharing random numbers between two compared rollouts reduce variance, and when can it fail?
Var(X - Y) = Var X + Var Y - 2 Cov(X, Y), so evaluating both policies on the same sampled deterministic MDP removes the shared part of the return variance [full text, Section 1.2]. But Proposition 1 gives MDPs where full sharing (X_D) has higher variance than independent sampling (X_I), because covariance can be negative when policies reach different states under the same noise [Section 1.2, Proposition 1]. The depth-dependent estimator X_DD shares randomness only for steps after depth d, where the policies agree; Theorem 2 proves Var(X_DD) <= Var(X_I) always, strictly lower when the shared tail has return variance [Section 2, Theorem 2]. The authors find the X_D anomaly "quite uncommon in practice" [Section 7].

### Goal: What is the provenance of the CRN principle in RL?
CRN comes from simulation (Glasserman and Yao) and in MDPs from PEGASUS (Ng and Jordan 2000), which treats a stochastic MDP as a distribution over deterministic MDPs by fixing seeds [Section 6]. Seeds are set as functions of state, action, step and simulation index [Section 7].
