# GraphPO: Graph-based Policy Optimization for Reasoning Models

## What this paper does
GraphPO is an RLVR method for reasoning LLMs. It grows a step-level rollout DAG and summarizes each node's path into a semantic state. Non-ancestor nodes whose embeddings have cosine similarity >= kappa are merged into equivalence classes, and the next-layer budget of a path that reaches an existing class is halved. Node scores pool terminal correctness across the class with one global coefficient w. Edge step rewards are score differences, and a dual-group advantage (correctness group plus path-length efficiency group) feeds a DAPO/PPO objective. Appendix F gives variance and bias analyses of the pooling.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are node scores pooled across merged states, and is the pooling weight a single global scalar?
S(v) = (c_v^own + w * sum_{q in Q(v)\v} c_q^own) / (t_v^own + w * sum t_q^own). Here c^own and t^own are the correct count and the terminal count reachable from the node through original direct edges, and w in [0,1] is one fixed pooling coefficient [full text, Section 4.3 Eq 3]. Step reward r_step(u,v) = (S(v) - S(u))(1 - eta(u,v)), where eta is endpoint similarity [Eq 4]. The correctness advantage standardizes r_step over E_cor(u), which holds the actual generated edges of every child in the graph suffix C_g(u) (own children plus the children of class partners) [Section 4.4 Eq 5]. Each edge's advantage multiplies its tokens' ratio in a clipped PPO/DAPO loss [Section 4.5 Eq 8]. No per-class or per-edge weight is learned or optimized.

### Goal: What does Appendix F.2 assume and prove about variance, covariance and bias of pooled scores and edge rewards?
Assumption 1: class members' true values differ by at most delta_kappa = O(1 - kappa). Assumption 2: children are i.i.d. given the parent, and the own-subtree terminal samples are independent and disjoint across class members. All variances are conditional on the sampled graph and the counts [Section F.1]. Lemma 2: Var(V^Q) <= rho_i * max p(1-p)/m with rho = (1 + (k-1)w^2)/(1 + w(k-1))^2, and bias <= delta_kappa. Theorem 1 compares advantage variance with Tree and GRPO and assumes conditionally independent child-score estimates. Corollary 1 keeps the endpoint covariance Gamma_uv = Cov(V^Q(u), V^Q(v)) in the edge-reward MSE bound and gives an MSE-improvement condition (S.12-S.14). It then drops Gamma "in the conditionally independent endpoint case". Proposition 1 (lower correctness-advantage variance via the enlarged group) assumes the per-edge step rewards in E_cor(u) are conditionally independent with a common variance [Section F.2, S.15-S.16].

### Goal: Do shared terminal outcomes create correlation that the analysis ignores?
Yes, in practice. S(u) counts every terminal reachable from u, so a parent's and a child's scores share the child's terminals. Class pooling also puts the same partner terminals into S of every member. So edges in E_cor(u) that come from different class members, or that share endpoints, have correlated rewards. That breaks Proposition 1's independence assumption. The paper handles this only through the Gamma term in Corollary 1. It never chooses w to account for it. It reports no gradient-level (score-weighted) analysis.
