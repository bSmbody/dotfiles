# Parallelizing Linear Transformers with the Delta Rule over Sequence Length

## What this paper does
Introduces a hardware-efficient training algorithm for DeltaNet (linear attention with the delta update S_t = S_{t-1}(I - beta_t k_t k_t^T) + beta_t v_t k_t^T). It reparameterizes the product of generalized Householder transitions with a WY representation so per-chunk transitions take O(d) memory, then uses the UT transform (a lower-triangular inverse per chunk) to turn the remaining recurrence into matmuls, giving a chunkwise-parallel form that runs on tensor cores. Trains 1.3B DeltaNet and hybrids on 100B tokens.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism makes the delta-rule recurrence chunkwise-parallel, and what property of the update does it rely on?
The update is affine in the previous state: unrolling gives S_t = sum_i beta_i v_i k_i^T prod_j (I - beta_j k_j k_j^T) [full text, Section 3.2 / Eq 4]. Products of (I - beta k k^T) are rewritten as I - sum w_i k_i^T (WY form, Eq 6-7), and the w/u vectors are obtained in matrix form by T = (I + tril(diag(beta) K K^T, -1))^{-1} diag(beta), W = T K, U = T V [Eq 10-11]. The chunk-level state update is then S_{[t+1]} = S_{[t]} + (U - W S_{[t]}^T)^T K [Eq 8]. Everything hinges on the transition being a (rank-1-perturbed) linear map of S whose coefficients depend only on keys/betas, not on S.

### Goal: What does it report as its limitation on hardware efficiency?
Training is still slower than GLA because state-to-state dependencies (the W S product) require "marginalizing" over the head dimension inside the kernel, so the head dimension cannot be tiled arbitrarily as it can for elementwise-gated GLA; this limits DeltaNet's state size and hence recall [full text, Section 5.3]. They suggest block-diagonal Householder transitions sized to SRAM as a possible fix. The fully parallel (attention-like) form needs a matrix inverse that scales cubically in length, so it is not used for training [Section 3.2, "Fully Parallel Form"].
