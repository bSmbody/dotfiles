# Tiled Flash Linear Attention: More Efficient Linear RNN and xLSTM Kernels

## What this paper does
Proposes TFLA, a kernel algorithm for chunkwise-parallel linear RNNs (mLSTM, and by reformulation other linear RNNs) with two levels of sequence parallelism: across chunks (states materialized in HBM) and within each chunk (tiling the intra-chunk QK^T along the chunk dimension, FlashAttention-2 style), plus parallelization over the embedding dimension. This removes the SRAM-imposed cap on chunk size (~64 in FLA), trading memory for speed, and introduces mLSTM with sigmoid input gate for faster kernels.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What computational form must a linear RNN have for TFLA's kernel to apply?
The intra-chunk computation must reduce to H = (Q K^T) V + Q C_{k-1} (three fused matmuls) [full text, Section 3 / Eq 12]. Appendix A.2 shows DeltaNet fits only after defining pseudo-values V* = U - W C_{k-1}, where U, W are precomputed by a separate kernel (the UT transform) and V* is materialized in HBM by the recurrent kernel [Appendix A.2 / Eq 17-18]. "TFLA can be applied to any linear RNN that either follows or can be reformulated into the simplified, chunkwise-parallel form of Equation (12)."

### Goal: What does it report about chunk size and remaining efficiency limits?
Chunk size L mediates runtime vs. memory; L=1 is the recurrent form, L=T the parallel form; there is an FLOP-optimal L, the runtime-optimal L (128-256 empirically) grows with sqrt(head dim) and with accelerator compute intensity, and newer hardware needs larger chunks [Section 5.3]. Theoretical optimum exceeds the empirical one, i.e. kernels do not yet reach peak [Section 5.3]. For mLSTM the exponential input gate needs FlashAttention-style max-state rescaling during accumulation [Section 3, "Tiled Computation"].
