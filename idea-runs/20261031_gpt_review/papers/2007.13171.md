# Train Like a (Var)Pro: Efficient Training of Neural Networks with Variable Projection

## What this paper does
Applies variable projection (VarPro) to supervised DNN training when the last layer is affine. The final linear weights W are eliminated by solving their (Tikhonov-regularized) convex subproblem exactly for the current feature-extractor weights theta, and the reduced objective is then minimized over theta alone. For least squares the inner problem is solved by SVD. The Gauss-Newton variant (GNvpro) extends this to cross-entropy with an inner Newton-Krylov solve. It is an offline, sample-average-approximation method with large batches, tested on surrogate modeling, segmentation and CIFAR-10.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What exactly is eliminated, how is the reduced gradient obtained, and under what conditions does VarPro work?
Model W F(y, theta), objective Phi(W, theta) = mean L(W F(y, theta), c) + R(theta) + S(W) with Tikhonov S (Eq 3-4). Eliminate W(theta) = argmin_W Phi (Eq 5-6). "The reduced objective function ... can be minimized using gradient-based optimization, without differentiating the inner problem": by Eq 7 (envelope argument), grad_theta Phi_red = grad_theta Phi(W(theta), theta). Gradients "can become inaccurate when the inner problem is not solved to sufficient accuracy" [full text, Section 2.1 / Eq 7; Appendix A]. The stated benefit is that minimizing over theta "implicitly accounts for the coupling with the eliminated variable W, which can dramatically accelerate the convergence" [Section 2.1]. Conditions: the loss must be convex in the output layer, and the inner problem must be "smooth, strictly convex, and of moderate size". The least-squares implementation assumes "the number of samples is greater than the number of output features". The method is an SAA scheme in which "batch sizes remain larger and typically significantly fewer optimization steps are needed" than SGD [Section 2.1-2.2 / Eq 8-10]. The convergence benefit is shown over many outer iterations, not for a single step.
