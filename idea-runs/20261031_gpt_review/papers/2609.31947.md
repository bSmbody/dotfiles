# On-Policy Attention Linearization

## What this paper does
Opal converts Qwen3-4B and MiMo-7B-RL into hybrids keeping every fourth layer as full attention (9 of 36) and GDN elsewhere, then adds prolonged on-policy distillation (student samples its own rollouts, frozen teacher gives dense token-level reverse KL), with KL-plateau-triggered rollout-length doubling (512 to 32K), a difficulty curriculum, and a WSD schedule to 2B OPD tokens. Recovers 100% teacher NIAH up to 32K and 83-93% of math reasoning (MATH-500 thinking 92-93.8%).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What failure of off-policy distilled hybrids on long generation does it identify, and why?
Off-policy KL (Eq 4) supervises only corpus (or teacher) prefixes; once a student error changes the generated prefix it also changes the recurrent state S_t, and the student is never trained on states induced by its own mistakes, so deviations compound over long horizons into degenerate/repetitive rollouts [full text, Section 2, Figure 2]. Prior methods (RADLADS, Zebra-Llama, HALO) all optimize Eq 4 [Section 2]. Matched off-policy controls are clearly worse than OPD on AIME (e.g., AIME'24 33.3 vs 17.1 at 400M) [Section 4.2, Table 3].

### Goal: How does it choose which layers keep full attention?
It does not search: it retains every fourth full-attention layer by fixed rule [Section 3, first paragraph], and SWA/MLA baselines also use the same 1:3 ratio [Section 4.3]. The on-policy signal is used only to train weights, not to decide allocation; it notes that some prior work adds a selection stage before distillation [Section 1].


### Goal: Which weights does Opal train in each stage, so is the all-full-attention configuration of the trained student still the teacher?
Stage 1 updates only GDN layers, with retained full-attention layers and all other parameters frozen. Stage 2 (off-policy KL) trains "all of the hybrid student's model weights", with a 10x smaller learning rate for retained full-attention layers. Stage 3 (OPD) uses one flat learning rate for all layers, so GDN and retained full-attention layers train jointly [full text, Section 3]. MLPs, norms and embeddings are therefore adapted to the GDN layers after Stages 2-3, and the student with its converted layers swapped back to the original attention would no longer equal the teacher.


### Goal: What AIME scores and evaluation protocol does Opal report for the 9/36 Qwen3-4B hybrid vs the teacher, how much memory does 1:3 save, and does it vary the full-attention ratio?
Table 2: Qwen3-4B teacher AIME'24 pass@1 73.8 / pass@8 86.7, AIME'25 pass@1 63.7 / pass@8 80.0, MATH-500 96.4; OPAL-Qwen3 (9 FA of 36) AIME'24 52.5 / 80.0, AIME'25 40.4 / 63.3, MATH-500 92.0; reasoning average 67.6 (83.4% of teacher) [full text, Section 4.2, Table 2]. So the AIME pass@1 gap to the teacher is about 21-23 pp, while MATH-500 is within 4.4 pp. AIME is reported as pass@1 and pass@8; the number of samples per problem and the number of seeds are not stated in the setup [Section 4.1]. AIME keeps improving to the end of the 2B-token OPD budget while MATH-500 saturates early [Section 4.2, Figure 3]. At the 1:3 ratio, cache plus recurrent-state memory is about 4x lower than full attention at 128K, with about 2x throughput at highest concurrency in vLLM [Section 4.3, Figure 4]. The ratio is never varied: SWA baselines also keep 1:3 "to approximately match long-context memory budgets" [Section 4.3]. All runs used 3-4 H100/H200 GPUs; total GPU-hours are not reported [Appendix B.2].


### Goal: Which prompt pools does Opal use for OPD, and how does it decontaminate them against AIME/MATH-500?
OPD prompts come from OpenThoughts-114K-Math, Dolly-15K and synthetic long-context retrieval tasks; later checkpoints use DeepScaleR and DAPO-Math-17K in place of OpenThoughts-114K-Math to push harder problems. All datasets are filtered by 13-gram overlap with the evaluation tasks [full text, Section 4.1 "Models and Datasets"]. Training budget per student is about 3B tokens: 100M alignment, 600M short-context KD, 300M 32K long-context KD, 2B student-generated OPD tokens, with rollouts growing from 512 to 32K [Section 4.1 "Training Parameters"].


### Goal: Does Opal report seed variance or per-problem sample counts for AIME, and does it apply any KV quantization or eviction to the 9 retained full-attention layers?
No to both. The evaluation paragraph lists the benchmarks and says only that AIME is reported as pass@1 and pass@8 [full text, Section 4.1 "Models and Datasets", evaluation paragraph]; no number of seeds, no confidence intervals and no samples-per-problem count appear in the setup or in Tables 1-2. A search of the body finds no mention of KV quantization, FP8 or eviction: the efficiency comparison serves full-attention and hybrid models in vLLM at the 1:3 ratio with uncompressed caches [Section 4.3 "Efficiency Gains", Figure 4]. The OPD ladder for Qwen3-4B grows the max horizon from 512 to 16K by 200M tokens and to 32K between 600M and 800M tokens, with WSD branches at each rung [Appendix B.3, Table 8], so a reviewer cannot tell from Opal how large run-to-run noise on 30-problem AIME is, and no published 9/36 + compressed-KV point exists to compare against.
