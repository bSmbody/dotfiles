# On State Reduction in Linear Attention

## What this paper does
Analyzes why trained linear-attention states are low-rank and proposes post-training structured pruning of the key/query dimension to shrink the recurrent state while staying compatible with fla kernels. Because depthwise causal convolutions on q/k forbid dense rotations (PCA), pruning is restricted to axis-aligned channel selection; selectors include L1, S-Wanda, gradient saliency, and a rank-revealing-QR selector (DRRQR) on calibration key/query activations. Evaluated on DeltaNet/Gated DeltaNet 370M/1.3B, Mamba-Codestral-7B and Qwen3.5-9B with LoRA recovery fine-tuning.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism selects which recurrent-state units are removed, and is the selection fixed or input-dependent?
A semi-orthogonal axis-aligned projection P_I selects a fixed index set I of key channels per head, sliced out of W_Q, W_K and the conv filters [full text, Sections 3.2-3.3]. I is chosen once from weight magnitudes, calibration-set input norms (S-Wanda), first-order Taylor saliency on a calibration set (Grad), or strong RRQR on the stacked calibration activations M=[K;Q] (DRRQR) [full text, Section 3.4]. The selection is static: the same channels are kept for every input and every position. DRRQR transfers across calibration corpora (PPL 41.5-41.7) while Grad degrades out of domain [full text, Table 2].
### Goal: What does it report as its limitation on long-context recall under state pruning?
State reduction "is generally not lossless" and needs recovery fine-tuning [full text, Section 6]. For purely recurrent GDN-1.3B at 50% reduction, RULER-4K falls 38.8->27.5 and NIAH-4K 81.3->61.9, while hybrid Qwen3.5-9B is unchanged (93.9->93.6) [full text, Table 4]; at 20% reduction NIAH-4K already drops 81.3->75.9 and SWDE 51.2->45.5 [full text, Appendix C.4, Table 11]. Authors say "at 50% the finite recurrent capacity begins to bind" and that combining with token-axis methods is untested [full text, Appendix C.4].
