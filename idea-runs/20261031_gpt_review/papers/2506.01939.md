# Beyond the 80/20 Rule: High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning

## What this paper does
Analyzes token-level generation entropy in long chain-of-thought of reasoning models (Qwen3-8B, AIME, over 10^6 tokens at T = 1.0). Finds that a minority of tokens have high entropy and act as "forks" that pick the reasoning direction, while most tokens, including mathematical expression components, are generated at near-zero entropy. Shows RLVR mainly changes high-entropy tokens and that restricting the policy gradient to the top 20% entropy tokens matches or beats full-gradient RLVR.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: In a reasoning model's own long CoT, which tokens carry high generation entropy (surprise), and are computed values / math expression tokens high or low?
Entropy is that of the generating distribution at each index [full text, Section 2.1, Eq 1]. Over half the tokens (about 50.64%) have entropy below 10^-2, and only 20% exceed 0.672 [Section 3, Entropy Pattern 1, Fig 2a]. The highest-entropy tokens are logical connectors and framing words: "wait", "however", "unless", "thus", "also", "since", "because", "suppose", "assume", "given", "define". The lowest-entropy tokens "are often word suffixes, source code fragments, or mathematical expression components, all of which exhibit high determinism" [Section 3, Entropy Pattern 2, Fig 2b-c]. Raising temperature only on low-entropy tokens can make the model produce nonsensical output, which marks them as the execution of an already-chosen path [Section 3, Fig 3]. The paper measures entropy, not the realized NLL of the sampled token, but the two coincide closely when the distribution is near-deterministic. So in a model's own trace, the tokens that state self-computed values are among the least surprising, and high surprise marks forks in direction, not new facts.
