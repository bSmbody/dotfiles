# WitCert: Sound Runtime Risk Observability and Gating for KV-Cache Quantization

## What this paper does
A runtime meter giving a sound per-(layer, head, step) upper bound on the total variation between exact and compressed attention for KV-cache quantization. Tier A: a deterministic band-norm witness of the key residual (Cauchy-Schwarz within RoPE frequency bands, position-invariant), valid for any cache-preserving quantizer and adaptive queries. Tier B: a sub-Gaussian certificate for a subtractively dithered INT8 quantizer under a request-level failure budget. The meter drives certify-fallback gating (page FP16 KV back in from CPU when the bound exceeds tau) inside SGLang. arXiv v1 and v2 are the same paper.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the scope of the Tier-A witness, and does it exclude latent caches?
Tier A covers schemes that materialize a per-token reconstructed key of the same shape; "Eviction, token pooling/merging, latent caches and recurrent-state compressions store no per-token reconstruction, so no residual exists and Tier A does not apply to them" [full text, Section 4.1]. Section 2 points to a companion paper [2608.05863] that extends the meter to latent caches, sparse selectors and recurrent state.
### Goal: How tight or vacuous are sound per-step bounds at low bit widths, and what does that imply for worst-case cell bounds?
The band-norm bound is "1289x loose at 4 bits", and the universal meter saturates for 1-2 bit schemes: "per-step certification of such schemes is vacuous" [full text, Section 4.1, Theorem 2 remark iii; Fig 1]. The data-independent worst-case bound of Runtime-Certified Quantized Attention [2605.20868], TV <= tanh(max|eps|) with Delta from quantization step and query norm, "authorizes zero compression" at every practical threshold tau <= 0.2 [full text, Section 1]. Open problem (1) is tightening the witness below 8 bits; (2) is cross-layer composition, since in a 28-layer sweep no single layer's pollution alone loses accuracy, so "any per-step bound, however tight, must be conservative for aggressive schemes" [full text, Sections 4.1, 6.4.1, 6.4.6].
### Goal: How do key and value errors combine in the output bound, and how does fallback work?
Output bound ||y - y_hat|| <= 2 TV max_t||v_t|| + sum_t p_t ||v_hat_t - v_t||, a key term and a value term handled separately; joint coverage is reported as pass_K and pass_V [full text, Sections 3, 4.2]. Closed loop: when the certificate exceeds tau, that KV head (or, block-decomposably, the highest-contribution blocks) is paged in from an FP16 CPU backup and recomputed; the median fallback case needs only 1 block [full text, Sections 6.3.4-6.3.5].
