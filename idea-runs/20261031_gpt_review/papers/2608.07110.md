# Modular TTT: Rethinking Test-Time Training as Composable Modules

## What this paper does
A framework that represents a TTT inner learner as a DAG of primitives (fast-weight network, loss, learning rate, weight decay, normalization) and composes their train-view and query-view rules automatically. It is used for systematic ablations at 160M-1.45B scale. Findings: small learning-rate initialization, weight decay and a single-layer nonlinearity help; MSE and inner-product losses perform similarly; deeper fast-weight networks and normalization hurt. The best variant matches Gated DeltaNet at 410M/1.45B on 100B tokens.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Do two-layer (deeper) fast-weight memories beat shallow ones under one-step TTT updates, and what does the paper blame?
No. "Deeper graph learners do not surpass the shallow frontier ... This difficulty already appears in the two-layer linear case" [full text, Deep Memory ablation]. At 160M with 2048 context, the best deeper row (Linear-SiLU-Linear-Norm) is still 0.094 validation loss worse than the shallow Linear-SiLU reference (3.0205) [Appendix, Table 21]. Explanation: "In the one-step Modular TTT setting, depth changes the geometry of the inner update". With MSE, a single fast weight sees a convex quadratic, but a deeper learner's "factor updates depend on activations produced by upstream factors and gradients backpropagated through downstream factors" (factor coupling), plus excessively large activations [Appendix, deep-memory subsection]. This identifies inter-layer coupling under one-step updates as a real obstacle. It does not test a solver that eliminates the readout.
