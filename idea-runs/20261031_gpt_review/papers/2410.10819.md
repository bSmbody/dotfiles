# DuoAttention: Efficient Long-Context LLM Inference with Retrieval and Streaming Heads

## What this paper does
Splits attention heads into retrieval heads (full KV) and streaming heads (attention sinks + recent window, constant KV). A per-KV-head gate alpha mixes full and streaming attention outputs; gates are optimized with model weights frozen on a synthetic multi-passkey dataset, by L2 distillation of final hidden states on the passkey positions plus an L1 sparsity penalty; gates are then binarized at a target sparsity. Reports up to 2.55x (MHA) / 1.67x (GQA) memory reduction with minimal long-context loss, and combines with 4-bit KV quantization.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are full-KV heads selected, and on what data?
Retrieval heads are defined by output deviation when restricted to sink+recent tokens, not by attention scores; gates alpha_{i,j} per KV head, attn = alpha*full + (1-alpha)*streaming, trained 2,000 steps on a synthetic passkey set with L_distill (L2 on last hidden states at passkey tokens) + 0.05 * L1 [full text, Section 2.2, Eqs 1-3]. Optimization-based identification beats attention-profiling (FastGen/RazorAttention style) and language-modeling-loss identification in ablations [Section 3.5]. All signals are off-policy synthetic retrieval, no long-CoT rollouts.
### Goal: Does it stack head-level KV removal with quantization?
Yes: combined with QServe 8-bit weights and 4-bit KV, Llama-3-8B decodes 3.3M tokens on one A100-80G, 6.4x capacity over BF16 full attention [Section 3.4, Figure 12]. This is a capacity demonstration, not a matched-bytes accuracy frontier.
