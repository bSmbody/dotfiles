# EPIG-Tree: Compute-Optimal Branching for Gradient-Efficient Reinforcement Learning

## What this paper does
EPIG-Tree treats tree-rollout construction for GRPO-style RL as a compute-allocation problem for policy-gradient estimation. By the law of total variance, the local gradient contribution mu_h psi_h(A) Y splits into a decision-uncertainty term and a continuation-uncertainty term, and the trace serves as a gradient-MSE proxy. From this it derives a Neyman-style suffix allocation n_e proportional to w_e ||psi_e|| sigma_e / sqrt(c_e) and a score-weighted branch score. It validates these on gradient MSE against a high-budget reference in cloned-state control, frozen LLM (Qwen3-8B GSM8K) gradient calibration, LLM math, and Wordle.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does EPIG already use a score-weighted gradient-MSE criterion, rather than value MSE, for tree rollouts?
Yes. Proposition 1 gives Var(Z_h|h) = mu^2 Var_A[psi Q] + mu^2 E[psi psi^T sigma^2], and its trace is the gradient-MSE proxy [full text, Section 4 Eq 6-8]. Theorem 1 minimizes sum_e w_e^2 ||psi_e||^2 sigma_e^2 / n_e under a cost budget [Section 5.1 Eq 9]. The paper says the psi-weighted score (EPIG-grad) beats value-variance-only EPIG-Lite where score norms differ [Section 5.2].

### Goal: Does it optimize estimator or pooling weights, or account for covariance among edges that share suffixes or terminals?
No. It decides where to spend rollouts, not how to weight existing ones. Suffixes are "independent" per edge, the per-edge variance terms are simply summed, and no cross-edge covariance or merged-state (graph) structure is modeled [Sections 5.1-5.2]. The estimator is the tree mean Q^ - V^ [Section 3 Eq 5].

### Goal: How much do estimator refinements change LLM gradient quality in practice?
Not much. In frozen Qwen3-8B/GSM8K calibration, tree-method gradient cosines to the 64-leaf reference are about 0.07 (EPIG-Lite) and 0.02 (entropy), against 0.23 for flat GRPO. The authors warn that "tree-local advantages and token masks are themselves consequential estimator choices" [Section 7.3]. In online single-turn math, branch-placement variants "fall within single-seed noise", and token masking can dominate branch placement [Section 7.4, Section 8].
