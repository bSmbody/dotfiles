# Reliability Theory for AI Control

## What this paper does
Applies classical reliability theory to a layered AI-control stack (Google DeepMind's defences against rogue deployment). The safeguards form a coherent binary system with minimal paths and cuts. Birnbaum importance ranks which component's improvement buys the most system reliability. The paper then separates visible safeguards from the basic failure events behind them: when one common-cause event disables several safeguards (a shared failure domain), the same block diagram drops from third-order to second- or first-order rare-failure suppression. It recommends making failure-domain separation an explicit invariant and measuring joint failures, because nominal redundancy is not real redundancy unless the backups fail independently.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is Birnbaum importance defined, and under what distribution of the other components is it evaluated?
B_i is the probability that component i is pivotal under the *current distribution of the other component states*: forcing i to work makes the system succeed while forcing it to fail makes it fail, B_i = P(phi(1_i, X_-i) − phi(0_i, X_-i) = 1) = dh/dp_i [full text, Section 3, Eq 3]. Equivalently B_i = P(success | i works) − P(success | i fails), or Cov(phi, X_i)/(p_i(1−p_i)). Each importance depends on the partners' readiness, e.g. B_M/B_R = r/m, so "the value of improving either one depends on whether its partner is usually ready" [Section 3]. Within the independent model, the system gain of a change Delta p_i is exactly B_i Delta p_i.

### Goal: What does a shared failure domain (common-cause event) do to the value of redundancy, and how should it be handled?
A common-cause event disables several visible safeguards at once; the safeguards exposed to it form its failure domain [Section 4]. With the diagram unchanged, a shared event c_AM lowers the minimum cut from three basic failures to two, and c_AMQ to one, so the rare-failure order falls from 3 to 2 to 1 [Section 4, Figure 2]. Componentwise Birnbaum importance cannot describe such an event, since both marginals and their dependence change; importance must be recomputed on the refined basic-event model [Section 4; Section 6.2]. Joint failure measurements reveal dependence that the independent model misses, quantified as P(R)[P(A^c ∩ M^c) − P(A^c)P(M^c)] [Section 4]. Recommendation: keep at least one failure boundary that the failure cause does not automatically cross, and do not treat redundant safeguards as independent without evidence [Sections 1, 6.1].

### Goal: Is the reliability model's independence assumption stated, and what are its limits?
Section 3 assumes independent readiness states; Section 4 relaxes it via basic events. The paper gives no calibrated numbers for GDM's stack ("a numerical top-event estimate would not be meaningful") and analyses a fixed deployment route [Sections 2, 6]. Binary components only; multistate extensions are mentioned as further directions [Section 7].
