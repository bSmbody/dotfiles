## Axis: object counting and spatial relations in training-free text-to-image guidance

### What has been done
- **Read the instance layout mid-trajectory, then regenerate.** CountGen [2406.10210] runs SDXL to t=500. It clusters self-attention features of one decoder layer (up_52) inside an Otsu mask taken from cross-attention, fixes the count with a *trained* ReLayout U-Net, then regenerates using a cross-attention BCE layout loss plus self-attention masking. Single class, counts up to 10, about 4.5x SDXL time. NUMINA [2604.08546] (Wan DiTs, plus CogVideoX MMDiT) stops pre-generation at step 20 of 50. It picks the most instance-separable self-attention head and the peakiest cross-attention head per noun, counts the fused regions, and edits the layout heuristically (remove the smallest region; add a copied or circular template at the cell that minimises overlap and distance to the layout centre). It then regenerates with cross-attention bias or score overwrite. ATHENA [2603.19676] (SD3.5-L, FLUX) decodes x0-hat at step 20 and counts with GroundingDINO. On a miss it restarts from the same noise and steers only the first steps with a global prompt-difference drift, eps + gamma(eps - eps(prompt with the observed count)), doubling or halving gamma once.
- **Shape early-step attention by latent gradients.** CountCluster [2508.10710] (U-Net only) optimises the latent once, at the first step: a KL loss pulls the thresholded object cross-attention toward k Gaussian clusters carved from it. Optimising over more steps is unstable, and random targets fail. ISAC [2505.20935] K-means-clusters foreground self-attention into exactly the parsed N groups at every step and repels their maximum pixel overlap, then binds semantics inside the clusters. It runs on SD3.5-M at 74.8 GB / 140 s (base: 22.9 GB / 40 s). On FLUX and Qwen-Image it falls back to best-of-10 selection.
- **Act on the initial noise.** D2D [2510.19278] turns detector logits into a soft count. It backpropagates through the frozen generator into a small MLP that rewrites x_T, and is shown only on one-step distilled models. [2510.11117] shows that in FLUX the initial noise fixes both the count and the object positions ("noise determines layout, text activates locations"). CFG 2–7 moves exact count accuracy only within 18.4–20.0%. Its box-located noise priors need a layout and were used with fine-tuning. PSG [2506.23418] gets large FLUX/SD3 position gains only by best-of-N search over noises (FLUX PSE 45.5 to 93.6 at N=16).
- **Relation losses on token-level attention.** InfSplign [2512.17851] applies centroid-margin, variance-presence and variance-balance losses to each noun token's U-Net decoder attention at every step. CoBind [2607.16307] activates entity-completeness and relation hinges (centroid order, distance, containment) in an early time window and attribute binding in a mid window, on SD1.5/2.1/XL. Gradient PSG [2506.23418] maximises the probability of superiority between two tokens' attention distributions, and is shown only on U-Nets and PixArt-alpha cross-attention.
- **Externally planned layouts on MM-DiT.** Stitch [2509.26644] (FLUX, SD3.5, Qwen-Image) takes per-object boxes from GPT-5/GPT-4. It generates each object under hard joint-attention masks for 10 of 50 steps, cuts it out with one selected text-to-image head, composites the pieces and finishes unconstrained. AST [2504.10148] finds that FLUX instance tokens act in layers 6–34 within the first ~16 steps, and reweights the T2T, I2T and I2I attention blocks toward GPT-4V sketches that were then manually refined.
- **Mechanism.** In T5-conditioned DiTs the spatial relation is decoded from the second object token's contextual embedding, not from the relation word. Ablating the relation word barely matters, and filler words break the circuit [2601.06338]. Relations in T5-based SD3.5/FLUX therefore have to be imposed geometrically on object regions, not by boosting relation tokens.

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Training-free count correctors for flow-matching DiTs detect a miscount from a mid-trajectory read-out: instance-separable attention heads, or a detector on the decoded x0-hat around step 20 of 50. They then restart from the same noise and push the first steps with a correction that is not computed from the early state, either a global prompt-difference drift or a heuristically edited layout imposed as attention bias. Single-pass alternatives instead force a fixed-N cluster partition onto early attention that is not yet instance-separable. Neither design attributes the final instance count to the early-step latent that actually decides it. Correction at the decisive steps is therefore open-loop and costs a second trajectory, and on SD3.5-class MM-DiTs the gradient variant needs about 75 GB.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2603.19676] ATHENA comes closest on SD3.5-L/FLUX: it reads the count at step 20 and corrects within the first steps of a restart. Its correction is a spatially global drift whose strength is set by a one-shot gamma doubling or halving. [2604.08546] NUMINA reads instance layout from selected heads at t*=20 of 50, where separability peaks, and regenerates. Its edits (remove the smallest region, add a template at the most central free cell) are heuristics that ignore how the early latent produced the miscount, and its over-segmentation errors are "irrecoverable". [2406.10210] CountGen has the same read-at-t=500, regenerate structure, with a trained ReLayout. [2508.10710] CountCluster and [2505.20935] ISAC act early in a single pass, but impose a target partition: CountCluster uses k Gaussians from a thresholded first-step map and becomes unstable beyond one step; ISAC runs K-means into exactly N clusters each step and loses 25/18 points on SD3.5-M when N is off by one. [2510.19278] D2D does assign credit to x_T by backpropagating a detector loss, but only through one-step generators. [2506.23418] PSG reaches MM-DiT only through best-of-N. [2510.11117] confirms the target: count and layout are fixed by the noise and early dynamics, and CFG cannot override them. The same deficiency recurs across five read papers. Query trail: "training-free counting text-to-image diffusion", "counting text-to-image", "object count guidance diffusion transformer", "count-aware generation", "numeracy diffusion", "instance layout early denoising", "object quantity text-to-image", "counting DiT text-to-image", plus a --since 2025-10-01 recency pass. No MM-DiT method that predicts the final count from the early state was found.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Credit-assignment flaw. The count is observable only around step 20 (ATHENA: earlier decoded images are "too noisy for reliable automated count estimation"; NUMINA: separability rises until t*=20 and then degrades). The count is decided by the noise and the first steps ([2510.11117]; [2508.10710]). Nothing links the two except restart plus a scalar strength search or a heuristic layout edit. So the correction applied at the decisive steps is not derived from how those steps determine the instance set. This shows in the reported behaviour: ATHENA reports gamma overshoot that flips the error direction, and its Static variant beats Adaptive on FLUX CoCoCount (73.3 vs 70.2). ISAC substitutes a structural prior (exactly N clusters) for a prediction, so a wrong or unformed partition is enforced rather than corrected.
- **Why it matters:** SD3.5/FLUX users on 24 GB GPUs currently get either ~1.6x latency for modest gains (ATHENA on SD3.5-L: 38.9 to 56.1% at 43.8 to 70.4 s), or a 3x memory bill (ISAC_LO: 74.8 GB), or N-fold search. Closing the gap would show whether an MM-DiT's eventual instance count is predictable and controllable from its early state. That would make single-trajectory count correction possible at the step where the count is decided, which matters for the problem's "stronger guidance does not fix the count" failure.
- **Evidence route:** (i) Step-wise predictability on SD3.5-M: agreement between a count read-out taken at step k and the final detector count, plotted against k, compared with NUMINA-style head read-outs and with x0-hat+GroundingDINO at the same k. (ii) Intervention efficacy: the share of miscounted trajectories fixed by acting at step k, at matched NFE and peak VRAM under 24 GB, against ATHENA restart, ISAC best-of-N and plain best-of-N. (iii) GenEval counting and T2I-CompBench++ numeracy at matched NFE, with HPSv3/ImageReward to show quality parity.

- **Gap:** Training-free relation guidance treats each noun as a single token-level attention distribution (InfSplign centroid margins, CoBind centroid and containment hinges, PSG probability of superiority). Training-free count guidance models instances only per class or class-agnostically, with no relational term (ISAC N-cluster repel, NUMINA/CountGen instance layouts, CountCluster k Gaussians). So for "three red apples ... to the left of a blue book" no inference-time objective binds a counted instance set of A to a relation with B. On MM-DiT the only methods that satisfy both delegate the instance set and positions to an external per-instance layout enforced by hard masks.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2607.16307] CoBind is the closest relation method: entity completeness plus relation constraints, both scheduled early. Its graph nodes are semantic entities, and it states that it "lacks explicit instance slots", that same-category attention maps "merge during the early stage", and that counting is not addressed; it is also U-Net only. [2512.17851] InfSplign collapses each noun to one centroid and variance, and its presence loss rewards a single tight blob. [2506.23418] gradient PSG compares whole token distributions and has no instance variables. [2505.20935] ISAC and [2604.08546] NUMINA have instance variables but no relation term; NUMINA places added instances by centrality, so a count fix can move an apple to the wrong side. [2509.26644] Stitch gets both on FLUX/SD3.5 only through LLM per-instance boxes: Region Binding alone reaches 0.81 on 2-object position with 32% blend failures, and adding Cutout trades this to 0.70 accuracy at 0.95 blend. [2504.10148] AST needs GPT-4V sketches that were manually refined. [2601.06338] shows the relation in T5 DiTs rides on the object token, so the constraint has to be geometric over object regions, which is exactly where instance identity is missing. Query trail: "spatial relationships text-to-image generation", "spatial relation diffusion", "layout-free spatial guidance diffusion", "MM-DiT attention control layout", "FLUX training-free layout control", "MMDiT spatial", "multi-instance generation training-free", "compositional text-to-image inference-time" (with recency pass).
- **Methodological deficiency:** Representation limitation. Relation losses are defined on a per-token aggregate (one centroid/variance, or one normalised distribution), which has no variables for individual instances. The constraints "exactly n instances of A" and "each instance of A satisfies R with respect to B" therefore cannot be written in the same objective. Count methods do have instance variables, but ISAC re-derives its clusters by K-means under stop-gradient at every step and NUMINA labels regions only by category, so the instances carry no identity linking them to the relation's subject or object. The two families cannot be composed without conflicts such as repel-apart versus pull-to-one-side. CoBind names the missing instance slots as its failure cause.
- **Why it matters:** The problem's concrete failure ("three red apples and a green cup to the left of a blue book" giving the wrong count, the book on the wrong side, or both) is exactly a counted relation. GenEval counting/position, T2I-CompBench++ numeracy/spatial and DPG-Bench all contain such prompts. Today MM-DiT users must either accept an LLM layout with hard masks and a coherence trade-off, or do N-fold search. Closing the gap tests whether instance-level structure inside MM-DiT joint attention can carry relational constraints without an external layout, and it addresses count and position failures in one mechanism rather than one failure type at a time.
- **Evidence route:** On prompts that combine counts and relations (a controlled "n A R m B" set, plus GenEval counting/position and T2I-CompBench++ numeracy/spatial), report exact-count accuracy jointly with per-instance relation satisfaction (fraction of detected A instances satisfying R with respect to B). Compare against a naive composition of a count method and a relation loss (ISAC plus a PoS or centroid term) and against Stitch with LLM boxes. Joint success should exceed what the naive composition achieves, with blend/HPSv3 parity and no external layout.


## Axis: attribute leakage and object omission through MM-DiT joint attention

### What has been done
- **Latent-gradient guidance on image-to-text attention maps, with text tokens as fixed keys.**
  - EnMMDiT [2411.18301] (SD3) builds one map per subject word by averaging the image-to-text and text-to-image quadrants, separately for CLIP and T5 tokens, over blocks 5–12. It steers z_t in early steps toward three goals: block 5–8 maps should match the detached block 9–12 maps, CLIP maps should match T5 maps, and subjects should overlap less. With three or more similar subjects it also needs an overlap detector at step 5, then restart and reject sampling. This costs 68.7–118.6 s per image against 20.4 s for SD3.
  - FOCUS [2510.02315] (SD3.5, FLUX) treats the same idea as stochastic optimal control. Its cost is a Jensen–Shannon subject-separation term on image-query maps of subject tokens. It leaves out FLUX's single-stream blocks and adds 90–138% latency.
  - Self-Cross [2411.18936] penalises overlap between one subject's aggregated image self-attention and the image-text maps of the other subjects. Adjective tokens are excluded.
  - ISAC [2505.20935] clusters image self-attention into instances, then repels and binds token pairs on the propagated maps. It needs an LLM parser and falls back to best-of-10 selection on FLUX.
  - Detail++ [2507.17853] runs one branch per sub-prompt and injects each attribute's change through masks taken from attention.
  - U-Net precedents: T-SAM [2411.15236] shows that map overlap follows key (text-embedding) similarity, and pushes map similarity toward the text encoder's self-attention. CoBind [2607.16307] adds stage windows and weights scaled by how well each constraint is already satisfied. It is U-Net only and fails on same-class instances.
- **Direct map editing.** Self-Coherence Guidance [2503.17675] multiplies an attribute token's image-to-text attention inside its concept's mask from the previous step. Its main backbone is PixArt-alpha; FLUX results are qualitative only. The text-query rows of joint attention are left untouched.
- **Text-side interventions with a fixed or global strength.**
  - OSI [2605.14270] trains probes on concept-token keys, reaching up to 91% accuracy in middle-layer heads. It then adds one fixed "omission" direction to those keys in the top-K heads for the first 15 of 30 steps.
  - Delta-K [2603.10210] first runs a preview pass, and a VLM lists the missing concepts. It then injects into the keys the difference between the prompt and a copy with those concepts replaced by [MASK]. The strength alpha_t is tuned so that missing concepts receive as much attention as present ones.
  - TACA [2506.07986] multiplies all visual-to-text logits by one gamma at early steps. It needs LoRA to remove the artifacts this causes.
  - Prompt Reinjection [2602.06886] adds shallow-layer text features to all deeper text states with one weight w.
  - ToRA [2510.03886] scales the principal singular values of all text tokens by one sigma before every block.
- **Joint-attention routing that needs a layout.** DreamRenderer [2503.12885] (FLUX) lets each instance's text tokens attend only to "bridge" copies of that instance's image tokens. It adds hard image-token masks in the middle layers. Instance regions come from user boxes or masks on a depth or canny map.
- **Diagnostics of the text stream.**
  - MM-DiT text tokens encode whether their concept is present only after they have absorbed image state (OSI probes [2605.14270]).
  - Text-token states drift with depth and collapse into a concentrated, less separable region (Prompt Reinjection [2602.06886]).
  - In FLUX, colour leakage ("a red apple and a blue cup") traces to weak text-stream red features in the attribution graph of the cup's blue feature, and the graph varies across seeds. Text-stream features account for 89.9% of attribution at step 0 [2606.15796].

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Layout-free, training-free leakage fixes for MM-DiTs (SD3/SD3.5/FLUX) work in one of two ways. Some steer the image latent with losses on the image-to-text quadrant. Others apply one prompt-wide transform (one w, sigma or gamma) to text tokens or text logits. Both treat each text token as a fixed carrier of its own phrase. In joint attention, however, a noun-phrase token such as "cup" is rewritten in every block by what it attends to among image tokens and other text tokens. No method that works without a layout controls, per phrase, which image regions and which other phrases a token may absorb. Leakage that enters through the text stream is therefore corrected only downstream, in the image latent.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2503.12885] DreamRenderer applies exactly this per-phrase routing: each instance's text tokens attend only to bridge copies of that instance's image tokens. Its gain grows with instance count (Instance Success Ratio gain from 3.5% at 2 instances to 6.2% at 6), and naive isolation of text and image tokens lowers quality. But its routing masks come from user boxes or masks on a depth/canny map, so it cannot run on a plain prompt. [2602.06886] Prompt Reinjection shows that text states drift and collapse with depth. It re-adds shallow features to every token with one shared weight that "can not reliably" be large, so it restores token identity uniformly and isolates no phrase. [2510.03886] ToRA scales every token's principal coordinates by the same sigma. All distances grow by one factor, so an attribute's closeness to its own noun versus another noun is unchanged. [2411.18301], [2510.02315], [2411.18936] and [2505.20935] all read image-query maps, keep text tokens fixed and update only z_t. EnMMDiT finds that CLIP and T5 tokens disagree on where each subject is, but corrects this only through latent gradients, and still restarts for 29–42% of 3–4-subject prompts. [2411.15236] shows in U-Nets that map overlap follows key similarity; in an MM-DiT that similarity changes with the image and is not controlled by these methods. Recent searches (since 2025-06) for "attention masking text tokens multimodal diffusion transformer attribute", "text token isolation attribute leakage", "noun phrase attention mask text-to-image", "attribute binding without layout diffusion transformer" and "semantic leakage joint attention" found no layout-free method that routes text tokens.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Credit-assignment flaw: the controlled variable is not where this leakage path is written. [2606.15796] traces "blue cup turns red" to spurious text-stream red features. Text-stream attribution dominates the early steps (89.9% at step 0), the same steps where latent guidance is applied. [2503.12885] shows that blocking cross-instance absorption by text tokens is what fixes text-side binding, while isolating all tokens harms quality. Latent losses on image-to-text maps reach the text state only indirectly, through the image tokens it attends to, and cannot express "this phrase's tokens must not absorb that region or that phrase". Global text transforms contain no phrase structure at all.
- **Why it matters:** For plain prompts with several attribute–noun pairs (the problem's green cup turning red), every layout-free method fixes leakage downstream, at 2–6x cost or with restarts, and degrades as phrases are added. A per-phrase control of the text stream would show how much MM-DiT leakage passes through text-token updates. It would also give a fix that needs no LLM layout and no latent backpropagation, and that can coexist with omission fixes that act on the same keys.
- **Evidence route:** For each noun phrase on SD3.5-M and FLUX, measure the attention-update mass its tokens take from other phrases' tokens and from other objects' regions. Also measure a probe or attribution signal for the wrong attribute in its state. A method closing the gap should lower both, and improve T2I-CompBench colour/texture and GenEval colour attribution without layout input. It should beat latent-only guidance (FOCUS, EnMMDiT) at matched runtime with preference-score parity. Applying the same control only on the image-query side should not reproduce the gain.

- **Gap:** MM-DiT omission fixes raise a concept's pull in joint attention with a strength set in advance. TACA uses one temperature for all visual-to-text logits, OSI one concept-agnostic key direction over a fixed window, and Prompt Reinjection one shared reinjection weight. Delta-K instead tunes its strength against a VLM-checked preview. None sets the per-token, per-step strength from the model's own read-out of whether that concept is already realised and where. The amplification that recovers a missing object therefore also adds extra instances or pulls attributes onto the wrong object. Separation-based leakage fixes have the opposite side effect.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2605.14270] OSI shows that the read-out exists: a linear probe on concept-token keys reaches 91% in middle-layer heads and tracks emergence step by step. But it uses the probe only offline to choose a direction, and adds the same alpha to every selected token for the first 15 steps. It reports over-generation, and intervening on object tokens alone lowers FLUX colour binding (0.7923 to 0.7678). [2506.07986] TACA uses one gamma for all tokens; raising it from 1.2 to 1.3 improves shape and spatial scores but drops colour from 0.839 to 0.787. The authors state that it cannot adapt the factor "based on the actual degree of text alignment", and the training-free version needs LoRA to remove artifacts. [2603.10210] Delta-K is the closest to closed loop: alpha_t is tuned online toward present concepts' attention. But it is triggered by a VLM verdict on an extra preview pass, uses a fixed key difference from that preview, and controls attention magnitude only, with no binding term. [2602.06886] uses one w that cannot be large. [2607.16307] scales its weights by how well each constraint is already satisfied, but only on U-Net cross-attention, and its map-based estimate merges same-class instances. On the separation side, [2510.02315] reports that an entropy regulariser "pushed mass away from the subject", and [2411.18301] needs restarts because its overlap loss is too weak for three or more subjects.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Missing mechanism: closed-loop, per-token feedback. These methods decouple the intervention from the token's internal state of presence and binding. They cannot stop pushing a concept once it is realised, and they cannot tell "missing" from "present but bound to the wrong region". Amplification (OSI, TACA) and separation (FOCUS, EnMMDiT) are therefore separate open-loop pushes whose side effects run against each other. Over-generation and colour drops follow from amplification, and mass pushed off the subject follows from separation.
- **Why it matters:** "Three red apples and a green cup" needs both repairs at once: recover an omitted cup without adding apples or spreading red. With open-loop amplification, fixing omission costs count and binding accuracy. That is why the problem's Challenge notes that current MM-DiT fixes target one failure type. Per-token control based on the token's state would let one mechanism arbitrate between amplification and isolation, and would show whether the model's own presence signal is reliable enough to steer by.
- **Evidence route:** On prompts that mix omission and binding constraints (GenEval two-object, colour-attribution and counting; T2I-CompBench colour), log each concept token's probe state and the applied intervention per step. A method closing the gap should show (a) intervention strength that falls once the probe reports presence, (b) omission recovery at least as large as OSI and TACA without lower colour-attribution or counting accuracy at matched strength, and (c) no preview pass or VLM call, with preference-score parity.
