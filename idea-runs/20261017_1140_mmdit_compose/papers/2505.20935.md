# ISAC: Training-Free Instance-to-Semantic Attention Control for Multi-Instance Generation

## What this paper does
ISAC (ECCV 2026) is a training-free latent-optimisation objective for multi-instance prompts. It averages self- and cross-attention over all layers and heads. Phase 1 gates the foreground with instance-aware semantic masks (SA x CA), K-means-clusters foreground self-attention rows plus coordinates into exactly N clusters (N parsed by an LLM), and minimises the maximum pixel-wise overlap between cluster masks. Phase 2 applies repel and bind losses to the instance-aware semantic maps. A linear schedule moves weight from instance to semantic loss. Results cover SD1.5, SD1.4, SDXL and SD3.5-M (latent optimisation) and FLUX, Qwen-Image and FLUX.2 (best-of-10 latent selection). It beats CountGen and Counting Guidance on IntraCompBench instance counting.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism forms instances and how is the count used?
At each step, SA_t and CA_t are averaged over all layers and heads (Eq 3). CA^ins = SA_t CA_t is binarised by its mean, and the union over class tokens gives the foreground M_fg (Eq 4-6). Foreground SA rows plus normalised (x,y) are K-means-clustered into exactly N = sum n_i groups, with assignments under stop-gradient. Masks M = SA[I,I] K (Eq 7) enter L_ins, the maximum over pairs of the maximum pixel-wise overlap (Eq 8-9), and the latent takes a gradient step [full text, Section 3.2, Alg. 1]. The count enters only as the number of clusters. The loss pushes the N clusters apart but contains no term that checks whether exactly N objects are forming.

### Goal: How sensitive is it to the specified count and what does it cost on SD3.5-M / FLUX?
Setting the internal count to N-1 or N+1 drops multi-class accuracy by 22/16 points on SD1.5 and 25/18 points on SD3.5-M. Too few clusters merge instances; too many fragment one object [full text, Appendix 0.C.4]. On SD3.5-M, ISAC_LO takes 140 s and 74.8 GB per image versus 40 s and 22.9 GB for the base model. Other gradient attention-control baselines on SD3.5-M also need 73.8–76.4 GB and 124–147 s (Table 2) [full text, Section 4.2]. For FLUX and Qwen-Image the authors switch to gradient-free best-of-10 selection with the ISAC score as verifier [full text, Section 4.3]. Even with latent optimisation, SD3.5-M multi-class accuracy for 4 and 5 classes is 40% and 20% (Table 2).

### Goal: Does it include spatial-relation constraints?
No. Phase 2 has only repel and bind pairs from the parser; no directional or relational term exists [full text, Section 3.3]. HRS-Bench spatial scores improve (SD3.5-M 0.264 to 0.350), attributed to better instance separation, not to relation control [full text, Section 4.2]. Ambiguous-count prompts are out of scope [full text, Section 3.1].
