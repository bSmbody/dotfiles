# Enhancing MMDiT-Based Text-to-Image Models for Similar Subject Generation

## What this paper does
Diagnoses subject neglect/mixing in SD3 for semantically similar subjects as three ambiguities: inter-block (blocks 5-8 confuse subjects that blocks 9-12 separate), text-encoder (CLIP and T5 token maps disagree on location) and semantic (similar subjects attend to the same place). It repairs the latent by test-time gradient steps in early denoising using a Block Alignment Loss, a Text Encoder Alignment Loss and an Overlap Loss on subject-word attention maps. An Overlap Online Detection at step 5 can trigger Back-to-Start resampling with a conflict-mask restriction loss, then reject sampling. Evaluated on SD3-Medium (and SD3.5) on a new similar-subject dataset.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use against attribute leakage or object omission, and which part of MM-DiT joint attention (image-to-text, text-to-image, text-to-text, image-to-image quadrant, or token states) does it read or modify?
It takes the two cross-modal quadrants of SD3's joint attention (image-to-text and text-to-image), averages them into a map A per subject word, separately for CLIP and T5 tokens, from blocks 5-12 [full text, Section 3.1]. Losses: L_ba aligns block 5-8 maps to detached block 9-12 maps (Eq 1); L_ta aligns CLIP and T5 maps (Eq 2); L_ol penalises products of normalised maps of different subjects (Eq 3). The latent is updated z_t' = z_t - alpha grad L for steps 1-14, with multiple iterations at steps 3-5 [full text, Section 3.3, Section 4]. Overlap detection thresholds maps at 0.2 and computes an overlap ratio per subject (Eq 5); if any ratio exceeds 0.2, sampling restarts from the same noise with a restriction loss on the most ambiguous subject's conflict region (Eq 6) [full text, Section 3.4]. Only subject (noun) tokens enter the losses; attributes are not modelled.

### Goal: Does it account for text tokens being rewritten by image tokens across MM-DiT blocks, or does it treat text tokens as fixed keys or apply a uniform text-side transform?
Text-token states are never modified; the text side is used only as a source of spatial maps and the only control variable is the image latent. The paper's own diagnosis is a text-state inconsistency: "incorrect semantic information has been injected into intermediate latent hidden representations of the 5th to 8th blocks", and CLIP vs T5 tokens "exhibit a clear disagreement on where cat and dog should be" [full text, Section 3.2]. Both are corrected indirectly through gradients on z_t. Reversing the block-alignment direction (early blocks as target) "exacerbates" the problem [full text, Section 4.2].

### Goal: What limitation, failure case, quality or runtime cost, or external dependency (layout, LLM/VLM, fine-tuning) does it report on this axis?
For three or more similar subjects the overlap loss "is implicit and does not provide more direct and effective guidance ... there are still semantic information of similar subjects injected into the same location", which motivates the restart [full text, Section 3.4]. Restart is triggered for 0.4% / 29.3% / 42.1% of 2/3/4-subject prompts; sampling time is 68.7 s (L_amb) and up to 118.6 s (full) vs 20.4 s for SD3 on an A6000 48 GB [full text, Section 9.2]. On SD3.5, success rate rises 52.0% to 65.6% without reject sampling [full text, Section 9.3]. No layout or LLM is needed (subject indices from the prompt template).
