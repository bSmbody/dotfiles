# DifFRACT: Diffusion Feature Reconstruction and Attribution for Circuit Tracing

## What this paper does
Trains timestep-conditioned transcoders that replace the MLP sublayers of FLUX.1[schnell] double-stream blocks, giving attribution graphs over interpretable features in the text and image streams. Findings: the text-stream share of attribution falls from 89.9% at step 0 to 5.4% at step 3, and cross-modal edges from 14.9% to 2.0%. It also covers circuit-guided steering, colour circuits, and diagnoses of colour leakage, counting and negation failures.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use against attribute leakage or object omission, and which part of MM-DiT joint attention (image-to-text, text-to-image, text-to-text, image-to-image quadrant, or token states) does it read or modify?
Not a generation method; a causal diagnostic. Colour leakage: for "a red apple and a blue cup", the attribution graph of the image-stream blue feature contains weak text-stream red features (text layer 3). Amplifying them turns the cup red on 3 of 5 seeds; on the other 2 seeds the spurious features are absent from the graph and steering colours the background instead. The cause of leakage "is localizable to specific features in the graph, but the graph itself differs across seeds" [full text, Appendix D.8].

### Goal: Does it account for text tokens being rewritten by image tokens across MM-DiT blocks, or does it treat text tokens as fixed keys or apply a uniform text-side transform?
It models the text stream explicitly. Early steps are "dominated by text-driven semantic reasoning with strong cross-modal interactions"; suppressing semantic text-stream supernodes affects the image only at early steps [full text, Section 3.2, Appendix D.1]. In a "cross-stream disconnect" the text stream represents the prompt correctly but fails to propagate to the image stream (negation, square wheels) [full text, Appendix D.10].

### Goal: What limitation, failure case, quality or runtime cost, or external dependency (layout, LLM/VLM, fine-tuning) does it report on this axis?
Restricted to the double-stream blocks of FLUX.1[schnell] (4 steps); single-stream blocks and other architectures are left out [full text, Appendix A]. Interventions are per-feature with hand-set alpha, found per prompt and seed.


### Goal: Is the colour-leakage evidence a trace of naturally occurring leakage, and does it show that the spurious text-stream features were absorbed from image tokens?
Neither. For "a red apple and a blue cup" the graph of the image-stream blue feature contains "a small number of" text-layer-3 red features "with attributions one to two orders of magnitude weaker than the dominant ones". The leakage is produced by amplifying them with alpha = +100: the cup turns red on 3 of 5 seeds, and on the other 2 seeds the background turns red instead. The baseline row of Fig 19 is the unsteered generation [full text, Appendix D.8, Fig 19]. The paper does not say at which token position the red features sit, or whether they come from text-text or image-to-text attention. The 89.9% text-stream share is at step 0 of 4-step FLUX.1[schnell], when image tokens are still mostly noise [full text, Section 3.2, Fig 4]. Late text-stream features fire mostly on control tokens such as </s>, which the authors read as the text stream "having largely handed its lexical content over to the image stream" [full text, Appendix, text-transcoder feature analysis].
