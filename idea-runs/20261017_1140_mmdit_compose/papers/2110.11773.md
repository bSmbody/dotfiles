# Sinkformers: Transformers with Doubly Stochastic Attention

## What this paper does
Replaces the row-wise softmax in Transformer attention with a few iterations of Sinkhorn's algorithm (alternating row and column normalisation of exp(QK^T)), giving doubly stochastic attention; the first Sinkhorn iteration is exactly the softmax, so Transformers are a special case. Motivated by the observation that trained softmax attention drifts toward doubly stochastic. Theory: Sinkformer layers are a discretised Wasserstein gradient flow and, in the mean-field limit, a heat diffusion. Experiments: ModelNet40, sentiment analysis, IWSLT'14 translation, ViT on cats-vs-dogs and MNIST, all trained with the new normalisation.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How do the column sums of attention in trained softmax Transformers behave, and what does that imply about adding a query-axis normalisation to a trained model?
On a ViT (MNIST), a fairseq Transformer (IWSLT'14) and a Point Cloud Transformer (ModelNet40), "the learning process makes the attention matrices more and more doubly stochastic"; "the majority of columns naturally sum closely to 1" [full text, Section 3, "Attention matrices during training", Fig 2]. So in trained models, also normalising over queries (columns) mostly moves attention toward a regime the model already approaches; the paper uses this to justify double stochasticity as "an informative prior" [full text, Abstract, Section 3]. The column normalisation (N_C) makes every key's total received attention equal, so queries share each key instead of all taking it [full text, Section 3, Eq 3; Fig 1: with softmax "one cluster is ignored", with Sinkhorn "all points are involved"].

### Goal: Is Sinkhorn normalisation a drop-in change, and at what cost?
It is implemented "simply by plugging Sinkhorn's algorithm into self-attention modules in existing architectures, without changing the overall structure of the network"; 3 to 5 iterations suffice, and training time is about equal (3m25s vs 3m20s per ViT epoch) [full text, Section 3, "Sinkformers", "Computational cost"; Section 6.4]. All reported accuracy gains are for models trained with Sinkhorn (e.g. ViT cats-vs-dogs 79.0% to 79.5%) [full text, Section 6.4]; the paper itself does not test swapping it into a frozen checkpoint.
