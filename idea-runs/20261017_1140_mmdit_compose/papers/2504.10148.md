# Hierarchical and Step-Layer-Wise Tuning of Attention Specialty for Multi-Instance Synthesis in Diffusion Transformers

## What this paper does
This paper analyses FLUX's joint attention by splitting the attention map into T2T, I2I, I2T and T2I blocks. It finds a step- and layer-wise hierarchy: instance tokens act in early layers (6–34), background in middle layers (20–24) and attributes in later layers (25–57), with the key integration in the first ~16 steps; token-exchange experiments confirm this. The proposed training-free Attention Specialty Tuning reweights each block toward sketch-derived masks, using T2T within sub-prompts, I2I within instance sketches and I2T from sketch regions to sub-prompt tokens, applied on the layer and step windows found. It is shown on FLUX and SD3.5 with sketches made by GPT-4V and then manually refined.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does the attention analysis of FLUX/SD3.5 reveal about when/where instance tokens act?
Joint attention is split into T2T, I2I, I2T and T2I blocks. T2I scores are low, suggesting weak influence [full text, Section 3.1, 3.2]. I2T shows the instance token ("cube") active in layers 6–34, the attribute ("red") in 20–57 and the background ("forest") in 20–24. I2I shows instance-internal structure in early steps, which suggests "interventions in the instance are most effective in early stages" [full text, Section 3.2]. Step-layer token exchange shows concepts can be swapped in specific layers and that elements "prominently emerge by step 6" [full text, Section 3.2]. Tuning therefore applies in the first 16 steps, with instance tuning on layers 6–34 [full text, Section 3.3].

### Goal: What external inputs does the training-free method require?
Instance sketch masks for every sub-prompt. All masks (M_T2T, M_I2I, M_I2T) are built from sketches S_k [full text, Section 3.3, Eq 2-5]. For T2I-CompBench the sketches come from GPT-4V and "all generated sketch images are manually refined" to keep layouts and relations correct [full text, Supplementary 5]. Count and relation correctness are therefore supplied by the external sketch rather than inferred by the method.

### Goal: Does it address counting/spatial evaluation reliably?
Only partly. The authors report that T2I-CompBench's B-VQA, UniDet and CLIP metrics often score its images 0 because of distribution mismatch, and rely on customised cases with user-study scores [full text, Supplementary 5, 6]. Repeated small instances are shown qualitatively [full text, Section 4.3]. No counting benchmark is reported.
