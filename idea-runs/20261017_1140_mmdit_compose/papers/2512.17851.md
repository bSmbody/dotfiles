# InfSplign: Inference-Time Spatial Alignment of Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
InfSplign is a training-free guidance method for two-object spatial prompts <A, R, B> on Stable Diffusion U-Nets (SD1.4, SD2.1, SDXL). At every denoising step it reads the cross-attention maps of tokens A and B from coarse and mid-level decoder layers. It computes each token's attention centroid and variance and adds three losses to the noise prediction: a margin loss on the centroid difference along the relation axis, a presence loss (sum of variances), and a balance loss (|var_A - var_B|). It reports gains over inference-time and fine-tuned baselines on VISOR and T2I-CompBench spatial.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to enforce spatial relations?
The prompt is parsed into a triplet <A, R, B> with R in {left, right, above, below} [full text, Section 3.2]. Decoder cross-attention maps are grouped into coarse, mid and fine levels; coarse and mid are used. Centroid c_A and variance sigma_A^2 are attention-weighted means over spatial coordinates (Eq 4-5). L_spatial = f(alpha (m - Delta)), where Delta is the signed centroid difference on the relation axis and m a margin (Eq 6-7). L_presence = sigma_A^2 + sigma_B^2, and L_balance = |sigma_A^2 - sigma_B^2| (Eq 8). eta times the latent gradient is added to the CFG noise prediction at every reverse step (Eq 3, Alg. 1) [full text, Section 3.2].

### Goal: What representational assumption does the relation loss make about each object (instances, counts)?
Each object is one token with one attention distribution summarised by a single centroid and variance [full text, Section 3.2, Eq 4-5]. If a noun has several instances, its map is still collapsed to one centroid, and the presence loss rewards a single tight blob, so the formulation cannot express "each of n instances of A is left of B". The paper evaluates only one-instance-per-object relation prompts (VISOR, T2I-CompBench spatial) [full text, Section 4; Appendix A].

### Goal: Which backbones; does it transfer to MM-DiT; stated limitations?
Experiments use SD v1.4, v2.1 and SDXL only. The coarse/mid/fine grouping is defined over U-Net decoder resolutions [full text, Section 1 (Fig. 1), Section 3.2]. The conclusion claims applicability "to any diffusion model", but no DiT/MM-DiT experiment is reported [full text, Section 5]. Stated limitation: when one object fails to appear (uncommon pairs), spatial correction fails, and "object accuracy becomes the bottleneck" [full text, Section 5].
