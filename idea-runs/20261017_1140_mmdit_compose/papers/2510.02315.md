# FOCUS: Optimal Control for Multi-Entity World Modeling in Text-to-Image Generation

## What this paper does
Casts flow-matching sampling as stochastic optimal control, giving a single-pass test-time controller (a latent-gradient term with step size eta(t)) and an Adjoint-Matching LoRA fine-tuning route. FOCUS is a Jensen-Shannon attention-binding cost: low dispersion of each subject's attention maps across layers and encoders, high divergence between subjects. Evaluated on SD3.5 and FLUX.1-dev on a 150-prompt corpus with 2-4 subjects (half similar), with a human study.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use against attribute leakage or object omission, and which part of MM-DiT joint attention (image-to-text, text-to-image, text-to-text, image-to-image quadrant, or token states) does it read or modify?
Controlled velocity v* = v_base - eta(t) grad_X f(X_t, t) (Eq 15) [full text, Section 3.2]. f_FOCUS averages within-subject normalised JSD and (1 - JSD of subject means) over image-query-to-subject-token maps [full text, Section 3.4]. Maps are averaged over "all blocks that process text and image tokens separately"; "blocks that jointly process text and image tokens are excluded" (FLUX single-stream blocks) [full text, Appendix A]. Subject token indices are annotated for CLIP and T5.

### Goal: Does it account for text tokens being rewritten by image tokens across MM-DiT blocks, or does it treat text tokens as fixed keys or apply a uniform text-side transform?
Text tokens are treated as fixed identity carriers: only the image latent is steered, and only subject tokens enter the cost; attribute tokens are not in the objective [full text, Section 3.4, Section 5].

### Goal: What limitation, failure case, quality or runtime cost, or external dependency (layout, LLM/VLM, fine-tuning) does it report on this axis?
Test-time control adds latency (+138% SD3, +90% FLUX) [full text, Section 5]. The local single-pass approximation is weaker than fine-tuning [full text, Section 5.2]. An entropy regulariser "pushed mass away from the subject rather than stabilizing it" [full text, Appendix A]. Self-Cross Guidance showed "pronounced segmentation artifacts" and failed under fine-tuning [full text, Section 5.3].
