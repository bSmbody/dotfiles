# FreeFuse: Multi-Subject LoRA Fusion via Adaptive Token-Level Routing at Test Time

## What this paper does
Training-free fusion of several subject LoRAs in FLUX.1-dev. A first pass with LoRAs disabled extracts one mask per subject from block 18 at step index 4 of 28 (FreeFuseAttn: cross-attention anchors minus competing concepts' maps, propagated by image-feature similarity, plus a background prompt, morphology and contention resolution). A second pass, restarted from the same noise, gates each LoRA's residual to its subject's mask and adds a mask-derived attention bias.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How does it obtain subject regions without layout, and which attention rows or paths does it modify?
Subject maps come from image-query attention over each concept's text tokens, normalised spatially and averaged over the concept's tokens; a discriminative score subtracts the other concepts' maps (S_hat_c = M S_c - sum_j S_j), the top 10% of tokens become anchors, and the mask is spread by latent similarity [full text, Section 3.2, Eq 4-7]. Ambiguous regions go through "territorial voting and gravity aggregation" [full text, Section 3.3; Appendix B.2]. In the second pass a bias matrix encourages "image tokens to attend exclusively to their corresponding subject prompts", that is, the image-query rows; the Router gates LoRA residuals, including the subject text tokens' residuals in the add_q/k/v projections [full text, Section 3.3, "Implementation scope"].

### Goal: Does it control which image regions text tokens absorb in joint attention?
No. The base model's joint attention is otherwise untouched ("routing gates only the additive LoRA residuals; the frozen base-model attention remains active") [full text, Section 3.3]. Masks are fixed after the first pass, and the two-pass design adds overhead, as the authors acknowledge [full text, Section 5].
