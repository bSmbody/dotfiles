# Demystifying Numerosity in Diffusion Models — Limitations and Remedies

## What this paper does
The paper tests whether scaling fixes counting, using synthetic count-exact datasets (GrayCount250, NaturalCount6) and FLUX.1-dev fine-tunes from 2B to 12B with 1K to 500K samples. Scaling does not help. A 300K-image study (100 noises x 3,000 counting prompts) shows each initial noise has a preferred count and object layout that overrides the text: "noise determines layout, text activates locations." The remedy is count-aware noise priors that inject box-located structure (uniform scaling, fixed patch, Gaussian bumps) into the initial noise, used with fine-tuning. This raises GrayCount250 accuracy from 20.0% to 85.3% and NaturalCount6 from 74.8% to 86.3%.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What determines object count in FLUX-type rectified-flow models according to this paper?
The initial noise. With FLUX.1-dev fine-tuned on GrayCount250, different noise vectors bias the count toward specific values regardless of the requested count (Fig. 8). For a fixed noise, objects appear at nearly identical positions across prompts, and images of different counts from the same noise share a layout (Fig. 9). Hence "the noise strongly determines the spatial layout, while the text prompt primarily influences which objects appear at those pre-determined locations", and training amplifies this [full text, Section 5.1]. The appendix finds the same noise has the same preferred count in two models fine-tuned on 5K and 100K samples (e.g., noise 57 prefers 23 objects) [full text, Appendix F.2].

### Goal: Do inference-time knobs (CFG, more samples) fix the count?
No. CFG scales of 2.0–7.0 change exact accuracy only within 18.4–20.0% (Table 18). Larger sampling batches with the Gaussian prior slightly reduce accuracy (39.2% to 36.6–37.0%, Table 19). Training-time early-timestep sampling (logit mean -1.5) gives +5.0%, and shared-noise batching +0.4% [full text, Appendix K.1, K.2]. Instance-enumerating prompts give only modest gains [full text, Section 5.3].

### Goal: Is the proposed remedy training-free, and what inputs does it require?
Not in the reported form. The priors modify noise inside target bounding boxes, so they need a layout (boxes from the dataset's placement algorithm), and every reported result uses FLUX.1-dev fine-tuned with that prior; the baseline is "the model trained without any noise prior" [full text, Section 5.2, 5.3; Appendix C, I]. Stronger noise edits raise accuracy but hurt aesthetics; the Gaussian prior is the mildest [full text, Appendix I].
