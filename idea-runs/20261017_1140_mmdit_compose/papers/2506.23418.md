# Why Settle for Mid: A Probabilistic Viewpoint to Spatial Relationship Alignment in Text-to-image Models

## What this paper does
The paper models "A is right of B" as the Probability of Superiority (PoS): the probability that a random point of A's spatial distribution projected on a direction exceeds a random point of B's. From this it builds PSE, an evaluation metric over Grounded-SAM masks plus depth, and PSG, a generation method. PSG has two forms: gradient-based "generative semantic nursing" that maximises PoS between two tokens' normalised cross-attention maps, and search-based best-of-N selection by PSE score. Gradient PSG is shown on SD1.4/SDXL and PixArt-alpha; search PSG on SD2.1, SDXL, SD3, FLUX and SANA1.5.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does gradient-based PSG represent each object and relation?
Each object is one token's normalised cross-attention map, treated as a probability distribution over locations. The relation r between tokens i and j is the projected PoS along v_r (Eq 3-4), and the latent is updated by z_t <- z_t - alpha_t grad(-PoS^2) (Eq 6). Compound relations (top-left) apply the leftward and topward losses in sequence [full text, Section 3, 3.2]. PoS compares whole distributions, which is stricter than centroid order, but the object remains one token-level distribution with no notion of separate instances or count [full text, Section 3, 3.2].

### Goal: How is PSG applied to MM-DiT backbones (FLUX, SD3), and at what cost?
Only as search: best-of-N over initial noises scored by PSE (Grounded-SAM masks, depth for 3D). With N=16, FLUX PSE rises from 45.52 to 93.59 and SD3 from 47.41 to 88.13, increasing monotonically with N (Table 14) [full text, Section 3.2; Appendix B.2]. Gradient PSG beyond U-Nets is shown only on PixArt-alpha's averaged cross-attention maps (PSE 28.50 to 57.87, 20 guided steps) [full text, Appendix B.2]; no gradient-based variant on joint-attention MM-DiT is reported. So the MM-DiT gains cost N full generations plus detector and segmenter calls per image.

### Goal: Stated limitations?
Training data underrepresent spatial relations, so training-free methods "may not offer a comprehensive solution ... particularly for complex text prompts involving numerous entities". The CLIP text encoder is a suspected bottleneck, and search needs extra segmentation/depth models and memory [full text, Appendix B.8].
