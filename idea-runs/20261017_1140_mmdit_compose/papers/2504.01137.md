# Follow the Flow: On Information Flow Across Textual Tokens in Text-to-Image Models

## What this paper does
Uses activation patching on the text-encoder outputs of FLUX-schnell, FLUX-dev, SDXL-Turbo and SANA to ask (1) which tokens of a lexical item carry its meaning and (2) whether information flows between tokens of different items. Finds that one or two tokens usually carry an item, and that items mostly stay isolated, but that cross-item flow sometimes causes misinterpretation (e.g. "pool" becoming a pool table next to "table"). Mitigation: patch the wrongly resolved item with its uncontextualised encoding (item plus local modifiers).

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: Does the T5 text-encoder output already mix attribute information across noun phrases (cross-item flow), and how often?
Rarely. Generating from an item's contextualised representation alone, "89% of the tokens do not lead to images containing other items"; in the abstract's example, "in the prompt 'a green dog', the token 'dog' encodes no visual information about 'green'" [full text, Abstract, Section 5, Table 3]. Harmful cross-item flow shows up mainly for polysemous words resolved by a wrong context (bats near a baseball stadium); on a curated set of 110 such prompts, 93% of errors appear already at the contextualised item [full text, Section 5.1]. So, for T5-conditioned FLUX, the encoder output mostly keeps phrases apart, and any later mixing of explicit attributes between phrases has to happen downstream, in the denoiser.

### Goal: What mitigation does it propose, and at what stage (encoder vs denoiser)?
Encoder stage only: wrongly resolved items are replaced by their "cleaned" encoding (item plus local modifiers); on FLUX-dev, misinterpretation falls from 79% to 20% (RAG-Diffusion with boxes: 39%) [full text, Section 5.2, Table 4]. Joint-attention updates inside the MM-DiT are not analysed or modified.
