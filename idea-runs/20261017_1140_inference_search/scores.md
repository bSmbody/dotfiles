### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [1509.06461] Source correctly stated: Theorem 1 gives positive max-bias even for unbiased estimates, the bias grows with the number of options under independent errors, and the partially decorrelated target network keeps most of the gain. But in Double DQN both estimators target the same Q(s,a), and selection still uses the first estimator (Eq 4); the second one only fixes the reported value, and the policy gain comes through bootstrapped targets. In C1 the second estimator is the verifier on *different images*, so it estimates the generator-smoothed reward E[r(x')|x], not x's quality, and C1 also uses it to re-rank finalists. The Double-Q guarantee therefore does not carry over; the benefit has to come from r' being a better selector than r, which is a separate empirical claim.
  - The drop r(x) − r'(x) mixes two things the mapping treats as one: fine-scale verifier error, and fine-scale *genuine* quality that x keeps and its siblings regress away from (correct hands, legible text, clean faces, sharp texture). Best-of-N selection inflates both. So the discount penalises lucky, genuinely better fine detail, which is the same failure C1 holds against Caution [2604.04648] (penalising rare candidates that really are better). The stated key assumption, that a sibling "keeps the candidate's true quality", holds only if true quality has no fine-scale variance across candidates.
  - [2603.21175] The scale-separation evidence is weaker than claimed. RSA-FT's −0.80 correlation is a dataset-level average per fine-tuning checkpoint, not a per-sample diagnostic. Its sharpness is measured with off-manifold, gradient-crafted pixel perturbations against a generator that is being trained on reward gradients. [2205.07460] DiffPure likewise removes *adversarially crafted* local perturbations. [2511.20889] Null-TTA attributes hacking to "non-semantic noise patterns" only for gradient-based noise optimisation (DNO), and gives no scale analysis. None of these shows that the error exploited by gradient-free selection among on-manifold samples lives at a finer scale than true quality.
  - [2501.09732] In the regime the Challenge names (best-of-N and particle search), the documented hacking is content-level. Aesthetic search lowers CLIPScore and vice versa (prompt drift), and DINO/CLIP search collapses recall/FID (loss of diversity). A partial re-draw keeps layout, objects and composition by design, so these errors survive in r'. The same applies to the "per-prompt stopping point": r' of new winners keeps rising while content-level bias is exploited, so the stopping rule detects only fine-scale exploitation and is not a gold-free stand-in for HedgeTune's threshold [2506.19248].
  - [2608.21748] With a VLM selector on FLUX at N=16, the within-prompt selection term added no held-out risk (−0.009, CI −0.035 to 0.017), and best-of-N selection lowered it further. The winner's-curse bias C1 removes may be small for VLM judges at moderate N, and the errors a VLM judge makes (counts, relations) are content-level, as C1 concedes. So the claimed applicability to black-box VLM judges is mostly nominal.
  - Novelty is real but narrower than framed. Searches ("re-noising test-time scaling", "SDEdit reward", "denoised smoothing reward model", "reward hacking inference-time alignment diffusion" since 2025-06, "decoupled selection evaluation", "test-time augmentation reward model", "noisy verifier selection inference scaling") found no search method that scores candidates by the verifier on generator re-draws. Re-noising is used only to propose candidates ([2501.09732], [2610.15281]) or to edit them (GEARS, 2608.29322, abstract only). But a per-sample trust signal against diffusion reward hacking already exists: GARDO [2512.24138] flags samples whose proxy win rate exceeds that of auxiliary reward models and penalises only the top ~10%, at negligible cost (fine-tuning only). A GARDO-style cross-verifier discount ported to best-of-N is a zero-NFE competitor that C1 must beat, so the gap.md claim of "no per-candidate signal" holds only for inference-time search.
  - [2608.01207] There is a cross-domain warning about the evaluation design. In VLM test-time scaling, a perturbation-consistency selector lost its apparent gain once the same compute was spent on matched draws of the original input, and the stability gap had near-zero instance-level correlation with gain. C1 already charges re-draw cost to the budget. Note, though, that at the problem's 4× budget, re-drawing even 2–3 finalists × 3 siblings at t*≈0.3 uses roughly half of the extra compute, so the method can only show gains at larger N.
  - Feasibility and contribution type pass. It is training-free, uses the assets already deployed (Wan2.1-1.3B, HPSv3, VideoAlign, a ≤3B image flow model), and image-side sweeps fit in 40 GPU-days if candidate pools are cached. It is a methodological contribution (a new selection/evaluation rule for search).
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The re-drawn score estimates a generator-smoothed reward rather than the quality of the candidate that is actually output, so its "drop" penalises genuine fine-scale quality as much as verifier error. The only evidence that exploited error is fine-scale comes from gradient/adversarial regimes, while the best-of-N and particle hacking the Challenge targets is documented at content level, which re-draws keep [2501.09732]. A revision must either redefine the second estimator so that its target matches the output (e.g., what is selected and what is released are the same re-drawn family), or narrow the claim to the gradient-based search regime and name a pilot that measures how much of the selection-inflated score is fine-scale.


### C2 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [2106.05706] The source mechanism is stated correctly. Each sensor reading gets its own Bernoulli trust Ω^i, set by that sensor's squared residual against q(x_k) in units of its known nominal noise R^ii (Eq 16, 20), with a neutral θ=0.5, and the filter tolerates sporadic NLoS bias. What the candidate leaves out is the redundancy the rejection depends on. q(x_k) is pinned down by a dynamics prediction with small process noise and by *other* sensors: m sensors in simulation, and 11 UWB anchors with up to 4 reporting per step. Outliers are also independent of the state. C2 has one other sensor plus a prior built from the prompt's ordinary candidates, and that prior's spread is the full spread of candidate quality, which is exactly what the search must discriminate. On a candidate in the selected tail, the prior always sits nearer the lower reading. So whenever the two verifiers disagree on a top candidate, the posterior flags the *higher* one, whichever it is. In practice the "symmetric" rule becomes: average the two calibrated scores when they are close, and take roughly the lower one when they are far apart. That is WCO (min = mean − |Δ|/2 [2310.02743]) with a dead zone, not an attribution of which verifier is wrong on this candidate.
  - [1808.09375] Kotlarski's identity assumes Y1 = X + U1 and Y2 = X + U2, with unit loadings and X, U1, U2 mutually independent (Section 2, Eq 1, Assumption 1); the paper does not cover measurements on different, unknown scales. HPSv3 and PickScore/ImageReward are on different scales, so the relative loading has to be fixed by assumption. If both are standardised with equal loading, the two fitted nominal noise scales are equal by construction (σ1² = σ2² = 1 − ρ). "Each verifier's normal error fitted from joint scores" then reduces to a single correlation coefficient. The per-verifier noise split, which decides whose reading counts as an outlier, is not identified by the second moments of two verifiers. It needs a third measurement (FUSE's moment estimator works on triplets for exactly this reason [2604.18547], Theorem 2.3, Eq 8-9) or higher-moment and parametric assumptions that the data cannot check.
  - [2312.09244] In LLM best-of-n reranking with 5-member reward ensembles, "differences between aggregators are small, with mean usually performing at, or near, the top", and min was "inferior to the alternatives". The gains came from member diversity (pretraining seeds), not from the aggregation rule. C2's operative contribution is the aggregation rule over a fixed pair, so this is direct prior evidence that the rule may add little beyond "add a second, different verifier". The same paper shows hacks that every member shares (length, list format, copying) surviving ensembling, because members "erroneously extrapolate in the same manner".
  - [2601.03468] The key assumption is only partly supported for verifiers of the same target. Under HPS RL, Aesthetic rises along with HPS (a shared over-saturation exploit), while PickScore shows no consistent upward trend, and structural artifacts are shared by every reward. HPSv3+PickScore may therefore decorrelate exploits, but HPSv3+Aesthetic would not. The rest of the candidate's independence evidence pairs verifiers of *different* constructs: Aesthetic vs CLIPScore [2501.09732], HPS vs GDino/ORM [2601.03468], and GARDO's OCR/GenEval proxy vs Aesthetic/ImageReward auxiliaries [2512.24138]. That is exactly where the single-latent model fails and construct differences would be flagged as faults. The evidence for independent exploits and the requirement of a shared latent pull in opposite directions, and the candidate cites the former in support of the latter.
  - [2604.18547] Novelty: label-free, query-specific estimation of verifier reliability for best-of-N already exists. FUSE fits a per-query ensemble from spectral moments over triplets (LLM, binary correctness, no fault indicator per response). Add WCO/UWO [2310.02743], mean/median/min/mean−std [2312.09244], the median panel [2606.30931] and Weaver [2506.18203], and the claim of "no gold scorer, no global knob" is not new by itself. The real delta is (a) continuous per-candidate fault indicators, and (b) their use inside diffusion/flow search. My searches found no diffusion/flow search that does (a): "robust aggregation multiple reward models best-of-N", "worst-case reward ensemble text-to-image", "multi-reward test-time scaling diffusion", "reward model disagreement image generation selection", "conservative reward ensemble diffusion inference-time", "verifier ensemble test-time scaling", "reward ensemble overoptimization", "Bayesian aggregation reward models", plus recency queries since 2025-06/2025-10: "verifier ensemble image generation", "multiple verifiers image generation", "reward ensemble diffusion", "label-free verifier weighting", "best-of-N text-to-image multiple reward models", "reward hacking best-of-N image generation ensemble" and "unsupervised ensemble reward models image". The domain gap is real, but (a) is exactly the part that two verifiers cannot identify (bullets 1-2).
  - [2608.21748] At the problem's 4× budget, the winner's-curse bias that a posterior removes is likely small: with a VLM selector at N=16, selection within a prompt added no held-out risk. With N=4 and two verifiers, the fault branch will rarely fire, so at the Challenge's budget C2 will mostly act as the mean of two verifiers. Its distinctive gains must be shown at larger N or under SMC/FK tilting. Feeding fused values into intermediate FK/DAS resampling also breaks the independence assumption, because both verifiers score the same blurry x̂0 and so share its error.
  - The following checks pass. The contribution is methodological: a new selection/aggregation rule for search. It is feasible: zero generator NFE, one extra pass of a ≤1B scorer, the deployed HPSv3 and VideoAlign, and cached pools, well inside 40 GPU-days. Held-out evaluation that excludes both search verifiers is doable (e.g. MPS/UnifiedReward/VQAScore plus GenEval). The fix to C1 is real: the object scored is now the object released.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: With only two verifiers and a prior built from the prompt's ordinary candidates, nothing in the mapping can tell which verifier is wrong on a tail candidate: the pool prior always sides with the lower reading, and the two noise scales are not separately identified (Kotlarski assumes unit loadings; FUSE needs triplets). The "per-candidate fault rejection" therefore collapses to a min-of-two (WCO-style) aggregator with a dead zone, and LLM best-of-n evidence finds such aggregators differ little from the mean [2312.09244]. A revision must give the role of "prediction + other sensors" to a genuine reference, such as three or more verifiers so that faults and per-verifier noise are identified from triplet moments, and must show the gain over mean, min, rank-average and UWO of the *same* verifiers.
