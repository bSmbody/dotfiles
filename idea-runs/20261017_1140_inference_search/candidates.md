### C1 — Re-draw before you trust: a double estimator for verifier-guided diffusion search

- **Intuition**: Verifier-guided search over-trusts its winners for the same reason Q-learning over-estimates a max: one noisy score both picks a candidate and vouches for it. Decouple the two by re-scoring each finalist on a few copies that the generator re-draws from it (re-noise part way, denoise again), and let the share of its score that survives the re-draw set how much the search trusts that candidate.
- **Gap it attacks**: Axis "verifier reward hacking under inference-time search", Gap 1. Today's search limits verifier exploitation with one trust level fixed before search: the tilt temperature α/λ [2501.05803][2501.06848], the image-space KL weight β [2510.01549], or the selection strength n/θ/M [2506.19248][2603.05739]. That level gives the same discount to a candidate whose high score comes from real quality and to one whose score comes from verifier error, and it can only be tuned offline against a gold or held-out scorer. The concrete failure is the one in problem.md: the search's own score keeps rising with compute while held-out preference and GenEval stay flat. A per-candidate signal that needs no gold scorer would let search keep full selection pressure where the verifier is reliable, withdraw it exactly where the verifier is being exploited, and find each prompt's stopping point at inference time instead of by a per-verifier grid search.
- **Source inspiration**: Double Q-learning / Double DQN [1509.06461], from reinforcement learning. When the same noisy estimates are used to choose the max and to report its value, the chosen item tends to be the one whose error happened to be largest. The resulting bias is provably positive even when the estimates are unbiased on average, it grows with the number of options, and in Atari it degrades the policy, not just the value estimate. Evaluating the selected action with a second estimator, whose errors are not the ones that drove the selection, removes most of the bias and gives better policies. A second estimator that is only partly decorrelated (the target network) already captures most of the gain. Supporting source: DiffPure [2205.07460], from adversarial robustness. Re-noising an image to a small diffusion time and denoising it with a pretrained diffusion model washes out small, local structure that fools a classifier while keeping global semantics. It works for any frozen classifier and for unseen attacks, so the generative model itself can supply a fresh measurement that keeps the content and re-draws the fragile detail. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts.
- **How it maps to this problem**: The verifier's score on a finished candidate plays the role of the online Q-value: search uses it to select, resample or prune. The second estimator is the same frozen verifier applied to a few re-drawn siblings of that candidate. The generator produces each sibling by partially re-noising the candidate and running its own stochastic sampler back down, as DiffPure does. The siblings share the candidate's layout, objects and composition, on which true quality mostly depends, but get fresh fine detail, where verifier exploits mostly live. Search may still propose candidates using the original score, but a finalist's standing comes from its re-drawn score, so no candidate is valued by the same draw that picked it. The difference between the two scores is a per-candidate trust signal. A small drop means the verifier's judgement rests on content, and the candidate keeps its full weight. A large drop means its lead was verifier error, and it is discounted. Across a prompt's finalists, the same signal shows when more candidates, more particles or a stronger tilt stop paying off: the re-drawn scores of new winners stop rising while their original scores still climb. This gives a per-prompt, gold-free stand-in for the hacking threshold that HedgeTune can only find with gold calibration [2506.19248]. Because it needs only the verifier's score on images or videos, it applies to black-box VLM judges, HPSv3 and VideoAlign, and to best-of-N, FK/SMC terminal weights and noise optimisation alike.
- **Why it could work / feasibility**: Double Q-learning needs only that the evaluating estimator's errors be largely uncorrelated with the errors that drove the selection, and partial decorrelation already removes most of the bias [1509.06461]. Here that holds to the extent that the verifier error which search exploits lives at a finer scale than true quality. RSA-FT supports this for image reward models: hacking happens where the reward is locally sharp, and reward sharpness correlates with held-out PickScore at Pearson −0.80 over fine-tuning [2603.21175]. MIRA likewise attributes hacking to the verifier's unreliability off the base distribution [2510.01549]. DiffPure shows that a small re-noise strength removes such local structure while preserving semantics, without touching the frozen scorer [2205.07460]. The method is training-free. It costs partial denoising from an intermediate step plus a few verifier calls for each of a handful of finalists, using the generator and verifier already in the pipeline (Wan2.1-1.3B or a ≤3B image flow model), and this cost is charged against the matched budget. Key assumption: at some re-noise strength, a re-drawn sibling keeps the candidate's true quality (prompt adherence, composition) while the selection-inflated part of the verifier's error is largely redrawn.
- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: RSA-FT [2603.21175] also scores how much reward survives a perturbation. However, it uses an off-manifold, gradient-based pixel perturbation with one global radius, applied uniformly as a fine-tuning objective, and it lists per-sample sharpness weighting as future work. Here, on-manifold generator re-draws serve as a second, independent measurement inside inference-time search, and the per-candidate drop sets per-candidate trust and a per-prompt stopping point. Unlike Caution [2604.04648], the signal measures how fragile the score is, not how atypical the candidate is, so rare candidates that are genuinely better are not penalised. Unlike Search over Paths [2501.09732] and CS-VGB [2610.15281], which re-noise to propose new candidates that are then selected on their own scores, the re-draw here is used only to evaluate. Recency queries since 2025-09 ("re-noising denoising reward consistency", "verifier overestimation best-of-N selection bias", "decoupling selection and evaluation best-of-N", "reward sharpness diffusion", "inference-time reward hacking image generation") returned no search method of this kind.
- **Main risk**: The verifier errors that matter most under best-of-N may be content-level, such as HPS's style preference or a VLM judge accepting a wrong object count. Such errors survive the re-draw, so the second estimate shares them. Moreover, the re-noise strength that separates detail from content may vary with the verifier and the prompt (DiffPure's best t* varies by threat [2205.07460]), which would bring back a tuned global knob.
