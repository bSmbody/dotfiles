### C1 — Re-draw before you trust: a double estimator for verifier-guided diffusion search

- **Intuition**: Verifier-guided search over-trusts its winners for the same reason Q-learning over-estimates a max: one noisy score both picks a candidate and vouches for it. Decouple the two by re-scoring each finalist on a few copies that the generator re-draws from it (re-noise part way, denoise again), and let the share of its score that survives the re-draw set how much the search trusts that candidate.
- **Gap it attacks**: Axis "verifier reward hacking under inference-time search", Gap 1. Today's search limits verifier exploitation with one trust level fixed before search: the tilt temperature α/λ [2501.05803][2501.06848], the image-space KL weight β [2510.01549], or the selection strength n/θ/M [2506.19248][2603.05739]. That level gives the same discount to a candidate whose high score comes from real quality and to one whose score comes from verifier error, and it can only be tuned offline against a gold or held-out scorer. The concrete failure is the one in problem.md: the search's own score keeps rising with compute while held-out preference and GenEval stay flat. A per-candidate signal that needs no gold scorer would let search keep full selection pressure where the verifier is reliable, withdraw it exactly where the verifier is being exploited, and find each prompt's stopping point at inference time instead of by a per-verifier grid search.
- **Source inspiration**: Double Q-learning / Double DQN [1509.06461], from reinforcement learning. When the same noisy estimates are used to choose the max and to report its value, the chosen item tends to be the one whose error happened to be largest. The resulting bias is provably positive even when the estimates are unbiased on average, it grows with the number of options, and in Atari it degrades the policy, not just the value estimate. Evaluating the selected action with a second estimator, whose errors are not the ones that drove the selection, removes most of the bias and gives better policies. A second estimator that is only partly decorrelated (the target network) already captures most of the gain. Supporting source: DiffPure [2205.07460], from adversarial robustness. Re-noising an image to a small diffusion time and denoising it with a pretrained diffusion model washes out small, local structure that fools a classifier while keeping global semantics. It works for any frozen classifier and for unseen attacks, so the generative model itself can supply a fresh measurement that keeps the content and re-draws the fragile detail. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts.
- **How it maps to this problem**: The verifier's score on a finished candidate plays the role of the online Q-value: search uses it to select, resample or prune. The second estimator is the same frozen verifier applied to a few re-drawn siblings of that candidate. The generator produces each sibling by partially re-noising the candidate and running its own stochastic sampler back down, as DiffPure does. The siblings share the candidate's layout, objects and composition, on which true quality mostly depends, but get fresh fine detail, where verifier exploits mostly live. Search may still propose candidates using the original score, but a finalist's standing comes from its re-drawn score, so no candidate is valued by the same draw that picked it. The difference between the two scores is a per-candidate trust signal. A small drop means the verifier's judgement rests on content, and the candidate keeps its full weight. A large drop means its lead was verifier error, and it is discounted. Across a prompt's finalists, the same signal shows when more candidates, more particles or a stronger tilt stop paying off: the re-drawn scores of new winners stop rising while their original scores still climb. This gives a per-prompt, gold-free stand-in for the hacking threshold that HedgeTune can only find with gold calibration [2506.19248]. Because it needs only the verifier's score on images or videos, it applies to black-box VLM judges, HPSv3 and VideoAlign, and to best-of-N, FK/SMC terminal weights and noise optimisation alike.
- **Why it could work / feasibility**: Double Q-learning needs only that the evaluating estimator's errors be largely uncorrelated with the errors that drove the selection, and partial decorrelation already removes most of the bias [1509.06461]. Here that holds to the extent that the verifier error which search exploits lives at a finer scale than true quality. RSA-FT supports this for image reward models: hacking happens where the reward is locally sharp, and reward sharpness correlates with held-out PickScore at Pearson −0.80 over fine-tuning [2603.21175]. MIRA likewise attributes hacking to the verifier's unreliability off the base distribution [2510.01549]. DiffPure shows that a small re-noise strength removes such local structure while preserving semantics, without touching the frozen scorer [2205.07460]. The method is training-free. It costs partial denoising from an intermediate step plus a few verifier calls for each of a handful of finalists, using the generator and verifier already in the pipeline (Wan2.1-1.3B or a ≤3B image flow model), and this cost is charged against the matched budget. Key assumption: at some re-noise strength, a re-drawn sibling keeps the candidate's true quality (prompt adherence, composition) while the selection-inflated part of the verifier's error is largely redrawn.
- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: RSA-FT [2603.21175] also scores how much reward survives a perturbation. However, it uses an off-manifold, gradient-based pixel perturbation with one global radius, applied uniformly as a fine-tuning objective, and it lists per-sample sharpness weighting as future work. Here, on-manifold generator re-draws serve as a second, independent measurement inside inference-time search, and the per-candidate drop sets per-candidate trust and a per-prompt stopping point. Unlike Caution [2604.04648], the signal measures how fragile the score is, not how atypical the candidate is, so rare candidates that are genuinely better are not penalised. Unlike Search over Paths [2501.09732] and CS-VGB [2610.15281], which re-noise to propose new candidates that are then selected on their own scores, the re-draw here is used only to evaluate. Recency queries since 2025-09 ("re-noising denoising reward consistency", "verifier overestimation best-of-N selection bias", "decoupling selection and evaluation best-of-N", "reward sharpness diffusion", "inference-time reward hacking image generation") returned no search method of this kind.
- **Main risk**: The verifier errors that matter most under best-of-N may be content-level, such as HPS's style preference or a VLM judge accepting a wrong object count. Such errors survive the re-draw, so the second estimate shares them. Moreover, the re-noise strength that separates detail from content may vary with the verifier and the prompt (DiffPure's best t* varies by threat [2205.07460]), which would bring back a tuned global knob.


### C2 — Two sensors, one quality: per-candidate fault rejection for verifier-guided diffusion search

- **Intuition**: Score each candidate with two independent verifiers of the same target and treat them like two sensors reading one hidden quality, the way a robust tracker fuses sensors that occasionally glitch. If only one verifier sees a candidate's lead, and that lead is implausible given the other verifier and the prompt's ordinary candidates, the lead is read as that verifier's fault and discounted. A lead both verifiers see keeps its full weight. Each verifier's normal error is fitted from the two verifiers' joint scores on unlabelled candidates, so the per-candidate trust needs no gold scorer and no global trust knob.
- **Gap it attacks**: Axis "verifier reward hacking under inference-time search", Gap 1. This also takes over Gap 2's compensatory aggregation. Today's search trusts its verifier at one level fixed before search (α/λ [2501.05803][2501.06848], β [2510.01549], n/θ/M [2506.19248][2603.05739]), and that level discounts every candidate equally. The exploits that matter under best-of-N and particle search are content-level, and a single score cannot reveal them: Aesthetic search lowers CLIPScore and vice versa [2501.09732], HPS optimisation over-saturates images while compositional correctness drops [2601.03468], and ImageReward guidance ignores prompt constraints [2606.02884]. Theory says that whether harder selection still raises true quality depends on how heavy the verifier's error tail is compared with the quality tail [2407.14503], and one verifier's scores cannot reveal that. Closing the gap would let search keep full selection pressure on candidates whose lead is corroborated and withdraw it exactly where one verifier is being exploited. The same setting would carry across verifier pairs, and the search would get a per-prompt signal for when more compute stops paying off, all without held-out calibration.
- **If improving prior candidate**: C1's second estimate scored generator re-draws, so it estimated a generator-smoothed reward rather than the released image. It shared content-level errors, and at 4× budget it spent about half of the extra compute. In C2 the second estimate is a different verifier applied to the very image that will be released, and it targets the same quantity (preference/quality for this prompt). What is scored and what is released are now the same object, so the two-estimators-of-one-value structure of Double DQN holds as intended [1509.06461]. Content-level exploits are caught to the extent that the two verifiers' biases differ. The cost is zero generator NFE plus one extra verifier pass per candidate. Kept from C1: decoupling selection from evaluation, per-candidate trust, and the per-prompt stopping signal.
- **Source inspiration**: Outlier-robust filtering with selective observation rejection, from signal processing and robotics sensor fusion [2106.05706]. Several independent sensors observe one state. Each reading gets its own trust indicator, inferred jointly with the state from how far that reading lies from the state implied by the prediction and the other sensors, measured in units of that sensor's normal noise. A reading judged faulty has its gain driven to zero, so only that sensor is ignored at that step and the others still update the state. A trusted reading is fused normally. With a neutral prior the filter needs no rejection threshold. Treating faults per sensor beats one shared outlier parameter, and it also handles sporadic systematic bias in specific sensors (UWB non-line-of-sight). Supporting source from econometrics: Kotlarski's identity [1808.09375]. When two measurements of the same latent have mutually independent errors, the joint distribution of the measurements identifies the latent's distribution and both error distributions without ever observing the latent. This gives the fusion its "known normal noise" without labels. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts.
- **How it maps to this problem**: The tracked state becomes each candidate's true quality for this prompt. The sensors become two frozen verifiers of the same target, e.g. HPSv3 plus ImageReward or PickScore for images, or VideoAlign plus a VLM judge for video. The dynamics prediction becomes a prior over quality formed by the prompt's ordinary, unselected candidates, pooled across prompts so that it works at N=4. Each sensor's normal noise becomes the verifier's ordinary disagreement with the shared quality on typical candidates, fitted once from the two verifiers' joint scores on unlabelled base samples. The per-reading outlier flag becomes a per-candidate, per-verifier flag meaning "this score is an exploit". The fused state estimate replaces the raw verifier score wherever search consumes one: the best-of-N argmax, the FK/DAS terminal potential and resampling weights, and the noise-optimisation objective. When the two verifiers agree, the fused value is a precision-weighted average and selection pressure stays full. When one verifier makes an extreme claim the other does not back, that reading is flagged and the value falls back to the other verifier plus the prior. The rule is symmetric: either verifier can be the faulty one on a given candidate. Because the fused value is a posterior given both readings, taking the maximum does not inflate it the way taking the maximum of one reading does (when the fault model is right). As N or the tilt grows, new winners' leads increasingly get flagged and the winner's fused value stops rising, which gives a per-prompt, gold-free stopping point.
- **Why it could work / feasibility**: The filter relies on four conditions [2106.05706], and each plausibly holds here. (1) The sensors fail independently. Documented exploits are verifier-specific: Aesthetic vs CLIPScore [2501.09732], HPS vs GDino/ORM [2601.03468]. GARDO's cross-model gap already flags fine-tuning samples that have high proxy reward and very low unseen rewards [2512.24138]. (2) Each sensor's normal noise is known. Here it is identified from joint scores without gold when both verifiers read the same latent with independent errors [1808.09375]. Nonparametric deconvolution converges slowly, so a parametric heavy-tailed family fitted on a few thousand base candidates is the practical route. (3) There is an informative reference. Here it is the prompt's candidate pool plus cross-prompt calibration, which works at N=4, whereas per-prompt tail estimation needs N≥50 [2603.06797]. (4) Faults are sporadic. Exploitation concentrates in the selected tail, and in LLM best-of-N, ensemble conservatism already removes overoptimisation up to n=12,500 [2310.02743]. The method is training-free. The fault model is fitted on scores in seconds, the cost is one extra pass of a ≤1B scorer per candidate, and it uses the deployed HPSv3 and VideoAlign. With cached candidate pools it fits well inside 40 GPU-days. Held-out evaluation must exclude both search verifiers. Key assumption: the verifiers' exploitable errors are mostly not shared, and their disagreement on typical candidates carries over to the tail that search selects.
- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: The closest is GARDO [2512.24138]. It flags samples whose proxy win rate within a batch exceeds that of auxiliary models and gates a KL penalty on about the top 10% during RL fine-tuning. Its check is asymmetric (only the proxy can be wrong), its auxiliaries measure other constructs than the OCR/GenEval proxy, and a win rate within a batch of 4 has only four levels. C2 instead infers faults symmetrically between verifiers of the same target, with magnitudes and noise scales fitted without gold and a prior from the candidate pool, and it is used inside search for selection, resampling and stopping. LLM ensemble pessimism treats members as exchangeable and sets one global level that taxes every disagreement: WCO/UWO [2310.02743] and the entropic risk measure [2606.09073]. Weaver binarises votes and learns global per-verifier accuracies with a labelled class prior [2506.18203]. RoPoLL takes a median over a mostly clean panel for evaluation, not search [2606.30931]. Best-of-Tails adapts per prompt from one reward model's score tail [2603.06797]. My recency queries since 2025-06 ("reward ensemble diffusion", "multi-reward test-time scaling", "verifier disagreement", "reward uncertainty best-of-N", "multiple verifiers image generation", "reward model uncertainty diffusion alignment", "pessimistic reward ensemble", "heavy-tailed reward error", "measurement error reward model", "outlier robust reward aggregation", "sensor fusion reward models", "verifier reliability per sample test-time scaling") returned no diffusion/flow search method that does this.
- **Main risk**: The fault model cannot see errors that both verifiers share, such as the structural artifacts every T2I reward misses [2601.03468] or a shared CLIP-like backbone, so fused search can still hack them. In addition, genuine construct differences between two "preference" verifiers could be mistaken for faults and discount candidates that only one verifier rightly prefers.



### C3 — Freeze-thaw seed search: prune diffusion trajectories by their chance of still winning

- **Intuition**: Treat each seed's sequence of intermediate verifier scores as a partial learning curve, and run diffusion seed search the way modern hyperparameter search runs training jobs. A small in-context model, trained once on the generator's own score curves, reads all sibling seeds' partial curves and predicts, for each seed, a distribution over its final score. Compute then goes to the seeds that still have a real chance of finishing best. Clear losers are dropped as early as the evidence allows, and near-ties are paused rather than killed, so they can be resumed.
- **Gap it attacks**: Axis "intermediate reward estimation for pruning and resampling diffusion trajectories", Gap 1. Today's pruned and particle search keeps or kills a seed by one scalar score, under a keep ratio, temperature and prune time fixed before search. The concrete failures are as follows. PSP's fixed halving loses the final-best seed on about 1 in 5 prompts at its first prune [2607.21591]. DOG-Trim's recall at its earliest prune point is 0.81 even after noise-aware fine-tuning [2511.22242]. PSP also notes that a larger seed pool would require pruning earlier, at less informative stages, so with a fixed ratio its pool stays at 2× the BoN seed count [2607.21591]. How reliable early scores are depends on the prompt category [2508.12361] and on how close the candidates are [2607.23159]. VASR's own analysis needs the conditional spread σ_t of the final reward, but it substitutes a proxy and falls back to argmax [2604.06779]. Closing the gap would let one budget explore many more seeds, because hopeless seeds are killed very early while close contenders are protected until they separate. It would also remove the per-(model, verifier) schedule search, and it would make pruning safe on Wan2.1, where each wasted rollout costs about a minute [2607.23159].
- **Source inspiration**: Bayesian learning-curve extrapolation and freeze-thaw optimisation, from AutoML/hyperparameter optimisation [2310.20447][2404.16795]. Successive halving rests on "the crude heuristic that learning curves mostly do not cross" [2310.20447]. LC-PFN instead trains a transformer on many sampled curves so that, in one forward pass, it returns the posterior predictive distribution of a run's final performance given its partial curve. A run is stopped when its predicted chance of beating the best run so far falls below a small threshold. This gives 2–6× speed-ups across most of 53 tasks, consistently, whereas patience-style heuristics work on some tasks and fail on others. It fails where real curves have shapes absent from its prior (NAS-Bench-201), and when it sees only one curve it is "unaware of task hardness" [2310.20447]. ifBO's FT-PFN fixes the second weakness by conditioning in context on the partial curves of all configurations of the same task, which share start and saturation levels. It also changes discard into freeze: runs not chosen are paused and can be resumed "in the light of new evidence", something halving-style methods cannot do. Continuation goes to the run with the highest predicted probability of exceeding the incumbent. FT-PFN's uncertainty is better calibrated than deep-GP and ensemble surrogates, it is 10–100× faster, and one model trains in about 8 GPU-hours [2404.16795]. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts.
- **How it maps to this problem**: A hyperparameter configuration becomes an initial noise seed. A training epoch becomes a denoising step, or a block of steps. The validation curve becomes the verifier's score on the clean estimate x̂0 at each checked step, which pruned search already computes at no extra generator cost [2607.21591]. Final accuracy becomes the frozen verifier's final score on x0. An HPO task becomes a prompt, and the other configurations' curves become the sibling seeds' curves for that prompt. The synthetic curve prior becomes an empirical one: score curves from cached trajectories of the same generator and verifier on training prompts. Such curves are cheap here, unlike in HPO, and this removes LC-PFN's prior-mismatch failure. The stopping rule becomes "freeze a seed when its predicted chance of finishing above the prompt's best contender is negligible". A thaw becomes resuming a frozen seed from its stored latent when siblings stall; a deterministic flow sampler loses nothing by pausing. Starting a new configuration becomes launching a fresh seed with the compute that early freezes release. This is exactly the spread-versus-gap comparison the gap asks for. A seed far below its siblings with a narrow predicted spread is frozen at once, while seeds whose predicted final scores overlap are carried forward until they separate. Learned point estimators (NARF [2511.22242], Probe-Select [2603.02829], StitchVM [2605.19804]) can replace r(x̂0) as the per-step observation. For stochastic samplers, the same predicted spread supplies the σ_t that VASR's offspring allocation needs [2604.06779].
- **Why it could work / feasibility**: The source mechanism needs four things, and each plausibly holds. (1) Partial curves are informative but can still cross. PSP's intermediate-to-final correlation rises quickly, yet the final-best seed survives only 80–90% of halvings [2607.21591]. This is the regime where curve extrapolation beats halving in HPO [2310.20447]. (2) The prior covers real curve shapes. Here the prior is built from real trajectories. PSP already caches deterministic trajectories and intermediate rewards per prompt for offline schedule tuning [2607.21591], and DOG-Trim trains on about 5,000 self-generated trajectories [2511.22242]. (3) Siblings share prompt-level structure that predicts reliability. Reliability varies by category [2508.12361] and by candidate spread [2607.23159], and in-context conditioning on sibling curves is how FT-PFN captures task-level structure [2404.16795]. (4) Inference is cheap. A PFN call takes under 100 ms [2310.20447], against seconds of generator compute per step batch. On a ≤3B image flow model, curves for a few thousand prompts × 16–32 seeds cost GPU-hours to a few GPU-days, and a smaller Wan2.1-1.3B set fits too, so the whole study fits within 40 GPU-days. Key assumption: the curve so far, together with the sibling curves, predicts how much each seed's final score can still move better than a step-only schedule does. In other words, prompts and seeds must differ measurably in how settled their ranking already is.
- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: PSP [2607.21591] calls its fixed halving at predetermined steps "similar to" successive halving. It tunes the schedule offline per (model, reward), applies the same schedule to every prompt, and discards pruned seeds. C3 replaces the fixed ratio with each seed's predicted chance of winning, given its own curve and all sibling curves. It also freezes rather than discards, and it can start fresh seeds mid-search. Neither VASR [2604.06779], which derives σ_t but never estimates it, nor LatSearch [2603.14526], which accumulates softmax weights over scoring steps under one global τ, represents spread. CachedSearch's per-prompt adaptation needed an extra probe that cost more than it returned [2607.23159], whereas C3 uses only scores the search already computes. Recency queries since 2025-06 found no diffusion or flow search that does this: "freeze-thaw diffusion", "learning curve diffusion sampling trajectories", "prior-data fitted network diffusion", "probability of improvement pruning generation", "pause resume trajectories diffusion search", "dynamic seed allocation diffusion", "reward trajectory forecasting denoising", "adaptive pruning diffusion seeds uncertainty", "successive halving test-time scaling" and "Bayesian early stopping best-of-N".
- **Main risk**: At the problem's 4× budget, PSP's halving already has mean regret near 0.01 ImageReward, and tuned schedules barely help [2607.21591]. Pruning errors also concentrate on near-ties, where the loss is small [2607.23159]. The gain therefore has to come from a much larger seed pool, with clear losers frozen very early. That requires the earliest score curves, where Tweedie-based values are close to random [2506.20701], to separate losers reliably. If they do not, the model will correctly report wide spreads and behave like PSP.
