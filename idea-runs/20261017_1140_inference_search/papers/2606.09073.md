# A Unifying Lens on Reward Uncertainty in RLHF

## What this paper does
This is a short theory paper on RLHF. It treats the reward as a distribution p(r|x,y), for example the spread of a deep ensemble, and derives a closed-form effective reward from a Bayesian or a KL-DRO argument. The pessimistic branch is the entropic risk −β log E_p[e^{−r/β}]. The ensemble rules of Coste et al. appear as special cases: Mean is the limit β→∞, WCO the limit β→0, and UWO a Gaussian truncation. The paper also discusses what each rule implicitly assumes. There are no experiments.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the unified view give a per-member, per-response reliability, or remove the global pessimism coefficient?
It does neither. The members are pooled into a single distribution p(r|x,y), for example "a deep ensemble ... whose empirical variance approximates σ²(x,y)" [full text, Section 4]. One global β controls pessimism, and the paper ties it to the KL coefficient "for simplicity" [Section 3.3, "Choice of KL coefficients"]. The rule only helps if σ² "tracks true epistemic uncertainty", so the ensemble must be calibrated [Section 4 (ii)]. The paper notes that WCO "can underperform Mean when the ensemble is too small or correlated", because it "discards all distributional information beyond the minimum" [Section 4]. For heavy-tailed p it suggests quantile pessimism such as CVaR [Section 4, "Beyond Gaussian"]. Disagreement is always read as spread around one shared reward. It is never attributed to a fault in one particular member.
