# Rethinking Test-Time Scaling for Flow-Matching Generative Models (DOG-Trim)

## What this paper does
DOG-Trim argues local trajectory search (SDE branching, noise-then-denoise) is too costly for deterministic flow-matching models and instead prunes a large pool of global candidates: candidates are denoised in parallel to predefined steps, their Euler clean estimates are decoded and scored, and a fixed fraction gamma is kept. Two additions: Repel, a token-level cross-trajectory repulsion for diversity, and NARF, a noise-aware reward model self-distilled to predict the final clean-image reward from intermediate Euler estimates, trained with a near-clean-to-noisy curriculum. On Flux (and SDXL/SD3 transfer) it roughly doubles the TTS gain of prior methods at matched compute.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
Euler estimate x_{0|t}=x_t+v_theta t, decoded to pixels and scored by an image reward [full text, Section 2.2]. NARF fine-tunes the reward model so that, at each target timestep, its score on the decoded Euler estimate regresses (MSE) the clean reward of the same trajectory's final image, using ~5000 self-generated trajectories, Repel augmentation and a curriculum that saves separate weights per target timestep [full text, Section 2.4 "Pipeline design", "Training strategies"].

### Goal: What does it report about the reliability of intermediate estimates?
Off-the-shelf rewards misrank partially denoised samples with a high-frequency bias ("cartoonish" images preferred), causing mis-pruning [full text, Section 1, Fig 3; Section 2.4]. On 100 unseen prompts x 20 samples, Kendall tau between early and final scores at t=21/14/7 is 0.693/0.521/0.258 without fine-tuning and 0.736/0.645/0.410 with full NARF; pruning recall at t=7 rises from 0.712 to 0.812 [full text, Section 3.3, Table 3]. So even after noise-aware fine-tuning, the earliest prune point keeps a weak ranking.

### Goal: How are intermediate estimates turned into prune decisions, and is that rule adapted per prompt from measured reliability?
A fixed retain ratio gamma=0.5 at predefined timesteps, chosen by sweeping gamma [full text, Section 2.2; Section 3.3 "On the Pruning Heuristics", Fig 7a]. Adaptive alternatives (tempered-softmax multinomial, SSP, adaptive pruning) use a softmax temperature that "progressively increases as denoising advances, reflecting growing confidence", set by a hyperparameter search, and all four heuristics perform comparably [Section 3.3, Fig 7c]. No rule uses per-prompt or per-candidate uncertainty.

### Goal: What limitation does it state or show that bears on intermediate reward estimation for pruning/resampling?
Less suitable for computationally expensive VLM-based rewards; requires VAE decoding at every check; future work: noise-aware reward directly in latent space [full text, Section 3.3 "Limitations and Future Work"]. SDE sampling on Flux underperforms ODE even with 3x steps, which motivates abandoning local resampling [Section 2.2, Fig 7b].


### Goal: What adaptive pruning rules does DOG-Trim compare with fixed-ratio pruning, and how do they perform at matched compute?
Four rules are compared at fixed compute after a temperature search: fixed-ratio, "adaptive-pruning" (normalized tempered-softmax weights W_i; candidate i is kept deterministically if N·W_i ≥ 1 and otherwise with probability N·W_i, so the number kept depends on how spread out the intermediate scores are), unique SSP, and unique multinomial [full text, Section 3.3 "On the Pruning Heuristics"]. The temperature rises as denoising advances, "reflecting growing confidence in the reward predictions". Result: "all heuristics perform comparably and, notably, the simplest fixed-ratio strategy is competitive" [Section 3.3, Fig 7c]. Fixed-ratio performance peaks at γ between 0.4 and 0.6 when the initial pool is scaled to hold compute fixed [Fig 7a].
