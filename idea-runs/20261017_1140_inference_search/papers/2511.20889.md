# Test-Time Alignment of Text-to-Image Diffusion Models via Null-Text Embedding Optimisation (Null-TTA)

## What this paper does
Null-TTA aligns a frozen text-to-image diffusion model to a reward at test time by optimising the unconditional (null-text) embedding used in classifier-free guidance, rather than the latent or noise, with a KL-regularised objective and a greedy search over the backward process. The argument is that the text-embedding space is a semantic manifold, so optimisation there cannot exploit non-semantic pixel patterns. On SD1.5 and SDXL with HPSv2, Aesthetic and PickScore targets, it reports Pareto-better target vs held-out reward trade-offs than DNO, DAS, TDPO and DPS, plus a small user study.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What evidence does it give that inference-time reward hacking exploits non-semantic/fine-scale patterns, and in which search regime?
The claim is an attribution, not a measurement: latent/noise-space methods "sample or maximise in either pixel-space x or, almost equivalently, latent-space z", which "makes them prone to relying on non-semantic noise perturbations to satisfy r(x)" [full text, Section 3.2]. Empirically, the over-optimising baseline is DNO, a gradient-based noise optimiser, which raises the target reward while degrading held-out PickScore/ImageReward; DPS and TDPO under-optimise [full text, Section 4.2, Fig 1]. The paper does not analyse the spatial scale of the exploited features, and it does not separate sampling-based SMC/best-of-N hacking from gradient-based hacking beyond the general statement in Section 1. So it supports "fine-scale exploitation" mainly for gradient-based noise optimisation.
