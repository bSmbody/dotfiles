# Inference-Time Scaling for Flow Models via Stochastic Generation and Rollover Budget Forcing (RBF)

## What this paper does
To apply particle sampling (SVDD-style best-of-K children per step) to deterministic flow models, the paper converts the flow ODE into an SDE at inference time and further converts the linear interpolant into a variance-preserving one to enlarge the search space. Rollover Budget Forcing allocates a per-step NFE quota: as soon as a child's estimated reward exceeds the current sample's, it moves on and rolls the unused NFEs to later steps. On FLUX for compositional and quantity-aware generation it outperforms prior particle and search baselines at matched NFE.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
v(x_t) ≈ r(x_{0|t}) with the Tweedie posterior mean, following prior work [full text, Section 3.2]; child weights w=exp(v/beta) and SVDD picks the max-weight child [Section 3.2, Eq 3-4].

### Goal: How are intermediate estimates turned into compute-allocation decisions?
RBF: at each step, if a child's estimated value exceeds that of the current sample within the quota, accept it immediately and roll over remaining NFEs; otherwise take the highest-expected-reward child as in SVDD [full text, Section 6]. The acceptance test compares two noisy point estimates with no margin or uncertainty term; the motivation is that the NFEs needed to find an improving child "significantly varies across different runs" [Section 6; Appendix C].

### Goal: What limitation bears on intermediate reward estimation for pruning/resampling?
Particle sampling in flow models requires injecting stochasticity because "all particles x_{t-Δt} drawn from x_t are identical" under the ODE [Section 3.2]; the method "introduces additional inference-time overhead" [Section 8].
