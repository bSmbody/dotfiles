# Inference-Time Text-to-Video Alignment with Diffusion Latent Beam Search (DLBS)

## What this paper does
DLBS keeps B latent beams, samples K stochastic-DDIM children per beam at each step, scores each child by a reward of its predicted clean latent, and keeps the top-B of KB, reducing the error accumulation of greedy argmax search. A lookahead (LA) estimator replaces the one-step Tweedie estimate by running T' (≈6) deterministic DDIM steps from the child to t=0 before scoring. It also calibrates a weighted combination of perceptual metrics to VLM/human preference, and reports better perceptual video quality than best-of-N and greedy search at lower cost on Latte, CogVideoX and Wan 2.1.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
Practical value approximations are unified as Jensen (exchange log and E) plus posterior-mean reward [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 5]. DLBS uses the lookahead estimator: T'-step deterministic DDIM from z_{t-1} to a clean latent, then the reward [Section 3.2, Algorithm 2]. Enlarging T' "monotonically tightens the upper bound on the reward-approximation error" [Appendix F].

### Goal: What does it report about the reliability of the estimator, and does it represent the spread of the final reward?
Measured against the actual reward of rolling out z_t with the stochastic sampler (DDIM eta=1, or SDE-DPMSolver++ on Wan2.1), estimation error decreases with T' but search gains saturate by T'≈6; T'=12-20 gives no measurable gain at higher cost [full text, Appendix M.4, Figs 23-24]. The estimator is a single deterministic point prediction of an outcome that the stochastic sampler realises randomly; no spread is modelled.

### Goal: How are estimates turned into selection decisions?
Hard top-B over KB candidates at every step with fixed B, K [Section 3.2, Algorithm 1]; no temperature, no per-prompt adaptation.
