# Revisiting the (Sub)Optimality of Best-of-N for Inference-Time Alignment

## What this paper does
A theory paper on Best-of-N for LLM inference-time alignment measured by win-rate instead of expected true reward. It models reward-model quality by the pairwise win-rate error eps_pw of r_hat vs r* on typical pairs from pi_ref, shows properly tuned BoN is computationally and statistically optimal for win-rate, and proposes E_M-regularised BoN, whose optimal policy is pi_ref conditioned on r_hat above its (1-1/M) quantile (sampled as uniform over the top ceil(N/M) of N candidates), which is monotone in N and so avoids reward hacking.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism removes reward hacking, and what does it assume about the reward model's error?
The objective max_pi E_pi[r_hat] - R_max * E_M(pi || pi_ref) has the closed-form solution pi_ref(. | r_hat >= lambda) with lambda the (1-1/M) quantile, i.e. a top-quantile selector [full text, Section 4, Eq 10, Lemma 1]. The reward-model error is only assumed small on typical samples, eps_pw = E_{y,y'~pi_ref}|phi_rhat - phi_r*| [full text, Section 2.2, Eq 3]. The regret bound is E_M(pi*||pi_ref) + M*eps_pw + 1/N, so the error term grows linearly with the regularisation level M [full text, Theorem 5, Eq 11].

### Goal: How is the regularisation level set, and is it sample-adaptive?
M is a single global parameter that "still requires tuning"; the paper expects plain BoN to remain dominant when monotonicity is not needed [full text, Section 4, after Theorem 5]. The selector depends on the candidates only through their r_hat ranks, so it has no per-candidate signal of where r_hat disagrees with r*. It argues chi^2-regularised BoN can be arbitrarily worse for win-rate [full text, Proposition 2]. No experiments on images or diffusion are part of the read sections.
