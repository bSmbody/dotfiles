# Flash-BoN: Instant Drafts for Inference-Time Scaling in Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
Flash-BoN argues that comparing inference-time scaling methods by NFE hides verifier cost, and that under end-to-end wall-clock budgets plain best-of-N beats guided search with frequent intermediate verification (BFS/DFS/zero-order search). It generates a large pool of cheap draft seeds with a per-model configuration combining early timestep stopping, layer skipping and activation proxies, picks the best draft with a multi-stage pointwise-then-pairwise VLM verification, and resumes only that draft from its cached latent at full compute. On Wan2.1-1.3B/14B and FLUX.1-dev across GenAI-Bench, GenEval and UniGenBench it leads every baseline in score-vs-wall-clock AUC.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How much do intermediate verifier calls cost relative to denoising, and how do guided/pruned searches compare with best-of-N once verifier cost is counted?
NFE "only count denoising forward passes and often ignore verifier costs entirely", and "the verifier step is often more compute intensive than a single denoising step" [full text, Section 1]. On Wan2.1-1.3B, BFS is marginally best at fixed NFE, but "under equal runtime, the trend reverses", because methods with frequent verifier calls take longer to reach the same NFE [Section 1, Fig 1; Appendix 0.B.1, Fig 11-12]. Across Wan2.1-1.3B, Wan2.1-14B and FLUX.1-dev, "BoN-style approaches that prioritize broad exploration consistently outperform guided search methods that invest compute in intermediate verification", and guided curves are nearly flat on the larger models because "repeated verifier calls leave little room for additional candidates" [Section 5, Fig 3]. Normalized AUC on GenAI-Bench: BoN 0.65/0.60/0.60 vs BFS 0.50/0.43/0.44 [Table 1]. Timing is end-to-end on H200 and includes generation, verification, refinement and data transfer [Section 5].

### Goal: How does it screen many seeds cheaply and resume the chosen one, and is there any per-seed uncertainty or adaptive stopping?
Stage 1 draws M drafts from different seeds under a configuration φ* found once per model by discrete optimization over truncation, layer skipping and activation proxies [full text, Section 4.1, Eq 8]. Stage 2 scores all drafts pointwise, keeps the top ρ1, runs sparse adjacent pairwise comparisons with Elo, keeps the top ρ2, then runs a full pairwise tournament (defaults ρ1=ρ2=0.5, κ=1.5) [Section 4.2, Eq 9-12]. Stage 3 resumes the selected draft from its cached latent z_{t_S'} with full computation; cached tensors are offloaded to CPU [Section 4.2, Eq 13]. Retention ratios are fixed; there is no per-seed predictive distribution and only the single winner is resumed. Pointwise VLM scores tie at the top on over 80% of prompts [Section 4.2].

### Goal: Which baselines does it compare against?
BoN, BFS, DFS and zero-order search [full text, Section 5]. No seed-pruning method (e.g. PSP or DOG-Trim) is compared. With ImageReward as verifier the selected score saturates within the first batch and stays flat, read as over-optimization [Appendix 0.B.2, Fig 13].
