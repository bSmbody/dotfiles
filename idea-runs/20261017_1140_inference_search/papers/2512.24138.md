# GARDO: Reinforcing Diffusion Models without Reward Hacking

## What this paper does
GARDO is an RL fine-tuning framework for diffusion/flow models (built on Flow-GRPO, also DiffusionNFT, SD3.5 and FLUX) that fights reward hacking without a uniform KL penalty. It (i) gates the KL penalty to the subset of samples whose proxy reward looks untrustworthy, using a per-sample uncertainty computed from auxiliary reward models, (ii) periodically resets the reference policy to the current policy, and (iii) shapes advantages to reward diverse high-quality samples. It reports less hacking on held-out metrics with the sample efficiency of an unregularised run.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does GARDO measure per-sample uncertainty of the proxy reward, and what is it computed from?
Uncertainty is U(x_i) = w(R_proxy(x_i)) − mean_n w(R_aux,n(x_i)), where w is the sample's win rate within the batch [full text, Section 4.2, Eq 7]. A high value flags a sample whose proxy rank is anomalously high compared with the ranks given by K auxiliary reward models; they use Aesthetic and ImageReward (K=2), so the cost is "negligible" [Section 4.2]. It is motivated by ensemble disagreement over bootstrapped Q-functions in RL, simplified because learning ensembles of value functions is too costly [Section 4.2]. So the per-sample trust signal is cross-verifier rank disagreement, not a re-measurement by the same verifier.

### Goal: Where is the signal used (fine-tuning vs inference-time search) and how is the gate set?
Only during RL fine-tuning: the KL penalty is applied to the top-k% most uncertain samples, starting at k=0.1 and adapted by ×1.1/×0.9 according to a 20-epoch window of uncertainty extremes [full text, Appendix A.1 "GARDO's gated KL"]. The key empirical finding is that penalising about 10% of samples suffices to prevent hacking [Section 4.2]. There is no inference-time search component. The stated main limitation is the dependence on auxiliary reward models for uncertainty, and scalability to video models is left open [Section 6].


### Goal: Is GARDO's per-sample signal symmetric across reward models, what constructs do proxy and auxiliaries measure, and how fine-grained is it?
The signal is asymmetric. U(x) = w(R̃(x)) − mean_n w(R̂_n(x)) flags only samples whose proxy win rate is "anomalously high compared to the ensemble". The auxiliaries "only serve as a metric" and are never treated as the faulty reading [full text, Section 4.2, Eq 7]. The proxies are rule-based (OCR, GenEval), while the auxiliaries are Aesthetic and ImageReward [Section 4.2; Section 5 "Benchmarks"]. They measure other constructs, and both also appear among the "unseen" evaluation metrics [Section 5 "Benchmarks"]. The win rate is computed within a batch, so its resolution is 1/B. Training uses groups of G=24 [Section 5 "Setup"]. About 10% of samples get the KL penalty [Fig 8a]. Fig 7 shows that flagged samples have high proxy reward and very low unseen rewards, which is evidence that cross-model disagreement catches hacked samples during fine-tuning. A weighted multi-reward objective (0.8·OCR + 0.1·Aesthetic + 0.1·ImageReward) was less sample-efficient on the proxy [Section 5.1, Fig 8b]. The stated limitation is the dependence on auxiliary reward models, and scaling to video is left open [Section 6].
