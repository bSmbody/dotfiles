# RoPoLL: Robust Panel of LLM Judges

## What this paper does
RoPoLL studies panels of LLM judges, juries that report a consensus score. It models each judge with Huber contamination: a judge is usually competent and unbiased, but sometimes emits an arbitrary corrupted score. Under this model the arithmetic mean (PoLL) has unbounded bias under any positive contamination rate. RoPoLL therefore replaces the mean with the geometric median, which needs no tuning and has breakdown point 1/2. The experiments use 13 open-weight judges, three reward-model benchmarks and four synthetic corruption regimes.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does RoPoLL handle a judge that fails in a biased way on some items, and what does that require?
The model is ŷ_i ~ (1−α_i)P_i + α_i Q_i, where a Bernoulli indicator chooses between the competent component P_i and an arbitrary corruption Q_i for each judge and item [full text, Section 3.4, Assumption 2]. Concrete corruptions include parser zeros, sycophancy, cross-attribute confusion and "cauchy-far" heavy-tailed hallucination [Section 1, "The problem"]. Aggregation by the geometric median needs minority corruption [Assumption 5] and conditional independence of the judges [Assumption 3]. RoPoLL is a drop-in replacement for the aggregation step in evaluation; the paper describes no use for selecting among generated candidates under search pressure [Section 1, "Overview"]. The aggregate uses only the panel's scores for the item, with no prior from other candidates. My inference: with two judges a median cannot tell which one is corrupted, so the method needs a panel of three or more.
