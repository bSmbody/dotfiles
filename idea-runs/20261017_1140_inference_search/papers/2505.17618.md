# Scaling Image and Video Generation via Test-Time Evolutionary Search (EvoSearch)

## What this paper does
EvoSearch treats the denoising trajectory as an evolutionary path: a population of initial noises is evaluated, selected by tournament, and mutated; then at a few scheduled intermediate timesteps the population of partial states is re-evaluated, elites are kept and the rest are mutated with Gaussian perturbations aligned to the SDE sampler, continuing from cached intermediate states. Flow models (FLUX, Wan, Hunyuan) are run with an ODE-to-SDE conversion. It reports gains over best-of-N and particle sampling for image and video at matched compute.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and why?
Fitness at x_{t_i} is the reward of the FULLY denoised x0 continued from it: the authors avoid Tweedie and lookahead estimators because they "can induce significant prediction inaccuracies and approximation errors", and "evaluate the reward directly on fully denoised x0" [full text, Section 4.6, Eq 6]. Accurate fitness thus costs a full continuation per candidate per generation.

### Goal: How are estimates turned into selection decisions, and what limitation bears on pruning/resampling?
Tournament selection plus m elites at a uniform evolution schedule with a population-size schedule (first generation 2x larger) [Section 4.4-4.7; Appendix A.1.1]. Flow ODEs are converted to SDE (sde-dpmsolver++) for Wan/Hunyuan/FLUX so that mutation and sampling are stochastic [Section 2.1; Appendix A.1.1]. Exploration depends on a hand-set mutation rate (beta=0.3) [Section 6 Limitations; Appendix A.1.1].
