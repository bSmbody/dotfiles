# FUSE: Ensembling Verifiers with Zero Labeled Data

## What this paper does
Lee et al. combine several LLM verifiers for best-of-N selection without any correctness labels. First, each verifier's score is binarised with a threshold chosen to minimise a label-free statistic that measures violations of triplet conditional independence. Second, each verifier's query-specific sensitivity and specificity are estimated with the spectral method of moments of Jaffe et al. (2015). Third, pseudo-labels are formed from triplet posteriors, and a logistic-regression ensemble is fitted to maximise the estimated accuracy. FUSE matches or beats semi-supervised baselines (Weaver, and logistic regression with 5% labels), and it beats naive averaging and majority vote on LLM benchmarks including GPQA, HLE and the IMO Shortlist.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does FUSE estimate without labels, and at what granularity (per verifier, per query, per response)?
FUSE estimates each verifier's sensitivity ψ_j and specificity η_j, conditioned on the query q, from the N×m score matrix of that query's responses [full text, Section 2.1, Theorem 2.3, Eq 1-3]. The final selector is a logistic regression f_θ over the raw score vector. It has one θ per query, or one per batch of queries [Section 2.3, Eq 6-7, Algorithm 1; Section 1.3]. Reliability is therefore set per verifier and per query. No fault indicator is inferred for an individual response: every response to a query is combined with the same weights. The target is binary correctness y ∈ {±1} [Section 1.3].

### Goal: What assumptions, and how many verifiers, does identification need?
It needs two assumptions: more than half of the verifiers are better than random (Assumption 2.1), and every triplet of verifiers is conditionally independent given the label (Assumption 2.2) [Section 2.1]. The qualities are read off the off-diagonal entries of the second-order covariance (rank one, uu^T) and of the third-order tensor with three distinct indices (w⊗w⊗w) [Theorem 2.3]. Posteriors are estimated per triplet of verifiers and then averaged [Section 2.3, Eq 8-9]. The method is therefore built for m ≥ 3. My inference from Theorem 2.3: with two verifiers the off-diagonal covariance is a single number and cannot separate the two verifiers' qualities. The joint-conditional-independence ensemble of Jaffe et al. does worse than naive averaging in 7 of 10 settings because LLM verifiers are conditionally dependent [Section 2.1, Fig 3-4]. FUSE therefore picks the binarisation thresholds that reduce violations of triplet independence [Section 2.2, Proposition 2.4].

### Goal: Does it handle continuous scores, and has it been applied to images or to search under selection pressure?
Real-valued scores are handled by binarising them with learned thresholds, with a real-valued extension of the moment result [Section 2.2; Appendix A.2]. All experiments are LLM best-of-N on MMLU, GPQA, HLE and the IMO Shortlist [Section 3]. There are no images, no diffusion models, and no sequential search or resampling. The related-work section calls the use of ensembles against reward hacking (Coste et al.; Eisenstein et al.) a "distinct but related setting" [Section 4.1]. The stated limitation is the cost of running several verifiers [Section 5].
