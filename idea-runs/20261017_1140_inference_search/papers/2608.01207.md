# It's the Decoding Format, Not the Perturbation: Auditing Consistency-Based Selection for Vision-Language Test-Time Scaling

## What this paper does
The paper audits Perturbation Grounded Selection (Pgs), a training-free rule for VLM test-time scaling that scores each candidate answer by how often the model re-derives it under label-preserving input perturbations (crops, masking, mild photometric/geometric jitter), added to majority-vote counts. Pgs appears to beat chain-of-thought majority voting by up to +31.8 points on TextVQA, but a format- and budget-matched control (the same short draws spent on the original image) tracks or beats it on every benchmark. The result is framed as negative and diagnostic.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does a perturbation-stability signal predict per-instance selection gains once compute and format are matched?
No. The selection rule is g(c) = original-view votes + λ Σ_m w_m ρ(c | t_m(x), q), with ρ the re-derivation rate under perturbed views [full text, Section 3.3, Eq 1-2]. Against the format-matched control MatchedCtrl, "no category yields a significant positive Δ" and the ViLP interval lies below zero [Section 5.4, Figure 2]. "Instance-level correlations between gap and gain are near zero", gap quartiles are non-monotone, and "a large measured re-derivation advantage therefore does not forecast when perturbation-weighted selection beats" the control [Section 5.4]. Blanking the perturbed inputs collapses accuracy, so the signal is image-dependent, yet "image-dependence and the stability gap decouple" [Section 5.3, Table 4]. The domain is VLM question answering with input-side perturbations, not generator re-draws.
