# Shrinking the Generation-Verification Gap with Weak Verifiers (Weaver)

## What this paper does
Weaver combines many weak verifiers (reward models and LM judges) into one stronger verifier for best-of-N selection in LLM reasoning, where the true label is the binary correctness of an answer. It uses weak supervision to estimate each verifier's accuracy without labels. It handles scale mismatch by normalising and binarising scores, and it filters out low-quality verifiers. It also distils the ensemble into a smaller model.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does Weaver combine verifiers without labels, and is the weighting per verifier or per candidate?
Each verifier's score is binarised into a vote [full text, Section 4.2; Appendix B.2.2]. The votes are assumed conditionally independent given correctness Y [Section 4.2.1]. Each verifier's TPR and TNR are fitted so that the predicted pairwise vote agreements and marginal vote rates match the observed ones (Eq 2-5). The class prior Pr(Y=1) comes from a small labelled dev set [Section 4.2.1; B.1.2]. Responses are then ranked by a naive-Bayes posterior Pr(Y=1 | votes) (Eq 1, 7). The accuracy parameters are per verifier and global over responses, optionally per difficulty cluster [B.4]. No reliability is inferred for an individual response. Binarisation is used because "a score of 0.8 may have different meanings for different verifiers" [B.2.2], so the size of a disagreement is discarded. The model also needs most verifiers to be better than random, otherwise the solution is not unique [B.2.3]. The domain is LLM math and QA with binary correctness, and no images are studied.
