# Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning for Diffusion Models (RSA-FT)

## What this paper does
RSA-FT (CVPR 2026) treats reward hacking in reward-gradient diffusion fine-tuning (DRaFT, AlignProp, ReFL, DRTune) as an adversarial attack on the reward model. It shows that reward sharpness, the drop of the reward under a small worst-case input perturbation, correlates negatively with held-out preference over training, and fine-tunes the generator on a "flattened" reward: the reward of a one-step worst-case pixel perturbation of the sample, combined with a SAM-style worst-case perturbation of the generator's weights. On SD1.5, SDXL and SD3 with HPSv2.1 it improves held-out PickScore and ImageReward and reduces visible hacking.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is its explanation of reward hacking and how does it measure reward sharpness?
Hacking is "a generalization failure of r"; the hypothesis is that r "generalizes best where its reward landscape is locally flat, while sharp regions indicate misalignment with the true human preference" [full text, Section 3, "Hypothesis"]. Sharpness S1 = E_x[r(x) - min_{||eps||<rho} r(x+eps)], approximated by one normalised-gradient step [Section 3, Eq 10]. Tracked over DRaFT-K training of SD1.5 on HPSv2, S1 correlates with held-out PickScore at Pearson -0.802 and with ImageReward at -0.669 [Section 3, "Empirical Validation", Fig 4]. This is a dataset-level average per checkpoint, not a per-sample diagnostic.

### Goal: What does the flattened reward do mechanically, and where is it used?
The flattened reward is min over a neighbourhood d(x,x')<rho of r(x') [Eq 12], implemented as r(x0 + delta) with delta = -rho * grad_x r / ||grad_x r|| in pixel space (needs a differentiable reward) plus a weight-space perturbation eps_theta [Eq 15-19, Algorithm 1]. It is used only as the training objective for weight updates of the generator; rho and rho_w are global constants chosen by grid search (both 1e-2) [Section 5.1 "Implementation Details"; Appendix E]. There is no inference-time search, selection or resampling component.

### Goal: Is there any per-sample trust signal or inference-time use, and what limitations are stated?
No. "Our current approach uniformly applies flattened rewards"; the authors leave "selective sharpness-aware weighting that down-weights overly sharp regions" to future work, also mention Gaussian averaging as an untried alternative to local minimisation, and note that evaluation relies on model-based proxies with a small human study (17 evaluators) [full text, Section 6]. The perturbations are off-manifold pixel-space directions, not generator-produced re-samples.
