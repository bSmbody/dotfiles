# Are we really tilting? The mechanics of reward guidance in flow and diffusion models

## What this paper does
The paper analyses why gradient-based reward guidance fails to sample the reward tilt rho(x) e^{lambda r(x)}. It shows, in closed form for Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture targets with quadratic rewards, that the k-particle plug-in estimator of the Doob h-function used by practical guidance causes (i) within-mode reward hacking (mean overshoot, covariance collapse) and (ii) inability to select distant high-reward modes. It proposes a closed-form time-dependent reward damping schedule that corrects (i) at no extra cost, and shows best-of-n over seeds compensates for (ii). Experiments: Gaussian mixtures, 2D checkerboard, FLUX.1-dev with blueness/masked-intensity, ImageReward and a Qwen2.5-VL yes/no reward.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it identify as causing reward hacking under inference-time guidance, and what does its fix correct?
Hacking is attributed to sampler bias: the plug-in estimate h_hat = (1/k) sum exp(lambda r(X1^(i))) of h_t(x)=E[e^{lambda r(X1)}|X_t=x] with small k drives the plug-in flow to overshoot the mean and shrink covariance relative to the exact tilt [full text, Section 2 "Plug-in estimation", Eq 5; Section 4.1, Theorems 2-3]. The fix replaces constant lambda by a damping schedule lambda_t derived for an isotropic Gaussian target, with sigma treated as a tunable hyperparameter [full text, Section 4.1, Proposition 4]. It notes that simply lowering lambda cannot recover the analytic tilt [full text, Section 4.1, text after Fig 3]. Plug-in guidance with any finite k cannot select modes (correct-mode probability 1/2), while best-of-n gives 1-2^{-n} [full text, Theorem 6].

### Goal: Does it treat the reward itself as correct, i.e. what happens if the verifier is misspecified?
Yes: the stated target is always the exact tilt of the given reward, rho_1 e^{lambda r}, and success is measured as fidelity to that tilt (e.g., checkerboard mean reward / covariance trace vs the analytic tilt) [full text, Section 2 Eq 3; Section 5.2, Table 11]. The introduction explicitly sets aside the explanation that hacking comes from "the complexity of neural reward functions" in favour of the estimator explanation [full text, Section 1]. Nothing in the method addresses a proxy reward that is wrong on some samples; an exactly-sampled tilt of a flawed verifier would still favour its flaws.

### Goal: How is held-out quality evaluated in the image experiments, and what limitations are stated?
FLUX results are qualitative figures (welder removed, fox fur turned blue, ImageReward hacks ignoring prompt constraints) with damping preserving prompt content; the VLM mode-selection numerics are deferred to an appendix; no held-out preference model or GenEval-style metric is reported [full text, Section 5.1 Figs 5-10; Section 5.2 Figs 13-14]. Limitations: theory only for Gaussian/Gaussian-mixture targets with quadratic rewards under the memoryless schedule; the damping schedule is from the Gaussian analysis; empirical study limited to checkerboard and FLUX [full text, Section 6 "Limitations and future work"].
