# Best-of-Tails: Bridging Optimism and Pessimism in Inference-Time Alignment

## What this paper does
Best-of-Tails (BoT) analyses LLM inference-time alignment as regret minimisation. It argues that optimistic selection (best-of-N, soft BoN) suits light-tailed reward distributions and pessimistic selection (χ²-style ITP) suits heavy-tailed ones. It then selects with a Tsallis-divergence regulariser whose order α interpolates between the two. α is set per prompt from a Hill estimate of how heavy the tail of the proxy reward is among that prompt's candidates. Experiments cover GSM8K, MMLU, MATH and AlpacaFarm with four proxy reward models.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does BoT adapt selection strength, for what unit (prompt or candidate), and from what signal?
Selection maximises E[r̂] − λ·D_α(π‖π_ref) with a Tsallis divergence. α→1 recovers soft BoN (KL) and α=2 recovers ITP (χ²) [full text, Section 4, Eq 11-12]. The order is set per prompt as α(x) = 1 + κ̂/(κ̂+κ0), where κ̂ is the Hill estimator over the top-K proxy scores of that prompt's N candidates [Section 4.2, Eq 17-18]. The signal is how heavy the tail of the single proxy's score distribution is. No second measurement is used, and every candidate of a prompt receives the same α. The hyperparameters are κ0 (set to the median κ̂ across prompts) and K=√N [Section 5]. Stated limitations: the Hill estimator "can exhibit high variance in small-sample regimes (N<50)", and multi-modal reward distributions confound it [Section 6]. Experiments use N up to 1,024, drawn from pools of 4,096 per prompt, on LLMs only [Section 5]. Ensembles are named as future work "to mitigate heavy-tailed errors at the source" [Section 6].
