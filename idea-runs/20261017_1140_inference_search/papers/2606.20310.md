# Through the PRISM: Preference Representation in Intermediate States of Video Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
PRISM is a latent video reward model that keeps a pretrained video DiT frozen, reads features from its first 12 blocks, and maps them to a timestep-conditioned preference reward through a query-based aggregation head with about 21.8M trainable parameters. It is trained on about 26k human preference pairs. Because it scores noisy latents directly, it enables best-of-N selection after a few denoising steps without VAE decoding. It is evaluated on VideoGen-RewardBench, a new VLRM-Bench, and N=5 BoN on VBench prompts with CogVideoX-2B and Wan2.1-1.3B.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How early can candidates be selected on Wan2.1-1.3B with a latent reward, and what does later selection add?
With N=5, selecting at Step 1 cuts the normalized time to 19% of the VideoScore2-BoN baseline on Wan2.1-1.3B (10% on CogVideoX-2B, where step-1 selection is close to Random); "slightly later selection already surpasses the strongest pixel-space baseline on each generator", and "selecting later yields only marginal gains on Wan2.1-1.3B and none on CogVideoX-2B" [full text, Section IV-D2, Fig 6]. Selection is a one-shot top-1 at a fixed step; no uncertainty is modelled. The Random baseline gains about 0.3 VBench points at N=5, which the authors call sampling variance, so differences of that size "should be interpreted with caution"; on Wan2.1-1.3B, VideoReward and UnifiedReward fall below Random [Section IV-C2, Table II].

### Goal: What dominates latency in pixel-space video best-of-N?
Pixel-space video reward models "incur substantial costs in all three stages": full denoising of all N candidates, VAE decoding of every candidate, and VLM reward inference (pronounced for VideoScore2) [full text, Section IV-D2, Fig 7]. PRISM decodes only the selected output. Limitation: the reward head is tied to the generator's VAE latent space [Section V].
