# Stitched Value Model for Diffusion Alignment (StitchVM)

## What this paper does
StitchVM turns a pretrained pixel-space reward model into a value model for noisy latents by stitching a truncated frozen diffusion backbone (head) to a truncated reward model (tail) through a linear-initialised stitching layer chosen by closed-form feature matching, then briefly fine-tuning on forward-noised unlabeled images to regress the clean reward. The value model replaces Tweedie or Monte Carlo estimates in DPS, FK steering (best-of-several local proposals per particle), and training-time methods (DiffusionNFT, direct reward fine-tuning), on SD3.5-M/L and FLUX with CLIP, aesthetic and HPSv2 rewards, at ~10 GPU-hours per stitch.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
V_omega(z_t)=r_phi^{>=j}(s_psi(u_theta^{<=i}(z_t))) [full text, Section 4.1 Eq 6], fine-tuned with L=E||V(z_t)-r_phi(z_0)||^2 where z_t ~ p_t(z_t|z_0) is FORWARD-noised from clean (data) images [Eq 8]. The minimiser is E[r|z_t] under the data posterior p_{0|t} ∝ p_t(z_t|z_0)p_0(z_0) [Appendix A.4, Proposition 1]; the authors note off-policy and on-policy objectives coincide only "when the diffusion policy is exact" [Section 4.1, footnote 2].

### Goal: Does the method represent the spread/uncertainty of the final reward given x_t?
No. It deliberately regresses the standard value (mean) rather than the soft value "as this resulted in more stable training" [Section 4.1]. Appendix A.4 derives V^(lambda)=lambda*mu_t + (lambda^2/2) Var[r|z_t] + O(lambda^3) [Eq 54-55], i.e. the soft value used by tilted/selection targets contains a conditional-variance term that the mean regressor drops; they argue the two "match to leading order" in lambda.

### Goal: What limitation does it state or show that bears on intermediate reward estimation for pruning/resampling?
Applies only to feedforward reward models; non-feedforward rewards would need surrogate training [full text, Appendix F]. Each new (backbone, reward) pair needs a stitch search and fine-tuning (6-31 GPU-h on GH200) [Section 5.4, Table 4]. Timestep-aware training is left to future work [Appendix F].
