# Inference-Time Scaling of Diffusion Models via Progressive Seed Pruning (PSP)

## What this paper does
PSP relaxes the constant-memory constraint of particle methods: it starts many seeds, scores the Tweedie/flow denoised estimate x̂0 with a black-box reward at a few predetermined steps, and keeps the top-k_t survivors (default: geometric halving, two prune points, 2x the seeds of BoN at the same NFE), fully denoising only the survivors. It needs no stochasticity. On SD1.5/SDXL/SD3.5 with ImageReward or HPS guidance it beats BoN, FK steering and tree search on GenEval and a human study at matched compute, and allows offline schedule tuning from cached deterministic trajectories.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
s_t = r(x̂0(x_t,t,c)), computed from the noise or velocity prediction already produced by the generator, so scoring costs only reward-model calls [full text, Section 3.1 "Intermediate Reward Estimates", Eq 3].

### Goal: How are intermediate estimates turned into prune decisions, and is that rule adapted per prompt or per step from measured reliability?
Hard TopK on the scalar s_t at "a small set of predetermined steps" with fixed survivor counts [full text, Algorithm 1; Section 3.2 "Pruning intervals"]. The default halving schedule is motivated by compute matching; a grid search over schedules per (model, reward) on a held-out prompt set gives "little to no improvement" over the default [full text, Section 4.6, Fig 4; Appendix S5]. No per-prompt adaptation: the same schedule is applied to every prompt, chosen for predictable memory/runtime.

### Goal: What does it report about the reliability of intermediate estimates, and does the method represent uncertainty of the final reward?
Pooled Pearson correlation between intermediate and final reward rises quickly; probability that the final-best seed survives a K->K/2 prune is ~80% when pruning from 8 at 25% progress and ~90% from 4 at 50% [full text, Appendix S1, Fig S1]. Thus ~1 in 5 prompts loses its best seed at the first prune. The method keeps only the scalar score; nothing models how uncertain s_t is for a given candidate or prompt.

### Goal: What limitation does it state or show that bears on intermediate reward estimation for pruning/resampling?
Gains shrink for objectives decided late in the trajectory (aesthetics/HPS) because early pruning cannot see them [full text, Section 5 Discussion]. Resampling is avoided because with deterministic samplers "resampling collapses to repeated children" [Section 5]. Beyond a high-IR regime, guidance-reward gains translate weakly to GenEval [Appendix S2].
