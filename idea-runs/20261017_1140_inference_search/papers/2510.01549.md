# MIRA: Towards Mitigating Reward Hacking in Inference-Time Alignment of T2I Diffusion Models

## What this paper does
MIRA studies reward hacking in inference-time noise optimization (DNO-style gradient updates to the initial / injected noises of SDv1.5 and SDXL). It argues noise-space norm constraints are insufficient because tiny noise changes can produce very different images, and adds an image-space regulariser: a score-based upper-bound surrogate of KL(p(x0|z) || p(x0|z0)) between the image distribution induced by the optimised noise and by the original noise. MIRA-DPO extends this to black-box rewards via a DPO-style pairwise loss whose implicit reward is the same score-based log-ratio. Evaluation uses GPT-4o / human head-to-head win rates, CLIPScore and CMMD drift.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to stop the search from exploiting (reward-hacking) the verifier?
Loss L = -r(x0,c) + beta * KL[p(x0|z,c) || p(x0|z0,c)], with the KL replaced by the tractable surrogate sum_t sigma_t^2 (||s(x_t|z0,c)||^2 - ||s(x_t|z,c)||^2) computed from the frozen diffusion model's scores along the trajectory [full text, Section 4 / Eq 3-4; Algorithm 1]. The anchor is the trajectory of the *unoptimised* noise z0 of the same run. Hacking is framed as "distributional drift": reward models are trained on base-model images and become unreliable on out-of-distribution samples, so keeping outputs in-distribution is the defence [full text, Section 1 "The problem of reward hacking"; Section 5.1, Fig 6, CMMD 0.281 for DNO vs 0.063 for MIRA].

### Goal: How is the regularisation strength set, and is it reward- or sample-adaptive?
beta is a single global scalar hand-set per reward: 0.2 (Aesthetic), 0.5 (HPSv2, PickScore), 1 (brightness), 0.8 (darkness), 1 (JPEG) [full text, Appendix A.1]. The paper states that low beta reward-hacks and high beta over-constrains/under-optimises, and beta is tuned by sweeping the reward-vs-CLIPScore/win-rate trade-off, i.e. against held-out signals [full text, Section 5.4; Appendix A.5 Fig 4]. Nothing in the method adapts beta to where the verifier is or is not trustworthy for a given sample.

### Goal: What does it report as its limitation, and how does it compare with sampling/SMC search?
Limitations: extra compute, surrogate may degrade on complex/OOD prompts, tightness of the surrogate unanalysed, combination with sampling methods left to future work [full text, Section 6]. Under matched wall-clock on SDv1.5, MIRA wins 57.8% vs FK steering and 53.3% vs Best-of-50 on Aesthetic, but only 37.8% vs DAS (8 particles) on Aesthetic, while winning >73% on the artificial brightness/darkness rewards [full text, Section 5.4, Table 2]. A MIRA+FK hybrid is only a feasibility probe [full text, Appendix A.4]. The regulariser is gradient-based (needs differentiable reward and backprop through sampling) for the main variant.
