# LatticeSMC: Where to Spend Inference-Time Compute in Chunked Sequence Generators

## What this paper does
LatticeSMC models inference-time steering of chunk-by-chunk diffusion/flow generators (music-to-dance, text-to-music) as one Feynman-Kac model on the lattice of chunk index and denoising step. Two telescoping results show that chunk-additive rewards give identical weights on both axes and that prefix scores of a terminal reward are exact intermediate potentials. The sampler resamples on these exact potentials with ESS gating and returns a weighted draw or the best particle. It also analyses when learned lookahead (twists) can help, under matched denoiser, decoder and reward-call budgets.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does it find about learned predictors of future reward used to rank particles within a set?
A twist can only help to the extent that the prefix determines the future reward within a set of particles for the same condition, because any condition-level component cancels in the normalized weights [full text, Section 5.1, Eq 3]. A learned regressor with cross-prompt R²=0.90 has within-set correlation −0.04 with an oracle twist after the first chunk (0.35 after the second) and lowers reward by 0.053 at N=32 relative to using no twist [Section 5.1, Table 4]. Design rule: "validate any estimated twist by its within-set correlation and residual in nats, never by its cross-prompt fit" [Section 5.2]. Settings are small (4M-38M) dance and music generators.

### Goal: How does it set commitment strength, and is greedy top-M pruning worse?
It argues that commitment at each boundary should follow "the information carried by its exact potential, which ESS gating can read off adaptively and greedy pruning cannot" [full text, Section 6]. On the repetition reward, top-M with M=1 commits to an arbitrary lineage when all prefix rewards are tied at zero, whereas the ESS gate keeps the set intact [Appendix C.5]. On beat alignment, however, the greediest setting M=1 is best (0.473 vs 0.415 at M=8) [Appendix C.5, Table 7], so the benefit of adaptive commitment depends on the reward.
