# From Curiosity to Caution: Mitigating Reward Hacking for Best-of-N with Pessimism

## What this paper does
"Caution" is a per-response pessimistic reward for Best-of-N in LLMs. A lightweight predictor network is trained offline (MSE) to reproduce layer-L hidden features of the frozen reward model on typical responses sampled from the base policy; at inference its prediction error alpha(x,y) is an uncertainty/OOD score and selection uses r_LCB = r_hat - lambda*alpha. On GSM8K (and transfer to MATH-500, BigBench-Hard) with Llama-3.2-3B and OASST/Skywork RMs, plain BoN rises then falls with N while caution keeps improving up to N=512. A linear-setting theorem shows a sqrt(log N) gain over BoN.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to stop selection from exploiting the reward model, and what does the penalty actually measure?
Pessimism at the reward level rather than the distribution level: r_LCB(x,y) = r_hat(x,y) - lambda*||P_theta(x,y) - T(x,y)||^2 with T the frozen RM's layer-L features and P_theta trained on base-policy samples [full text, Section 2.2 "Implementing Caution", "Pessimistic Reward Estimation"]. The penalty measures how atypical a response is relative to the predictor's training distribution (samples y ~ pi(.|x) from the benchmark train split), used "as an efficient empirical proxy for the training distribution of the reward model" [full text, Section 2.2 "Training Process"]. It is not a direct estimate of the RM's error on that response; the case study shows it flags a verbose, wrongly formatted answer as OOD [full text, Section 3.3, Fig 4]. Distilling RM features beats RND with random targets at every lambda [full text, Section 3.2, Table 2].

### Goal: What does it need, where is it applied, and what are its limits?
It needs a trained predictor per task/prompt distribution (trained on GSM8K, then tested OOD on MATH-500/BBH) and a mixing weight lambda (0.6-0.8 best; "larger lambda being required in situations where r_hat is less reliable") [full text, Section 2.2; Section 3.2 "Strength of regularization"]. It is applied only to the final argmax of independent BoN candidates; there is no intermediate resampling or sequential search [full text, Section 2.1]. All experiments are LLM text with binary correctness as r* [full text, Section 3 "Experimental Setup"]. It contrasts itself with chi^2-regularised BoN (Huang et al.), which is monotone but "overly conservative in practice" [full text, Section 1; Section 4 "Related Work"].
