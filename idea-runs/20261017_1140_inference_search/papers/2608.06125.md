# Sample-Adaptive Latent Rewards for Uncertainty-Guided Diffusion Post-Training (SURE)

## What this paper does
SURE trains a latent reward model (SURE-LRM) on the generator's own DiT backbone that, for each noisy latent, predicts a mean reward and a candidate-specific residual variance, learned from pairwise preferences with a heteroscedastic probit likelihood plus a log-variance regulariser, margin and anchor terms. SURE-REFL then fine-tunes image and video diffusion models with transition-local reward feedback, weighting each sample's reward by a detached inverse-standard-deviation reliability normalised across samples at the same transition. Reported: better pairwise accuracy than DiNa-LRM and top VBench scores.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it provide a per-candidate estimate of verifier reliability, and how is it obtained?
Yes, but only for its own specially trained reward model: f_theta(z_sigma, c, t) outputs (mu, a), residual variance softplus(a)+eps, total variance v0(sigma)+v_res [full text, Section 3.2, Eq 3-5], learned from preference labels via Phi(m/sqrt(S)) [Eq 6-8]. A single pair constrains only the variance sum; candidate-level structure comes from the shared variance head [full text, Section 3.2, after Eq 11]. It cannot attach a reliability to an arbitrary frozen off-the-shelf verifier (HPS, VLM judge) without retraining such a model.

### Goal: How good is the uncertainty, and is it validated under the distribution shift that search or optimisation induces?
Incorrect rankings have 24.26% higher residual variance; AUROC 0.6941 for detecting ranking errors; keeping the lowest-variance half raises pairwise accuracy 79.40% -> 90.10%; the authors stress these pairs were seen in training, so it is "an in-distribution diagnostic rather than held-out calibration" [full text, Section 4.2 "Uncertainty reliability"]. Future work: "uncertainty calibration under distribution shift" [full text, Section 5].

### Goal: Is it used for inference-time search?
No. The reliability is used only as detached weights rho = V^{-1/2} in post-training gradients of the generator (SURE-REFL), i.e. with weight updates [full text, Section 3.3, Eq 14-19].


### Goal: Does the method represent uncertainty of rewards at noisy latents, and what uncertainty is it?
Yes: v = v_0(sigma) + softplus(a_theta) [full text, Section 3.2, Eq 3-5], learned only from pairwise preference labels through Phi(m/sqrt(S)) [Eq 6-8]. It is uncertainty about the preference ordering of a labelled pair, and "a single pair constrains only the variance sum S, so candidate-level variance is not independently identified by one label" [Section 3.2]. On (in-distribution) pairs, variance flags ranking errors with AUROC 0.694 and keeping the lowest-variance half raises pairwise accuracy from 79.4% to 90.1% [Section 4.2 "Uncertainty reliability"].

### Goal: How is the uncertainty used, and does it drive inference-time pruning or resampling?
Only for training: SURE-REFL normalises detached variances across samples at the same transition into bounded weights on local reward-feedback gradients [full text, Section 3.3]. There is no inference-time search, pruning or resampling; the generator's weights are updated.

### Goal: What limitation bears on intermediate reward estimation for pruning/resampling?
The authors call for examining "uncertainty calibration under distribution shift"; their reliability evidence is in-distribution, not held-out calibration [Section 4.2; Section 5]. The model is tied to the generator backbone and to preference datasets.
