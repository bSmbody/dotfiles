# Catastrophic Goodhart: regularizing RLHF with KL divergence does not mitigate heavy-tailed reward misspecification

## What this paper does
Kwa, Thomas and Garriga-Alonso model a proxy reward as U = V + X, where V is true utility and X is the error. They prove that when X is heavy-tailed, both KL-regularised optimisation and plain conditioning on a high proxy can yield no gain in V ("catastrophic Goodhart"). When X is light-tailed and independent of V, harder optimisation yields unbounded utility. They also check the tails of reward distributions from open LLM reward models.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: When does selecting on a proxy stop improving true quality, as a function of the error distribution?
Theorem 5 covers conditioning on high proxy values. If X and V are independent, X is subexponential, and V's tail is sufficiently lighter than X's, then lim_{t→∞} E[V | X+V ≥ t] = E[V]. In that case selection on the proxy buys nothing in the limit [full text, Section 3.4.1]. Theorem 6 covers the light-tailed case: if X's tail decays faster than e^{-cx}, then E[V | X+V > t] → ∞ [Section 3.4.2]. The authors frame proxy optimisation as "a mix of desirable optimization for V and undesirable optimization for X", where too much variance in X "redirect[s] the optimization pressure" [Section 2.3]. Whether harder selection helps therefore depends on the tail of the error relative to the tail of quality. The proxy's own score distribution does not reveal this.

### Goal: What do the results assume, and what evidence is there about real reward models?
Theorems 4–6 require X ⊥ V, "which seems to be violated in practice" [Section 5.2]. A footnote gives a counterexample: errors that are light-tailed but depend on V (X|V ~ N(0,4) for V in [−1,1], else 0) still give a limit of 0 [Section 5.2]. Empirically, Starling-7B and Pythia reward distributions on sampled text look roughly normal, which "does not imply light-tailed errors but suggests it" [Sections 1, 4.1]. Searches strong enough to probe the extreme tail were not run [Section 5.3]. All experiments are on LLM text; no images are studied.
