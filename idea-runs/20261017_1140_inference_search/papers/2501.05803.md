# Test-time Alignment of Diffusion Models without Reward Over-optimization (DAS)

## What this paper does
DAS frames test-time alignment as sampling from the KL-regularised target p_tar(x) ∝ p_pre(x) exp(r(x)/alpha) rather than maximising r, and implements it with Sequential Monte Carlo over the diffusion trajectory. Intermediate targets are tempered approximate posteriors p_t(x_t) exp(lambda_t r(x̂0(x_t))/alpha) with lambda_t rising from 0 to 1, and the proposal is the gradient-guided (locally optimal) Gaussian kernel. It claims high target reward with preserved diversity and cross-reward generalisation on SDv1.5/SDXL (aesthetic, PickScore, CLIPScore, compressibility), Pareto-optimal multi-objective trade-offs, and online black-box optimisation.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to prevent over-optimisation / verifier hacking during search?
Two mechanisms: (1) the target itself is KL-regularised, p_tar ∝ p_pre exp(r/alpha), so the sampler aims at a tilted distribution rather than argmax r [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 2-3]; (2) tempering lambda_t=(1+gamma)^t-1 down-weights early x̂0-based rewards that are noisy at high noise [full text, Section 3.3.2, Eq 11]. Ablation: without tempering SMC "suffers from over-optimization, even with 32 particles, resulting in low ImageReward", while with tempering 4-8 particles give high aesthetic and ImageReward [full text, Section 4.1.2, Fig 5].

### Goal: How is the strength of regularisation chosen, and does it depend on the verifier's reliability on a given sample?
alpha is a single scalar per reward, "scaled to reward magnitude": 0.01 for aesthetic, 1e-4 for PickScore, 0.005 for multi-objective; chosen by grid search over {1e-1..1e-4} then a finer grid, guided by matching approximate-guidance norm to classifier-guidance norm [full text, Appendix D]. The proxy reward r is treated as the correct objective inside the tilt; no term reflects whether r is reliable on a particular particle.

### Goal: Does held-out (unseen) reward keep improving as compute grows, and how is multi-reward handled?
Unseen-reward evaluation uses HPSv2/ImageReward when targeting aesthetic/PickScore [full text, Section 4.1.1]. In the test-time scaling study (1-128 particles), DAS beats Best-of-N and untempered SMC on both PickScore and HPSv2 [full text, Appendix E, Fig 8]. Multi-objective uses a fixed weighted sum w*Aesthetic + (1-w)*20*CLIPScore swept by hand [full text, Section 4.2, Eq 15]. Non-differentiable rewards need a learned differentiable surrogate because the proposal uses reward gradients [full text, Section 4.3].
