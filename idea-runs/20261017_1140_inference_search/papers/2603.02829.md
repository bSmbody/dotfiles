# Toward Early Quality Assessment of Text-to-Image Diffusion Models (Probe-Select)

## What this paper does
Probe-Select attaches a lightweight probe (tiny encoder + MLP) to selected intermediate denoiser activations at an early checkpoint (e.g. 20% of steps) and trains it to predict an external evaluator's score of the final image, using a listwise ranking loss plus an InfoNCE text-alignment loss. At inference it runs N seeds to the checkpoint, scores them, and continues only the top K. Across SD2, SD3-M/L and FLUX.1-dev it reports Spearman correlations that are already high at t=0.2 and about 64% sampling-time savings with better retained-image quality.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
E_phi(h_t,t) predicts the final evaluator score R_m(x_1) from denoiser activations h_t [full text, Section 3.1 Eq 4; Section 3.2], trained with a listwise softmax ranking loss within a batch and a CLIP-text InfoNCE term [Section 3.3, Eq 5-7]. A separate probe is trained per (backbone, evaluator) and per checkpoint [Section 5.3].

### Goal: What does it report about reliability, and how does it compare with scoring the decoded x̂0?
Spearman correlation with the final metric is near 1.0 for ImageReward/BLIP-ITM on SD2 and lower (about 0.65-0.86, e.g. HPSv2.1 0.78 and PickScore 0.86 on FLUX) for other metrics, with texture-sensitive metrics (CLIPScore, HPS) that "become reliable only later" [full text, Section 5.3, Table 1]. Decoding x̂0 at t=0.2 and applying BLIP-ITM directly gives Spearman ~0.52 versus >0.9 for the probe, because evaluators "are not designed to assess the potential of blurry or partially denoised outputs" [Appendix 8.5].

### Goal: How are estimates turned into prune decisions, and is uncertainty represented?
One checkpoint, fixed N and K (e.g. N=5, K=1; best trade-off K=1-3 with N≈50), hard top-K on the scalar prediction [full text, Section 3.4; Section 5.4, Fig 5]. The probe outputs a single score; no confidence or per-prompt adaptation of t or K. Future work mentions dynamic timestep control and closed-loop active sampling [Section 6].
