# Lookahead Sample Reward Guidance for Test-Time Scaling of Diffusion Models (LiDAR)

## What this paper does
LiDAR rewrites the expected future reward (EFR) at x_t as an expectation over MARGINAL samples x0 ~ p_theta(x0|c) weighted by the forward kernel p(x_t|x0), so that the EFR and its gradient need no backpropagation and no per-timestep rollouts. It generates n cheap lookahead samples with a few-step solver (or distilled model), annotates them with the reward, and guides the target sampler with a closed-form Stein-score correction that attracts particles to high-reward lookahead samples and repels low-reward ones. It matches the GenEval of the latest gradient guidance on SDXL at 9.5x speed and composes with SMC and best-of-N.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
Empirical lookahead reward r^_t(x_t)=log (1/n) sum_i [p(x_t|x̂0^i)/((1/n) sum_j p(x_t|x̂0^j))] exp(lambda r(x̂0^i)) with x̂0^i from a delta-step lookahead sampler [full text, Section 3.1 Theorem 3.1 / Eq 11; Section 3.3 Eq 15]. It contrasts this with backward rollout (Eq 6, unbiased but costly) and the Tweedie/Taylor approximation r(E[x0|x_t]) used by gradient guidance and SMC (Eq 7), whose error "increases proportionally with lambda" [Section 2.2.2; Appendix A.6, Fig 8].

### Goal: What does it report about reliability of intermediate estimates and about Tweedie-based SMC versus best-of-N?
The TV gap to the reward-tilted target is bounded by O(1/sqrt(delta)) in lookahead steps and shrinks with lookahead sample count n [full text, Section 3.4, Theorems 3.4-3.5] — a weak-to-strong mismatch because the lookahead sampler q differs from p_theta (Eq 12-14). SMC with Tweedie rewards "tends to collapse to a single particle" [Section 2.2.3, Fig 2]. In Table 8 (SD v1.5, ImageReward), FK-style SMC is below best-of-N at every N: 0.568 vs 0.739 at N=4 and 1.221 vs 1.346 at N=64 [full text, Section 4.3, Table 8].

### Goal: Does the method represent the spread of the final reward given x_t, and what limitation bears on pruning/resampling?
It estimates a soft value (log-mean-exp) only; lambda is fixed very large (5000) after an ablation, slightly reducing diversity [Section 4.2]. The kernel-weighted estimator is a guidance signal for a single trajectory, not a prune/resample criterion; its use with SMC/BoN is "orthogonal" composition [Section 4.3]. Discrete extension treats tokens independently, a stated limitation [Section 4.2].
