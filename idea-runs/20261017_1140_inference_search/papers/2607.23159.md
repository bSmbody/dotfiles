# CachedSearch: Training-Free Cached Exploration for Test-Time Search in Video Diffusion

## What this paper does
CachedSearch studies whether lossy training-free feature caching preserves the verifier ranking of candidates in video best-of-N search. On Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B with an adaptive caching wrapper (~2x faster per rollout), seed-matched cached and full rollouts scored by ImageReward have median per-prompt Spearman 0.905 and 72% top-1 agreement. The method explores all N candidates with aggressive caching, then re-generates only the winner at full compute (deterministic seed). It keeps 94.7% of best-of-8 gain at 63% cost, transfers across six models, and stacks with mid-trajectory pruning (3.11x exploration speedup at 88.6% capture).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What intermediate/cheap estimator of the final reward does it use, and how reliable is it compared with alternatives?
The estimator is a full but cached rollout of the same seed, scored by the verifier [full text, Section 3.2, Algorithm 1]. At matched ~2x speedup, caching retains 90.1% of search gain whereas step truncation (T=25 instead of 50) retains 72.6% with ~6x more regret and an uncorrelated worst-decile ranking, because a truncated rollout "is an honest sample of a different, shorter-schedule distribution, so its verifier scores rank its own outputs, not the 50-step outputs that commit will deliver" [full text, Section 5.3, Table 4].

### Goal: Where do ranking errors concentrate, and is the decision rule adapted per prompt from measured reliability?
Ranking corruption concentrates on prompts whose candidates are nearly tied (corr(spread, rho)=+0.31), so median regret is 0 [full text, Section 1 Contributions; Section 5.4]. A spread-based per-prompt adaptive cache threshold did not beat a fixed threshold: "its noisy two-sample probe costs more than the flat capture-versus-speedup curve returns" [Appendix B.6 "Scope and limitations"; Section D.3]. Its theory models ranking noise with a Gaussian copula at a single calibrated rank correlation per (model, tau, verifier) triple, measured per prompt offline [Appendix B.1-B.6].

### Goal: What limitation bears on intermediate reward estimation for pruning/resampling?
Each cached exploration still costs about half a full rollout, so it is a costly estimator; mid-trajectory pruning is only composed multiplicatively, not integrated [Section 5.6 "Application"]. Assumption 3 excludes reward hacking [Appendix B.6].
