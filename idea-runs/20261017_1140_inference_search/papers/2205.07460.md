# Diffusion Models for Adversarial Purification (DiffPure)

## What this paper does
DiffPure defends a frozen, pre-existing classifier against adversarial inputs without retraining it: the input is diffused forward with a small amount of noise (timestep t*) by a pretrained diffusion model and then denoised back with the reverse SDE, and the purified image is classified. A theorem shows that forward diffusion monotonically shrinks the KL divergence between clean and adversarial distributions, and a second bound shows that semantic drift grows with t*, giving a sweet spot. It reports state-of-the-art robust accuracy on CIFAR-10, ImageNet and CelebA-HQ against adaptive and unseen attacks, across ResNet, WideResNet and ViT classifiers.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does diffuse-then-denoise with a small t* preserve and what does it remove, and why?
Forward diffusion "gradually remove[s] the local structures of data by adding noise", and adversarial perturbations, "a form of small local structures added to the data", are smoothed with them [full text, Section 3.1]. Theorem 3.1: dKL(p_t || q_t)/dt <= 0 for clean p and adversarial q, so perturbations are "washed out"; the reverse SDE then "stochastically recover[s] the clean data" [Section 3.1, Eq 3-4]. Theorem 3.2 bounds the distance between the clean image and the purified one by ||eps_a|| plus terms that grow with gamma(t*), so a small t* keeps global structure [Section 3.1]. Fig 2 shows attribute-classifier attacks removed at t*=0.3 with faces preserved.

### Goal: How does the choice of t* trade off removing perturbations against preserving semantics, and is it universal?
Standard accuracy "monotonically decreases with t*" while robust accuracy "first increase[s] and then decrease[s]" [full text, Section 5.5, Fig 3]. The optimal t* "remains small but also varies across different threat models" (l_inf 0.075, l_2 0.1, StAdv 0.15): stronger perturbations need larger t* [Section 5.5]. Randomising t* in a narrow band can help slightly; a wide band hurts [Appendix C.4, Table 13]. So the purification strength is a tunable knob without a single correct value.

### Goal: Does it depend on the attack or on the classifier?
No retraining and no assumption on the attack form: the generative model is "trained independently from both threat models and classifiers", so it can "defend pre-existing classifiers against unseen threats in a plug-n-play manner" [full text, Section 1]. It gives large gains against unseen threat models (up to +36% robust accuracy) [Section 1; Section 5.3]. Stochastic reverse SDE sampling matters: the deterministic ODE variant is less robust, which the authors link to randomized smoothing [Section 5.5, "Impact of sampling strategy"].
