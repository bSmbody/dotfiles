# Reward Model Ensembles Help Mitigate Overoptimization

## What this paper does
Coste et al. train ensembles of five proxy reward models in the synthetic-gold setup of Gao et al. (Pythia policy and proxies, a 7B AlpacaFarm gold reward model). They optimise against each ensemble with best-of-n and with PPO, combining the members by Mean, Worst-Case Optimisation (WCO, the minimum) or Uncertainty-Weighted Optimisation (UWO, the mean minus λ times the variance). Ensembles, especially WCO and UWO, reduce overoptimisation and improve the gold score over a single reward model.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are ensemble members combined, and what controls the degree of conservatism?
The three rules are R_μ (the mean), R_WCO = min_i R_i, and R_UWO = mean − λ·intra-ensemble variance, with λ a hyperparameter [full text, Section 3, Eq 3-5]. WCO "does not have any hyperparameters" but "can sometimes result in a performance penalty due to its highly conservative nature" [Section 3]. The members are trained "using identical data and hyperparameters but with different random seeds" [Section 4.3]. They are therefore exchangeable: no rule gives a member its own reliability, and no rule decides which member is wrong on a particular response.

### Goal: Do the ensembles remove best-of-n overoptimisation, and how sensitive is that to the knobs?
For BoN, "we do not observe any overoptimization when using WCO and UWO", up to n = 12,500 (about 8.4 nats). Mean still overoptimises under 25% label noise [Section 5, Fig 3]. The gains reach about 30% (no label noise) and about 75% (25% label noise) over single reward models [Section 5]. For UWO, λ = 0.5 works best and "many reasonable values" work well [Sections 5.1, 5.4]. The setting is LLM text, with a synthetic gold model and same-architecture, same-data ensemble members. BoN evaluation cost about 700 A100 hours [Section 4.3].
