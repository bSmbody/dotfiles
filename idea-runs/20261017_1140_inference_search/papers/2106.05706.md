# Outlier-Robust Filtering For Nonlinear Systems With Selective Observations Rejection

## What this paper does
Chughtai, Tahir and Uppal build a Kalman-type filter for a dynamical system that is observed by several independent sensors, any of which may emit a corrupted reading at any time step. Each sensor reading gets its own Bernoulli indicator (trusted or outlier), and these indicators are inferred jointly with the state by variational Bayes. When a reading is judged an outlier, its Kalman gain goes to zero. Only that sensor's reading is discarded, and the other sensors still update the state. The method is presented as tuning-free (a neutral prior θ=0.5 and ε≈0). It is compared with VB filters that share one outlier parameter across all measurement dimensions, in simulation and in UWB indoor-localisation experiments.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism lets the filter reject one sensor's corrupted reading while keeping the others, and how is the per-reading trust inferred?
In the measurement model, each dimension's noise covariance is R^ii/I^i, where the indicator I^i is either ε (outlier) or 1 (trusted) [full text, Section II, Eq 3-6]. VB alternates between q(x) and q(I). Each q(I^i) is Bernoulli with Ω^i = 1/(1+√ε(1/θ−1)·exp(W^ii(1−ε)/(2R^ii))), where W^ii is sensor i's expected squared residual against the current state posterior [Section III-B, Eq 15-20]. When Ω^i → 0, "the ith column of K_k approaches zero leading to the rejection of the corresponding ith measurement", and Ω^i = 1 recovers the standard filter [Section III-A]. Trust is therefore set per sensor and per time step. It depends on how far that sensor's reading lies from the state implied by the prediction and the other sensors, measured in units of that sensor's nominal noise.

### Goal: What must be known or assumed, and is there a tuned rejection threshold?
The assumptions are independent sensors, diagonal nominal noise R that is "predetermined" (known), and outliers that occur independently across sensors and over time [Section II]. The authors call threshold-based rejection "mostly subjective" and present their filter as "learning-based tuning-free" [Section I], with θ set to a neutral 0.5 and ε≈1e-6 [Section III-C]. When ε is drawn from U(1e-7,1e-3) and θ from U(0.05,0.95), the filter is "quite robust ... except at large values of λ", i.e. at very high outlier frequency [Section IV-C, Fig 6]. A reading is judged against a reference, the dynamics prediction combined with the other sensors. Rejection therefore needs both an informative reference and known nominal noise scales.

### Goal: What does per-sensor (selective) treatment buy over a shared outlier parameter, and does it handle biased rather than random corruption?
Methods that treat outliers separately in each dimension reach comparable, lower RMSE. The original shared-parameter VB filters show "a sharper rise" in error as the outlier rate grows [Section IV-C, Figs 2-5]. With many sensors and an outlier rate λ=0.9, the selective filters still give low error [Fig 7]. In UWB experiments, some anchors suffer non-line-of-sight bias, a sporadic systematic offset in specific sensors. There the proposed filter has the lowest RMSE and run time, while one baseline "severely degrades" [Section IV-D, Table II]. So the rejection also handles biased corruption in a subset of sensors, not only symmetric noise.
