# A General Framework for Inference-time Scaling and Steering of Diffusion Models (FK steering)

## What this paper does
FK steering samples k interacting diffusion particles and resamples them at intermediate steps with potentials G_t built from intermediate rewards, constrained so that prod_t G_t = exp(lambda r(x0)), i.e. the target is p_theta(x0) exp(lambda r(x0)). Potentials: difference, max, sum; intermediate rewards: r at the Tweedie x̂0, many-sample log-mean-exp, or a learned r_phi. With ImageReward as reward and k=2-8 particles, SDv1.5/2.1/SDXL improve held-out GenEval and beat DPO/DDPO fine-tuned models and best-of-4.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What controls how hard the verifier is optimised, and is there any explicit safeguard against verifier hacking?
The only knobs are the tilt strength lambda, the potential type, the resampling schedule and adaptive (ESS-based) resampling [full text, Section 3.3 Eq 5, "Choosing the potential"; Appendix C.3]. Higher lambda raises human-preference scores but lowers particle diversity; the max potential gives better GenEval than difference/sum but lower diversity because it favours high-scoring particles more [full text, Section 4.2, Table 5; Appendix A Tables 9-10]. The reward r itself is treated as the correct terminal potential; there is no term reflecting verifier reliability.

### Goal: How is held-out quality measured and what limitation is stated about rewards?
Held-out GenEval (prompt fidelity) is reported alongside the optimised ImageReward and HPS, mostly at k<=8 particles; FK beats best-of-4 on both [full text, Section 4.1 Table 1-2; Fig 4]. Stated limitation: "reliance on the availability of strong reward functions" and the need to "better understand the limits of inference-time particle scaling" [full text, Section 5]. The paper notes ideal intermediate rewards could use the distribution of r(x0) given x_t, e.g. low-variance or 10th-percentile choices, but this needs training [full text, Section 3.3 "Choosing intermediate rewards"].
