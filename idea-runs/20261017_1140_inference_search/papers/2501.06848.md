# A General Framework for Inference-time Scaling and Steering of Diffusion Models (FK steering)

## What this paper does
FK steering samples k interacting diffusion particles and resamples them at intermediate steps with potentials G_t built from intermediate rewards, constrained so that prod_t G_t = exp(lambda r(x0)), i.e. the target is p_theta(x0) exp(lambda r(x0)). Potentials: difference, max, sum; intermediate rewards: r at the Tweedie x̂0, many-sample log-mean-exp, or a learned r_phi. With ImageReward as reward and k=2-8 particles, SDv1.5/2.1/SDXL improve held-out GenEval and beat DPO/DDPO fine-tuned models and best-of-4.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What controls how hard the verifier is optimised, and is there any explicit safeguard against verifier hacking?
The only knobs are the tilt strength lambda, the potential type, the resampling schedule and adaptive (ESS-based) resampling [full text, Section 3.3 Eq 5, "Choosing the potential"; Appendix C.3]. Higher lambda raises human-preference scores but lowers particle diversity; the max potential gives better GenEval than difference/sum but lower diversity because it favours high-scoring particles more [full text, Section 4.2, Table 5; Appendix A Tables 9-10]. The reward r itself is treated as the correct terminal potential; there is no term reflecting verifier reliability.

### Goal: How is held-out quality measured and what limitation is stated about rewards?
Held-out GenEval (prompt fidelity) is reported alongside the optimised ImageReward and HPS, mostly at k<=8 particles; FK beats best-of-4 on both [full text, Section 4.1 Table 1-2; Fig 4]. Stated limitation: "reliance on the availability of strong reward functions" and the need to "better understand the limits of inference-time particle scaling" [full text, Section 5]. The paper notes ideal intermediate rewards could use the distribution of r(x0) given x_t, e.g. low-variance or 10th-percentile choices, but this needs training [full text, Section 3.3 "Choosing intermediate rewards"].


### Goal: What intermediate reward/value estimator does it use for partial trajectories, and how is it computed?
Three options [full text, Section 3.3 "Choosing intermediate rewards"]: (1) reward at expected x0, r_phi(x_t)=r(x̂0(x_t)) via Tweedie; (2) many-sample r_phi=log (1/N) sum_j exp(r(x0^{i,j})) with x0^{i,j} ~ p_theta(x0|x_t), i.e. extra rollouts; (3) learned r_phi trained by regressing exp(r(x0)) on x_t ~ q(x_t|x0) with x0 from the DATA distribution [Eq 8], whose minimiser equals log E[exp r | x_t] only "when p_theta = q". Text-to-image experiments use option (1) with the max potential, lambda=10, k=4 and resampling at steps [0,20,40,60,80] [full text, Section 4.1 / 4.2].

### Goal: Does the method represent the spread of the final reward given x_t, and how does it turn estimates into resampling decisions?
The paper states that "the ideal rewards for the intermediate state x_t would require knowing the distribution of rewards of x0 generated from x_t: p_theta(r(x0)|x_t)", and that with it one could pick high expected reward "when reward variance is low, or based on the 10th percentile" — but notes this "requires training with samples from the model" and implements only the scalar options above [full text, Section 3.3]. Decisions are multinomial resampling on exp(lambda * potential) with a single global lambda and a hand-fixed resampling schedule; adaptive resampling skips a step only based on ESS of the same scalar potentials [full text, Appendix C.3]. Higher lambda and the max potential raise reward but cut particle diversity [full text, Section 4.2; Appendix D, Tables 9-10].

### Goal: What limitation does it state or show that bears on intermediate reward estimation for pruning/resampling?
Fig 5 shows r(x̂0) correlates only partially with r(x0) for T2I models; on ImageNet, learned rewards raise class probability from 0.59-0.65 (x̂0 reward) to 0.88 [full text, Section 4.3, Table 7]. The authors frame "better rewards vs more particles" as an unresolved compute trade-off: better intermediate rewards need training or extra reward/rollout calls [full text, Section 4.3 "Better Rewards vs. More Particles"]. Future work names adaptive particle allocation [full text, Section 5].
