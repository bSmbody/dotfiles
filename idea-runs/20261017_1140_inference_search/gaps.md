
## Axis: verifier reward hacking under inference-time search

### What has been done
- **Tilt the target toward the base model with one fixed temperature (particle / SMC search).** DAS [2501.05803] samples the KL-regularised target p_pre·exp(r/α) with SMC. Its tempering λ_t down-weights early x̂0-based rewards, and without tempering SMC over-optimises even at 32 particles (ImageReward falls). α is grid-searched per reward. FK steering [2501.06848] resamples with potentials whose product is exp(λr). Raising λ or using the max potential increases preference scores but reduces particle diversity. Held-out GenEval improves at k ≤ 8 particles. Both methods treat r as the correct terminal potential.
- **Constrain the output distribution during noise optimisation.** MIRA [2510.01549] shows that noise-norm constraints do not stop drift. It instead penalises a score-based surrogate of KL(p(x0|z) ‖ p(x0|z0)) against the trajectory of the unoptimised noise. Its weight β is hand-set per reward (0.2–1): low β reward-hacks and high β under-optimises. CARINOX [2509.17458] uses a χ-norm likelihood regulariser on the initial noise (γ = 0.01) plus gradient clipping.
- **Make the sampler faithful to the tilt of the given verifier.** [2606.02884] traces within-mode hacking to the finite-particle plug-in Doob-h estimator, which overshoots the mean and collapses covariance. It fixes this with a closed-form reward-damping schedule and uses best-of-n for mode selection. CS-VGB [2610.15281] alternates renoising and denoising so that its stationary law equals r·π0 even when intermediate verifiers are wrong. It assumes the terminal verifier is exact. Both papers measure success as fidelity to the tilt of the proxy.
- **Search locality and multi-verifier aggregation.** [2501.09732] names verifier hacking: under DINO/CLIP-logit search, FID worsens and recall drops; Aesthetic search lowers CLIPScore and the reverse also holds. Zero-order neighbourhood search and search over paths reduce the problem, but it returns when the neighbourhood is enlarged. Its Verifier Ensemble selects by the unweighted average rank of Aesthetic, CLIPScore and ImageReward. CARINOX [2509.17458] sums, with weight 1 each, four rewards chosen offline by correlation with human labels. DAS [2501.05803] uses a fixed, hand-swept weighted sum.
- **Hedge or pessimise selection (LLM best-of-N, plus one visual RM).** HedgeTune [2506.19248] proves that the true reward is unimodal in the selection strength. It finds a single hacking threshold θ with gold-reward calibration. E_M-regularised BoN [2603.05739] selects uniformly from the top N/M candidates, which is monotone in N. M is a tuned global scalar, and its regret term M·ε_pw assumes the verifier is accurate only on typical samples. Caution [2604.04648] subtracts a per-response penalty from the reward, namely the error of a network distilled on reward-model features of typical base samples, and applies it only at the final argmax. SURE [2608.06125] learns a per-latent reward variance from preference data but uses it only to weight fine-tuning gradients.

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Verifier-guided search over diffusion/flow trajectories (best-of-N, SMC/FK resampling, noise optimisation) limits verifier exploitation with one trust level that does not depend on the candidate and is fixed before search. That trust level is the tilt temperature α/λ of p_base·exp(r/α) [2501.05803][2501.06848], the image-space KL weight β [2510.01549], or the selection strength n/θ/M [2506.19248][2603.05739]. This assumes the frozen verifier's error is the same on every prompt and every candidate. As a result, the search has no signal for when a higher verifier score stops meaning higher true quality, and the trust level can only be tuned offline against a held-out or gold scorer that is not available at inference.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2501.05803] — DAS is the closest image-side method. Its tempered SMC toward a KL-regularised target beats best-of-N and untempered SMC on unseen HPSv2 from 1 to 128 particles. However, α is grid-searched per reward (0.01 for aesthetic, 1e-4 for PickScore), its tempering addresses x̂0 noise rather than verifier error, and untempered SMC over-optimises even at 32 particles. [2510.01549] — MIRA's image-space drift penalty keeps measured drift near zero (CMMD 0.063 vs 0.281 for DNO). But β is hand-set per reward and tuned against CLIPScore and win rate, and its own sweep shows that low β hacks while high β under-optimises. [2506.19248] — HedgeTune locates the hacking threshold, but only with gold-reward calibration, and it returns one θ averaged over prompts. [2603.05739] — this BoN variant is monotone in N, but M is a tuned global scalar and its bound assumes the verifier is accurate only on typical base samples. [2604.04648] — Caution is the only method I read that applies a per-candidate penalty. It is LLM-only, is trained per task on base-policy samples, measures atypicality rather than verifier error, and acts only at the final best-of-N argmax. [2608.06125] — SURE has a per-latent reward variance, but only for its own specially trained latent RM. It is validated only in-distribution (AUROC 0.69 on training pairs) and is used for fine-tuning, not search. [2606.02884] and [2610.15281] make sampling more faithful to the tilt of the given verifier: one through damping, the other through an exact r·π0 stationary law that assumes the terminal verifier is exact. With a flawed verifier, more compute therefore moves samples closer to the flawed tilt. [2501.06848] — FK steering treats r as the correct terminal potential and names its "reliance on strong reward functions" as a limitation. All ten papers I read share the same deficiency: trust in the verifier is a scalar fixed before search. Queries checked: "reward hacking diffusion", "inference-time alignment diffusion reward over-optimization", "reward hacking best-of-N", "imperfect verifier inference-time scaling", and the post-2025-06 recency queries "pessimistic reward image generation", "reward ensemble disagreement diffusion" and "uncertainty-aware reward test-time scaling image" (0 hits). None returned a diffusion/flow search method that scores verifier reliability per candidate.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Missing mechanism, a credit-assignment flaw in the resampling and selection weights. These weights depend only on the raw verifier score (exp(r̂/α), argmax r̂, the top quantile of r̂). The only protection is a global distance-to-base term, which applies the same discount to a candidate whose score comes from real quality and to one whose score comes from verifier error on that candidate. The digests support this. MIRA's β sweep shows that no single β both avoids hacking and reaches high reward [2510.01549]. DAS needs a per-reward grid search over α [2501.05803]. HedgeTune's optimal operating point is reachable only with gold-reward access [2506.19248]. The one per-candidate signal, Caution, estimates distance from typical base samples [2604.04648]. Its mechanism would therefore also penalise rare candidates that are genuinely better, and those are exactly what search is meant to find.
- **Why it matters:** The failure this problem describes — the search's own verifier score rises at 4× compute while held-out preference and GenEval stay flat — is what a fixed trust level produces when the verifier is unreliable on the candidates the search selects. Without a per-candidate reliability signal, α/β/n must be retuned for each verifier and model against held-out scorers. Compute-efficient search then cannot be carried over to new verifiers (VLM judges, HPSv3, VideoAlign) or to video, where held-out calibration is costly. Closing this gap would show whether extra inference compute can turn into held-out gains that rise monotonically. In other words, it would tell whether search-scaling plateaus come from the generator or from how much the verifier is trusted.
- **Evidence route:** At matched generator NFE plus verifier calls on a model of ≤ 3B parameters, compare against DAS/FK with tuned α, MIRA with tuned β, best-of-N, and best-of-N with a HedgeTune-style tuned n. Three observations would show the gap is closed. (i) Held-out preference and GenEval/T2I-CompBench keep rising with compute beyond the point where the fixed-trust baselines plateau, using the same hyperparameters across at least two different search verifiers. (ii) The difference between the gain on the optimised verifier and the gain on held-out scorers stays bounded as compute grows. (iii) On candidates the search selects, the per-candidate reliability signal predicts rank flips between the proxy and held-out scorers (AUROC) better than typicality or drift scores such as Caution-style distillation error or MIRA's KL surrogate.

- **Gap:** When several frozen verifiers are used to resist single-verifier hacking in diffusion search, prior methods combine them with rules that do not depend on the candidate and let a gain on one verifier offset losses on the others. These rules are the unweighted average rank of Aesthetic, CLIPScore and ImageReward [2501.09732], a uniform sum of four rewards chosen by human correlation [2509.17458], or a hand-swept weighted sum [2501.05803]. They assume the verifiers' errors are independent and cancel. Over many candidates, the argmax can therefore still be won by a candidate that exploits one verifier's bias while the other verifiers rank it lower.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2501.09732] — introduces its Verifier Ensemble specifically "to alleviate overfitting to a single verifier", and the ensemble helps on DrawBench. On T2I-CompBench, however, "ImageReward outperforms Verifier Ensemble across all evaluation categories", which the authors attribute to the Aesthetic member. Separately, Aesthetic-only search lowers CLIPScore and the reverse also holds. The averaged rank thus inherits a member's exploitable bias. [2509.17458] — CARINOX fixes its verifier set offline by Spearman correlation with T2I-CompBench++ human labels and sums the scores with weight 1. Its only adaptive variant, per-category correlation weights that need ground-truth category labels, changes the mean by at most 0.0053. The aggregation never responds to which verifier the current candidate pool is exploiting. [2501.05803] — multi-objective DAS uses the fixed sum w·Aesthetic + (1−w)·20·CLIPScore with w swept by hand. [2604.04648] scores each candidate but uses a single reward model, not evidence from several verifiers. Queries checked: "multiple reward models inference-time diffusion", "multi-verifier test-time scaling image generation", "verifier ensemble image generation inference-time" and "reward ensemble disagreement diffusion". Among the papers I read and the titles returned, none conditions the aggregation on whether the verifiers agree on each candidate.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Wrong inductive bias in the aggregation rule. Averaging ranks or summing scores lets one member's gain compensate for the others, and the rule is the same for every candidate. A large exploited gain on one verifier therefore cancels out disagreement from the others, and the combined score is maximised by the same selection pressure that hacks a single verifier. The digests support this. In [2501.09732], the ensemble scores below its own best member (ImageReward) in every T2I-CompBench category because of its Aesthetic member. In [2509.17458], the only adaptivity is category-level weights set offline from benchmark labels, and it has a negligible effect.
- **Why it matters:** Combining several verifiers is the default practical defence against hacking, and this problem treats preference models, VLM judges and alignment scorers as interchangeable verifiers. If the most exploitable member can hijack the aggregate, then adding verifiers costs verifier calls without buying robustness. The verifier set must also be retuned for each benchmark, as both [2501.09732] and [2509.17458] did. Closing this gap would show whether disagreement among verifiers that are already available carries enough information to keep search on candidates that held-out judges also prefer.
- **Evidence route:** Use a fixed pool of at least three frozen verifiers and a separate held-out set (a held-out preference model plus GenEval/T2I-CompBench), and compare against rank-average and uniform-sum aggregation at matched compute. Three observations would show the gap is closed. (i) The aggregate matches or beats its best single member in every category, reversing the T2I-CompBench result of [2501.09732]. (ii) Held-out scores do not drop when a deliberately hackable verifier (Aesthetic, a brightness score) is added to the pool. (iii) As compute grows, the share of selected candidates whose win comes from a single verifier falls.
