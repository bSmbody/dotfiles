# Safety Guardrails for LLM-Enabled Robots (RoboGuard)

## What this paper does
RoboGuard is a two-stage guardrail for LLM-enabled robots: a root-of-trust LLM, shielded from user input, grounds designer-specified safety rules (e.g. "do not harm humans") into context-specific LTL specifications using the robot's world model (semantic graph); a control-synthesis module checks the LLM planner's proposed plan against the LTL specification and synthesizes the closest compliant plan. Evaluated against RoboPAIR attacks in simulation and on a Clearpath Jackal.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism enforces safety and what does it assume?
Safety reasoning happens once per context in the root-of-trust LLM (rules + world model -> LTL), then plan-level formal checking/synthesis; safety is contingent on the correctness of that specification [full text, Sec III-A/III-B, Theorem III.2]. Design desiderata: contextual awareness, applicability across planners, utility, efficiency [Sec III].

### Goal: What limitations does it report?
(1) requires an accurate world model — a compromised world model induces safety failures and needs perception safeguards; (2) scaling to larger world models and rule sets; (3) LTL may be inappropriate when system dynamics are critical to safety [Sec V].
