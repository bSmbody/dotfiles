# Safe Embodied AI for Long-horizon Tasks: A Cross-layer Analysis of Robotic Manipulation

## What this paper does
63-page survey organizing safety for long-horizon manipulation by intervention locus (planning-time, policy-time, execution-time, contact-level) and by strength of evidence (formal guarantee, statistical support, empirical heuristic).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What execution-time safety mechanisms exist and what do they miss?
Runtime monitoring/anomaly detection (state-level scores, VLA-internal features + conformal thresholds, VLM-generated spatio-temporal constraint checks), diagnosis (taxonomies, foundation-model reasoning), shielding (control-theoretic CBF/reachability filters; learned/latent/semantic shields incl. VLM-formulated CBFs for VLAs), and runtime policy steering (verifier-based or representation steering) [full text, Sec 5.1]. Formal shields depend on explicit physical models; learned/semantic shields operate in an empirical regime [Sec 5.1.3].

### Goal: Which open problems does it name?
Safety information is lost at abstraction boundaries (instruction -> symbolic goal -> trajectory -> contact) [Sec 7.1.1]; no principled mapping from calibrated risk signals to intervention type [Sec 7.1.4]; no trace-level record of hazard onset and mitigation [Sec 7.1.5]; semantic guardrails should move from static text-image filtering to dynamic, long-horizon risk taxonomies during execution and active elicitation [Sec 7.2.1].
