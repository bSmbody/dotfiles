# RT-Safe: Benchmarking Agent Safety in Real-Time Embodied Environment

## What this paper does
Simulated (Unreal Engine) urban navigation benchmark where the world keeps evolving during VLM inference and action execution; compares static (paused) and real-time modes across eight VLM agents and trains a Qwen3.5-4B offline-RL policy (IQL-style expectile + CQL regularizer, LoRA) to reduce collisions.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What safety mechanism or training does it use, and what does pausing the world hide?
Checked against the body (reusing an earlier-run digest after verification): task completion is similar in static vs real-time hard settings (91.3% vs 94.1%) while safe success falls from 19.8% to 0.7% [full text, Sec 1/3.3]. Higher reasoning effort does not consistently improve safety [Sec 3.4]. As a mitigation it trains an offline-RL policy on expert demonstrations (plus RL-generated data), which lowers collision rate several-fold relative to the prompted VLM under a fixed 3 s decision delay [Sec 3.5]. Navigation only; safety is collision/hazard/traffic-rule events, not instruction-level harm.

# RT-Safe: Benchmarking Agent Safety in Real-Time Embodied Environment

## What this paper does
A simulated urban navigation benchmark (Unreal Engine) in which the world keeps evolving during VLM inference and action execution. Compares matched static (paused-during-inference) and real-time modes across eight VLM agents and three difficulties (36 routes each), varies reasoning effort, and trains offline-RL policies under a fixed 3 s decision delay to reduce collisions.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How do paused and real-time protocols differ in outcome, and how is latency charged?
In real-time mode the simulator "resumes immediately before the blocking planning call and is paused after that call returns"; the measured planning interval (model-call wall time plus agent-side processing) is charged as "modeled inference exposure"; static mode keeps the world paused throughout planning [full text, App A, lines on real-time mode]. Hard setting: task completion 91.3% static vs 94.1% real-time, but safe success 19.8%→0.7% and collisions 3.31→40.68 per episode (12.3x); passive contacts during inference ≈84% of real-time collisions; active collisions also rise 3.31→6.38, "consistent with actions becoming less suitable as the scene changes between observation and execution"; model rankings change between modes [Sec 3.3]. Mean response latency explains 78.4% of collision variance; latency x decisions explains 93.4% [Sec 3.2].

### Goal: Does any agent-side mechanism compensate for deliberation latency?
No mechanism in the evaluated VLM agents: the only adaptation is a mode-specific prompt telling the agent whether the world pauses [Sec 2.1; App A.2]. Raising reasoning effort above provider default raises collisions 40.7→62.0 without improving completion (94.1%→94.8%); Fable's latency rises 5.8→51.9 s for 1.69→1.88 m progress per decision [Sec 3.4]. RT-Safe evaluates VLMs "without a separate reactive policy that revises their selected actions"; related VLA-level delay methods (real-time chunking, AsyncVLA, Real-Time EXPO-FT) are cited but not applied to frontier-model agents [Related Work]. Offline RL reduces collisions per 100 m by 8.9x vs prompting but uses a fixed 3 s delay [Sec 4].

### Goal: Relevance to manipulation?
Navigation only, discrete actions; establishes that paused-world evaluation hides latency-induced failures and re-orders models.
