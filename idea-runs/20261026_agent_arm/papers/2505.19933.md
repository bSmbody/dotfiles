# Subtle Risks, Critical Failures: A Framework for Diagnosing Physical Safety of LLMs for Embodied Decision Making

## What this paper does
SAFEL evaluation framework plus EmbodyGuard, a PDDL-grounded benchmark of 942 LLM-generated, symbolically and manually verified household scenarios split into explicitly malicious (Mal) and situationally hazardous benign (Sit) instructions. Tests: Command Refusal; Plan Safety decomposed into goal interpretation, transition modeling (risky effects / safe preconditions) and action sequencing checked by a symbolic PDDL executor. 13 LLMs (Llama-3, Qwen-2.5, R1-distilled, GPT-4o, o1, o1-mini).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Do LLMs that refuse malicious commands also execute benign-but-hazardous tasks safely?
Refusal recall on Mal is 82.8–99.1% (except Llama-3.2-1B 63.3%); all models accept Sit instructions (100%) [full text, Sec 4.1–4.2]. But Sit action sequencing success is low: small models ~0%, 70B models ~10–30%, o1 best at 44.75% with ER >50% [Sec 4.2].

### Goal: What is the dominant failure mechanism for side effects of benign instructions?
Missing-step errors — omitting the required safety-preserving action (e.g. removing wrapping before toasting): 34.00 of 55.25 points of error for o1, 33.25/58.25 for GPT-4o, 29.50/63.75 for R1-Llama-70B; models are worse on unary safety predicates (slippery, killed) than relational ones; reasoning models overthink transition effects [Sec 4.1–4.2]. Authors conclude models have limited capacity to reason about and enforce safety-preserving preconditions, diverging from refusal-test performance [Sec 4.2, Sec 6].

### Goal: Limitations / scope?
Evaluates LLMs on symbolic PDDL, not agents acting with perception; future work suggested: RL to improve goal understanding, transition modeling and execution [Sec 7, App B].
