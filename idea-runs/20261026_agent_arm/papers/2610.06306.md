# Inspect Robots: Evaluating the Capabilities and Safety of Embodied AI

## What this paper does
An open-source, modular framework (extending Inspect AI to physical robots) for specifying and running capability and safety evaluations of embodied agents: tasks with scenes, instructions, multiple rubrics, time budgets and trial counts; policies and policy adapters that expose robot-control tools (`move_joints`, `move_to`) and termination tools (`done`, `give_up`) to language models; an action manager plus composable guards; embodiments; and records/record editors/analyzers. Demonstrated on a 4-task RoboDojo capability mini-benchmark and a 4-task RoboHarm safety mini-benchmark on a real I2RT YAM bimanual robot, 20 trials per model per task.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does the framework sequence model inference and robot motion (does the robot wait while the model thinks)?
Each tool call generates an action sequence; "whenever an action completes, the action manager decides whether to execute the next action in the sequence or rerun the policy with the latest observations" [full text, Sec 3, Action Manager and Guard]. Inference is therefore invoked between completed actions; the framework does not describe executing or forecasting during inference. Evaluating each additional model took "3–8 hours, depending on model latency" [Sec 4].

### Goal: How is success judged and how are costs reported?
A web-based record editor lets human evaluators assess each trial's record; analyzers produce mean score vs mean cost with 95% bootstrap CIs [Sec 4, Fig 2]. Guards clip actions to operating limits and limit motion per action, motivated by Astra's unsafe actions that damaged hardware in prior work [Sec 3].

### Goal: What do the demonstration results show?
Astra (M) outscores GPT-5.6 Sol (M) by 33 points; Opus 5.5 (M) beats Opus 5 (M) by 24 points [Sec 4]. On RoboHarm, most models refuse the doll-stabbing instruction in most trials but no model refuses the other three hazardous tasks more than 5% of the time [Sec 4].

# Inspect Robots: Evaluating the Capabilities and Safety of Embodied AI

## What this paper does
Workshop paper (SPAIS@CoRL 2026) introducing an open-source evaluation framework (tasks with multiple rubrics, policies, policy adapters exposing control and termination tools, action managers, guards, embodiments, records/analyzers). Demonstrates a 4-task RoboDojo capability mini-benchmark and a 4-task RoboHarm safety mini-benchmark (doll, burner, toaster, brick; 20 trials each) on a YAM bimanual robot.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What runtime safety mechanisms does the harness provide?
Guards review each action before execution and may modify or reject it; the common guards clip parameters to operating limits and limit per-action motion magnitude; termination tools (done, give_up) are described as letting a model refuse [full text, Sec 3]. No guard reasons about the semantic harm of an action; guards are hardware-protection filters.

### Goal: What safety result does it report?
Astra, Fable, Opus 5.5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash refuse to stab the human-shaped doll in the majority of trials, but no model refused any other task (burner, toaster, brick) more than 5% of the time [Sec 4].
