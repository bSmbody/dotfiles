# Beyond Binary Success: Sample-Efficient and Statistically Rigorous Robot Policy Comparison

## What this paper does
N-SCORE: a sequential, safe anytime-valid (SAVI) test for comparing the mean of a bounded progress metric (binary success, discrete partial credit, continuous reward) between two robot policies, stopping as soon as evidence suffices while controlling Type-1 error. Validated on simulated data, LBM 1.0 (simulation and hardware) and RoboArena; reports up to 70% fewer trials than batch tests and up to 50% fewer than binary sequential procedures (STEP).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does the method model, and what does it assume about evaluation data?
Each policy's outcome is "compressed to a (possibly complicated) distribution over the performance measure D_R"; the test compares the means of two such distributions [full text, Sec III, Eq 1]. Quality is measured by α, β and expected number of trials E[N] [Sec IV-B–C]. SAVI methods "rely crucially on i.i.d. evaluation data", and guarantees "are only meaningful if the evaluation paradigm is similarly rigorous and reproducible" [Sec VII]. Stratified (exchangeable, not i.i.d.) sampling is argued to be practically negligible when contexts are few relative to N [Supplement].

### Goal: How does it handle many configurations, cost per trial and small gaps?
Multi-policy comparison splits α by Bonferroni across pairs (e.g. six pairs at α=0.0083 each); efficient multi-test allocation is future work [Supplement, RoboArena p-value figures]. Cost is counted in trials, not money or time [Sec IV-B]. With ~10-point gaps and binary metrics, both N-SCORE and WSR "fail to distinguish policies within 641 trials" in two RoboArena comparisons; a ~20-point binary gap needs ~80 trials [Supplement]. Partial credit separates policies faster than binary success [Sec VI-B4, VI-C].

### Goal: Does it address budget-dependent outcomes or run-level variation of agent systems?
No. The policy is a fixed map with a fixed outcome distribution; there is no notion of an interaction budget, of outcome as a function of budget, or of correlated outcomes within a run/session/library [Sec III–V].
