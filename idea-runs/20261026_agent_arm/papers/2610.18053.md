# LADDER: A Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation Benchmark

## What this paper does
LADDER is a long-horizon manipulation benchmark (SimplerEnv-Long, RobotArena-∞-Long, RoboLab-Long; 145 tasks, 1–10 subgoals, two embodiments, 2,024 simulated episodes per system) with programmatic per-subgoal predicates. It evaluates low-level policies (π0.5 etc.) alone and under orchestrators (VoLo text subgoals; tool-selection systems CaP-X, GPT-Policy on GPT-6 Astra, PSP-Agent, TiPToP). Single-subgoal success is 85.4% but collapses with horizon; orchestration reaches ~25%; GPT-Policy on Astra ~62% at high time and cost.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the simulator pause while the high-level model computes, and why do they choose that?
"The budget counts simulated time only: the simulation pauses while a system computes" [full text, Sec 3]; "time spent computing, for example waiting for a VLM, does not use the budget" [App G, Robot time]. They do not compare wall-clock time because "the same π0.5 policy ... takes 51–56 ms per step on an RTX PRO 6000 but 130–177 ms on an A100, several runs share one GPU, and VLM requests wait in queues shared with other jobs" [App G, Why not wall-clock time] — i.e. measured latency is not reproducible across hardware and API queues, so the protocol removes it.

### Goal: How much does privileged simulator state help the orchestrator, and how good is a non-privileged verifier?
A privileged verifier "reads the simulator's success predicate for the current subgoal" and loses 0.0 points of subgoals to orchestrator decisions [App B; App H, Table 18]; it exceeds the Proprioceptive verifier by 4.5 [2.6, 6.6] and VoLo by 4.2 [2.3, 6.3] points [App F]. The online VLM verifier has 88.6% precision and 61.8% recall against simulator ground truth [App H]. Without verification, 32.6–43.4% of subgoals fail because the orchestrator moves on before completion [Sec 5.2].

### Goal: How are lost subgoals attributed, and is any attribution used by the system online?
Post-hoc only: each episode is reconstructed from logged object poses, per-subgoal predicates, gripper state and orchestrator events; every lost subgoal gets the first applicable of twelve causes; a loss is drawn as an orchestrator cause "when one made the loss final" [full text, App H]. The first cause checked is "Undone later: the subgoal was done at some step but not at the end" (picked up again, knocked off by a collision, slid/rolled off) [App H]. Nothing is fed back to the orchestrator.
### Goal: How does the orchestrator handle an action that succeeds locally but disturbs a previously satisfied or later subgoal?
By hand-written prompt heuristics in PSP-Agent: order goals "so that anything placed on an open surface, where a later reach could knock it off, comes after anything dropped into a container", and do not re-issue a goal that took, because "repeating a goal that took sends the arm back over it and knocks it over" [App C, PSP-Agent planning / attempted goals prompts]. On RoboLab-Long, "Orchestrated configurations undo more of their completed subgoals" [App H, RoboLab-Long paragraph].
### Goal: How large is the orchestrator share of failures compared with execution failures?
Without verification 32.6-43.4% of subgoals are lost because the orchestrator moves on before completion; with verification this falls to 3.4% and orchestrator-related failures to 7.1%, so the low-level policy's failure to manipulate targets dominates; half the failed episodes complete no subgoal after the first 28% of budget [Sec 5.2].
