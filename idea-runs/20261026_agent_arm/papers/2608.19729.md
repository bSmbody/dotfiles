# SafeBranch: Branch-Pair Safety Alignment for Embodied Agents

## What this paper does
Findings EMNLP 2026. Trains a VLM-based embodied planner (actor) for "interactive safety" (hazards that emerge as the agent acts, e.g. leaving a burner on). From the actor's own unsafe rollouts, a benchmark BDDL predicate triggers, a VLM critic localizes the responsible safety-critical step and gives feedback, the environment is rolled back exactly to that step, the actor samples a safe alternative under the feedback, then the feedback is removed to form a same-context branch pair (y+, y-); BranchPO (step-level preference loss) aligns the actor, which then runs without a critic. Evaluated on IS-Bench, SafetyALFRED and OOD variants.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is its credit-assignment mechanism for safety and what does it assume?
Safety is treated as a step-level outcome decided at sparse safety-critical steps; the objective maximizes log pi(y+|h_safe) - log pi(y-|h_safe) [full text, Sec 3.1–3.2]. It argues SFT on safe trajectories lacks the rejected alternative and trajectory-level DPO scatters the signal across many differing steps [Sec 1, Sec 4.1]. Assumes simulator-provided safety predicates and exact state restoration; actions are high-level planner outputs in household simulators [Sec 5.1, Limitations].

### Goal: What limitations does it report?
Critic still needed at training time; critic can mislocalize the step or give an ineffective repair; "hazards that require longer causal reasoning remain a limitation"; no real-robot transfer; irreversible hazards need simulation or pre-execution intervention [Limitations].
