# RoboReward: General-Purpose Vision-Language Reward Models for Robotics

## What this paper does
Builds a robot reward dataset/benchmark from Open X-Embodiment and RoboArena with discrete 1–5 end-state progress scores, augments success-heavy data with synthetic negatives, benchmarks 22 VLMs on RoboRewardBench (2,831 human-verified episodes), trains RoboReward 4B/8B, and uses the 8B model as reward for real-robot RL.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are failure / near-miss examples for training the judge constructed?
"Because OXE is success-heavy and lacks failure examples", negatives come from (i) counterfactual relabeling — a VLM describes a successful video and LLM calls propose alternative instructions for which "the same video would result in only partial success or failure", and (ii) negative clipping of successful videos to earlier endpoints [full text, Abstract, Sec 4.2]. These generate negatives "without fabricating new videos" [Sec 4.2]; physical failures appear only via RoboArena rollouts, and "RoboArena failures alone are insufficient for broad generalization" [Sec 5.3].

### Goal: How reliable are frontier VLMs and the trained model as judges, and what limitation is stated?
"No model excels across tasks"; large non-uniform gaps across embodiments and views (GPT-5 MAE 0.31 on Austin Sirius vs 1.37 on Tokyo PR2) [Sec 5.1]. RoboReward-8B ranks best overall but is not uniformly best [Sec 5.1]. Future work: longer-horizon, multi-stage tasks "where credit assignment and progress estimation become more challenging" [Sec 6].
