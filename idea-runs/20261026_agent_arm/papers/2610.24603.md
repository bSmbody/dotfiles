# Refuse the Harm, Complete the Task via Graph-of-Rules Planning for LLM-Enabled Robots

## What this paper does
RuleGraph: a five-agent, training-free pipeline (Instruction, Rule Generator, Goal Synthesis, Planning, Verification agents; GPT-4o) for "safe task completion" — refuse the harmful sub-task of a compound instruction while completing the legitimate part. Rules are grounded from general principles into the symbolic world; one planning node per rule runs generate–judge–refine; plans are aggregated pairwise up a graph until one plan satisfies all rules. Evaluated on the RoboGuard benchmark (70 RoboPAIR attacks) and a new 97-instruction Compound set.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Where and on what representation is the safety decision made?
Entirely before execution, over a natural-language instruction plus a symbolic world state w (semantic graph) and a discrete skill set A; plans are action sequences a_t in A checked against preconditions in w and LLM-judged rule compliance; a final gate checks goal reach and residual malicious intent [full text, Sec 3.1–3.4]. No perception, no continuous control, no monitoring during execution.

### Goal: What do the results and analysis say about single-stage safety decisions?
ASR 0% and TSR 98.57% / 84.54% on RoboGuard / Compound; baselines (single-plan judge, RoboGuard, SafePlan) keep 2.9–14.4% ASR and fall to 16–22% TSR on Compound [Sec 4.2, Table 1]. Every harmful plan passing RoboGuard traced back to an incorrect LLM-generated LTL specification; SafePlan's later checkpoints only verify against a specification produced without safety filtering [Sec 4.2]. Adaptive white-box attack raises baseline ASR (RoboGuard 10%) but not RuleGraph [Table 2].

### Goal: What limitations does it state?
Safety judgments are made by an LLM, not formally verified; adversarial robustness beyond tested settings unknown [Sec 5]. Implicitly limited to symbolic plans given a known world model.
