# MG-VQA: Manipulation Grounded Visual Question Answering with VLMs

## What this paper does
Defines manipulation-grounded VQA: a VLM agent must answer spatial questions about a cluttered tabletop where the evidence is occluded, using manipulation (grasp, push) as an intermediate evidence-gathering operation. MG-VQA-Bench (600 human-verified questions, four tasks) evaluates eight VLMs under Direct (image), Perception (pointing, segmentation, scene graphs) and Manipulation tool access.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does acting to gather evidence improve VLM judgments about physical state, and how do models decide when to stop?
Direct and Perception average 36.2% and 37.6% (image-blind chance 32.7%); Manipulation raises the average to 56.8%; GPT-6 Astra 83.0%, Gemini 3.8 Flash 69.3%; stronger models "search persistently and re-ground after unsuccessful interactions, while weaker models often answer without gathering sufficient evidence or stop prematurely after failed actions" [full text, Abstract, Sec 4.2]. Stopping is left to the model's own judgment within the tool loop [Sec 3.3, 4.2].

### Goal: Is it applied to verifying task/step success?
No: questions concern scene contents (count, find, beneath, compare), not whether an executed manipulation step achieved its goal; tool APIs are a fixed discrete library on a tabletop [Sec 3.1, Limitations].
