# The Bitter Lesson Comes to Robotics

## What this paper does
Evaluates nine frontier multimodal LMs in fifteen (model, reasoning-effort/service-tier) configurations as direct end-effector controllers of a real bimanual I2RT YAM robot on six RoboDojo tasks (20 trials per task per configuration), via the Inspect Robots framework with `move_to`/`done`/`give_up` tools, a 40-action budget and two joint-configuration guards. Scores cumulative subtask progress, API cost per subtask and subtasks per hour; fits a METR-style log-linear trend to record-setting configurations and reports a 53.9-day performance doubling time (95% bootstrap CI 36.1–59.5 days).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Under what protocol is the agent evaluated (world paused during inference? episodes per task, success judge)?
Closed loop: the model selects an action, the framework executes it and appends observations collected during execution; up to 40 actions or a hardware fault [full text, Sec 3]. On real hardware the world does not pause, but tasks are static scenes, so inference time shows up only as wall-clock cost and as a physical side-effect: Gemini 4 Argon's "high reasoning latency can leave the robot's arms in positions that likely require high joint torques ... for extended periods of time, occasionally causing an arm to overheat and terminating the trial early" [Sec 5]. Each trial "is graded by an instance of GPT-6 Astra (H) based on the footage"; a trial gets the highest subtask reached "at some point during the trial, even if the model undoes its progress" [Sec 3]. 20 trials per task per configuration; uncertainty by task-stratified bootstrap [Sec 4].

### Goal: How strongly do results depend on reasoning effort, service tier, budget and model version?
Configurations differ materially: GPT-6 Astra (H+UF) beat Astra (XH) and GPT-6.1 Sol (H) beat (H+UF) by 1.9 points each [Sec 4]. The doubling-time CI is wide because Gemini 4 Argon beats Opus 5.5 (XH) by 0.13 points; it is a record setter in only 45% of bootstrap replicates; leave-one-out estimates range 44.3–61.1 days [Sec 4]. Scores "reflect how much each model can accomplish with a limited action budget, rather than what it can achieve given unlimited time"; raising the budget "would almost certainly improve completion rates" [Sec 4].

### Goal: Which costs are counted?
API cost per subtask (from uncached/cached/output tokens) and subtasks per wall-clock hour including execution and inference time; these "account for the cost and time associated with unsuccessful attempts" [Sec 5]. No single model dominates: Astra (H+UF) 1.1 min/subtask at $4.51/subtask; Gemini 3.8 Flash $0.38 but 4.18 min/subtask [Sec 5]. Rate trends for time and cost rest on only four and three record-setting points and are "exploratory" [Sec 5].

# The Bitter Lesson Comes to Robotics

## What this paper does

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is its safety / dual-use framing, and does it contain any safety method or measurement?
The abstract and introduction frame tracking these capabilities as important for benefits (industrial automation) and risks "such as enabling malicious users to produce dangerous chemical or biological agents" [full text, Abstract, Sec 1]. The body is a capability study only: no safety tasks, refusal measurement or safety mechanism. Physical failure notes relevant to safety: unrecoverable errors such as launching a pen lid out of reach, and Gemini 4 Argon's long reasoning latency leaving arms in high-torque poses that occasionally overheat and terminate trials [Sec 4, Sec 5].

# The Bitter Lesson Comes to Robotics

## What this paper does
Workshop paper evaluating nine frontier multimodal LMs (15 model/configuration pairs) as direct controllers of a real bimanual I2RT YAM robot on six RoboDojo tasks, 20 trials each, through Inspect Robots with move_to/done/give_up tools and a 40-action budget. Reports subtask-based task score, subtasks per hour and API cost per subtask, and fits trend lines over July-October 2026.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How fast are frontier-model robot cost and speed improving?
Performance doubling time 53.9 days (95% CI 36.1-59.5) over July-October 2026 [full text, Sec 4]. Best subtasks/hour rose 12.4 -> 54.4 (4.4x in 57 days); best API cost per subtask fell $2.36 -> $0.38 (6.2x in 55 days); fitted cost-per-subtask halving time 23.2 days, labelled exploratory (three record points) [Sec 5].

### Goal: What trade-offs between cost, speed and performance exist today?
No model dominates: GPT-6 Astra (H+UF) completes a subtask every 1.1 min but costs $4.51 per subtask; Gemini 3.8 Flash (H) costs $0.38 but takes 4.18 min; Gemini 4 Argon (H+PR) has the top score (48.4%) at $0.97 but completes 7.1 subtasks/hour, and its reasoning latency leaves arms holding poses long enough to overheat [Sec 5].

### Goal: What separates strong from weak models as controllers?
Efficiency and physical verification: weaker models close the gripper on nothing 59-64% of the time vs 32-39% for stronger ones; on Stack Bowls only stronger models "consistently performed this check" (lift to verify a pinch grasp), while weaker ones "misinterpreted the gripper reading and wasted actions regrasping" [Sec 4]. Strong models' low scores usually come from unrecoverable errors [Sec 4].
