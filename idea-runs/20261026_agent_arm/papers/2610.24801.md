# When Better Policies Leave Harder Failures: Coverage Shift in VLM Success Monitoring

## What this paper does
Studies a fixed VLM success judge while a scripted-controller policy improves (ManiSkill3 Franka, cube stack and cube-in-tray). Truth-filtered refitting raises success 66.8%→97.0% and reduces accepted hovers 5.0%→0.9% per execution, but the judge's acceptance conditional on a hover rises 31.1%→50.0% because residual hovers move below the tray rim. Includes a nine-judge, four-view static diagnosis (1,392 images, including GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini), boundary probes, and judge-filtered learning experiments with safeguards.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does the judge decide success and what evidence does it get?
Most VLM judges receive "one final frame and a frozen yes/no question" from a fixed camera; RoboReward-8B gets 16-frame clips [full text, Sec 4.1]. Errors "vary strongly with type and view"; the same physical state is judged differently across views [Sec 4.1, Fig 3]. Cue regressions show "some judges report a grasp yet still accept"; GPT-6 Astra's grasp-cue weight is ≈0 (−.02/−.05) vs ideal −.86/−.57 [Sec 4.1, Table 1].

### Goal: Which failures does the judge miss and why?
Boundary probe: the fixed judge accepts 148/160 below-rim hovers vs 0/160 above-rim hovers [Sec 4.2, Fig 4]. A static error map fails to predict loop failures: "Coverage is a measurement problem" [Sec 4.2, Sec 6]. Learning "changes which failures reach a fixed judge" [Abstract].

### Goal: What safeguards help or hurt when the judge's verdict is consumed?
An extra checking view (second query on another camera after first-view acceptance) gives 93.8–97.4% final success across eight cells (J1/tray/V1: 75.6%→94.0%) "without establishing equal-cost superiority"; a stricter threshold or a grasp veto can harm (J1/stack/V1 91.4%→72.8% / 71.0%): "A stricter acceptance rule can discard useful successes and impair learning" [Sec 5.3, Table 4]. "Efficient choices of states, labels, and additional views remain open" [Sec 6]. Scope: two simulated tasks, scripted low-dimensional policy [Sec 6].
