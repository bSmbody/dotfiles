# FailBench: How Reliable are VLMs at Judging Robot Task Success?

## What this paper does
FailBench pools 2,197 manipulation attempts from 14 public sources (12 real, 2 simulated; most failures natural rather than constructed) under one schema (instruction, visual recording, binary outcome) and evaluates 13 VLM success/failure detectors: general VLMs (Gemini 3 Flash, Gemma-4-31B-it, GPT-4o, Qwen3-VL-Thinking 2B/4B/8B), two embodied VLMs, and five purpose-built detectors (Guardian, RoboReward-8B, ViFailback-8B, RoboFAC-7B, FailSense-3B). It reports balanced accuracy, base-vs-specialist comparisons, a manual error analysis, and a localize-then-crop pipeline.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How reliable are VLM detectors at judging physical success, and which evidence types are hardest?
Best detector Gemini 3 Flash at 0.77 macro balanced accuracy [full text, Sec 4.2, Table 1]. "Detectors are reliable when failure can be inferred from which object moved, but approach chance when success depends on whether physical contact was established; no model exceeds 0.60 balanced accuracy on contact-rich assembly" [Sec 1]. Every robot-failure fine-tune scores below its own base model: "Fine-tuning for failure detection made these models worse at it outside the data they were tuned on" (Guardian 0.627 vs InternVL3-8B 0.629; 0.596 vs 0.639 without its two home subsets) [App B.1, Table 7]. Existing failure datasets mostly perturb successful trajectories or pair a recording with a different instruction, which "can make failure detection partly a matter of recognizing one data-generation procedure" [Sec 1].

### Goal: What systematic error do detectors make, and does more reasoning or cropping fix it?
Manual analysis (Gemma-4-31B-it, not statistically supported): the model names the uncertain point "and then scores success anyway... Failure is treated as what must be proven"; it scores the approach rather than the outcome (bolt "inserted" after being carried to the hole), hallucinates (dropped apple reported in bowl), and "does not reconcile one view against another" (grapes held in one camera, on table in two others) [App B.2, Figs 2–3]. Semantic failures are mostly caught; physical ones (released in wrong place, jaws close on nothing, slip) mostly missed [App B.2]. Wrong answers carry longer reasoning (1,868 vs 1,464 chars) [App B.2]. Cropping to the outcome region: 0.773→0.797 [Sec 4.4], but "it does not teach the detector to infer physical state" [Sec 6].

### Goal: What limitations and future directions does it state for verification methods?
One binary label per completed attempt; no progress during execution, no anticipation [Sec 5]. Vision-only: "four sources record force-torque or audio" but these are unused [Sec 5]. Future work: "models and training objectives explicitly designed to reason about fine-grained spatial and contact relationships" [Sec 6].
