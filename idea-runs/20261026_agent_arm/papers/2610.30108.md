# Multimodal Embodied Agents: A Unified Capability-centric Perspective from Computer-Use to Robot-Use (PAPAV survey)

## What this paper does
A survey that frames multimodal agents, robotic systems and multimodal embodied agents through five capabilities in the task loop — Perceive, Anticipate, Plan, Act, Verify (PAPAV) — compares how each is realized in digital vs. physical settings, and codes 64 benchmarks for which capabilities they explicitly assess.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it define embodied Verify, and how many benchmarks assess it?
Verify "judges intended change, task progress, and constraint satisfaction from post-action evidence"; it requires post-action evidence and "an independently specified account of what should have occurred" [full text, Sec 3.5, 3.5.1]. "Across 64 benchmarks, explicit evaluation centers on Act; only 5 assess Anticipate and 3 assess Verify" [Abstract]; "Checks performed only by external evaluators do not assess Verify" [Sec 4].

### Goal: What does it say a physical verifier must output and what method properties it lacks today?
"Embodied verification remains retrospective, but physical interaction often lacks an independent, fully observable success signal" [Sec 3.5]. A verdict should "distinguish terminal success, intermediate progress, recoverable mismatch, insufficient evidence, an invalid expectation, or persistent risk" and "locate the mismatch", reporting "the failed condition and supporting evidence" rather than correctness alone [Sec 3.5.1]. Physical evidence may be "partial or delayed"; "Timeliness becomes part of correctness"; shared sensors/backbones between actor and verifier "may produce correlated errors", and uncertain verdicts "may become unreliable training targets" [Sec 3.5.1–3.5.2]. It recommends measuring false acceptance, false rejection, calibration and verdict latency [Sec 3.5.3]. It is a survey: no new method or experiment.
