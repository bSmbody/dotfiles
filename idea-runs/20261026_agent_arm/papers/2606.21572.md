# Robot Critics that Sweat the Small Stuff

## What this paper does
Fine-tunes a VLM as a pairwise progress critic ("which of two frames shows more progress") using success-success progress pairs from successful rollouts and success-failure pairs from rollouts of the deployed policy that start from matched initial conditions. At test time it samples K action chunks from a diffusion policy or GR00T N1.5, imagines each chunk's terminal frame with an action-conditioned video model (HunyuanVideo 1.5), and executes the candidate winning most pairwise votes.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How well do off-the-shelf VLMs and progress estimators judge fine-grained success/failure?
"Gemini 3 discriminates success versus failure with only 54.7% accuracy", ROVER 72.1%, ProgressLM 61.0% on eight tasks (chance 50%); they "fail to discriminate between fine-grained progress and failures. A subtle 1mm gripper misalignment is close, but still insufficient" [full text, Sec 1].

### Goal: How is the critic's training data tied to the policy's failures?
Policy rolled out repeatedly from the same initial conditions to obtain paired success/failure trajectories; success-failure pairs (S^f_t, S^s_t) at the same time step are labeled +1 for the success frame; "fine-tuning the VLMs on the successful demonstrations is insufficient as they cannot identify failures without ever having seen failure data" [Sec 3.1, Eq 2]. Fine-tuned critics exceed 90% accuracy; +11% real-world, +5.9% simulation success [Sec 1].

### Goal: What limitations affect it as a physical-state verifier?
It judges imagined (video-model) terminal frames: the video model sometimes hallucinates "grasping the Lego in the predicted video when the real-world rollout fails to grasp"; ~25 s per 5 videos on 8 A100s [Sec 5.3]. Inputs are two RGB frames; the critic is trained once on a fixed policy's rollouts [Sec 3.1].
