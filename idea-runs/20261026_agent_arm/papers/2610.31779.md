# FLORA: An Open Model Suite for Scaling Robotics Data Engines

## What this paper does
FLORA is NVIDIA's open data engine (Qwen3.5-based) used for training GR00T models: a segmenter + captioner for timestamped action captions and a single reward model (Flora-Rewarder, from Qwen3.5-27B, SFT only) that outputs outcome rewards (1–5), dense per-0.5 s execution-quality labels (failure/suboptimal/normal), and rubric-derived rewards. It reports SOTA on WGO-Bench, RoboRewardBench and RobometerBench and ~125x lower cost than GPT-6 Astra.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How accurate is it as a success/failure judge, and how does GPT-6 Astra compare?
RoboRewardBench outcome MAE (x100): Flora 40.0, GPT-6 Astra 55.4, RoboReward 66.5; "The advantage comes from partially completed tasks" [full text, Sec 2.2]. DenseRewardBench (40 bimanual episodes): Flora recalls 53.3% of failure steps "whereas no baseline exceeds 11.7%"; "GPT-6 Astra comes closest in bad-action F1 but detects no failures"; Flora also labels ~11% of normal steps as failures [Sec 2.2]. VOC "does not establish failure-detection accuracy" [Sec 2.2].

### Goal: Can it be used online to verify a step during execution?
Not as designed: dense labels "are retrospective: later frames may reveal a mistake"; windows of 16 s with the last 4 s dropped "so that retained labels have later visual evidence"; "These retrospective annotations are intended for offline labeling" [Sec 4.2]. Inputs are tiled multi-view mosaics plus instruction; vision only [Sec 4.2]. Training uses human-annotated outcome, action-quality and rubric labels from open, licensed and in-house data [Sec 4.3].
