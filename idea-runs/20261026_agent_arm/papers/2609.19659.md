# EmbodiedMind: Adaptive Data Curation and Prefix-Tree Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Embodied Intelligence

## What this paper does
Trains an embodied foundation model (Qwen3-VL-8B based; 27B variant in appendix) in three stages: rejection-sampling SFT, iterative rejection GRPO with difficulty-stratified queues and hybrid rewards, and Trie-GRPO, which groups sampled plans by shared action prefixes to compute step-level advantages for long-horizon task planning. Reports 70.02% average over 18 benchmarks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use for step-level credit assignment in long-horizon planning, and what does it assume?
Trie-GRPO parses each natural-language plan step into a canonical tuple (action_type, target_obj, receptacle_obj), merges trajectories into an action trie, propagates a discounted trajectory reward gamma^(T-t) r_i to nodes, and normalizes Q among siblings sharing a parent prefix [full text, Sec III-D1, III-D2 Eqs 1-2]. The trajectory reward r_i in {+1,-1} comes from majority voting over M reward-model evaluations, not from executing the plan in an environment [Sec III-D2]. The mechanism assumes discrete, exactly matchable action prefixes and open-loop text plans; it does not involve observations returned by execution.

### Goal: What limitation does it report?
Reliance on difficulty stratification and task-specific rewards; extending to unstructured domains "typically requires large foundation models with prohibitive cost"; rejection sampling dominates SFT time on unstructured data [Sec VI].
