# AgenticRoboRL: Bringing Coding-Agent Post-Training to Agentic Robotics

## What this paper does
Post-trains an open Qwen3.5-9B robot planner inside fixed robot harnesses (RPent, which invokes a frozen VLA; CaP-X, which runs generated control code). Stage 1 is SFT on successful trajectories of a Qwen3.8-27B teacher (with memory-dependent text rewritten); stage 2 is multi-turn RL with terminal task-success reward and a leave-one-out group advantage applied to every selected turn. A recording proxy captures each planner call and harness adapters link calls to episode outcomes. On LIBERO-PRO (RPent) success goes 18.1% -> 65.1% (SFT) -> 71.0% (RL); with CaP-X 2.7% -> 18.7% -> 28.5%.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is credit assigned across the turns of a multi-turn robot-planner episode during RL?
Reward is R(tau)=1[task success] from the simulator's success check, "without intermediate rewards or a length penalty" [full text, Sec 3.1 Eq 2, App B Table 5]. Advantage is leave-one-out over the 6 attempts of one (task, initial state) group, A_i = R_i - mean of the others; "Every selected response in episode i receives A_i; groups with identical rewards contribute no policy gradient" [Sec 3.3.2 Eq 4]. At most 16 turns per episode are selected and weighted 1/K_i; no critic, no KL or entropy term [Sec 3.3.2 Eq 5, App A]. So a turn's credit is the whole episode's outcome; there is no turn-level value estimate and no way to separate planner decisions from frozen-executor (VLA) outcome noise.

### Goal: How much of the gain comes from RL in the planner's own environment versus supervision from a stronger model?
Most comes from SFT on the 27B teacher: "SFT accounts for most of the improvement, while RL adds another 5.9 percentage points" (65.1 -> 71.0) [Sec 4.2]. On the paired 800 episodes RL wins 121 and loses 74 versus SFT [Sec 4.2]. The teacher's trajectories were collected with the Harness VLA task-specific memory, and the student "can learn task knowledge from the trajectories of the teacher with memory" [Sec 4.2]. The evaluation follows the task-specific experience setting: the same 80 LIBERO-PRO tasks are trained on initial states 11-44 and evaluated on states 1-10 [Sec 4.1 Data split]. Long-horizon suites stay lowest after RL (L10-T 41.0, L10-S 39.0 vs 86-96 on Spatial/Object) [Table 1].

### Goal: What limits RL where the planner (or teacher) rarely succeeds?
With CaP-X the RL task pool is restricted "to the tasks where the SFT planner solves between one and four of the five validation episodes. Tasks that it always or never solves are left out because their groups would receive identical rewards" [App C.3]. Teacher data covered only 26 of 60 tasks (19/20 Object, 5/20 Goal, 2/20 Spatial); after RL, Goal and Spatial remain at 0-16% while Object rises to 67-72% [Sec 4.4, Table 3]. RL "requires no teacher data and raises the student above the teacher" (28.5 vs 20.3) but only where some success already exists. CaP-X runs use privileged state [Sec 4.4].

### Goal: What does the RL run cost in episodes and wall-clock?
RPent: 6 rounds x 384 episodes = 2,304 episodes, 26,052 selected responses, 206 optimizer steps; an episode may run up to 100 planner turns or 7,200 s [Sec 4.1 Training, App C.2, C.4]. The paper does not report GPU-hours or per-episode wall-clock for training. Limitations are not discussed in a separate section.
