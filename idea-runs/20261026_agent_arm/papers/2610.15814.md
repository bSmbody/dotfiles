# ReTrace: Cross-Stage Failure Attribution for Self-Improving Robotic Manipulation

## What this paper does
ReTrace lets a frozen frontier model (GPT-6 Astra / Sol) that directly predicts action chunks for a Franka improve on a task across attempts without demonstrations or weight updates. An Executor acts; after each attempt an Attributor (same model) reads the trajectory, traces a downstream failure to an earlier decision, proposes single-factor corrections (problem, adjustment, magnitude) held in a capacity-K pending buffer, compares the next attempt with the previous one to label each correction effective / ineffective / uncertain, and consolidates validated ones into a task-specific natural-language skill. Four real tasks, 10 trials each; plug insertion and ball pouring are the "cross-stage dependent" ones (0% -> 80% / 100% for Astra-low with the learned skill).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does the method detect or handle an action that succeeds locally but makes a later stage fail, and is the dependency anticipated before commitment or only found after failure?
Only after failure, and only across attempts on the same task. Every attempt "begins with the robot in a fixed initial state"; when the r-th attempt ends, the Attributor "analyzes whether the problem arose from a local issue ... or from an earlier stage that achieved its immediate goal but hindered subsequent execution" [full text, Sec 3.1-3.2]. The worked examples: insertion collision traced to the grasp pose, corrected by tilting the gripper 0.5 rad, then 0.9 rad after attempt 2 was still insufficient; scoop instability traced to grasp position along the handle [Sec 4.3, Fig 4]. Within an attempt the Executor has no mechanism that predicts downstream consequences of a grasp; without the learned skill all models score 0% on both dependent tasks, and Astra-high (the model that learns the skill) gets only 10% / 20% [Table 1].
### Goal: How does it attribute a downstream failure to a cause, and is the attribution validated causally?
Attribution is free-form retrospective reasoning by the foundation model over visual observations, robot states, commands and feedback [Sec 3.2]. Validation is a whole next attempt from the fixed initial state: the Attributor "checks whether the intended change actually occurred and evaluates its effect", and the buffer limits how many factors change at once so effects are separable [Sec 3.2, Eq 1]. There is no counterfactual replay from the intermediate state; each test of a hypothesis costs a full real attempt. The learned artifact is a task-specific skill; transfer is shown across model / reasoning configurations on the same tasks, not across tasks [Sec 4.2]. Sol-xhigh reaches only 10% on plug insertion even with the skill, so "benefits of skill reuse vary across executing models" [Sec 4.2].
### Goal: What limitation does it report on composition / cross-stage dependency?
The paper states no limitations section beyond the conclusion; evaluation is 4 tasks x 10 trials, two of which have the dependency, and skills are learned per task by Astra-high [Sec 4.1, Sec 5]. Generalization of an attributed dependency to new objects/tasks is not evaluated.
