# ManiGuard: A Benchmark and Data Suite for Specification-Grounded Safety Evaluation and Improvement of Robotic Manipulation

## What this paper does
200 base tasks x (ID + 4 OOD) = 1,000 scenarios across six contact-rich household families, with safety specified in LTLf over physics-grounded predicates and checked by automaton runtime monitors (Isaac Sim / OmniGibson, plus physical Franka). Releases 8,000 safety-annotated demonstrations (motion planning + teleop, labeled by the same per-step monitor) and fine-tunes VLAs on them.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What method is used to improve safety, and how far does it get?
Supervised fine-tuning on monitor-filtered safe-and-successful demonstrations: safe success rises from near zero to 7.5–29.8%, engaged-and-safe from 16–40% to 51–72%; yet 21–42% of engaged rollouts still violate the spec, two families stay below 2% safe success for every policy, and failures persist under OOD shift and on hardware [full text, Abstract, Sec 5.2].

### Goal: Why does this method fall short, according to the authors?
Scaling the same demonstrations does not close the gap and is non-monotonic on Cabinet; "the resulting SFT objective still imitates successful trajectories rather than explicitly optimizing safety"; they suggest per-step violations could define costs/rewards, prioritize counterexamples, or support shielding [Sec 6, 'Safety may require different data' and 'Monitoring as a learning signal']. Explicitly stating the constraint gives inconsistent effects (Q4).

### Goal: Limitations?
Primarily simulation; physical verdicts operator-scored; finite authored LTLf library; safety labels only as complete as predicates [Sec 6 Limitations].
