# RESCUE: Is This Alarm Worth Acting On? Counterfactual Evaluation of VLA Runtime Monitors

## What this paper does
Evaluates VLA runtime failure monitors (steps-since-progress, action jerk, pi0.5 sampling disagreement, SAFE-style hidden-state probes) as intervention rules. It snapshots simulator+policy state at action-chunk boundaries of OpenVLA-OFT and pi0.5 rollouts on LIBERO-Spatial / LIBERO-Plus, branches seeded counterfactual continuations under {Continue, scripted Recover}, and estimates a state-intervention success probability, a recoverability horizon (latest checkpoint where some intervention still succeeds with probability >= tau), and stop-and-reset decision value. 1,562 trunk episodes, 128,340 branches; no new detector.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: When do failures become unrecoverable, and do failure monitors fire before that point?
On OpenVLA-OFT / LIBERO-Plus, among the 14 failures with a defined horizon under Continue, the median recoverability horizon is step 44 of 220, "a median of 7 steps after the first grasp attempt and 34 steps before a subsequent object drop" [full text, Sec IV-B]. Recoverability is intervention-relative: 14/66 failures have a qualifying checkpoint under Continue, 33/66 under Recover, 34/66 under either; Recover shifts the horizon by a median +40 steps, and under Recover recoverability is non-monotone in time on 8/20 episodes [Sec IV-B, IV-E]. "Under Continue, every real monitor alarms after the horizon in more than half of the episodes for which both quantities are defined" [Sec IV-E].
### Goal: What do the monitors score, and what limitation does the paper report?
All evaluated monitors score failure of the nominal policy; none conditions on which intervention would be applied [Sec IV-A, IV-D]. Limitations: only Continue and one fixed scripted Recover ("no claim about richer or learned recovery repertoires"), single-task LIBERO episodes, all failures are timeouts [Sec V].
