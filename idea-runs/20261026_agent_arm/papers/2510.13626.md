# LIBERO-Plus: In-depth Robustness Analysis of Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
Perturbs LIBERO along seven dimensions (object layout, camera viewpoint, robot initial state, language, light, background, sensor noise), evaluates OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, π0, π0-FAST, Nora, WorldVLA, UniVLA and RIPT-VLA, probes whether the models use language and vision, and releases the perturbed benchmark (LIBERO-Plus, later used to evaluate frontier agents) and a training set.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does near-saturated LIBERO success measure what it claims (instruction following, robustness)?
No. Performance drops "from 95% to below 30% under modest perturbations" of camera viewpoint and robot initial state [full text, Abstract, Sec 2.3]. With a blank instruction, OpenVLA-OFT's object-suite success "remained largely unchanged"; when the target object is swapped for another in the same scene, success "dropped nearly to zero" and the model "still tended to execute the original target action" [Sec 4.1–4.2, Findings 7–8]. Apparent language robustness comes from ignoring language, so high in-distribution LIBERO scores do not certify instruction following.

### Goal: Relevance to frontier-agent evaluation?
It is the perturbation suite on which frontier agents are later compared with VLAs (e.g. GPT-GEAR on LIBERO-Plus); its finding is about specialist VLAs, not frontier agents, and it does not address timing, cost or privileged state.
