# Jailbreaking LLM-Controlled Robots (RoboPAIR)

## What this paper does
RoboPAIR adapts the PAIR attacker–judge loop to robots with robot-specific system prompts/APIs and a syntax checker so the elicited output is executable; jailbreaks NVIDIA Dolphins (driving LLM), a Clearpath Jackal with a GPT-4o planner, and a Unitree Go2, with near-100% success in many settings.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Why are chatbot defenses argued not to transfer to robots?
Harm of a robot action is context-dependent ("walking forward is harmful if a human is in its path"); filtering defenses can't situate prompts in the robot's environment; prompt-optimization defenses need white-box access and are costly on hardware; fine-tuning defenses are ill-suited because robot companies call proprietary models via APIs [full text, Sec 6.1, 6.4]. Robots "lack robustness to even the most thinly veiled attempts" — LLM-controlled robots are "fundamentally unaligned, even for non-adversarial inputs" [Sec 6.1].

### Goal: What do they call for?
Physical safety filters that impose hard physical constraints and account for the robot's context and environment, while limiting the robustness–capability trade-off [Sec 6.4].
