# Recursive Harness Distillation across Agents for Robot Manipulation

## What this paper does
A strong agent (GPT-6 Astra) operates a frozen VLA (GR00T on SimplerEnv Bridge; fine-tuned pi0.5 on a real Franka) through instruction-edit, attention-bias and action-correction interventions, distills its experience into a text playbook, and recursively revises it from a light agent's (GPT-5.6 Luna) execution feedback; a revision is accepted when Luna's dev success reaches the teacher's. No model weights change. Luna+playbook reaches 66.7% on 48 held-out Bridge instances vs 41.7% for GR00T alone; real-world 37.3% -> 64.0%.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Can the light agent build useful guidance for itself, without a stronger teacher?
No. With Luna as both teacher and recipient the refined playbook reaches 22.9%, below Luna without a playbook (43.8%), versus 66.7% with an Astra-derived playbook; "having the light agent construct and recursively refine its own playbook is insufficient" [full text, Sec 4.3, Table 2]. The initial (unrefined) Astra playbook also hurts Luna (43.8 -> 31.3%) until refined on Luna's own rollouts [Sec 4.3].

### Goal: What does the deployment cost comparison between strong and light agents show?
On the same test set, Luna costs $0.63 per episode and Astra $3.43 (about 5.5x), at Luna $0.20/M input, $1.20/M output and Astra $10/$50 [Sec 5.3]. This cost gap is the stated motivation for the light agent.

### Goal: How is the improvement objective formalized and evaluated?
Objective is the recipient's success J_S(P); the performance-difference identity (Eq 3) states a revision should improve decisions "at the histories the recipient actually encounters", but values are not estimated: "we evaluate proposed revisions through complete recipient rollouts and their task outcomes" [Sec 3.3]. Acceptance requires recipient dev success >= teacher's, with no termination guarantee [Sec 3.4 Eq 5].
