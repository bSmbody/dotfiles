# Has GPT-6-Astra Achieved Embodied Intelligence? (SenseNova study)

## What this paper does
Large-scale study of GPT-6-Astra on 12 embodied benchmarks (RoboDojo, RoboLab, LIBERO-Long++/Ultra-Long, LIBERO-PRO, Bench2Dex, DexJoCo, SIMPLE, BEHAVIOR, VLN-CE, LHPR-VLN) under a unified protocol that allows in-context learning from each benchmark's training demonstrations (a frozen "knowledge package" Astra writes before testing) and an extended 10x step budget; harness ablations (System 2 over System 1, action representation, chunking); zero-shot deployment on 8 real platforms; and a proposed next-generation benchmark (needs-derived tasks, no in-domain demos, a world clock that runs during deliberation) with a household Isaac Sim proof of concept.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Which benchmark conditions does the paper show inflate frontier-agent results (ICL, budget, paused world)?
ICL from training demos: on SIMPLE, success 70.8%→90.8% [full text, Sec 2.2]; benefits weaken where tests leave the demo distribution (RoboDojo Open, RoboLab) [Sec 2.4]. 10x step budget: RoboDojo success roughly triples [Sec 2.3]. Paused world: "In simulation the environment pauses while the model deliberates" [Sec 4.3]; on RoboDojo "the standard budgets correspond to 8–76 s of commanded motion against an average of 55 minutes of computation" [Sec 5.1]. "Because inference latency is not counted in the action-step budget, this cost also limits deployment in settings that require real-time control" [Sec 2.4]. Benchmarks "measure in part how well it exploits what they provide" [Sec 5].

### Goal: What uncounted costs does it quantify?
LIBERO-Long++ (>97% success) costs ~1M tokens and nearly an hour per episode; LIBERO-Ultra-Long ~9M tokens and ~2 h; extended-budget RoboDojo >3 h per episode [Sec 2.4]. ~98% of tokens are cached input; rule of thumb 1M tokens ≈ $1 [Fig 5]. ICL presumes test-task demos on the test robot exist; real-world demos "are expensive to collect" [Abstract, Sec 4.3]. On G1, ~90% of each run is deliberation and one trial takes over an hour [Sec 4.3].

### Goal: What method-level failure appears once the world does not pause?
In the household proof of concept, deliberation advances world time: Astra decides to turn off a burner once an egg sets, but "each decision takes seconds of world time with the burner still on"; the egg chars ~90 s after setting. "The model reasons as though the world waits for it, which in the simulators of Sec. 2 it does" [Sec 5.2, Finding 3, Fig 12e]. It also leaves a fridge open 300–765 s while doing another subtask, which a final-state check would miss [Sec 5.2, Finding 1]. These episodes are "few and qualitative" [Sec 5.2]. The proposed protocol simulates the time between observation and next command "under the previous command, as on a physical robot" [Sec 5.1] — an evaluation design, not an agent mechanism.

### Goal: How is success judged?
Simulation: benchmark success criteria. Real world: wall-clock budget (normally 60 min), "a person judges success from the physical outcome rather than from the model's own completion statement" [Sec 4.1]. Fixed checks can contradict their instructions (Bench2Dex condiment task requires upright bottles not stated) [Sec 5.1].


### Goal: What does it report about GPT-6-Astra's physical-state verification, and which harness component addresses outcome checking?
Physical-state verification is one of five named future directions [full text, Abstract]. Residual failures under ICL + 10x budget: Astra "can detect that a task is not yet complete without identifying which physical condition remains unmet; consequently, its correction process fails to converge" (Bench2Dex jigsaw: corrections pull pieces apart); in RoboDojo Hang mugs it "takes an overlap between a mug's handle and the peg in its head camera as evidence that the mug is hung, without confirming that the peg passes through the handle", and in regrasps "the closing of its fingers is likewise taken as contact that has not occurred"; in Fill egg holder it reports eggs seated but the episode fails. "Reliable execution requires checks that resolve the task's actual contact, support, and alignment constraints" [Sec 2.4, Fig 6a]. A successful recovery in DexJoCo Fold Glasses involves Astra switching approach and "verifies the grasp with a test lift" [Sec 2.4]. Harness ablation: a feedback subagent that "evaluates execution outcomes and suggests continuation, adjustment, or retry" gives the largest gain (+7.22 score, +9.16 pp SR, +7.03% tokens) [App 10.1, Table 13]; authors call for "targeted verification mechanisms" [App 10.1]. For benchmark judging, "vision-language success detectors reach at most 60% balanced accuracy on contact-rich assembly" and VLM trajectory judges "can be misled by the agent's own reasoning" [Sec 5.3]. A real-robot Franka trial ends with Astra withdrawing and reporting the insertion "as unverified" [App 11.3].


### Goal: Which benchmarks are near saturation for Astra under this protocol?
With the extended budget, "DexJoCo and VLN-CE reach success rates of around 90%, while the LIBERO family and SIMPLE exceed 95% and approach saturation"; success is reported at cumulative cutoffs 1x, 2x, ..., 10x of the same episodes, which continue rather than reset [full text, Sec 2.1, Sec 2.3].
