# TabPFN beyond Tabular Data: Calibration and Accuracy on Multimodal Embeddings

## What this paper does
Systematically evaluates TabPFN v2.5 as a zero-gradient in-context classification head on frozen image, text and audio encoder embeddings (11 encoders, 14 datasets, 22,820 few-shot episodes), against kNN, logistic regression, linear SVM, tree ensembles and MLP heads, after z-normalisation and PCA to d ∈ {8,…,96, all}. Also tests TabPFN heads on LoRA/fully fine-tuned backbones, label noise, top-k calibration and other ICL tabular models.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does TabPFN work as a training-free head on frozen image-encoder embeddings, and how is dimensionality handled?
Yes. Frozen-encoder features are z-normalised with training-set statistics and PCA-reduced to d columns; the labelled support set is the in-context data and the queries are predicted in one forward pass [full text, Sec "Frozen-encoder pipeline"]. Across all episodes TabPFN has the best mean rank of nine heads on NLL and ECE, reducing NLL by 48-62% and ECE by 2.1-5.3x versus the baseline average while matching or exceeding their average accuracy [full text, Abstract, Exp 1]. On DINOv2-B/Mini-ImageNet at k=5, frozen TabPFN beats a trained linear probe 0.939 vs 0.872 [full text, Exp 3].

### Goal: Under which conditions is its accuracy advantage and calibration reliable?
Calibration advantage is broad: ECE lower than every baseline for all k≥20 and d≤96, NLL lower at all k and d [full text, Exp 2]. Accuracy advantage is conditional: it trails logistic regression at k=5 per class, leads from k≥50, concentrates at d∈[8,32], and vanishes at d=all (raw dimensions exceed effective capacity) and near ceiling [full text, Exp 2, App E]. TabPFN is more robust than classical heads to 10-20% label noise [full text, Ablation 1]. Limitations: only in-distribution discrete classification; domain shift and regression are untested [full text, Discussion, Limitations].
