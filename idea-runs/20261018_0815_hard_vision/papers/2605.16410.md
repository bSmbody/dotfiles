# Test-Time Hinting for Black-Box Vision-Language Models

## What this paper does
Trains a Qwen3-VL-4B hint generator (LoRA, SFT then GRPO) to prepend a short, contrastive, non-leaking natural-language hint to a black-box VLM's prompt in a single call. Training hints come from an agentic loop (GPT-5 proposer/editor, target VLM as verifier); the GRPO reward scores repair/no-op/harm/unrepaired across three target APIs. Evaluated on multiple-choice natural-image VQA (A-OKVQA, RealWorldQA, Visual7W) with transfer to unseen targets.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to help a black-box VLM, and what can the hint carry?
Hints are 1-3 natural-language items, at least one contrastive, that "surface perceptual or procedural cues rather than encode the answer", must not reveal a*, and avoid certainty language [full text, Sec 3 notation]. Gains: Claude 4.5 Haiku +6.02 pp overall, repair rate 49.4%; Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite repair 61.7% [full text, Sec 5.2, Tab 3]. Failure taxonomy includes counting and spatial relation, labelled post hoc by GPT-5 [full text, Sec 2].

### Goal: What does its training procedure do with instances the target cannot be steered to solve?
Step 2 keeps a hint only if the target verifier flips to the correct answer within 3 rounds; "unsuccessful repair targets" are "excluded from downstream phases" [full text, Sec 3.2]; Stage 1 SFT discards trials where the verifier never reached a correct answer [full text, Sec 3.3]. Stage 2 down-weights unrepaired outcomes to -0.5 because on hard inputs "every candidate hint is likely to leave the target wrong" and a symmetric penalty pushes assertive hints that cause harm elsewhere [full text, App C.6]. So supervision exists only where the target can already be steered to the right answer. Stated future work: gate by predicted failure risk; extend beyond MCQ [full text, Sec 6].
