# Set-of-Mark Prompting Unleashes Extraordinary Visual Grounding in GPT-4V

## What this paper does
Set-of-Mark (SoM) partitions an image with off-the-shelf segmenters (SEEM/SAM), overlays a unique alphanumeric mark (optionally with mask outline and box) on each region, and prompts GPT-4V with the marked image, optionally referring to marks in the text. Zero-shot GPT-4V with SoM beats fully fine-tuned referring-expression models on RefCOCOg and helps phrase grounding and segmentation tasks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How should marks be drawn so that a black-box frontier model reads the region they label, and what are the known failure modes on dense or number-rich images?
Marks are alphanumerics, chosen because they are compact and readable by GPT-4V's OCR; boxes and mask boundaries are auxiliary marks, and adding boxes "can improve the performance significantly" [full text, Sec 2.3, Sec 4.3]. Placing all marks at region centres "inevitably introduces some overlaps or conflicts" on dense images, so a mark-allocation algorithm places marks via a distance transform, moving them off very small regions [Sec 2.3, Fig 5]. Mark types must be image-dependent: "given an arithmetic image full of numbers, the numeric marks should be avoided", and numeric marks overlaid on number-rich images "will confuse the GPT-4V" [Sec 2.3, Sec 4.4]. Centered marks are not always best, and GPT-4V sometimes assigns a mark to the wrong neighbouring region [Sec 4.4, Fig 8]. With ground-truth masks, RefCOCOg mIoU rises by 14.5, so region quality bounds SoM [Sec 4.3].
