# ToolVision: Learning When and How to Use Visual Tools with Capability-Aligned Supervision

## What this paper does
Trains an 8B VLM to call a code-interface toolbelt that holds OCR, an open-vocabulary detector plus SAM, a monocular metric depth model, an open-world counter and pure image operations. SFT trajectories are synthesised by a planner and executor. Each step is scored by an eight-model committee for its "answerability gain" Δ_t, and steps that do not raise answerability for student-scale models are pruned. RL (GSPO) uses a necessity-conditioned reward: a frozen question-level weight w(x, π_SFT) ∈ {0.5, 0.2, 0} from paired tool-disabled and tool-enabled rollouts, multiplied by a tool-success indicator.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism decides whether to call a tool and whether to trust its output over the model's own reading?
At inference the trained policy decides implicitly. In training, "when" is taught by the reward term w(x,π_SFT)·R_mut [full text, Section 3.3, reward equation]. R_mut = 1 if the final answer is correct, at least one tool is used and no tool call raised an execution error. So the reward never checks whether the tool's returned content (count, depth, box) was right; it checks only execution success and the final outcome. After RL the policy calls the counter on 100% of FSC-147 questions and OCR on 84% of OCRBench questions [full text, Section 4.4]. That is task-type routing, with the specialist output taken as evidence. Case G.3 shows the model recovering from its own bad crops by handing localisation to the detector. The paper presents trusting the specialist over its own coordinates as the desired behaviour [full text, Appendix G.3].

### Goal: What signal (and at what granularity) is the trust/necessity decision based on, and does it require the VLM's weights or logits?
Necessity is question-level and model-specific. It is estimated once before RL with 16 no-tool rollouts and 8 tool-enabled rollouts per question (the GTC/NTC counts), bucketed into three tiers and frozen [full text, Section 3.3 "Measuring Tool Necessity"]. The authors stress that "the same question may require a tool for one policy but not for a stronger one". The signal is therefore tied to the trained model and needs RL on its weights. Δ_t uses a committee of models answering from the accumulated evidence [full text, Section 3.1].

### Goal: What limitation does it report or show on handling incorrect tool outputs or confidently wrong VLM readings?
No experiment injects wrong specialist outputs. The "safeguard" terms only catch malformed responses and tool overuse (overuse penalty active on 0.15% of trajectories) [full text, Appendix D]. Silent semantic failures, such as a crop landing on the wrong region, are flagged by no error signal [full text, Appendix G.3]. The reward has no counterpart for a specialist that executes cleanly but returns a wrong value.
