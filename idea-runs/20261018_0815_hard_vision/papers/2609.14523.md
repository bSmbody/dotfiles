# Selective Tool Use for Agentic Change Visual Question Answering in Remote Sensing

## What this paper does
For bi-temporal remote-sensing Change VQA, a single VLM either answers directly or emits a structured call to one of three deterministic analysis tools (transition, spatial, temporal). The tools operate on bi-temporal semantic maps, and the same VLM answers from the returned observation. The VLM is LoRA-tuned on trajectories whose response mode follows a fixed question-family policy, with tool observations computed from reference (oracle) semantic maps. At test time it is evaluated with reference maps and with maps predicted by a semantic change detection model.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism decides whether to call a tool and whether to trust its output over the model's own reading?
The LoRA-tuned VLM decides, imitating SFT targets in which "the target response mode and tool assignment follow the question-family policy defined during benchmark construction" [full text, Section II-C]. After the call, the observation is appended and the VLM answers. There is no step that checks or discounts the observation [full text, Section II-A, Eqs 1-3].

### Goal: What signal (and at what granularity) is the trust/necessity decision based on, and does it require the VLM's weights or logits?
Routing is learned at question-family granularity by supervised fine-tuning (LoRA on decoder attention). Training observations come from oracle reference maps, "an oracle evidence source ... without semantic prediction errors" [full text, Sections II-B, II-C]. It requires weight access.

### Goal: What limitation does it report or show on handling incorrect tool outputs or confidently wrong VLM readings?
With predicted rather than oracle maps, overall accuracy falls from 88.79% to 77.47% (−11.32 points). Families F4 and F6 drop from about 99.8% to 63.4% and 66.5% [full text, Section III-D]. The authors conclude that "the gains depend on the quality of the semantic maps". The policy trained on oracle evidence has no means to detect when the specialist's output is wrong; it still beats direct answering on aggregate (77.47 vs 73.77 OA).
