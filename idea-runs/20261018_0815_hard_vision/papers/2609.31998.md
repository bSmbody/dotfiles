# Type-Balanced Federated Learning for Visual Analog Meter Reading

## What this paper does
A federated meter-reading framework with a four-stage pipeline: YOLO dial localisation, a trained U-Net segmenting ticks and pointer (the only learned part, trained federatively), a fixed polar unwrapping of the masks into a linear tick ruler, and deterministic tick-count decoding into a reading. It adds type-balanced federated aggregation and releases MeterFL (1,382 mask-annotated meter images in pseudo-clients).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does the polar-unwrap + tick-count decoder read a meter, and what must be known in advance?
The dial centre is the centroid of predicted tick pixels; the masks are unwrapped to a 720×96 polar strip; the largest gap between neighbouring ticks is taken as the scale's start–end boundary. Per angular column, tick and pointer pixels are counted, thresholded at the mean, and grouped; each group's centre is a tick or pointer angle. The pointer position is p = i + (θ_ptr − θ_i)/(θ_{i+1} − θ_i) in tick intervals, and the value is v_min + p·Δv [full text, Sec 3.1, Eq 1–2]. The scale interval Δv and minimum v_min "of the gauge model are needed", i.e. metadata is supplied, and numerals are not read. Because the index i is counted from the start, a missed or spurious tick shifts every later index by one.

### Goal: How accurate is explicit tick geometry compared with VLMs?
With a 30.6M-parameter U-Net, the pipeline reaches 1.4%FS median error and 87.2% Acc, where Acc accepts errors within two minor divisions (median division 3.1% of span). Zero-shot VLMs reach 13.4–18.4%FS median; a LoRA-SFT 7B VLM on 122 pooled images 2.1%FS; a DialBench-style variant 2.4%FS and 77.0% Acc [full text, Sec 4.2–4.3]. "A small segmentation model with explicit tick geometry thus beats 7B-parameter fine-tuned VLMs that see all the images." The tolerance (two minor divisions) is looser than MeasureBench's per-image intervals, and the segmenter is trained on in-domain masks.

# Type-Balanced Federated Learning for Visual Analog Meter Reading (MeterFL)

## What this paper does
Proposes MeterFL, a 1,382-image analog-meter benchmark (internet images plus two open-source meter datasets) with polygon masks for ticks, pointer and background and a reading ground truth, split train/test with dHash near-duplicate control, plus a four-stage reader: YOLO dial crop, a 30.6M U-Net (ResNet50 encoder) segmenting ticks and pointer, polar unwrapping (720 x 96 strip), and a deterministic tick-count decoder that needs v_min and delta_v. The U-Net is trained federatively with type-balanced aggregation across VLM-derived pseudo-clients.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Is MeterFL public, how large is its test split, and how is end-to-end reading scored?
Public: "MeterFL is publicly available at https://github.com/weidazhaoooo/Meter-FL" [full text, Abstract]; the repo page (checked 2026-10-18) lists data/ with client folders, masks/, readings/ and metadata/train_test_split.json, and names MeasureBench among its image sources. Segmentation is scored on 272 test images of eight clients; complete-pipeline reading on the 269 GT-decodable test images by median %FS, share within 5%FS, and Acc, which "accepts errors within two minor divisions of human readings" (median division 3.1% of span) [full text, Sec 4.2]. Near-duplicate groups never cross train and test [Sec 4.1].

### Goal: How well do zero-shot VLMs and the trained pipeline read MeterFL test meters?
Centralized zero-shot: "Frontier VLM" median 18.0%FS, 7.4% within 5%FS, Acc 49.1; Qwen2.5-VL-7B 18.4 / 9.7 / 35.3; Qwen2.5-VL-72B 13.4 / 13.4 / 45.4 [full text, Table 1]. LoRA-SFT Qwen2.5-VL-7B on 122 pooled images: 2.1%FS, Acc 76.2. Trained U-Net pipeline: pooled 87.9 Acc (88.2 type-balanced), federated type-balanced 87.2 Acc at 1.43%FS [Table 1, Sec 4.3]. So explicit tick geometry with a trained segmenter and supplied metadata beats fine-tuned 7B VLMs.

### Goal: What exactly does the decoder do, and what does it need supplied?
Per angular column of the unwrapped masks it counts tick and pointer pixels, thresholds by the mean, groups runs into ticks, and computes p = i + (theta_ptr - theta_i)/(theta_{i+1} - theta_i) from the scale start chosen as the largest angular gap; the value is v_min + p * delta_v, with v_min and delta_v "needed" from the gauge model [full text, Sec 3.1, Eq 1-2]. Dial centre is the centroid of predicted tick pixels. Nothing ranks alternative tick counts or abstains.
