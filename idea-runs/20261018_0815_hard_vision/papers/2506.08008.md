# Hidden in plain sight: VLMs overlook their visual representations

## What this paper does
Compares VLMs to direct readouts of their own vision encoders (DINOv2, CLIP, SigLIP, IN-1k ViT etc., plus InternVL, Phi-3-V, QwenVL) on vision-centric tasks: depth ordering, semantic correspondence, affordance, low-level matching, 3D object awareness, art style. Localises the failure by layer probing, prompt-tuning, and component-wise LoRA tuning.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How large is the gap between encoder readout and VLM answer, and where is the signal lost?
Encoders read out directly perform well (e.g. 88.7% depth estimation, DPT head; other tasks zero-shot readouts), but VLMs drop to near chance; low-level matching drops most (45.5 points), depth least (21.7) [full text, Sec 3.1-3.2]. Probing intermediate projector/LLM layers shows task information is preserved throughout, with the sharpest drop only at the final LLM layer [full text, Sec 4.1, Fig 5]. VLM answer distributions closely match their blank-image ("blind") answer distributions, i.e. they fall back on LLM priors [full text, Sec 3.4, Tab 2].

### Goal: Which remedies were tried and what limits them?
Prompt-tuning 1-10 prefix embeddings gives marginal gains that saturate after 1-5 [full text, Sec 4.2, Fig 6]. LoRA-tuning the LLM (equal parameter budget, 5k task examples) beats tuning the ViT or projector and increases attention on key points [full text, Sec 4.3, Fig 7-8]. The authors explicitly do not propose task-specific training as a solution; it is a localisation tool [full text, Sec 4.3.1]. All remedies need weight access and per-task supervision; readouts are task-specific heads or zero-shot task-specific protocols (e.g. feature matching for correspondence), not a general question-conditioned interface.
