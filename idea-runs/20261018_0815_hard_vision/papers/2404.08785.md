# Under pressure: learning-based analog gauge reading in the wild

## What this paper does
A modular gauge reader (ICRA 2024, ETH): YOLOv8 gauge detection; a DINOv2-backbone keypoint head (trained) for major notches plus start/end notches; an ellipse fit through the notches; YOLOv8 needle segmentation with an orthogonal-distance line fit; off-the-shelf OCR (DBNet + ABINet) for scale numbers and units, run after perspective and rotation correction; numeral box centres projected onto the ellipse, a RANSAC linear angle→value fit, and needle–ellipse intersection to read the value. No prior range or unit is needed. This is the pipeline MeasureBench ran as "Reitsma et al." (8.5% on real images).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it obtain the scale and the needle, and what does it assume?
The scale comes from the photo's own numerals: each OCR'd numeric box centre is projected onto the notch ellipse to get its angle, then a line mapping angle to value is fitted with RANSAC to reject misread or unrelated numbers (serial numbers) [full text, Sec III-F1, Fig 5]. Minor ticks are not used; the reading is interpolated linearly between numeral anchors. It assumes a linear scale with ≥5 visible major notches and numerals along the arc. Non-linear scales need "another type of mapping", and meter_d of Howells was excluded because it is non-linear [full text, Sec III, III-F1, Sec IV]. The needle is a segmented blob with a line fit, which assumes the needle points along its symmetry axis. Tip and tail are told apart by a heuristic: pick the ellipse intersection closer to the needle's segmented end, since "the needle tip is usually closer to the gauge scale than the tail" [full text, Sec III-D, III-F2].

### Goal: Where does it fail, and how accurate is it when every stage works?
OCR is the dominant failure: stage failure rates of 40–57.5% across test sets, only 18–35% of scale markers detected, and no decimals or negative signs [full text, Tab III–IV, Sec V-B4, Fig 8]. When OCR reads at least two markers, the mean relative error is 1.15% (front) and 0.77% (angled) of the full range, but 444% and 246% over all images [full text, Tab I]. On Howells' data the mean is 2.01% of range [full text, Tab II]. Needle segmentation fails on needle shadows and on a white needle over a near-white face (IoU 0 on "untypical" gauges, 50% stage failure there) [full text, Sec V-B3, Tab III–IV]. The ellipse fails when fewer than 5 notches are found [full text, Sec V-B2, Fig 7]. A 1–2%-of-range error is about one minor division on a 50–100-division scale.
