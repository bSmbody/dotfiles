# Vision-based module for accurately reading linear scales in a laboratory

## What this paper does
A classical pipeline for reading syringes and measuring cylinders: a fine-tuned YOLO crop, colour segmentation and contour PCA for orientation correction, major/minor marker classification by contour width, PyTesseract OCR of the digit beside each major marker, a slope-mode auto-correction of misread digits, and meniscus/plunger-based level estimation via a linear position-to-value map. Evaluated on lab frames and a recorded syringe video against human readings.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it combine numeral reading with tick geometry, and does it handle misread or misassigned numerals?
Majors are found by relative contour width; OCR reads the digit next to each major; all pairs of (position, value) give slopes and the modal slope defines the correct pairs, from which value = slope * vertical position + offset; this corrects OCR misreads (Table 1-2) [full text, Sec D "Digits" and "Auto-correction", Eq 4-6]. It is a consistency vote over numerals under a linear model, not a ranking over tick-index labellings, and gives no abstention signal.
### Goal: How broad is the evaluation?
Narrow: two apparatus types (syringe, measuring cylinder) captured by the authors in the lab, MAE 0.070-0.094 ml and R2 0.994-0.996 on a syringe aspirating/dispensing video [full text, Results, Table 5]. No public benchmark, no VLM comparison.

# Vision-based module for accurately reading linear scales in a laboratory

## What this paper does
A hand-built OpenCV pipeline (TCS Research, Dec 2025) for reading the level on a syringe and a measuring cylinder: a fine-tuned YOLO crop, PCA-based orientation correction from contours of scale markings, colour segmentation and contour filtering to find linear tick markers, a relative-length grouping into major and minor markers, Tesseract OCR of the digit next to each major marker, a mode-of-pairwise-slopes "auto-correction" that fixes misread numerals and fills unread ones, and a meniscus/plunger level detector whose position is mapped through the fitted linear position-to-value relation. Evaluation is one syringe filmed in the lab.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it already combine locally measured tick geometry with numeral values and a consistency check across alternative labellings, and on what data is it evaluated?
Partly. Majors and minors are measured locally (contours, aspect ratio > 2.5 filter, sorted by length, new group when the jump exceeds 15% [full text, Sec D "Relative Contour Identifier"]); numerals are read by Tesseract only beside each major marker, which fixes the numeral-to-major attachment by proximity [Sec D "Digits"]. A consistency step computes slopes for every pair of (position, value) and keeps pairs whose slope equals the mode, then refits value = slope * position + offset and relabels every major, including unread ones [Sec D "Auto-correction", Tab 1-3]. This is a RANSAC-like global linear fit, not a ranked hypothesis space with a margin; minors are not counted or used in the reading, there is no abstention, and only linear scales are handled. Evaluation: 24 ground-truth levels (0-4.6 ml) on one syringe, aspirating and dispensing, MAE 0.07/0.094 ml, R2 0.996/0.994 [Sec IV, Tab 4-5]; no public benchmark, no comparison with any baseline.
