# Temporal phase unwrapping using deep learning

## What this paper does
In fringe projection profilometry, high-frequency fringes give precise but 2π-ambiguous phase; multi-frequency temporal phase unwrapping (MF-TPU) uses a unit-frequency (unambiguous) phase to recover the integer fringe order of the high-frequency one. The paper analyses MF-TPU's noise limit and proposes a CNN (DL-TPU) that predicts the fringe order from the two phases, allowing 64-period fringes to be unwrapped from one unit-frequency phase.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism of multi-frequency unwrapping and the exact condition under which it recovers the right period?
The absolute fine phase is the wrapped fine phase plus 2π·k, and the integer k is obtained by rounding ((f_h/f_l)·Φ_l − φ_h)/2π [full text, Sec II-B, Eq 5–7]. Rounding is correct only if the combined error stays below half an order: Δφ_max·(f_h+f_l)/(2π f_l) < 0.5, i.e. Δφ_max < π f_l/(f_h+f_l) [Eq 8–11]. The precision of the result comes from the fine phase; the coarse phase only has to pick the right period.

### Goal: Why does a large coarse-to-fine ratio fail, and what is the standard remedy?
The admissible error shrinks as the frequency ratio grows, so with one unit-frequency pattern MF-TPU "can only reliably unwrap the phase with about 16 periods" under real noise [Sec II-B]. The standard remedy is hierarchical: use "multiple (>2) sets of phases with different frequencies to hierarchically unwrap the wrapped phase step by step", at the cost of extra patterns [Sec II-B]. DL-TPU instead learns the order map, improving reliability under intensity noise, low modulation and projector nonlinearity [Sec III-B]. Fringe-order errors appear as whole-period jumps, not small drifts.
