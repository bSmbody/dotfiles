# Readout Blindness: VLM Scores Miss the Spatial Direction Their Frozen Encoders Retain

## What this paper does
Shows theoretically that image-text scores treating subject and object symmetrically cancel relational direction ("readout blindness"), and proposes Antisymmetric Displacement Readout (ADR): training-free patch-word Sinkhorn alignment on frozen CLIP/SigLIP features, object centroids from excess coupling mass, and a score equal to the signed centroid displacement along the relation axis. Adds prior deflation (derangement null) to measure grounded gain.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism recovers the lost perceptual signal from a frozen encoder, and does it need training?
ADR computes patch-word similarity S, a Sinkhorn coupling, keeps mass above uniform, takes subject/object centroids, and scores D = e_r^T (c_s - c_o) for a parsed relation triple (subject, object, axis) [full text, Sec 5, Prop 1, Fig 2]. No training or learned parameters. A hand-written parsing rule maps each caption relation to an axis [full text, Sec 5 "Directional scoring"]. SigLIP2-ADR gets the largest grounded gains among non-chat readouts on 4 of 5 direction-balanced sets and is competitive with Qwen3.6 chat at ~6% of its FLOPs / 9% runtime [full text, Sec 7.2, Tab 11].

### Goal: Does the readout give a usable reliability signal, and what are its stated limits?
The absolute margin |D| works as confidence: keeping the 30% largest-margin samples yields 99.8% accuracy for CLIP-ADR vs 51.6% for deployed CLIP; Qwen3.6 likelihood/chat reach 73.3%/90.4% on all samples; the authors suggest a validation-chosen threshold to route low-margin items to human review [full text, Sec 7.3, Fig 4c]. Failures come from diffuse localisation (e.g. "dining table") [full text, Sec 7.3]. Limits: scope is directed binary spatial relations between noun pairs; the antisymmetric kernel is derived by hand for this relation family; future work lists "more complex relations beyond noun pairs" and other architectures [full text, Sec 8]. It is not connected to a generative VLM's answer.
