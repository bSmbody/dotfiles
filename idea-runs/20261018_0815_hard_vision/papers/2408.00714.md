# SAM 2: Segment Anything in Images and Videos

## What this paper does
Extends Segment Anything to video with a streaming memory architecture: a Hiera image encoder (MAE-pretrained), memory attention over past frames and prompts, a SAM-style prompt encoder and mask decoder, and a memory bank. Trained on SA-1B plus the new SA-V video data collected with a model-in-the-loop data engine. On images it behaves like SAM: point, box or mask prompts give a mask, with multiple masks predicted for ambiguous single clicks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Can SAM 2 serve as a training-free, point-prompted segmenter on single images (e.g. a click at a gauge hub), and how accurate and fast is it zero-shot?
Yes. Prompts "can be positive/negative clicks, boxes, or masks" and "Spatially, the model behaves similarly to SAM" with a light promptable mask decoder [full text, Sec 3, Sec 4]. For an ambiguous single click it "predict[s] multiple masks" so that a valid mask is always output [Sec 4, "Prompt encoder and mask decoder"]. On the 23 zero-shot SA image/video datasets, 1-click mIoU is 58.9 (SA-1B training) / 61.9 (full mix) vs SAM's 58.1, and 5-click 81.7 / 83.5, at 130 FPS on an A100 vs 21.7 for SAM [Sec 6.3, Tab 5]. Skip connections from the hierarchical encoder add high-resolution embeddings for mask decoding [Sec 4], relevant to thin structures, but the paper reports no thin-structure-specific result, so needle-mask quality must be measured.
