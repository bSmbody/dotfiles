# Efficient Multi-Frequency Phase Unwrapping using Kernel Density Estimation

## What this paper does
For multi-frequency time-of-flight depth (Kinect v2), each modulation frequency gives a wrapped phase; the depth is ambiguous per frequency and must be unwrapped jointly. Instead of the sequential Chinese-remainder (CRT) unwrapping used by libfreenect2, the method enumerates all plausible unwrapping vectors, ranks them with a likelihood weighted by predicted phase noise and a spatial kernel density estimate, and uses that density as a single confidence for outlier rejection (ECCV 2016).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Why does sequential (CRT) unwrapping fail, and what does joint multi-hypothesis ranking change?
CRT unwraps one frequency pair at a time and "is fast but sensitive to noise as it unwraps each of the phase measurements in sequence. The consequence of this is that an error made early on will be propagated" [full text, Sec 2.2]. The proposed method considers "all meaningful unwrapping vectors" within the unambiguous range, including neighbouring unwrappings at wrap-around points (30 hypotheses for Kinect v2), ranks them, and keeps a high-likelihood subset for a fuller spatial density check, so "the true maximum ... is guaranteed to be checked" [Sec 3.2, 3.4]. Phase noise is predicted per measurement from its amplitude, so weak measurements count less [Sec 3.3].

### Goal: How is the ranking reused as a reliability signal, and what does it buy?
A single threshold on the KDE confidence replaces libfreenect2's five hand-tuned outlier filters [Sec 4.5]. At the maximal 8.75 m range it yields about 52% more valid measurements than libfreenect2 while suppressing salt-and-pepper outliers [Abstract, Sec 1, Fig 1]. The density of the winning hypothesis relative to its rivals therefore serves as both selector and abstention signal.
