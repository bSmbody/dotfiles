# Lost in Motion: Vision Language Models Fail the Dynamic Gauges Test

## What this paper does
Introduces the Dynamic Gauge Dataset (DGD): videos of three real instruments (uniform angular-rotation dial, linear scale, Vernier) under several motion and speed profiles, each with an in-band digital chronometer as reference. Evaluates GPT-5.4 Thinking, GPT-5.3 Instant, Gemini 3 Pro and Gemini 3 Flash off-the-shelf with a See-Think-Confirm visual chain-of-thought prompt, against a pass threshold of 5% normalised MAE and Pearson 0.95 (IEEE I&M Magazine 2026).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Do 2026 frontier models (including reasoning variants) still fail on real gauge reading?
Yes. "All evaluated models failed across all configurations", except GPT-5.4 Thinking passing in the opening and closing settings of the linear depth gauge [full text, quantitative paragraph after Fig 4]. Failure modes: wrong baseline value from the first frame, flat lines and staircase readings while the needle moves monotonically, wrong slopes [full text, Figs 2-4 discussion]. Gemini 3 Flash run-to-run: normalised MAE 14.15% ± 3.54%, Pearson 0.606 [full text, footnote 3]. Models read the in-band digital chronometer "with near-perfect accuracy" [full text, dataset section], so OCR is not the bottleneck.

### Goal: What division of labour do the authors recommend?
Hybrid architectures: "traditional deterministic pipelines (e.g., edge detection and Hough transforms)" for localised needle-coordinate tracking, with "VLMs best reserved for higher-level semantic tasks, such as initial instrument type recognition, context-aware calibration", plus rule-based validation wrappers [full text, design guidelines 1-3]. The setting is video (5 Hz frames) of three instruments; it is not a broad single-image benchmark.
