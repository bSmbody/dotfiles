# Hard Vision, Easy Vision: What GPT-6 Astra Reveals Across Computer Vision

## What this paper does
Evaluates GPT-6 Astra and five other frontier general-purpose systems on 34 capabilities / 55 benchmarks across nine areas of computer vision, against specialist models and humans, grouping capabilities into maturity tiers. It also measures how much higher reasoning effort, built-in tool use, and open specialist-as-tool pipelines close the remaining gaps, and at what inference-cost ratio.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Which perceptual capabilities remain below specialist/human level for the frontier model, and does more reasoning effort close them?
Gaps persist where output must be metrically precise, pixel-faithful, temporally consistent, or depends on fine-grained domain knowledge: depth estimation and multiview reconstruction (sensitive to metric scale, local surface geometry), video segmentation (precise boundaries, small structures, separation of nearby instances), faithful restoration, fine-scale remote-sensing grounding, and pathology nucleus typing (14.1-23.8 vs specialist 57.1 / human 82.0) [full text, Sec 3, Sec 5 "Task Properties Associated With the Remaining Gap"]. Higher reasoning brings 2D spatial reasoning and 3D detection to specialist level, but segmentation gains "plateau at higher effort and remain insufficient", and additional effort gives smaller gains in already-strong perception settings; higher reasoning costs up to 13.22x baseline, sometimes for small improvements [full text, Sec 6, Fig 8].

### Goal: What tool/specialist mechanism does it use, and what does it report as the limitation?
Tools: built-in tool use (e.g. counting by first pointing to individual objects, then counting) at 1.04-1.20x cost; specialist-as-tool pipelines where segmentation models supply instances for counting and depth models support distance comparison; dedicated grounding models for remote sensing [full text, Sec 6]. Benefits "vary across capabilities". The paper names as open: "determine when the generalist should act directly, when it should call a specialist, and how it should verify the resulting output, while accounting for accuracy, cost, and latency"; specialist tools are suggested for metric geometry, dense correspondence, restoration and fine-grained rare domains [full text, Sec 7]. It is an evaluation paper; it proposes no mechanism for arbitration or verification, and specialist pipelines exist only where a named specialist task (segmentation, depth, grounding) matches the query.
