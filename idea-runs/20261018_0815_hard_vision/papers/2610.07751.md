# How Well Do LLMs Reason with Noisy Evidence? An Active Visual Reasoning Benchmark

## What this paper does
VisualNoiseQA: a text-only LLM reasoner solves VQA by repeatedly querying a fixed off-the-shelf VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or InternVL3.5-8B), treated as a stateless noisy sensor. Each query draws K samples, and an LLM-judged self-consistency count u is shown to the reasoner as a black-box uncertainty signal. Instances are kept where the sensor is self-inconsistent but blind humans can still solve the task. Compares ReAct, belief-state prompting, UoT and human annotators, and probes belief trajectories and query types.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism decides whether to call a tool and whether to trust its output over the model's own reading?
The LLM reasoner decides through prompting (ReAct or belief-state prompting) what to ask, how to aggregate answers and "when to trust or override" the sensor. The trust signal is the self-consistency count u^(t) over K samples [full text, Section 3.1, Eqs 2-3].

### Goal: What signal (and at what granularity) is the trust/necessity decision based on, and does it require the VLM's weights or logits?
Self-consistency per query, black-box, at the cost of K samples per sensor call (K=5; raising it to 11 changes accuracy little) [full text, Section 3.1, Section 4.6]. No weights are needed.

### Goal: What limitation does it report or show on handling incorrect tool outputs or confidently wrong VLM readings?
When the VLM is confidently wrong, consistency "becomes a trap". On CVQA the true signal can hurt, being "a noisy or even adversarial proxy for reliability" [full text, Section 4.2 ablation, Section 4.6]. Reasoners "often stubbornly accept the highest-confidence response" and commit prematurely. The weaker reasoner anchors after one step in 46% of trajectories [full text, Sections 4.4, 4.6]. SFT self-distillation of successful traces gives no consistent gains [full text, Section 4.3]. Strong reasoners stay well below humans (40-60% vs 70-90%) [full text, Section 4.2].
