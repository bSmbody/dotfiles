# Learning to Read Analog Gauges from Synthetic Data

## What this paper does
A two-stage CNN gauge reader (DeepLabv3 segmentation of case/needle, then a MobileNetV2/EfficientNet reading network classifying the angles of the start marker, end marker and needle tip into 360 bins) trained only on 12,000 Blender-rendered synthetic gauges with randomised needle, case, sticker range, tint, dust, reflections, shadows, camera and light. The reading follows from the three angles plus the known unit and range. Evaluated on the Howells et al. real test set (6 gauges, 2,700 frames) and a new real landmark dataset of 4,813 images (WACV 2024).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does a needle-angle reader built only from rendered gauges transfer to real gauge photographs, and with what error?
Yes, with careful randomisation and augmentation. On the Howells real test set the mean absolute needle-angle error is 7.30° (EfficientNet-B2) / 7.82° (MobileNetV2), 4.94° on the corrected labels, versus 9.49° for the prior synthetic-trained keypoint model [full text, Sec 5.1, Tab 1]. On the real landmark dataset errors are about 2.3-5.5° depending on condition (natural lighting 2.29°, far camera 2.48°, dim light 3.20°, glass reflection 2.37° for B2) [full text, Sec 5.2, Tab 2]. No real training images are used [full text, Sec 3.1].

### Goal: How is the reading decomposed, and what does that imply about which part is hard?
The reading is factored into geometric landmarks (start marker, end marker, needle tip angles) and gauge metadata (unit, range) supplied separately; the network only localises angles [full text, Sec 3.2, Fig 3]. The authors note earlier deep readers "overfit the visual appearance of the gauges in the training set", including hybrid data where real needles are artificially rotated [full text, Sec 2]. The approach still needs a trained network per instrument family (circular single-needle gauges) and a Hough-circle crop for small gauges [full text, Sec 5.1].
