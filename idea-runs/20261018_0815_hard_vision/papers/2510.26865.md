# Do Vision-Language Models Measure Up? Benchmarking Visual Measurement Reading with MeasureBench

## What this paper does
Introduces MeasureBench: 2,442 instrument-reading questions, 1,272 human-annotated real-world images (711 dial, 361 linear, 96 digital, 104 composite; 26 instrument types) and 1,170 synthetic images from 39 procedurally generated appearances (2D LLM-drafted programmatic renderers plus 3D Blender scenes). Scoring is interval matching on the value plus unit matching. Evaluates 18 VLMs, tests thinking vs no-thinking, prompt variants, earlier special-purpose gauge readers, and GRPO/SFT on synthetic data.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Do frontier VLMs still fail on real dial instruments, and does more reasoning help?
Yes. Best model Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 30.2% overall on real images (31.5% on real dials), GPT-5 19.8% (18.3% dials), Claude-Opus-4.1 14.3% [full text, Sec 3.1, Tab 2]. Thinking (up to 10,240 reasoning tokens) "yields very little improvement, sometimes even degrades performance" across six hybrid models [full text, Sec 3.2, Fig 5]. Larger LLMs on the same ViT do not read better [full text, Sec 3.1]. Clocks and multi-needle dials are hardest for all models [full text, Sec 3.1, Fig 4].

### Goal: Which sub-skills do the models get right, and which fail?
Unit recognition is >90% for nearly all models (Gemini-2.5-Pro 96.2% unit vs 30.7% value). Models identify the instrument, locate the indicator roughly, infer unit and major tick spacing, then fail on "pointer localization: one minor tick left/right changes the reading" and minor-tick counting [full text, Sec 3.1, Sec 3.4, Fig 6]. Value reading, i.e. precise pointer/tick localisation, is the bottleneck; the paper names "fine-grained spatial grounding" as the fundamental limit.

### Goal: Does synthetic data transfer to real images, and how do specialist pipelines generalise?
Weakly. GRPO on 3,900 synthetic images lifts Qwen2.5-VL-7B synthetic 10.9→35.2 but real only 14.6→19.7; answer-SFT on the same data lowers real accuracy (14.6→12.5), rationale-SFT to 8.8 [full text, Sec 3.7 Tab 6, Sec 4.2 Tab 8]. Earlier special-purpose gauge readers barely generalise: Reitsma et al. 8.5% overall on real, Shu et al. 4.2% value [full text, Sec 3.8, Tab 7]. So trained readers overfit their training renders.

### Goal: How is the synthetic subset generated (could a frontier-written renderer copy it)?
The 2D back-end is LLM-drafted code from a prompt template, verified then registered; the 3D back-end randomises pointer angle, range, camera and lighting on Blender assets [full text, Sec 2.4, App C]. A frontier-written renderer could therefore resemble the synthetic subset's generator; the real-world subset (web, team photos, vendor images) has no generator [full text, Sec 2.3].


### Goal: How strict is MeasureBench's value scoring, what does the real "dial" subset contain, and where exactly do earlier gauge pipelines break?
Scoring is interval matching: each sample has one or more ground-truth candidates with a closed numeric interval set by annotators ("any value within this interval is considered correct"), plus optional unit substrings; fully-correct needs value and unit for the same candidate [full text, Sec 2.2-2.3]. The paper does not state a global tolerance rule (e.g. half a minor tick); intervals are per image. "Dial" means analog instruments with one or more pointers: single-pointer ammeters and pressure gauges, and clocks with two or three hands; multi-dial electricity meters with five pointers (adjacent dials rotating in opposite directions) are discussed as hardest [full text, Sec 2.1, App D.1]. The two earlier pipelines (Shu et al. 2023; Reitsma et al. 2024, ICRA, not the Leon-Alcazar synthetic-data reader) were run on the dial subset and fail "at different components including gauge detection, number recognition (OCR), and pointer segmentation"; their trained components "more or less overfit their training data", and OCR unit recognition is far below VLMs (17.8% vs 96.2%) [full text, Sec 3.8, Tab 7]. Related work notes classical pipelines "generalize poorly across device types, design variations, viewpoints, glare/occlusion" [full text, Sec 5].
