# Small Vision-Language Models Know When They Are Wrong But Cannot Say So: A Two-Model Study of Stated versus Internal Confidence Under Realistic Image Degradation

## What this paper does
Compares two deferral signals for Qwen2-VL-2B and SmolVLM under six photographic degradations at three severities (about 3,800 predictions): verbalised confidence (a 0-100 number in the prompt) and internal mean token probability over the generated answer. Measures error-detection AUROC and calibration.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism decides whether to call a tool and whether to trust its output over the model's own reading?
No tool. It studies the signal a deferral rule would threshold: verbalised confidence vs mean token probability c_int [full text, Section 3.4, Eq 1].

### Goal: What signal (and at what granularity) is the trust/necessity decision based on, and does it require the VLM's weights or logits?
Per instance. Verbalised confidence needs only text, while c_int needs per-token output distributions [full text, Section 3.4].

### Goal: What limitation does it report or show on handling incorrect tool outputs or confidently wrong VLM readings?
Qwen2-VL's verbalised confidence sits at 0.87-0.90 in every condition and detects errors at chance (AUROC about 0.50). At severe low light it stays at 0.87 while accuracy is 0.22 [full text, Section 4.2]. Token probability separates errors with AUROC 0.92-0.99 under mild degradation, but "neither signal tracks catastrophic failure". Internal AUROC falls to 0.54-0.62 (CI includes 0.5) at the severity where perception collapses [full text, Section 4.5, Table 3]. The authors recommend "an explicit image-quality check upstream of the model, not a confidence threshold downstream of it" [full text, Section 5.2].
