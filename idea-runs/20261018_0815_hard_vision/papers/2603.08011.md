# It's Time to Get It Right: Improving Analog Clock Reading and Clock-Hand Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

## What this paper does
Introduces TickTockVQA, 12,483 human-annotated real-world analog clock images (7,236 train / 5,247 test; wall, tower, wrist, post, desk, illustrated clocks; Arabic, Roman, no numerals; flipped and partial views), with hour/minute (and AM/PM where inferable) labels, and Swap-DPO, a LoRA-SFT + DPO recipe whose rejected answers swap hour and minute hands. Base models: Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Llama-3.2-11B, Gemma3-12B (CVPR 2026 Findings).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How well do current models and the synthetic-trained specialist read real clocks zero-shot?
Near chance: full-time accuracy 1.05-6.04% for open 3-12B VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B 6.04%, MAE 148.62 min); the synthetic-to-real specialist It's About Time reaches 18.54% full-time, MAE 135.15 [full text, Tab 1]. GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini-2.5 Pro are shown failing on examples only, no table numbers [full text, Fig 2]; the intro states leading multimodal models are below 10% on realistic clock benchmarks [full text, Sec 1]. Hour/minute hand confusion is a characteristic error [full text, Sec 5.3].

### Goal: Does synthetic training data transfer to real clocks, and does photorealism help?
Only partly. Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned on OpenCV-rendered SynClock reaches 16.12% full-time (MAE 129.51), on diffusion-restyled CtrlClock 14.75%, on real TickTockVQA 20.34% (MAE 104.31) [full text, Sec 5.4.2, Tab 3]. The authors attribute CtrlClock's loss to diffusion breaking "the absolute spatial fidelity of the clock hands"; spatially exact but less realistic renders transfer better than photorealistic ones for this task [full text, Sec 5.4.2, Sec 6].
