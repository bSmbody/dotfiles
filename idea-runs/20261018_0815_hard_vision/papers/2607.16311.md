# Seeing What Is Actually There: PriVE-Bench and PriVE-Tools for Counterfactual Evaluation of Agentic Visual Evidence in VLMs

## What this paper does
A diagnostic benchmark of paired original and counterfactual images across five domains (object existence, counting, fashion, industry, medical modality). It measures whether VLMs answer from pixels or from priors (the prior-following error rate, PFER). PriVE-Tools gives fixed, non-semantic evidence views (box overlays, crops, zoom panels, contours) as controlled interventions. This isolates whether extra evidence views help, independently of any tool-selection policy. Evaluates GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4 and open VLMs.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism decides whether to call a tool and whether to trust its output over the model's own reading?
None by design: evidence views are fixed per condition to remove "tool-selection policies, planning quality ... or model-specific agent behavior" [full text, Section 4]. It measures only whether the VLM uses the evidence once shown.

### Goal: What signal (and at what granularity) is the trust/necessity decision based on, and does it require the VLM's weights or logits?
Not applicable; black-box evaluation of API and open models [full text, Sections 5-6].

### Goal: What limitation does it report or show on handling incorrect tool outputs or confidently wrong VLM readings?
Across all models, crops lower accuracy by 0.9 points and zoom panels by 0.2 [full text, Section 6, RQ3]. Closed-source models gain, while open ones are weak or negative. For counting, "even the best displayed tool, Zoom panel, decreases accuracy by 3.1 points and increases PFER by 1.0 point, indicating that localization or magnification alone does not solve enumeration" [full text, Section 6, RQ4]. GPT-5 falls from 78.3% to 46.7% on counterfactual inputs, and errors are mostly confident prior-consistent answers rather than refusals [full text, RQ1]. Showing correct evidence therefore does not stop the model from overriding it with its prior.
