# Vision language models are blind: Failing to translate detailed visual features into words

## What this paper does
Introduces BlindTest, seven synthetic low-level tasks (line intersections, two circles overlapping/touching, circled letter, overlapping shapes, nested squares, grid rows/columns, single-colour paths) on which frontier VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude 3.5) average ~58% versus ~100% humans. Tests slow-thinking models, prompting variants, spacing manipulations, and linear probes on frozen vision-encoder features of open VLMs.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does longer reasoning or prompting fix these perceptual failures?
No. Gemini 2.0 Flash-Thinking and QVQ perform "even worse than their regular counterpart" on BlindTest [full text, Sec 4.7, Tab 4]. 2-shot examples and meta-prompting ("describe the image in detail first") lower GPT-4o/Sonnet-3.5 accuracy on two-circles (Sonnet 91.66 baseline -> 90.53 meta / 77.93 2-shot) [full text, App 0.D.1, Tab T2]; CoT also does not help [full text, Sec 8]. Asking Sonnet-3.5 to reconstruct the image as SVG/HTML/JS/PIL code fails to preserve the key features (number of intersections, touching vs not) [full text, App 0.E.1]. Failures concentrate where primitives overlap or are close; accuracy approaches 100% when spacing is increased [full text, Sec 5].

### Goal: Is the needed signal present in the vision encoder, and how is it read out?
Yes. Logistic-regression linear probes on average-pooled frozen patch features (before and after the projector) of LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B (SigLIP) and Phi-3.5 (CLIP) reach >=99.47% on two-circles and line-intersection tasks, while the same VLMs answer far lower; the authors conclude the LLM "fail[s] to decode this information into correct language outputs" [full text, Sec 6, Fig 14, Tab 5]. The readout is a supervised per-task classifier trained on 4.4k-8.9k synthetic images of that exact task; probes trained on hard (close) cases generalise to easy ones but not vice versa [full text, Sec 6]. No question-conditioned or training-free readout is proposed, and the probe is a diagnostic, not a deployable method.

### Goal: How were the probes' training data generated and does a probe trained on one parameter range transfer to another?
The probe data come from the same generator as the test items: 11,100 two-circle images with uniformly distributed distances (8,880 train) and 6,300 line-intersection images (4,410 train); patch features before the projector are average-pooled and a logistic regression (L2=1.0, 1,000 epochs) is fit per task [full text, Sec 6 "Tasks", "Method", App 0.N.1, Tab T18]. Train and test share the generator and visual complexity. A probe trained on near-boundary cases (circle distance ±0.05) is 100% on far cases (±0.25), but a probe trained on far cases drops by at least 18% on near cases [full text, Sec 6, App 0.N.2, Fig F48]. No cross-generator or natural-image transfer is tested.
