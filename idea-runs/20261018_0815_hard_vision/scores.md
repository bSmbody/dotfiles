### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=feasibility)`
- **Evidence**:
  - *Grounding: mostly accurate.* [2504.17660] does what C1 says: TabPFN in context on (θ, x) simulation pairs, no per-simulator training, wins at 10^2 simulations, unbiased nearest-simulation filtering, more graceful under misspecification [Sec 2.1–3.3]. C1 also states the paper's own limit correctly: with an embedding net fitted separately from the estimator, NPE-PFN "usually does not beat" trained NPE, and independent compression "may discard critical information" [App B.3/D.4]. That limit is exactly C1's setting, since the frozen encoder is a summary statistic not chosen for the quantity. [2407.06581] is cited correctly: probes reach ≥99.47% on average-pooled features, but at full dimension with 4.4k–8.9k same-generator images, only on two tasks, with no cross-generator test. Training on far cases loses ≥18% on near cases.
  - *Grounding: one stretch.* C1 uses [2607.11007] to support "a few hundred renders suffice". That paper tests only in-distribution discrete classification and says "domain shift and regression are untested". C1 needs both: a count or length is a regression target, and render→real is a domain shift. The evidence C1 cites does not cover the regime its key assumption lives in.
  - *Novelty: the cross-domain recombination is closer than claimed.* (a) An LLM writing the simulator and SBI inferring parameters is already published [2607.17540], including image observations (gravitational lensing). There the estimators are trained flows and ratio estimators, and counts from images come out "only weakly identified" when the signal is near pixel scale. (b) A test-time synthetic labelled support set read by a frozen encoder with a training-free head is SuS-X [2211.16198]. Query-specific synthetic exemplars matched in frozen-backbone space for counting is RECOUNT [2608.20621], but there the synthetic data carries category identity and a frozen counter supplies the count. (c) My searches did not find a published method in which a frontier model writes a quantity-parametrised renderer at test time and a frozen ≤3B encoder plus an in-context estimator reads the quantity as evidence for a black-box VLM. Searches included "simulation-based inference vision language model", "test-time synthetic data VLM counting", "synthetic support set", "program synthesis counting", "VLM creates tools", "synthetic images counting zero-shot", "renders frozen features", "LLM code synthetic training images" and "sim-to-real frozen features", each also with `--since` 2025-10. The in-domain delta over [2407.06581], [2608.00726] and [2601.11109] is real. C1 should present it as "SuS-X/RECOUNT-style test-time synthesis, extended from categories to measured quantities and handed to a black-box frontier model", not as a distant recombination.
  - *Feasibility (weakest): the key transfer assumption is not supported where it matters and is degenerate where it holds.* On BlindTest-style synthetic tasks the frontier model can write a renderer that nearly reproduces the benchmark's own generator, so render→real transfer is plausible there. There, though, C1 reduces to [2407.06581]'s same-generator probe with fewer samples and an in-context head, and a panel will call it re-implementing the benchmark generator. On natural images (gear photos, crowds) the real image will sit off the render manifold. Both SuS-X [2211.16198, Sec 4.4] and NPE-PFN's misspecification study report degradation once the synthetic/real gap is large. C1's abstain rule then decides coverage, and nothing bounds it. RECOUNT [2608.20621, Sec 3.3.1] also notes that a 32×32 patch grid merges dense instances, which limits pooled-feature readout of high-cardinality counts such as teeth or crowds.
  - *Coherence risk inside the mapping.* C1 inherits TabPFN's sweet spot of PCA to d≈8–32 [2607.11007]. Unsupervised PCA keeps the highest-variance directions, and those are the nuisance parameters C1 deliberately varies (colour, position, width). The quantity is a faint direction: [2608.00726] shows a nuisance-to-signal ratio that collapses global readouts under clutter, and [2608.20621] finds discriminative detail "beneath a dominant signal". The dimension regime where the in-context head is accurate is therefore the regime most likely to discard the quantity. A fix may exist, such as nearest-neighbour filtering first so that style variance is removed before reduction, but C1 does not say so. As written, the mapping step "summary statistic = pooled features reduced to a few dozen dims" is unsupported.
  - *Significance bar.* The cited GPT-6 Astra evaluation [2609.35718] names metric depth, segmentation boundaries, small structures and similar tasks as the remaining gaps. It does not list BlindTest-style primitives. C1 must name a standard benchmark where the frontier model still fails, the renderer is not a copy of that benchmark's generator, and a committed effect size is credible. At present the claim is directional only.
  - *Mapping and contribution type: pass.* The role mapping is explicit: renderer = simulator, answer range = prior, frozen encoder = summary statistic, TabPFN = posterior estimator, filtering and truncation = render selection and re-rendering, distance test = misspecification check. C1 names the key assumption explicitly. It is a methodological contribution (a new inference pipeline), not analysis or a benchmark.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The load-bearing assumption is that the quantity's direction in pooled, PCA-reduced frozen-encoder features transfers from a frontier-written render to the real image, and none of the cited sources test it (TabPFN-on-embeddings is in-distribution classification only; NPE-PFN's separately fixed embedding "may discard critical information"); on synthetic benchmarks the method degenerates into re-writing the benchmark's generator ([2407.06581]'s probe with fewer samples), and on natural images it falls off the render manifold, so the revision must commit to a task family and benchmark where render→real transfer is non-trivial yet plausible, with a cross-generator evaluation.


### C2 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=feasibility)`
- **Evidence**:
  - *The revision fixes C1's main faults.* C2 commits to one family, real-photo test sets with no generator to copy (MeasureBench real dials, TickTockVQA), a cross-generator protocol and a committed number. The frontier failure is well grounded. [2510.26865] reports 31.5% on real dials against 96.2% unit accuracy. Its errors are "one minor tick left/right", and thinking gives "very little improvement" [Sec 3.1–3.4]. [2604.22829] shows 2026 frontier models failing real gauges while reading in-band digits near-perfectly. [2604.26614, Tab 4] has Gemini-3.1-Pro within one minute on 22% of clean clocks. The within-sweep comparison is a sound answer to C1's pooled-feature objection. TriSCA's probe compares whole-image features *across* appearances, not counterfactual states within one appearance [2604.26614, Sec 3.4]. The cls/global descriptor carries "limited information about the object pose" [2311.18809, Sec 4.3].
  - *Grounding: FoundPose is described correctly, but its precondition does not hold here.* [2311.18809] uses frozen DINOv2 layer-18 patch descriptors, bag-of-words template retrieval, kNN patch matching with PnP, and featuremetric refinement, all without training [Sec 3–4.2]. That is accurate. But FoundPose assumes an *exact textured 3D model* and a correct mask. Wrong masks alone cost 6–19 AR, and the 14-px patch grid limits precision enough to need a refinement stage [Sec 3.1, 3.5, 4.3]. C2's "CAD model" is an approximate frontier-written program. The analogy therefore carries over the matching mechanism, not the regime in which FoundPose was shown to work.
  - *Grounding: two stretches.* (a) [2308.14583] shows that render→real needle-angle transfer reaches 2–5° error [Tab 2]. But that comes from a *trained* CNN with heavy domain randomisation over 12k renders, which classifies the start, end and needle angles. It shows that rendered geometry carries enough signal. It does not show that frozen-feature matching against an untrained, approximate render recovers that signal. Note also that 2–5° is about one minor tick on a 270° dial with 50–100 divisions. (b) In [2603.08011, Tab 3], "spatially exact renders transfer better than photorealistic ones" is a difference of 16.12% vs 14.75% after *fine-tuning* a VLM, and both trail real-data training at 20.34%. That is weak support for a training-free comparison.
  - *Feasibility (weakest): the reading depends on the scale fit as much as on the needle, and C2's scale fit is the least supported step.* The reading equals the needle angle mapped through arc span, zero position and tick spacing, and MeasureBench scores against per-image value intervals [2510.26865, Sec 2.2]. An error of one tick in the fitted arc gives the same wrong answer as an error of one tick in the needle. C2 fits arc span, zero offset and tilt by needle-masked patch matching of an approximate render. Tick rings are periodic, so a patch-similarity objective can lock onto a layout shifted by one minor or major division with almost the same score. That is exactly the "one minor tick" error MeasureBench reports. Rendered numerals break the periodicity only if the program reproduces their font, size and radial placement, which is the same precision the frontier lacks. C2 does not say how the layout fit reaches sub-tick accuracy, or why the needle's few-pixel-wide signal dominates the score differences once the layout is slightly off. Its key assumption names this condition, but no cited evidence makes it plausible.
  - *Novelty: the delta is real.* Searches covered "gauge reading training-free", "analog gauge reading vision language model", "render and compare gauge", "analog clock reading", "pointer meter reading", "dial reading", "clock reading reference images", "analysis by synthesis clock", "inverse graphics gauge", "meter reading template matching", "counterfactual renders" and "gauge reading agent", plus `--since` 2025-10 recency runs of the main queries. They found no method in which a frontier model writes a per-image dial renderer whose needle sweep is scored against the photo by a frozen encoder. The recent hits (a Qwen2.5-VL QLoRA gauge study, Aug 2026; the InSituMeasure benchmark, Sep 2026; the DialBench/MRLM trained reader, Nov 2025; a federated segmentation + polar-unwrap meter reader, Sep 2026; a T2I clock-bias study, Oct 2026) were judged from abstracts only and not read. They are trained readers or evaluations, not this mechanism. Among the papers read, VIGA [2601.11109] uses the VLM itself as comparator over up to 10 rounds, and [2604.26614] and [2308.14583] are trained.
  - *Significance: the obvious hybrid baseline is missing.* The dynamic-gauge authors recommend VLMs for "instrument type recognition, context-aware calibration" and deterministic geometry (edge detection, Hough) for the needle [2604.22829]. The panel will ask whether "frontier supplies range and unit, plus a training-free needle-line and tick fit on the rectified crop" already gets most of the gain. [2510.26865, Sec 3.8] partly answers this: earlier pipelines break at detection, OCR and pointer segmentation on MeasureBench. Those pipelines used trained components and their own OCR, though, not a frontier model for the semantics. C2 must name this baseline and say which failure (glare, shadowed needle, tapered or counterweighted needles, non-linear scales) its render-compare handles that a line fit cannot. Otherwise the DINOv2 sweep looks like a heavier route to the same result.
  - *Committed effect: no evidence behind the number.* "≥50% on 711 real dials" rests on no pilot. The dial subset includes clocks with 2–3 hands and five-pointer electricity meters [2510.26865, Sec 2.1, App D.1], where the joint sweep and the layout are hardest, so the abstain rate there sets the ceiling. The frontier's own MeasureBench number is a 2025 figure (Gemini-2.5-Pro). The GPT-6-class baseline must be measured before any delta is claimed.
  - *Mapping, transfer assumption and contribution type: pass.* The roles are explicit. The program is the CAD model, the detector crop and rectification are the mask and perspective crop, the needle-masked layout variants are template retrieval, the needle sweep is the pose hypotheses, the local sweep is featuremetric refinement, and peak sharpness is confidence or abstention. The key assumption is stated. The contribution is a new training-free inference pipeline, so it is methodological.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: A MeasureBench reading is only correct if the needle *and* the fitted scale (arc span, zero, tick spacing) are each within about a minor tick, but C2 fits the scale by patch-matching an approximate frontier-written render against periodic tick rings, a step that FoundPose's exact-CAD-model evidence does not cover and that is prone to exactly the one-tick aliasing that defines the failure; the revision should take the scale from the photo's own ticks and numerals (frontier supplies values, local geometry supplies their angles), keep the counterfactual sweep for the needle only, and beat a frontier + classical needle-line hybrid baseline in a small pilot before committing to ≥50%.
