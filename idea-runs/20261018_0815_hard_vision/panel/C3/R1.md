# R1 (area expert) review of C3: "Unwrapping the Dial"

Venue: CVPR 2027 main track. I judge a results-masked proposal and assume every falsifiable prediction comes out exactly at its stated threshold.

## Summary
The paper reads analog dials and clocks without training. One default-effort GPT-6 Astra call reads only the semantics: the values of numeral boxes found by DBNet, the unit, the scale type, minors per major and pointer roles. Every angle is measured locally: ellipse rectification, a polar-unwrapped tick lattice, and a needle found by pasting needle silhouettes and comparing frozen DINOv2 layer-18 patches. A small exhaustive search over neighbouring "labelling hypotheses" (numeral one major tick over, one missing or extra minor tick, scale direction, hand roles) picks the reading, and its score margin decides when to abstain and fall back to Astra. The authors predict: +15 points of value+unit accuracy over the best Astra configuration on 511 held-out MeasureBench real dials at ≤1.2x Astra-default cost; +20 points of exact-minute accuracy on 1,000 TickTockVQA clocks; +8 points over the same pipeline with a Hough needle; and margin-based error detection at AUROC ≥0.80, giving ≥75% selective accuracy at ≥50% coverage.

## Strengths
- The motivating gap is real, recent and well documented. Frontier models get the unit right on MeasureBench but miss the value: 96.2% unit vs 31.5% value on real dials, clocks are hardest, and they often answer "10:10" [2510.26865]. 2026 frontier models still fail real gauges while reading digits almost perfectly [2604.22829]. Astra-class effort scaling does not close fine-geometry gaps [2609.35718]. A training-free method that helps an API-only model fits the venue's current interest in "what GPT-6 still cannot see".
- The division of labour is principled. The frontier never supplies an angle and its own reading never centres a search window. There is an explicit ablation of a window centred on the frontier's reading. The abstention signal never goes through the frontier's own visual readout. This answers a known weakness of self-verification arbiters.
- The evaluation is unusually disciplined for the area:
  - a pilot/test split fixed in advance, with tags assigned before any run;
  - paired-bootstrap sizing;
  - stated kill conditions and a stated fallback paper (hybrid B) if the needle sweep fails;
  - measured API cost;
  - oracle splits (ground-truth needle, ground-truth labelling) that make the paper informative even in failure;
  - the strongest frontier baseline (xhigh effort with built-in Python/crop tools), which a specialist would otherwise demand.
- Joint enumeration of numeral, lattice and hand-role hypotheses is set against sequential rounding and against MeterFL's count-from-start decoder on the same lattice. That ablation isolates a concrete, plausible failure mode: one missed tick shifts every later index [2609.31998].
- It handles non-linear scales piecewise, which Reitsma explicitly excluded [2404.08785], and multi-dial meters through decade coupling.

## Weaknesses
- **[major] Novelty is mostly in the assembly.** The authors' own limitations section concedes that the phase-unwrapping framing "mostly re-describes ordinary tick counting". The remaining pieces each have a close precedent:
  - 2604.22829 already recommends the semantics-to-VLM, needle-to-deterministic-geometry split.
  - Numeral anchoring with outlier rejection is Reitsma's RANSAC angle-to-value fit [2404.08785].
  - Polar unwrapping and local interpolation between flanking ticks come from MeterFL [2609.31998].
  - The joint 720-state clock space is the output space of the CVPR 2022 clock reader [2111.09162]; it is learned there and enforced geometrically here.
  - Render-and-compare on frozen DINOv2 layer-18 patches is FoundPose [2311.18809], moved to 2D.

  What is new: a ±1-neighbourhood MAP search with a hand-weighted score and a margin, plus a 2D silhouette sweep. A reviewer who has seen this year's gauge papers will call it a well-engineered task-specific pipeline. The insight that survives is "don't let the VLM measure", which the two 2026 evaluation papers already state. The paper will be judged mainly on the size of its numbers.
- **[major] It skips the field's documented failure point: training-free geometry on in-the-wild photos.** The gain depends on every stage working on uncurated web images: Grounding DINO crop, RANSAC ellipse fit on radial edges, pivot estimate, radial-profile tick peaks, DBNet boxes. History is discouraging:
  - A Sobel+Hough clock reader scores 7.7–9.7% on COCO/OpenImages/Clock Movies, against ~80% for a trained reader [2111.09162, App. 12].
  - Reitsma's modular pipeline scores 8.5% on MeasureBench real images, failing at detection, OCR and needle segmentation [2510.26865].
  - Ellipse fitting fails with fewer than 5 detected notches [2404.08785].

  The proposal's answer is abstention plus fallback. So a +15-point gain at 100% coverage requires roughly ≥50% coverage at ≥75% selective accuracy on uncurated dials, both at once. G0 gates this, but the pilot gives no component-level evidence yet. This is the main reason my p_core is low.
- **[major] The frontier is asked for a fine-geometric quantity.** It must report "minor divisions per major interval", which enters S(h) through p(n_k | M). Minor-tick counting is exactly the sub-skill MeasureBench identifies as failing [2510.26865]. This contradicts the "frontier never reads geometry" claim and is a channel for correlated errors.

  Reading the value inside numbered box #j on a dense dial is also a set-of-marks grounding task, which VLMs get wrong on small, crowded labels. Neither error rate is measured as a component metric.
- **[major] The baselines for the needle and lattice are weak.** Hybrid B pairs the ranked lattice with a Hough/line needle, which is the weakest classical choice. The obvious 2026 training-free baselines are missing:
  - a point-prompted promptable segmenter (SAM-family, prompted at the hub) followed by a line or skeleton fit;
  - a radial line-integral (Radon-at-pivot) profile of needle-colour likelihood;
  - feeding Astra the rectified, zoomed or polar-unwrapped strip with no local measurement. This tests whether the gain comes from measurement or from a better view.

  Beating Hough by 8 points says little about whether the DINOv2 paste-and-compare sweep is needed.
- **[major] The test is in-distribution to the tuning and too narrow for the generality claim.** Five hand-set weights and thresholds are fit on 200 MeasureBench dials and tested on the other 511 of the same benchmark. "Training-free, cannot overfit designs" is the paper's central selling point against TriSCA, DialBench and Swap-DPO, yet no real-image dial benchmark is held out with frozen weights.

  Two candidates exist:
  - InSituMeasure (Sept 2026): 2,922 real industrial scenes with view-deviation, occlusion and pointer-like-interference tags, plus answerability labels and selective-risk metrics that fit the abstention claim exactly [2609.04014]. Its release status needs checking.
  - MeterFL's real test images [2609.31998], used for reading accuracy and not only as a component diagnostic.

  On clocks, the CVPR 2022 reader [2111.09162] is the standard specialist baseline. TickTockVQA reports it at 18.54% [2603.08011], and it should appear in the clock table.
- **[minor] The effect sizes are anchored to an unmeasured Astra.** All published frontier numbers are 2025–early 2026. If Astra xhigh with Python tools already reaches 45–50% on real dials (plausible: it can run its own Hough transform), +15 points means ≥60–65%. That is far above anything training-free reported on real images. The kill condition handles this, but it raises the risk.
- **[minor] The paste-and-compare signal works at the wrong scale.** At a 448 px crop, ViT-L/14 gives 32×32 patches of 14 px. A 2–4 px needle is a small fraction of each patch it touches. For white-on-white or low-contrast needles, the very cases claimed as wins, a paste changes features little anywhere, so the peak may be flat. The native-resolution tip refinement then carries the precision, which raises the question of what DINOv2 adds over pixel-space template matching. The proposal should report peak sharpness by nuisance tag.
- **[minor] The schedule is tight for a small team.** Building the full pipeline and hand-annotating 200 dials (tick angles, assignments, needle angles) both fall in 10-19 to 10-25. The constraints say engineering risk counts against an idea.
- **[minor] Scope is narrow.** Dials and clocks only. MeasureBench's linear, digital and composite items (561 of 1,272 real images) are excluded, which limits the broad-audience appeal at CVPR.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2604.22829] (Lost in Motion, IEEE I&M Mag. 2026): recommends exactly this hybrid, with VLMs for semantics and calibration and deterministic geometry for the needle. It does not build or evaluate it on single images. Does not kill novelty, but makes the main idea anticipated; the paper must cite it as the source of the design principle.
- [2404.08785] (Reitsma et al., ICRA 2024): numeral-anchored scale with RANSAC outlier rejection plus a needle line fit. C3 replaces OCR recognition with the frontier and adds minor ticks and ranking. Does not kill novelty; it is the direct ancestor, and hybrid A covers it.
- [2609.31998] (MeterFL, Sept 2026): polar unwrap, tick lattice, local interpolation. C3's measurement stage is MeterFL's decoder without the trained segmenter, plus local counting. Partial overlap, not killing; the count-from-start vs local ablation is the right response.
- [2111.09162] (It's About Time, CVPR 2022): joint 720-state clock reading, about 80% on COCO/OpenImages clocks. It also documents that edge/Hough geometry collapses below 10% in the wild. Not killing (it is trained, not training-free), but it is a missing baseline and strong negative evidence for training-free clock geometry.
- [2311.18809] (FoundPose): frozen-DINOv2 render-and-compare. C3's needle sweep is a 2D, low-parameter case of it. Not killing, but it limits how novel the sweep is.
- [2603.08011] (TickTockVQA / Swap-DPO, CVPR 2026 Findings): uses theta_h = 30h + m/2 to build training negatives. Using it as an inference-time constraint is a small step. Not killing.
- [2609.04014] (InSituMeasure, Sept 2026): a benchmark only, so it is no threat to novelty. It is the obvious second real-dial test set and is not used.
- [2608.17723] (VLM gauge-reading study, Aug 2026): finds that QLoRA-trained VLMs transfer poorly and token confidence gives confident errors, which motivates independent abstention. Not overlapping as a method.
- [2510.26865], [2604.26614] and [2511.21982] are already cited correctly as benchmark and trained readers. I found no paper that already builds a training-free frontier-semantics plus measured-lattice reader with labelling-margin abstention.

## Missing baselines or experiments
1. A strong training-free needle baseline: a promptable segmenter prompted at the hub, plus a line or skeleton fit, and a Radon-at-pivot radial profile. These replace or add to Hough in hybrid B.
2. Astra given the rectified, zoomed and polar-unwrapped crops (better views, no local measurement). This separates "measurement helps" from "views help".
3. A fully local variant: a local OCR recogniser or the Qwen3-VL-4B coarse code with no frontier at all, reported as a headline row. If it is close, the "frontier helper" story becomes a "classical reader" story.
4. A held-out real-dial benchmark with weights frozen from MeasureBench: InSituMeasure's analog-dial categories [2609.04014] or MeterFL's real test split [2609.31998].
5. On clocks, the trained It's About Time reader [2111.09162] run on TickTockVQA, and on COCO/OpenImages clocks if feasible.
6. Component error rates for the frontier's semantic outputs: box-value accuracy as a function of box density, and minors-per-major accuracy. Also, how often a wrong M flips h*.
7. Coverage and selective accuracy per instrument family (single gauge, clock, multi-dial meter, non-linear) at the frozen thresholds, not only overall.

## Questions for the authors
1. What happens when the frontier's minors-per-major is wrong? Is M a hard constraint or a soft prior, and what fraction of pilot errors trace to it?
2. How are hollow, white or very thin needles handled? Given 14 px patches, show that the DINOv2 score is sharper than a pixel-space template score on those subsets.
3. MeasureBench intervals are set per image. What tolerance does tol(sem) use when defining "answer-changing" rivals, and is it set without looking at the ground-truth interval?
4. What is the expected coverage on the clock set, where many TickTockVQA clocks have no minor ticks or numerals, or show Roman or illustrated faces? What fixes the lattice then?
5. If Astra xhigh with Python comes within 5 points (your kill condition), what is the fallback paper? Is it a negative result on frontier tool use, and would it still be a CVPR paper?

## Rating
- rating: 3
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.3
- one_point_fix: Freeze every weight and threshold after MeasureBench and report the full system on a second, held-out real-dial benchmark: InSituMeasure analog categories, or MeterFL's real test images. Pair it with a promptable-segmenter needle baseline in place of the Hough-only hybrid B. Together these turn "training-free, therefore it generalises" from an assertion into the paper's main evidence.
