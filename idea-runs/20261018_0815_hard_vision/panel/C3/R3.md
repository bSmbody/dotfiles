# R3 (experimentalist) review of C3: "Unwrapping the Dial"

## Summary
The paper proposes a training-free reader for analog dials and clocks. One frontier-VLM call (GPT-6 Astra, default effort) supplies only semantics: values of numerals that DBNet has located, unit, scale type, minors per major, and hand roles. All angles are measured locally: a polar-unwrapped tick lattice, a needle found by pasting silhouettes and comparing DINOv2 layer-18 patches, and a jointly ranked set of neighbouring "labellings" (numeral-to-tick, one inserted or deleted tick, hand roles) whose score margin drives abstention, with fallback to the frontier's own reading. The claimed insight is that the frontier fails on the fine readout, not on knowing what the dial means, so the reading should come from measured geometry anchored by frontier semantics. The predicted evidence, pilot-gated, is: at least +15 points value+unit accuracy over the best Astra configuration on 511 held-out MeasureBench real dials at no more than 1.2x Astra-default cost; at least +20 points exact-minute accuracy on 1,000 TickTockVQA clocks; at least +8 points over a same-call Hough-needle hybrid; margin AUROC of at least 0.80.

## Strengths
- The experimental design is unusually disciplined for a proposal. Gates (G0 to G3) are pre-registered with explicit kill conditions. The fallback plan is stated: if the needle sweep fails, hybrid B becomes the method and the sweep is reported as a negative result. A paired-bootstrap power argument is given (SE about 3.9 points at n = 200 with 30% discordance).
- Hybrid A and hybrid B use the same frontier call, so they isolate (i) local-interval lattice vs Reitsma-style linear fit and (ii) needle sweep vs Hough. These are the right controls for the claimed mechanism.
- Oracle splits (GT needle angle; GT scale labelling) separate scale failures from needle failures. This is what a skeptical reviewer would ask for first.
- The baseline set includes the frontier's own strongest configuration: xhigh effort plus built-in Python/crop tools, with cost measured. The kill condition "Astra xhigh+tools within 5 points at ≤ 2x C3 cost" is honest.
- Accuracy is always reported at 100% coverage (frontier fallback on abstention), so selective-prediction numbers cannot hide a coverage collapse.
- Abstention is compared against the frontier's verbalised confidence and 3-sample self-agreement, and also on the subset where Astra's samples agree but are wrong. That is the case where a frontier-derived signal must fail and an independent signal must earn its place. Independent evidence motivates this: token confidence from a fine-tuned gauge VLM still produces errors at confidence ≥ 0.99 [2608.17723], and 24 MLLMs show poor confidence calibration on industrial gauges [2609.04014].
- The qualitative pieces are ablated one by one: joint vs sequential ranking, DBNet positions vs frontier-estimated positions, local vs count-from-start minors, full-range vs frontier-centred window, coupling on/off, frontier vs Qwen3-VL-4B as coarse-code reader. Together they make the paper's causal story testable.
- Measured cost and latency are reported, including local GPU-seconds, and the compute fits the budget. My estimate is about 1–2k ViT-L/14 forward passes per image for the sweep, i.e. tens of GPU-hours per full pass over 1,861 images. That is well within 40 GPU-days.

## Weaknesses
- [major] The headline effect is relative to a baseline that has never been measured, and no absolute floor is committed. Astra has not been evaluated on dials [2609.35718]; the 31.5% figure is a 2025 Gemini number [2510.26865]. "+15 over the best Astra configuration" could mean 45% or 80% absolute, and those are very different papers. The gain is also mechanically produced only on the covered subset: Δ = coverage × (C3 selective accuracy − Astra accuracy on those same covered items). If coverage selects the easy, crisp, well-ticked dials, Astra is probably also above average on them. At the stated thresholds (≥ 50% coverage, ≥ 75% selective accuracy), +15 requires Astra-best to be at most about 45% on the covered half. The paper should report this decomposition explicitly and commit to an absolute held-out accuracy floor, not only a delta.
- [major] There is no out-of-distribution end-to-end test, yet robustness to unseen designs is the paper's main argument against trained readers. All weights and thresholds (λ1–3, τ_m, τ_s, sigma_n) are set on 200 MeasureBench dials and tested on 511 dials from the same benchmark and the same collection process. The claim "cannot overfit training designs" is exactly what a cross-dataset test with frozen thresholds would show, and the paper does not run one. MeterFL appears only as a component diagnostic. End-to-end reading on the MeterFL held-out split, DialBench/RPM-10K test, and, if released, the analog categories of InSituMeasure (2,922 real industrial tasks with viewpoint/occlusion tags and answerability labels [2609.04014]) would make the generality claim credible. A single frontier model also limits the "black-box VLMs" plural in the title. Running the coarse-code call with Qwen3-VL-4B is a good start, but it is listed as an ablation, not as a second "helped" system.
- [major] The needle sweep is the most novel and most expensive component, and it is compared only against weak alternatives (Hough line; a line fit on a DINOv2 edge map). The obvious training-free competitors are missing:
  - a SAM-family mask prompted with a point at the detected hub, followed by a principal-axis or skeleton fit;
  - a classical radial darkness or edge-energy profile over angle, which is cheap and robust to counterweights once the asymmetric tip is scored.

  There is also a resolution concern. DINOv2 ViT-L/14 at 448 px gives a 32×32 patch grid, so a 2–4 px needle is sub-patch, and a 0.25° refinement in feature space cannot resolve below the patch scale. FoundPose itself needs featuremetric refinement for this reason [2311.18809]. The value-bearing precision therefore comes from an unspecified "sub-pixel tip refinement against flanking ticks". That step needs a precise definition and its own ablation (sweep-only angle vs sweep + refinement).
- [major] The subset claims are underpowered, and the component metrics are measured on the tuning set. The +15-point nuisance-subset predictions (shadow/glare, counterweight, tapered, multi-hand) would be checked on a 200-dial pilot split six ways, about 30 images per tag. At 30% discordance the paired SE is about 10 points, so these predictions cannot be confirmed there. The tags, tick annotations and needle annotations exist only on the pilot, which is also the split used for thresholds and gating. Component accuracy and oracle splits on that split are therefore optimistic. A held-out annotated subset (e.g. 150 of the 511 test dials, tagged before any run) is needed for every subset and component claim the paper makes.
- [minor] Engineering and timeline risk. Six heterogeneous modules (detector-gated rectification, pivot estimation, DBNet, polar lattice, silhouette renderer + DINOv2 sweep, hypothesis enumeration) plus hand-annotating 350 images all fall in one week (10-19 to 10-25), by a team whose stated strength is diffusion transformers and RL. The gating plan limits the damage, but a slip removes the ablations that make the paper convincing.
- [minor] The TickTockVQA protocol is not comparable to published numbers. A random 1,000 of the 5,247 test images is evaluated, while Swap-DPO and the other references are on the full test set [2603.08011] and are cited, not rerun. The local pipeline is cheap, so the full test set should be reported for C3's local-only components. The 1,000-image list should be fixed and released, and the Swap-DPO checkpoint, if public, should be rerun on it. TickTockVQA also contains no-numeral, Roman-numeral, flipped, partial and illustrated clocks: accuracy and coverage should be reported per type, because tick-free illustrated clocks will mostly abstain.
- [minor] MeasureBench's "dial" subset mixes single-pointer gauges, multi-hand clocks and five-pointer electricity meters [2510.26865]. Per-type accuracy, coverage and gain are needed, otherwise a large gain on one family can mask a loss on another. MeasureBench intervals are set per image by annotators, so the paper should report how often an interval is narrower than one minor division, because that is where the interpolation matters.
- [minor] There is contamination risk on the baseline side. MeasureBench real images have been public since late 2025, so Astra may have seen them, which biases the comparison against C3 (conservative, but it should be stated). TriSCA's GRPO stage uses 1,273 real dial images [2604.26614], close to MeasureBench's 1,272 real images, so its 28.5% reference may not be a clean held-out number.
- [minor] The cost claim (≤ 1.2x Astra-default per image) compares a call carrying two images, a numbered-box overlay and a JSON schema against a single plain-image call. Input tokens differ, so the measured ratio could exceed 1.2. End-to-end latency including ~1–2k ViT-L passes should be shown next to the API cost, so the "need the frontier less" claim is not only about dollars.
- [minor] The AUROC target should be defined precisely: AUROC for detecting C3's own pre-fallback errors versus Astra's confidence for detecting Astra's errors are different tasks. Both sides of the comparison should be computed on the same error definition and the same items.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2404.08785] Reitsma et al.: same modular skeleton (detect, ellipse, numerals anchored to angles, needle line, value). C3 replaces OCR recognition with frontier values, adds minor-tick local interpolation, joint labelling ranking and a silhouette needle. Partial overlap; does not kill novelty, and hybrid A is the right control.
- [2609.31998] MeterFL: polar unwrap of a tick ring plus tick-count decoding with supplied v_min/Δv. C3's lattice module is a training-free, locally counted, frontier-anchored version of this. Partial overlap on one module; not a kill.
- [2604.22829] Dynamic gauges study: explicitly recommends VLM-for-semantics plus deterministic geometry. C3 is the realisation of that recommendation. This weakens the "insight" novelty but leaves the method and evaluation novel.
- [2609.35718] GPT-6 Astra evaluation: the built-in tool / specialist-as-tool configurations are the main competitor. No dial result is reported, so there is no overlap in results, but Astra with tools could close the gap; the baseline is correctly included.
- [2609.04014] InSituMeasure (Sept 2026): benchmark only, no reading method. No novelty overlap, but it is a missing evaluation target (real industrial dials, answerability, selective risk). Release status was not confirmed in the text I read.
- [2608.17723] VLM gauge-reading empirical study (Aug 2026): QLoRA specialisation of Qwen2.5-VL-7B, no geometric pipeline. No overlap; it supports the need for a non-VLM abstention signal.
- [2603.08011] Swap-DPO / TickTockVQA: uses the θ_h = 30h + m/2 coupling only for training negatives; C3 uses it at inference. Not a kill.
- My searches (gauge/dial/pointer-meter/clock reading, training-free meter reading, tool-augmented measurement, render-and-compare with DINOv2, abstention for VLMs, plus recency searches since 2025-10-18) found no training-free frontier-semantics + measured-lattice dial reader with ranked labellings. 2610.11320 ("It's Always 10:10") turned up as a hit but concerns text-to-image generation, not reading.

## Missing baselines or experiments
1. An end-to-end cross-dataset test with frozen pilot thresholds: MeterFL held-out readings (%FS and MeasureBench-style accuracy), DialBench/RPM-10K test, and InSituMeasure analog categories if available.
2. Needle baselines: a SAM-family point-at-hub mask plus axis fit; a classical radial darkness/edge profile; and the sweep without the native-resolution tip refinement.
3. A visual-prompting baseline that hands Astra the same measured evidence as pixels (ticks drawn and indexed, polar-unwrapped strip), to separate "evidence as numbers" from "evidence as an easier image". The hand-back ablation is close but not the same.
4. A held-out annotated test subset (≥ 150 dials, tags fixed before running) for component metrics, oracle splits and every per-tag claim.
5. A per-family breakdown (gauges / clocks / multi-dial meters; linear / non-linear; TickTockVQA numeral types) for accuracy, coverage and gain.
6. The gain decomposition: coverage, C3 selective accuracy, and Astra accuracy on the covered and on the abstained items.
7. Trained references rerun on the identical held-out splits where checkpoints are public (TriSCA on the 511 dials; Swap-DPO on the fixed 1,000 clocks), instead of numbers cited from different splits.
8. Run-to-run variance of the Astra baselines (at least 2 seeds or samples on a subset), because frontier outputs are stochastic and the margin of +15 is judged against "the best of three configurations". Taking the max of noisy configurations also biases the baseline upward, which is fine but should be stated.

## Questions for the authors
1. What absolute value+unit accuracy on the 511 held-out dials do you commit to, and what does the decomposition coverage × (selective accuracy − Astra accuracy on covered items) look like on the pilot?
2. How exactly is the "sub-pixel tip refinement against flanking ticks" computed, and how much of the final accuracy depends on it rather than on the DINOv2 sweep?
3. What is DBNet's numeral detection recall on the pilot dials, and what happens when fewer than two numerals are detected (abstain, or lattice-only with frontier v_min/Δv)?
4. Will the thresholds tuned on MeasureBench be applied unchanged to TickTockVQA, or are clock thresholds tuned separately on the 150 train-split clocks? If separately, please state that the method has per-benchmark tuning.
5. How is the 1,000-image TickTockVQA subset chosen and will the list be released? Can C3's local-only components be run on the full 5,247-image test set?
6. For multi-scale instruments (multimeters with several arcs), how does the method pick the arc that matches the question, and how often does the frontier's scale-type answer select the wrong arc?
7. What are the measured input tokens of the C3 call versus a plain Astra call, and what is the end-to-end latency per image?

## Rating
- rating: 4
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.25
- one_point_fix: Add an end-to-end cross-dataset evaluation with all pilot-set weights and thresholds frozen (MeterFL held-out readings plus DialBench/RPM-10K or InSituMeasure analog dials). Pair it with a committed absolute accuracy floor and the coverage-by-gain decomposition on held-out MeasureBench, so the "training-free generalises where trained readers break" claim is shown, not argued.

Rationale for p_core: the load-bearing prediction is G1 (+15 points over the best Astra configuration on held-out real dials). Training-free rectification, numeral detection and needle localisation are exactly the stages where earlier pipelines collapsed on in-the-wild images (Reitsma 8.5% on MeasureBench; OCR stage failing on 40–57.5% of images [2510.26865][2404.08785]). With realistic coverage of 40–60% on easier dials, and Astra xhigh with crop/Python tools probably several points above default, clearing +15 over the best configuration is possible but less likely than not. If it does hold at threshold, the evaluation as designed would convince me, hence the weak accept.
