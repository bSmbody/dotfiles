
## Axis: task-state tracking and sub-task success judgement by small VLM controllers

### What has been done
- **Distilled small-VLM stage monitors with self-written text state.** [2609.20791] (StageGuard) SFTs Qwen3.5-0.8B to emit {continue, advance, skip}, the next subtask and an updated free-text history h_t that it feeds back to itself next round. Supervision is compact self-explanations of teacher (397B) traces, and the teacher was shown the ground-truth decision. Training uses only annotated demonstrations (10 per task). Offline transition completion is 96% on LIBERO and 90% on BEHAVIOR-1K. Closed loop on BEHAVIOR-1K it gets 0.53 vs 0.63 with oracle transitions. The authors attribute the remaining errors to premature or missed switches and partial observability.
- **Frontier controllers keeping state implicitly in context.** [2610.17350] keeps the whole episode plus GPT-6-Astra's own free-text descriptions in one conversation. It needs 9.5 calls and $0.39 per episode, and 25 of its 63 failures are premature "task complete" declarations. As a planner, Qwen3-VL-32B scores 27.9% vs 77.8% for GPT-6-Astra. [2606.10267] finds that in-episode history length or summarization does not help Gemini planners. Its success detector reads privileged simulator state. Injected false positives of 30-50% "drastically" hurt, because the planner moves on after a false "done".
- **Explicit external state with event-triggered writes.** [2609.34554] (Ledger) builds a tracker-based object ledger read by a frontier planner (Claude Sonnet 5). Steps are logged complete from gripper-aperture regime changes, not from verified outcomes. Re-deciding from scratch drops Repick 60->28 because completed repetitions get re-attributed. [2608.29537] (AGM) keeps a fixed subgoal list and a pointer that advances only on verified achievement: a CoTracker lift test for grasps and a 2.43M SigLIP head for placements. Verified writes give 100% on PickXTimes vs 32% for attempt-based writes and 36% with no pointer. Its verification vocabulary covers only gripper events, and non-gripper subgoals get no gain.
- **Stand-alone VLM success / failure judges.** [2512.01946] (Guardian) LoRA-tunes InternVL3-8B with CoT on failures synthesized by perturbing successful trajectories (action perturbation in sim; text swap or end-image = start-image on real data). The judge is stateless and per-subtask. It recalls 91% of successes but only 76% of failures, and 68-93% of its errors are perception errors. [2609.03611] (FailBench) finds that every fine-tuned detector scores below its own base model across sources. Judges are biased toward "success" under ambiguous evidence ("failure is treated as what must be proven"), and longer reasoning does not fix it. Qwen3-VL-4B/8B reach only 0.65/0.69 balanced accuracy.

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Small-VLM controllers and monitors keep task progress as a state they write themselves, and a written step is never re-checked against later evidence. Examples are StageGuard's free-text h_t and Ledger's proprioceptive "step done" log, versus the only evidence-gated alternative, AGM's pointer with gripper-event verifiers. On multi-instance or multi-stage instructions (e.g. "both bowls, then the drawer"), a single unverified "done" therefore becomes a permanent task-state error. The assumption being challenged is that progress can be written once, at the moment the controller decides a sub-task ended.
  - **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2609.20791] comes closest on the small-model side: a 0.8B student tracks state via self-generated h_t, but nothing verifies or revises h_t, and closed-loop errors are premature or missed switches (0.53 vs 0.63 oracle). [2608.29537] gates writes on evidence and shows the effect is decisive (100 vs 32 on PickXTimes). Its verifiers exist only for grasp and release events, the subgoal list is fixed by a parser, and other subgoals are left to the policy. [2609.34554] logs completion from gripper events and miscounts when it re-decides (Repick 60->28). It needs a frontier planner carrying its plan across decisions to partly recover (54). [2610.17350] shows that even GPT-6-Astra, keeping state as its own free-text descriptions, fails mostly by premature completion. The repeated deficiency across these four is that progress is committed on an attempt or self-claim and never re-checked against later observations. Queries checked: "task state memory VLA", "progress pointer VLA", "stage completion robot", "subtask progress VLM", "hierarchical VLA memory".
  - **Methodological deficiency:** Representation limitation / credit-assignment flaw. The task state is one forward-written record (text history, step log, or pointer). It has no per-sub-goal belief that later evidence can lower, and no rule that decides which past write to revoke when the scene contradicts the record. AGM shows that the write rule, not memory capacity, decides counting success. But AGM implements the write rule as hand-specified verifiers tied to gripper events, so it does not extend to a VLM controller's open-vocabulary sub-tasks.
  - **Why it matters:** This failure mode is exactly what separates a 7B controller from a frontier controller in the problem's motivating example: the controller judges the first bowl as finishing the task and moves to the drawer. A frontier model avoids it partly through expensive full-context reasoning ($0.39 and ~9.5 calls per episode on RoboMME) and still fails by premature completion. A state that can revise its own writes would let a small controller recover from its own misjudgements without asking a frontier model. It would also show whether long-horizon controller failures come from perception or from irreversible state commits.
  - **Evidence route:** On long-horizon multi-instance and multi-stage suites (e.g. RoboMME Counting / LIBERO-Long / CALVIN chains), measure the rate of re-issued finished sub-tasks and skipped steps per episode, and success as a function of the number of repetitions N. Compare against a forward-written text history (StageGuard-style) and a gripper-event pointer (AGM-style) under the same ≤8B controller and VLA. A method that closes the gap should flatten the decline in success as N grows, and should recover from injected false "done" writes without a frontier call.
- **Gap:** Sub-task success judges for small controllers are trained by symmetric SFT/CoT imitation. The training states are demonstration states (StageGuard) or failures synthesized by perturbing successful trajectories (Guardian), so the judge never learns the asymmetric cost of a false "done" or the correlated, controller-induced failure states it meets in closed loop.
  - **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2609.20791] distils teacher reasoning into a 0.8B judge, but only from held-out-split demonstrations where the "skip" (failure) label is rare. Its decision-only variant falls to 37.7% transition completion on BEHAVIOR-1K, showing how strongly the judge depends on the supervision distribution. [2512.01946] adds failures, but they are synthesized from successful trajectories, and recall on failures (76%) stays below recall on successes (91%). [2609.03611] measures the result across 14 sources: every fine-tuned detector, Guardian included, scores below its base model, and all detectors default to "success" under ambiguity. [2606.10267] shows the system-level consequence: false positives, not false negatives, collapse Hi-VLA success. It also notes that real detector errors are correlated across consecutive states, which their independent-noise test does not capture. [2608.29537] is the one near miss that trains its verifier on its own closed-loop rollouts plus an attempt-based controller's failures, and it shows that matching deployment timing matters (a 20-frame shift degrades it). But it is a 2.43M head over gripper events, not a VLM controller's judgement.
  - **Methodological deficiency:** Training-objective mismatch. The judge is trained to reproduce labels or teacher rationales on an off-policy state distribution with a symmetric token-level loss. It is never trained on the states its own controller-VLA loop produces, and the false "done" errors that end episodes carry no extra cost. FailBench's finding that detection-specific fine-tuning lowers cross-source accuracy below the base model and keeps the bias toward "success" points to the objective and data distribution, not model capacity, as the cause.
  - **Why it matters:** A small controller can afford to query its own judge every few seconds; a frontier judge cannot (GPT-5.6 Luna runs at 0.29 Hz in StageGuard's measurement). Closing this gap is what turns a cheap judge into one that is safe to trust in closed loop. It would also tell the field whether success detection for embodied controllers is limited by perception, as Guardian's error audit suggests, or by how the judge is trained.
  - **Evidence route:** Measure false-positive rate on "done" decisions taken from the controller's own closed-loop rollouts, not demonstrations, and the run lengths of correlated false negatives, together with task success. Compare against SFT on demonstrations (StageGuard-style) and SFT on synthesized failures (Guardian-style) with the same ≤8B backbone. Also report cross-benchmark balanced accuracy (e.g. FailBench sim subsets), to check that the judge does not drop below its base model the way prior fine-tuned detectors do.
