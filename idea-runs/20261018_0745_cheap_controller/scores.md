### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=novelty)`
- **Evidence**:
  - **The cited safety guarantee is misstated (grounding).** [2605.04732] Theorem 2 is "never worse than independent" only because shared transitions are keyed by (state, action, time). Covariance arises only when both branches are at the same state (proof Case 1, s = s-bar), and "samples are anyway independent if s != s-bar". After a controller swap the two simulated worlds are at different continuous object poses. C1's (sub-task, chunk) key shares noise across different states, which is the full-dependence estimator X_D. Proposition 1 of the same paper shows X_D can have higher variance than independent sampling. So the "differ at one step, agree afterwards, provably safe" mapping does not hold as stated.
  - **CRN buys almost nothing in the canonical case.** The paired variance is Var(Y1)+Var(Y0)-2Cov [2603.11084, Eq 1]. A truly culpable decision, such as the premature "close drawer" with a bowl still on the table, makes the factual branch fail under almost every noise draw, so Var(Y_factual) ≈ 0 and the covariance ≈ 0 whatever the coupling. The flip rate then equals the swap's success rate, which independent draws estimate equally well. Coupling helps only for ambiguous decisions where both branches are uncertain, not for the ones the method is built to find.
  - **Identifiability comes from resets, not from keyed noise.** What C1 actually uses is the average over several noise draws of Y(swap) - Y(factual) at a snapshot. That is the interventional advantage Q(s,d') - Q(s,d), which is identifiable with independent streams wherever the environment can be reset. VinePPO relies only on reset-to-state with small K [2410.01679, Sec 4, 6.4], and ASCT evaluates alternative legal actions at the actor's own states with "an independent random stream" [2609.35215, Sec 4]. A MuJoCo/LIBERO-style simulator can save and restore its state during the original rollout, so it gives this without replaying the noise. The exact per-episode counterfactual under the original noise u0, which is the only thing keyed noise uniquely provides, is a single Bernoulli outcome, and C1 itself averages it away.
  - **The closest 2026 prior already does intervention-based failure attribution, and it names CRN as the next step.** Causal Agent Replay [2606.08275] models an agent run as an SCM, applies do(action) at step k, runs forward K times, and localizes the causal step with confidence intervals. Its Limitations state that isolating a step's direct effect "calls for common random numbers across branches ... left as a refinement" [Sec 7]. Fixing the random numbers to make a stochastic POMDP deterministic and compare policies on shared scenarios is PEGASUS [1301.3878, Sec 3.1-3.2]. C1's framing that "unlike [1905.05824], we drew the noise ourselves" is therefore 25-year-old practice, not a delta. What remains new is the setting, not the mechanism: credit for a language controller above a stochastic VLA executor, separating controller failures from execution failures, and using that credit to gate teacher calls. My searches ("counterfactual replay robot", "failure attribution embodied agent", "VLA noise seed", "high-level policy VLA credit", "planner distillation VLA", "on-policy distillation embodied planner", the last four restricted to papers since 2025-10) found no embodied work that does this.
  - **The localization rule is wrong as stated (mapping).** WebKD's first fatal step t* is defined by recoverability: the first step after which no action sequence of any agent succeeds [2610.19834, App B.2, Def 2, Prop 1]. That makes it the last point where a rescue existed. C1's "first decision with a high flip rate", measured with the student continuing after the swap, is the earliest point where perturbing the scene happens to steer the student around its later mistake. CAR shows that an early irrelevant step shows an effect because it re-rolls the pivotal downstream step, and it resolves this by taking the latest step whose effect is significant [2606.08275, Sec 4]. Under C1's rule the teacher would be sent to rewrite the wrong decision.
  - **Source facts that do check out.** For π0 the per-chunk Gaussian start is the only sampled input, at 73 ms per chunk on an RTX 4090 [2410.24164, Sec IV, App D]. Event-keyed counter-based RNG and the placebo replay check are as described [2603.11084, Sec 2.3, 4.1]. WebKD does assume deterministic transitions and replay [2610.19834, Sec 3.1, App B.2]. The contribution type is methodological, and the gap (no embodied on-policy, credit-assigned controller correction) appears real.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The headline mechanism, noise keyed to make the VLA exogenous so that blame becomes identifiable, adds little. Snapshot resets already identify the decision-level advantage (VinePPO/ASCT, and CAR's do()-and-run-forward). The cited CRN safety theorem needs state-keyed noise, and in the canonical culpable case (factual branch nearly always fails) coupling gives no variance reduction. That leaves the real novelty in the embodied controller-vs-executor credit and teacher-gating application, which C1 must re-center on and sharpen, together with a commitment-style localization rule instead of "first decision with a high flip rate".


### C2 verdict

- **Verdict**: `reject(reason=luck-adjusted blame needs the privileged sim predicates that already make blame, and a free on-policy expert, trivial; committed gains exceed the benchmark's controller headroom)`
- **Evidence**:
  - **The blame C2 estimates is already given exactly by the inputs it requires.** C2's progress state is the simulator's per-subgoal predicates, read in training [C2 mapping]. With those predicates, LADDER labels "moved on before done", "re-issued a satisfied subgoal", "deferred" and "stuck" deterministically: "the simulator's own success predicate at the moment of each switch tells whether the subgoal left behind was satisfied" [2610.18053, App H]. So the canonical error class (premature advance, the "second bowl" case) needs no executor-reliability tables, value tables or luck terms. Measuring triage precision against that same audit (committed effect 1) means a rule-based baseline that calls the teacher only on audit-flagged failures scores about 100% by construction. The luck adjustment is non-trivial only for "bad phrasing" losses. Those are a small share where subgoals are single pick-and-place commands with explicitly named objects [2610.18053, Sec 3].
  - **Under the same privileged access, the frontier teacher is not the cheapest on-policy expert.** LADDER's privileged verifier takes "the ground-truth subgoals from the simulator", advances only on the predicate, retries otherwise, and loses 0.0 points to orchestrator decisions [2610.18053, App B, Table 18]. A privileged agent can be "queried from any state ... gathered adaptively via online rollouts" of the student, which is DAgger with a free oracle [1912.12294, Sec 1-2]. That labels every student state, not only gated ones, with zero API calls. It is the baseline a CVPR panel will ask for first, and C2 omits it. It also removes the premise that frontier calls are the scarce resource to be triaged.
  - **The committed effect exceeds the headroom of the named benchmark.** On LADDER with π0.5, a perfect privileged orchestrator beats the VLM-verifier orchestrators by only 4.2–4.5 points (paired CIs [2.3, 6.3] and [2.6, 6.6]) [2610.18053, Sec 5.1, App F]. On Duplicates, the category of the "both bowls" example, "orchestration provides only tiny improvement" [2610.18053, Sec 5.1]. A failure-triggered correction baseline that learns verify-then-retry should land near those orchestrators. So "≥ 8 points over failure-triggered correction" would require beating the perfect controller. The only large controller headroom is against no-verification controllers (18.6 points), and any of the baselines closes much of that. LIBERO-Long, the alternative, leaves even less room for the controller.
  - **The source mechanism is misstated (grounding).** AIVAT only sums zero-mean correction terms at chance and known-strategy nodes into one unbiased estimate of "the expected player value". It assigns no per-decision credit to the evaluated player [1612.06915, Sec AIVAT Correction Terms]. Its guarantee (Lemma 1/Thm 1) concerns that sum and needs true chance probabilities. It says nothing about whether an individual "controller term" is correct, and C2 estimates the chance probabilities from the student's own pooled rollouts anyway. Claim (b), that a crude value table "only costs variance reduction" and so "answers the VinePPO objection", is therefore false for the per-decision blame C2 actually uses. A biased V moves mass between luck and controller terms, and that bias decides who gets the teacher call.
  - **The cross-domain borrowing is already standard in RL (novelty).** The per-step decomposition G = V(s0) + Σ A(s,a) (skill) + Σ B(s,a,s') (luck), with stochasticity modelled as an imaginary agent "nature" whose policy is the transition kernel, is Off-policy DAE [2402.12874, Sec 3.2, Eq 10, Fig 1]. C2's "controller term" and "luck term" are A and B, with the VLA as nature, and C2 does not cite this. What is left is the application: blame-gated teacher calls above a VLA. Rejecting MAGMA-GEN's ungated coaching [2609.20056, Sec 4.2, Table 2] by itself is a thin, efficiency-only delta, and the first two bullets largely cancel it in simulation.
  - **The stated key assumption is weak exactly where it matters.** C2 names it: executor reliability depends on progress state × command kind, not on unlogged scene detail. But a VLA failure changes the scene (knocked or displaced objects; "lost grip", "dropped on the way" causes [2610.18053, App H]), and the controller's next choice is a response to that scene. Keying the tables by "command kind" also folds phrasing differences within a kind into luck. So the one error class only the luck adjustment can catch is the one its tables cannot resolve.
  - **What checks out.** MAGMA-GEN calls its coach on every failed stage (3,585 calls) and validates only 54.5% of proposals [2609.20056, Sec 4.2, Sec 5.3]. LADDER's Table 18 shares (47.8/60.1 periodic, 4.0/49.0 proprioceptive verifier) are quoted correctly [2610.18053, App H]. PIVOT finds teacher hints marginal next to rollback [2609.35303, Sec 2.2]. The candidate is methodological.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The controller-versus-executor split that C2 builds from AIVAT-style luck terms is already given exactly, and for free, by the privileged per-subgoal predicates C2 itself depends on. Those same predicates provide a zero-API-call privileged on-policy expert (LADDER's privileged verifier, Learning-by-Cheating DAgger), and that expert is only 4.2–4.5 points better than existing VLM-verifier orchestrators. So the gating mechanism is redundant, and the committed ≥ 8-point gain lies outside the benchmark's controller headroom.
