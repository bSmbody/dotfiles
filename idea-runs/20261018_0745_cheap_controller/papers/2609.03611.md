# FailBench: How Reliable are VLMs at Judging Robot Task Success?

## What this paper does
FailBench pools 2,197 manipulation attempts from 14 public sources (12 real, 2 simulated, 75% natural failures) under one schema (instruction, visual recording, binary outcome) and evaluates 13 VLM success/failure detectors: six general VLMs (Gemini 3 Flash, Gemma-4-31B, GPT-4o, Qwen3-VL-Thinking 2B/4B/8B), two embodied VLMs, five purpose-built detectors (Guardian, RoboReward-8B, ViFailback-8B, RoboFAC-7B, FailSense-3B). It reports balanced accuracy, error-set overlap, a manual error analysis, and a localize-then-crop pipeline.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How accurate are small (<=8B) VLMs and fine-tuned detectors at judging from images whether a manipulation attempt succeeded?
Best is Gemini 3 Flash at 0.77 macro balanced accuracy; Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking 0.69, Qwen3-VL-4B 0.65, Qwen3-VL-2B 0.53; Guardian 0.63, RoboReward-8B 0.62, ViFailback-8B 0.59, RoboFAC-7B 0.51, FailSense-3B 0.50 [full text, Sec 4.2, Table 1]. No model exceeds 0.60 on contact-rich assembly [Sec 1].
### Goal: Does fine-tuning a VLM on failure-detection data fix this, and why not?
No: "All fine-tuned specialists score below the base model they were fine-tuned from"; Guardian 0.627 vs InternVL3-8B base 0.629, and 0.596 vs 0.639 once its two home subsets are removed; "Fine-tuning for failure detection made these models worse at it outside the data they were tuned on" [App B.1, Table 7]. RoboFAC-7B labels 28/30 successes as failure on the balanced set, its published 80.6% is near the all-failure prior of its 79.7%-failure split [App B.1]. Existing failure datasets mostly construct failures by perturbing successful trajectories, which can make detection "a matter of recognizing one data-generation procedure" [Sec 1].
### Goal: What systematic error does the detector make on ambiguous evidence?
A bias toward success: the model names the uncertain point and "scores success anyway... Failure is treated as what must be proven"; it scores the approach rather than the outcome (bolt "inserted" after being carried to the hole), hallucinates observations (apple reported in bowl though dropped), and does not reconcile views (grapes held in one camera, on table in two others) [App B.2, Figs 2-3]. Wrong answers carry longer reasoning (1,868 vs 1,464 chars), so more reasoning does not fix it [App B.2]. Semantic failures (wrong object) are mostly caught; physical ones (released in wrong place, jaws close on nothing, slip) mostly missed [App B.2].
### Goal: What does it report as its limitation / future work on method?
Scope is a single binary label per completed attempt; it does not evaluate progress during execution or planning failures [Sec 5]. Cropping to the outcome-relevant region gives +2.3 points (0.773 -> 0.797) "but it does not teach the detector to infer physical state"; future work should develop "models and training objectives explicitly designed to reason about fine-grained spatial and contact relationships" [Sec 4.4, Sec 6]. Non-visual streams (force, audio) are not used [Sec 5].
