# AIVAT: A New Variance Reduction Technique for Agent Evaluation in Imperfect Information Games

## What this paper does
Burch et al. evaluate poker agents, whose outcomes are dominated by chance (cards) and randomized play. AIVAT adds a control-variate correction term at every chance event and at every decision of a player whose strategy is known: the expected value before the event (averaged over the known outcome distribution) minus the value after the observed outcome, both under an arbitrary heuristic value function. Combined with imaginary observations over private cards, the estimator is provably unbiased for any value function and cuts the standard deviation of heads-up no-limit hold'em results by about 68%, so the same statistical conclusion needs about ten times fewer hands.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the correction term at a chance event, and how does it separate an agent's skill from luck?
Moving backwards through the observed game, each correction term "shifts an estimate of the expected value after a choice was made towards an estimate of the expected value before the choice" [full text, Sec AIVAT]. At a chance or known-strategy node H the term is k_H(z) = (probability-weighted mean of u_h(a) over all outcomes a) - u_h(a_observed) [Sec AIVAT Correction Terms]. Summed over the chance nodes, these terms remove the part of the outcome due to which card fell rather than to the evaluated player's choices; MIVAT did this for chance only, AIVAT extends it to player actions [Sec Introduction]. With perfect value functions and known strategies the estimate has zero variance: "the imaginary observation values and the correction terms would sum to the expected player value, regardless of the observed game" [Sec AIVAT].
### Goal: What does the method require, and what is guaranteed whatever the value function's quality?
It needs the publicly known outcome distribution sigma_c at chance nodes and the strategy of at least one player (the set P_a) [Sec Background, Sec AIVAT Correction Terms]. Lemma 1 / Theorem 1: every k_H has expected value 0, so AIVAT is unbiased for "an arbitrary fixed value function"; better value estimates only give more variance reduction [Sec Unbiased Value Estimate]. The proof uses the true reach probabilities, so the guarantee depends on the chance probabilities being correct, not on the value function.
### Goal: How much variance reduction does it achieve with an imperfect value function?
In HUNL, with value functions from a crude 8-million-information-set abstraction, SD falls from 25.96 to 8.10 chips (self-play) and 26.31 to 8.30 (dissimilar strategies), "a bit more than a 68% reduction", versus 18% for chance-only MIVAT [Sec No-limit Texas Hold'em, Fig 4-5]. "Three times reduction in standard deviation allows us to achieve the same statistical significance with ten times less data" [Sec Conclusions].

### Goal: Does AIVAT attribute the outcome to individual decisions of the evaluated player, or only give an unbiased estimate of that player's expected value?
Only the latter. Correction terms are added at chance events and at decisions of players whose strategy is known (the set P_a), and "AIVAT uses the sum of k_H(z) across all H" to form one estimate of "the expected player value" [full text, Sec AIVAT, AIVAT Correction Terms]. The terms have zero expectation, which is what makes the summed estimator unbiased; nothing in the method assigns a per-decision skill score to the evaluated player, whose strategy need not be known [Sec AIVAT, Sec Unbiased Value Estimate]. The quality of any intermediate split between "luck" and "decisions" depends on the value function: "value estimates which more closely approximate the true expected value will result in greater variance reduction" [Sec AIVAT Correction Terms].
