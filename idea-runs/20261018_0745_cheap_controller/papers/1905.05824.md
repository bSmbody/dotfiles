# Counterfactual Off-Policy Evaluation with Gumbel-Max Structural Causal Models

## What this paper does
Oberst & Sontag cast an episodic POMDP as a structural causal model (SCM) and generate counterfactual trajectories for observed episodes: infer the exogenous noise that produced the observed episode (abduction), swap the policy (action), and re-run the mechanisms with the same noise (prediction). Because counterfactuals are not identifiable under stochastic transitions, they propose a "counterfactual stability" assumption, prove that a Gumbel-Max SCM satisfies it, and give a Monte Carlo posterior sampler. They use the per-episode counterfactual reward difference to flag which observed episodes a new policy would most change, on a synthetic sepsis simulator.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is a counterfactual trajectory generated, and why does it need the exogenous noise?
The counterfactual distribution is obtained by "first inferring the posterior over latent variables" U given the observed data and "then passing that distribution through the structural mechanisms" in the intervened model ("abduction, action, and prediction") [full text, Sec 2.1, Def 3]. Under the SCM a counterfactual trajectory "is a deterministic function of the exogenous U variables" [Sec 3, Lemma 1]. The expected reward difference between two policies decomposes into per-episode counterfactual differences R(tau_new(u)) - R(tau) averaged over the noise posterior [Sec 3, Corollary 1]. This is a "but-for" question, the same one used to establish fault [Sec 1].
### Goal: What makes the counterfactual identifiable, and what breaks it?
With deterministic transitions (Buesing et al.'s grid world) counterfactuals follow directly; with stochastic transitions "multiple SCMs can all entail the same interventional distribution, but a different set of counterfactual trajectories" [Sec 3, Sec 3.1]. For binary treatment/outcome, monotonicity identifies the Probability of Necessity [Sec 2.2-2.3]. For categorical outcomes they introduce counterfactual stability: the outcome changes only if the alternative's relative likelihood rises [Def 5], which implies monotonicity for k=2 [Theorem 1]; the Gumbel-Max mechanism satisfies it because "the Gumbel variables are fixed" across worlds [Sec 3.3, Theorem 2]. When the noise is not observed it must be sampled from a posterior (rejection or truncated-Gumbel sampling) [Sec 3.4].
### Goal: How is the counterfactual used to localize where outcomes differ?
Decomposing the policy-value gap into per-episode counterfactual differences lets them rank observed episodes by how much the counterfactual outcome differs and hand the most dramatic ones to experts for review [Abstract, Sec 5.3]. They warn that the assumptions behind these counterfactuals "cannot be tested by data" [Sec 3].
