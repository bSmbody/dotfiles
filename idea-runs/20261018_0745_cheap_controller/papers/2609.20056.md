# MAGMA-GEN: Validated Recovery Supervision from Ambiguous Failures via Counterfactual Re-Execution

## What this paper does
MAGMA-GEN generates supervised data for a Qwen3-4B high-level tool-calling policy that orchestrates stochastic low-level skills in ManiSkill3 (interactive long-horizon tasks with evolving rules, injected grasp/detection/reachability failures). For every failed stage, a privileged 20B LLM coach types the failure, proposes the suspected decision-error step and a local correction or recovery action; each candidate is re-executed from the restored simulator state under the same stage perturbations, and the best-validated action per state becomes an SFT example. It beats expert-rollout distillation, CLEANER and AutoTraj on recovery and cumulative success, and transfers to a Franka robot.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it separate decision errors from execution failures of the low-level skills?
It names the ambiguity explicitly: a poor state "may result from an invalid high-level decision, a valid decision whose physical execution failed, or partial observation", and "treating every failure as a decision error corrupts credit assignment" [full text, Sec 1, Sec 3.3, Eq 6]. It does not estimate the split; validity is "never directly observable", so the coach (an LLM prompt with privileged task spec) proposes a step and re-execution filters proposals [Sec 4.2, Eq 8-10]. After a failed grasp, the decision error is the subsequent placement command, which the coach localizes [Sec 4.2, App C.3].
### Goal: When and how often is the coach called, and how many of its proposals hold up?
The coach runs on every failed stage ("When this subtrajectory fails ... the coaching system performs failure typing, diagnosis, and counterfactual intervention") [Sec 4.2]. On 180 task instances it used 3,585 20B calls plus 5,012 4B rollout calls [Table 2]. Only 54.54% of 3,114 coach proposals are counterfactually validated [Sec 5.3]. There is no triage of execution-only failures before calling the coach.
### Goal: What does re-execution hold fixed, and what are the low-level executors?
"Perturbations are sampled once at the beginning of the stage and held fixed across sibling branches"; branches restart from the parent simulator state and the current policy continues; the subtree success fraction over K continuations scores each action [Sec 4.3, Eq 12-13]. Low-level skills are scripted perception/grasp/motion modules (custom segmentation, M2T2, HPP) with injected errors, not a learned VLA [Sec 5.2 Q3, Sec 4.3]. Coach and re-execution are both necessary: CSR 16.79 vs 8.21 without re-execution and 4.99 without coaching [Table 4]. Limitations: needs restorable simulator states; continuous-control interventions out of scope [Sec 6].
