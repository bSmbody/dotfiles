# LADDER: A Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation Benchmark

## What this paper does
LADDER is a long-horizon manipulation benchmark (SimplerEnv-Long, RobotArena-inf-Long, RoboLab-Long; 145 tasks, 1-10 subgoals, two embodiments) with programmatic per-subgoal success predicates. It evaluates low-level policies (pi0.5 etc.) alone and under orchestrators that issue text subgoals, trigger, verify and recover, plus tool-selection systems such as GPT-Policy on GPT-6 Astra. Single-subgoal success is 85.4% but multi-subgoal success collapses; orchestration lifts it to ~21-23% in sim, GPT-6-Astra tool selection to ~62% at high cost. A forensic analysis attributes every lost subgoal to an orchestrator decision or to an execution cause.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are lost subgoals attributed to the orchestrator versus the low-level policy?
Each episode is reconstructed from object poses, per-subgoal predicates, gripper state and orchestrator events; a lost subgoal is drawn "as an orchestrator decision when one made the loss final", otherwise by its physical cause [full text, App H, Subgoal causes / Orchestrator decisions]. Orchestrator decisions include "moved on before done" (left it unsatisfied after judging it done and never returned), deferred, never issued/stuck, and re-issued a satisfied subgoal; the simulator's predicate at each switch decides whether the left-behind subgoal was satisfied [App H, Table 18]. The rule is a hand-written, post-hoc diagnostic for evaluation, not a training signal.
### Goal: How large is the orchestrator share of failures compared with execution failures?
Without verification, 32.6-43.4% of subgoals fail because the orchestrator moves on before completion and never returns; with verification this falls to 3.4% and orchestrator-related failures are only 7.1% (13.8% for VoLo), so "failure to manipulate subgoal target objects" by the low-level policy becomes the main bottleneck [Sec 5.2]. Half of failed episodes complete no subgoal after the first 28% of the budget [Sec 5.2].
### Goal: How well do small open VLMs perform the orchestrator's sub-capabilities offline?
On 562 annotated frames, Qwen3-VL-8B gets 91.0% plan decomposition, 69.0/79.9% TP/TN on subgoal completion, 47.9% state tracking and 72.3% valid next subgoal, versus GPT-6 Astra 91.7, 71.1/86.4, 53.2 and 85.4 [Sec 5.2, Table 4]. The online visual verifier has 88.6% precision and 61.8% recall against simulator ground truth [App H].
