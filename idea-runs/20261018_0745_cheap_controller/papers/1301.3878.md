# PEGASUS: A Policy Search Method for Large MDPs and POMDPs

## What this paper does
Ng & Jordan observe that any (PO)MDP with a generative model whose random-number interface is exposed can be transformed into an equivalent POMDP with deterministic transitions, by appending the infinite sequence of uniform random numbers to the state. Policies are then evaluated on a fixed finite set of sampled "scenarios" (initial state plus random numbers), so every candidate policy is compared on the same randomness, and policy search optimizes this deterministic estimate. They give uniform-convergence bounds polynomial in horizon and show results on a bicycle-riding task.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does PEGASUS turn a stochastic POMDP into a deterministic one, and how does it use the fixed randomness to compare policies?
A "deterministic simulative model" g(s,a,p) maps state, action and a uniform number to the next state; the transformed state is (s, p1, p2, ...) and each transition "uses up" one number p1 [full text, Sec 3.1]. Given a scenario, "the sequence of states that will be visited upon executing pi from s is exactly determined", so every policy is evaluated on the same m scenarios [Sec 3.2, Eq 4]. Note that the numbers are consumed sequentially from one stream per scenario, and the paper remarks that a physical simulator with resets "does not, however, readily lead to a deterministic simulative model" unless its interface to the random number generator is exposed [Sec 3.1]. It is about policy search, not per-decision blame.
