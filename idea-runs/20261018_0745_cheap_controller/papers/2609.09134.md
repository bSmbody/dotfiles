# Co-Evolving Harnesses and Models: On-Policy Correction Helps Weaker Models Catch Up Where Imitation Fails

## What this paper does
On seven enterprise agent tasks, the authors evolve a harness around a weaker model (Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, Gemma-4), then compare LoRA-SFT on a stronger expert's (Gemini) successful trajectories against an on-policy correction pipeline in which an MLE agent localizes the single failing turn in the weaker model's own failed rollout and the expert rewrites only that turn (best of N=3 under a judge), yielding ~500 training rows.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What happens when a weaker model imitates complete expert trajectories, and why?
Under the evolved harness, imitation lowers mean success 78.0% to 63.1%, regressing on all seven tasks (-4.2 to -29.9) and replicating with Gemma (55.6 to 41.1) [full text, Sec 3.3, 3.3.2]. Failure analysis: knowledge failures shrink but planning failures rise from 1.1% to 14.6% of failures; "imitation successfully transfers the expert's knowledge, but also transfers the expert's planning style, which the weaker model cannot follow faithfully" [Sec 3.3.3, Table 3]. Example: the student finds the correct answer but keeps re-checking and never submits [Sec 3.3.3].
### Goal: What correction mechanism does it use and what does it assume about locating the error?
An automated failure-locus step identifies "the single turn at which a failed rollout goes wrong", the expert sees the trajectory up to that turn and "the specific checkpoint that failed", and rewrites only that turn, leaving all other steps intact [Sec 3.4, App B]. This assumes text-observable, replayable tool trajectories with task checkpoints that name the failing step; correction is one turn per failed rollout.
### Goal: What gain and limitation does it report for on-policy correction?
78.0% to 79.7% (+1.7), planning failures stay at 1.8%; it "does not fully close the gap between the weaker and the expert model, likely due to a limit of the lightweight LoRA approach" [Sec 3.4]. Future work: combine RL with on-policy expert guidance [Sec 4].
