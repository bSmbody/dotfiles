# Dense Is Not Enough: Hierarchical Supervision Allocation for Long-Horizon On-Policy Distillation

## What this paper does
LENS-OPD distils a frozen Qwen3-32B teacher into Qwen3-1.7B/4B agents on ALFWorld, WebShop and ScienceWorld. It argues useful OPD guidance lies at the intersection of future utility and current learnability, and nests three allocations: a gap-adaptive horizon curriculum, a turn proposal (max teacher-student KL) followed by paired future validation of a teacher hint, and token-level focusing on confident disagreements within validated turns.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it decide where teacher guidance is worth spending, and what does the validation test?
The proposal t* = argmax of mean content-token KL is used "only as a low-cost, high-recall proposal signal", because local divergence is weakly tied to downstream benefit [full text, Sec 4.3, Eq 4]. From the same restored state the frozen student generates two branches, with and without a short teacher hint, and the gate keeps the hint if the teacher's length-normalized likelihood of the hinted continuation is higher [Sec 4.3, Eq 5-8]. Validated gains span both sides of zero across turns, so the gate is selective [Sec 5.3].
### Goal: What does it assume about the teacher and the environment?
The teacher is white-box (token log-probabilities for KL and for the continuation evaluator V) [Sec 4.3, Eq 8], so it is queried densely, and the environments are text games with restorable states [Sec 5.1]. Nothing separates the agent's errors from a stochastic executor's; the continuation score is a teacher likelihood, not an executed outcome.
