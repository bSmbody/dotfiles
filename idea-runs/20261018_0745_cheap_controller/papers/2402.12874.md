# Skill or Luck? Return Decomposition via Advantage Functions

## What this paper does
Pan & Schölkopf (ICLR 2024) treat environment stochasticity as the actions of an imaginary agent, "nature", whose policy equals the transition kernel. Combining this with the view of the advantage function as the causal effect of an action on the return, they decompose any trajectory's return into the average return V(s0), the agent's advantages A(s_t,a_t) ("skill"), and nature's advantages B(s_t,a_t,s_{t+1}) = V(s_{t+1}) - E[V(s')|s_t,a_t] ("luck"). They use this to extend Direct Advantage Estimation to off-policy data (Off-policy DAE), enforcing the zero-mean constraint on B with a CVAE, and test on MinAtar.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it already decompose an episode's outcome into the decision-maker's contribution and the environment's luck, per step?
Yes. Eq 10: G = V(s0) + sum_t gamma^t (A(s_t,a_t) + gamma B(s_t,a_t,s_{t+1})), read as "the average return, the causal effect of the agent's actions (skill), and the causal effect of nature's actions (luck)" [full text, Sec 3.2, Eq 7-10, Fig 1]. Nature is an imaginary agent with state (s,a) and action s', following p(s'|s,a) [Sec 3.2, Fig 1]. The decomposition is per step and per trajectory, and the luck terms have zero mean under the true transition kernel only, which they must enforce as a constraint ("one limitation ... lies in enforcing the B constraint in stochastic environments") [Sec 3.2, Sec 7]. It cites Mesnard et al. 2021 (future-conditional baselines) as prior work on luck [Sec 6, Luck].
### Goal: Is it applied to hierarchical agents, a separate learned executor, or to deciding where to spend teacher supervision?
No. It is a single-agent off-policy value-learning method evaluated on MinAtar with a CVAE model of transitions [Sec 5, App D]; there is no decision-maker/executor split and no teacher.
