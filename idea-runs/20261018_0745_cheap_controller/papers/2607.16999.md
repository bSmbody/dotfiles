# Counterfactual Shapley Credit Assignment

## What this paper does
Li, Lee and Bareinboim redistribute an episode's return over actions using counterfactual Shapley values (phi-values): for coalitions of steps, actions are replaced by a baseline policy and the trajectory is counterfactually re-simulated, reusing observed transitions when parents match and resampling otherwise. They prove the phi-MDP preserves the optimal policy, give a linear-time coalition estimator, and train phi-PPO with prioritized trajectory replay on toy credit-assignment testbeds (SkillLuck MDP, combinatorial lock, DoorKey).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it separate skill from luck, and what access to the environment does it need?
Credit is the causal contribution f(z) = Y - Y_{sigma_z}, the observed return minus the counterfactual return when the coalition's actions are replaced [full text, Sec 3.1, Eq 7]. This needs a counterfactual simulator: when (state, action) match the observed trajectory the observed transition is reused, otherwise it is resampled, under Assumption 3.3 (exogenous noise independent across steps) [Sec 3.1]. In SkillLuck it is the only method that gives zero credit to the last action on the luck branch, and at reward noise sigma=3 "shared noise cancels in the counterfactual difference" [Sec 5.1, Fig 1, Fig 5].
### Goal: Is it applied to hierarchical agents or used to decide where to spend teacher supervision?
No. It is single-agent policy-gradient reward redistribution on small synthetic MDPs and gridworlds [Sec 5]; there is no distinction between a decision-maker and a separate stochastic executor, and no teacher.
