# StageGuard: Learning Stage Transitions for Long-Horizon Robot Tasks via Agentic Distillation

## What this paper does
StageGuard distills a large teacher VLM (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) into a Qwen3.5-0.8B execution monitor that, at each monitoring round, outputs a decision in {continue, advance, skip}, the next subtask, and an updated text history h_t. The teacher runs a three-layer agentic prompt (state / task / decision layers) over annotated demonstration trajectories and is told the ground-truth decision, so its trace explains the annotated boundary; the student then rewrites that trace into a compact self-explanation, the teacher judges among 3 candidates, and the student is SFT'd with a weighted LM loss (lambda=0.5 on reasoning tokens). Evaluated offline on LIBERO-Logic and 6 BEHAVIOR-1K tasks, plus closed-loop BEHAVIOR-1K with a PDDL planner and pi0.5 skills.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to track task state and judge sub-task completion?
Task state is a free-text semantic history h_t that the student itself generates each round and feeds back into the next round's input x_{t+1}=(g,u_t,o_t,h_{t-1},P) [full text, Sec III-A, III-B, Eq 3-5]. Completion is a per-round 3-way classification (continue/advance/skip) emitted after a compact CoT [Sec III-A Eq 1, Sec V-B]. The task plan P is given (by a PDDL planner in closed loop), so the monitor does not choose subtasks, it only decides when to switch [Sec VI-E].
### Goal: What data is the student trained on, and does it include the states the deployed system visits (failures, off-teacher states)?
Only annotated demonstration trajectories: 10 trajectories per task for training, the rest of the demonstrations for evaluation, with ground-truth subtask segmentation and transition annotations from LIBERO-Logic and BEHAVIOR-1K [Sec VI-A]. The teacher is shown the ground-truth decision d* when building traces ("Ground-truth annotations are used during corpus construction only") [Sec V-A, decision layer]. No rollouts of the deployed hierarchical system, no failure trajectories, and no on-policy relabelling are used; the "skip" (unrecoverable failure) label exists in the decision space but the training corpus is successful demonstrations [Sec III-A, VI-A]. The offline metrics (transition completion within +/-3 s, next-subtask accuracy) are all measured on held-out demonstrations [Sec VI-F].
### Goal: What does it report as its limitation on state tracking / completion judgement?
Closed loop on BEHAVIOR-1K, StageGuard reaches 0.53 success vs 0.63 with ground-truth transitions (oracle) and 0.35 decision-only [Table III]; the authors say incorrect switching "can prematurely terminate a subtask or trigger an inappropriate next skill, allowing errors to propagate" [Sec VII-B]. Real-robot failure from "a missed transition between plate placement and drawer closing due to partial observability" [Sec VIII]. Stated limitations: relies on annotated demonstrations and expensive supervision generation, sensitive to partial observability, cannot compensate for low-level control failures; future work lists recovery policies and active perception [Sec IX]. Off-the-shelf GPT-5.6 Luna gets 37.9% transition completion on LIBERO and 1.96% on BEHAVIOR-1K, small open VLMs ~0% [Tables I, II].
### Goal: How does it handle the error of its own history h_t (self-generated state) compounding over rounds?
It does not treat it explicitly: h_t is generated by the student and carried forward without any verification or correction mechanism [Sec V-B, final paragraph]; on demonstrations the teacher-provided h_t used in training is consistent with successful execution, so the student never sees a history that disagrees with the scene.
