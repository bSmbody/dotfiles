# Super Apriel: One Checkpoint, Many Speeds

## What this paper does
Builds a 15B supernet from Apriel 1.6: every one of 48 decoder layers has four trained mixers (FA, SWA, KDA, GDN), trained by stochastic distillation (one mixer per layer sampled uniformly each step; activation MSE + reverse KL + forward KL against a frozen teacher, shared FFNs/embeddings frozen), then mixer-only SFT. A placement is chosen per speed preset by a cluster-expansion surrogate (unary + pairwise/triplet layer interaction terms, Bayesian ridge) solved exactly by dynamic programming under a linear cost model, refined with acquisition-driven real evaluations. Studies how placement rankings drift during training at 0.5B and 15B.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is a placement scored and searched, and does the score use the hybrid's own generations or teacher-forced text?
S(x) is a quality score from the supernet; the default objective is the uniform average over MATH500, AIME-24 and AIME-25 of the log-likelihood of teacher-generated traces under the candidate placement, "a proxy for generative accuracy", validated by correlation with exact match on a random sample [full text, Section 7.1 "Placement scoring"]. So the score is teacher-forced on long reasoning traces, not on the student's rollouts. The surrogate models layer interactions explicitly as n-body terms (order <=3, range <=3, selected by model evidence) and finds the optimum at every cost level by DP [Section 4]. The Limitations list both the likelihood proxy ("a gap may persist ... where likelihood and generation quality diverge") and the short-range interaction truncation [Section 8].

### Goal: Does it re-evaluate placements as training proceeds, and are rankings stable?
Yes, as an analysis: 1,000 random canary placements are re-scored at checkpoints. Rankings "crystallize almost immediately" over the full set, but at 15B the frontier is less stable, especially for medium-cost placements; early-selected frontier placements drift but stay close to the final frontier [Section 5.2.3]. Targeted training on a few presets speeds them early but stochastic training catches up at 0.5B [Section 5.2.2]. The placement is not re-chosen from residual failures; there is no per-example or per-position localization.

### Goal: Is there any on-policy or RL stage, and how does it handle long-range retrieval?
No on-policy stage in the released models; GRPO RL is listed as future work, with the idea that FA generations could supervise placements that fail to obtain reward [Section 9.1]. Faster presets regress drastically on RULER/NIAH [Section 8, Table 12].
