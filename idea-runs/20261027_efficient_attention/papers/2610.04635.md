# LatentIndex: Cross-Layer Sharing with Layer-Specific Selection for Sparse Attention

## What this paper does
Shares a latent indexer key cache across a group of layers, MLA style, while keeping layer-specific query and decoder projections, so each layer still selects its own tokens. Absorbing the key decoders into the queries lets each layer score the shared cache directly. The paper gives training-free calibration for DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5, cutting indexer-cache storage by 61.1% with four-layer sharing and improving attention-mass recall over IndexCache, plus a hierarchical-selection variant for faster decoding. It also studies a training-aware instantiation on DeepSeek-V2-Lite at 64K.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is the indexer trained given the hard top-K selection, and does it get a signal on tokens it did not select (dense teacher, exploration, or neither)?
In the training-aware variant, a dense-attention warmup first freezes the backbone and fits each layer's indexer by KL to the head-averaged dense attention over the full causal prefix [full text, Section 3.3 "Dense-attention warmup"]. Sparse training then activates top-2048 selection. The indexers keep receiving KL supervision "using each layer's sparse-attention probabilities, with both distributions normalized over the selected positions", and top-k passes no LM gradient to the selector [Section 3.3 "Sparse training"]. This is the DSA scheme: off-support signal exists only in the dense warmup, and there is no exploration. Warmup Recall@2048 stays about 1.3-1.5 points below DSA [Section 4.6, Figure 3a].
