# Reinforcement and Imitation Learning via Interactive No-Regret Learning

## What this paper does
Introduces AggreVaTe, an extension of DAgger that learns to minimize the expert's cost-to-go rather than to copy the expert's action. At each iteration it runs the current learner (optionally mixed with the expert) up to a random time, explores an action there, hands control to the expert to the horizon, and records the observed cost-to-go. The aggregated (state, action, cost-to-go) data train a cost-sensitive classifier or regressor through a no-regret online learner. It also gives NRPI, a reinforcement-learning variant, and a unified view of approximate policy iteration.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is AggreVaTe's data-collection mechanism, and why does it learn from cost-to-go instead of the expert's action?
Roll in with the current policy pi_i = beta_i pi* + (1-beta_i) pi_hat_i to a uniformly random time t, execute an exploration action a_t, roll out with the expert from t+1 to T, and record the estimate Q-hat of the expert's cost-to-go; aggregate (s, t, a, Q-hat) and train the next policy as a cost-sensitive classifier, e.g. by regressing Q and taking argmin [full text, Section 2.2, Algorithm 1; Section 2.3]. Cost-to-go tells the learner how much future cost an action commits it to, so it learns to avoid actions after which "falling off a cliff or crash is inevitable" even when the expert would rarely take them; DAgger's 0-1 imitation loss "neither require[s] nor benefit[s] from information about the cost of actions" [Section 1; Section 2.2]. Because data are collected on states the learner itself visits, the guarantee is linear in T: J(pi-hat) <= J(pi*) + T[eps_class + eps_regret] + O(Q_max T log T / (alpha N)) [Section 2.4, Theorem 2.1].
### Goal: What does it assume about the expert, and when does it fail?
It assumes the expert is good and that the policy class contains similarly good policies. "In cases where the expert is much better than any policy in Pi, the expert's cost-to-go may be a very optimistic estimate of the true future cost", and the learner can fail to find a safer, reachable policy (the narrow cliff road vs. the long safe road example) [full text, Section 2.5 "Limitations"]. The exploration action can be chosen uniformly or by a contextual-bandit rule [Section 2.3].
