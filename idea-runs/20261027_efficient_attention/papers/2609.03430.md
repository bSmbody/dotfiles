# Random Attention: Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Efficient Reasoning

## What this paper does
Tests whether the selection score of KV eviction matters for reasoning models. Random Attention pins the entire prompt and keeps a uniformly random top-K of the generated trace independently per KV head, with no scoring pass. Across Qwen3-4B/14B/32B and Phi-4-reasoning on six math/science/code tasks it matches the strongest baselines (SnapKV, R-KV, VaSE, TriAttention) and serves 32-43% more tokens/s in vLLM. Controlled studies attribute most baseline differences to prompt protection and show the reasoning trace is redundant in text and across heads; the residual case for a selection signal is a once-stated fact needed much later.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
No signal: s_i = +inf for prompt positions and s_i ~ Uniform(0,1) otherwise, drawn independently per KV head at every periodic eviction, then top-K [full text, Section 3]. Eviction is permanent.

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Once every method protects the prompt, SnapKV, R-KV and VaSE land within 2.2 points of one another and do not beat random; on Qwen3-4B they trail random by 4-6 points [Section 5.1, Table 2]. A planted-fact probe shows cross-head pooling is strongly superadditive: the best single head retrieves the fact in 3% of trials, two heads together 60%, three 83%, all eight 99%; the shape of surviving copies does not matter [Section 5.2, Fig 4]. The residual failure: a passcode stated once 57 compression rounds before the question is retrieved 0% by Random Attention, 0.4% SnapKV, 1.6% TriAttention, 34% VaSE, 84% R-KV, plausibly via R-KV's redundancy term favoring keys unlike the rest of the cache [Section 5.3, Table 3]. Open problems named: budgeting long prompts (code) and recovering once-stated facts [Section 1].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
The paper's critique of prior work: all prior evictors score each cached token independently and keep the top-scoring ones, premised on the score deciding accuracy [Section 1]. Its own finding is that what decides retrieval is whether some usable copy survives in some head — a set-level property across heads and positions, not a per-token ranking [Section 5.2].
