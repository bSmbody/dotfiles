# Spend Bytes on Breadth: Precision-Count Trade-offs for Decode-Time KV Compression in Long Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

## What this paper does
BreadthKV: at a fixed per-head KV byte budget during long-CoT decoding, quantize decode tokens older than a 128-token fp16 window to b bits (KIVI format) and keep proportionally more of them under SnapKV-D eviction; b in {2,3,4} is chosen per model and budget by a 60-problem end-to-end AIME calibration. Beats eviction alone in 17/18 settings on three 7-8B dense reasoning models; much of the eviction loss is derailed runs that hit the length cap.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it treat the attention-layer KV cache versus the recurrent state, and is the byte budget shared between them?
Dense models only; the budget counts the KV bytes of decode tokens per (sequence, layer, KV head); prompt excluded [full text, Section 3]. No recurrent state.
### Goal: Does a component-local distortion proxy predict the end-to-end best allocation?
No. "Offline distortion curves at equal bytes ... get the preference wrong on two of the three models; for Qwen3-8B they favor 4 over 2 bits, the opposite of what happens end to end"; neither offline attention error nor a per-head online probe finds the right width, so the paper falls back to end-to-end calibration [full text, Sections 1, 5.3]. Compression runs on a fixed schedule every 128 decode steps [Section 3].
