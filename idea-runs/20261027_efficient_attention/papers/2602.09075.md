# Learning to Remember, Learn, and Forget in Attention-Based Models

## What this paper does
Palimpsa casts in-context learning in gated linear attention as a continual-learning problem and solves it with Bayesian metaplasticity (building on MESU). Each memory state keeps a posterior mean and a diagonal precision ("importance"). The importance accumulates from write evidence and decays toward a prior, and it sets each state's learning rate and its effective retention. Gated linear models (Longhorn, DeltaNet, GDN, Mesanet) fall out as posterior approximations, and Mamba2 is the limit where forgetting dominates. It is tested on MQAR, 170M/760M language modelling, and a 2.7B Mamba2 model in which 8 slow-forgetting layers are converted by fine-tuning (LongBench, RULER).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it bring continual-learning consolidation into a linear-attention / delta-rule state, and what signal builds the importance that protects a state?
Yes, explicitly: "ICL essentially tackles a continual and online learning problem", and metaplasticity "dynamically modifies the learning rate to preserve important prior information" [full text, Section 1; Figure 1]. The update is I_t = alpha_t I_{t-1} + (1 - alpha_t) I_prior + beta_t ⊗ k_t^2 and mu_t = alpha_t (I_{t-1}/I_t) ⊙ mu_{t-1} + (1/I_t) ⊙ [(beta_t ⊙ v_t) ⊗ k_t] [Section 2.2, Eq 5]. Importance is therefore built only from *write* evidence (squared write keys weighted by beta), per element (diagonal covariance, "for computational tractability"). Queries appear only in the read y_t = mu_t q_t [Eq 2; Appendix A.12]. No read-side or query-side signal enters importance.
### Goal: Does importance shield the state from decay, and not only from new writes?
Yes. The carry-over factor alpha_t I_{t-1}/I_t approaches 1 when I_{t-1} is much larger than I_prior and the new write mass is small, and it is at most alpha_t for a state at the prior. So consolidated states decay less than plastic ones, and they also take smaller writes. On MQAR the ratio (I_max - I_min)/I_min reaches about 40 ("highly consolidated synapses become 40-fold more stable than plastic ones") [Section 3.2, Figure 3]. Protection is per element, not along key-space directions.
### Goal: What does it gain and cost?
Metaplasticity improves MQAR over its ablations, and the gap grows with length [Section 3.1, Figure 2]. At 760M, Palimpsa-D beats GDN by about 0.8 points on commonsense reasoning. At 2.7B, converting 8 Mamba2 layers gives +0.84 commonsense and +1.1 LongBench, but it is worse on RULER variable tracing at 4K-16K and better only from 32K [Section 3.4]. Training from scratch is harder than fine-tuning [Section 3.3]. The fused Triton scan is about 1.5x (170M) to 3x (760M) slower than Simple GLA in training because of register pressure, and recurrent decoding is about 4x slower [Appendix A.12-A.13].


### Goal: Does Palimpsa's importance act on a delta-rule erase (removal of old content at the written key), and does it distinguish the on-key overwrite from collateral removal at other keys?
No. Palimpsa's own update is a metaplastic gated linear rule: mu_t = alpha_t (I_{t-1}/I_t) ⊙ mu_{t-1} + (1/I_t) ⊙ [(beta_t ⊙ v_t) ⊗ k_t], with diagonal importance from beta ⊗ k^2 [full text, Section 2.2 / Eq 5; Appendix A.5 / Eq 28-31]. Importance divides the whole new write and raises the carry-over of the old state elementwise; there is no k k^T erase term in Eq 31. The delta rule appears only as the special case with identity precision and a linearised plasticity term, which gives mu_t = mu_{t-1}(alpha_t I - beta_t k k^T) + beta_t v k^T, i.e. Gated DeltaNet with no importance at all [Appendix A.6 / Eq 32-34]. Palimpsa-D is described as a drop-in replacement for the Gated Delta Rule "differing only by an additional projection for beta_t and an intra-layer residual term" [Section 2.2]. So importance is per state element, comes from write evidence, and scales stability and plasticity together; nothing separates overwriting the slot at k_t from collateral removal along other stored keys. Implementation: a fused Triton chunk-wise associative scan that must hold all intra-chunk M_t and I_t in registers, forcing small chunks [Appendix A.12].
