# MesaNet: Sequence Modeling by Locally Optimal Test-Time Training

## What this paper does
Builds a chunkwise-parallel, numerically stable Mesa layer that, at every token, outputs the prediction of the linear model that exactly minimises a (gated, L2-regularised) in-context least-squares loss over all past key-value pairs, using conjugate gradient (CG) to solve (H_t + Lambda)^{-1} q_t. Scales MesaNets to 1B parameters and compares with GLA, Mamba2, xLSTM, (Gated) DeltaNet and Transformers, including a test-time-compute analysis.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What memory update rule does it use and what determines the write/erase strength per token?
Section 2: linear attention state Phi_t = gamma_t Phi_{t-1} + beta_t v_t k_t^T with forget/input gates "usually a function of the current input e_t". The Mesa layer maintains both this and the key second-moment H_t (gated sum of k k^T), and reads out Phi_t (H_t + Lambda)^{-1} q_t, i.e. the locally optimal regression solution rather than one GD step. Other rules (GLA, DeltaNet, Longhorn, Omega/Atlas) are derived as cheaper approximations [Appendix B].

### Goal: What does the optimal (second-order) solve cost, and how does it scale with length?
Section 3: the Mesa layer is "roughly k times as costly" in flops as GLA/Mamba2/xLSTM and "k-1 times more costly" than (Gated) DeltaNet for k CG steps; k=0 recovers GLA. Section 5: condition numbers are bimodal across heads; in every layer 1-2 heads have "large and growing condition number with sequence length", so reducing CG steps uniformly raises NLL "especially on tokens later in the sequence"; a dynamic stopping criterion reaches parity with ~9 steps on average. Discussion: "The biggest shortcoming of the MesaNet in its current form is the increase in test-time compute".

### Goal: What does it report as its limitation on recall / memory capacity at long context?
Section 6: RNNs including MesaNets "outperform transformers significantly early in sequences, while underperforming in next-token prediction and benchmark performance when longer contexts are needed". Appendix J, "The fundamental limit of RNNs with finite memory": "The interpretation and the relevance of certain information in a sentence can drastically change even at the last token. Since softmax stores all information of the past ... it can recall information relevant to the current query ... RNNs need to anticipate when processing information which needs saving such that it can be accessed later on."
