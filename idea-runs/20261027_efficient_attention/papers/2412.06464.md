# Gated Delta Networks: Improving Mamba2 with Delta Rule

## What this paper does
Proposes the gated delta rule S_t = S_{t-1}(alpha_t(I - beta_t k_t k_t^T)) + beta_t v_t k_t^T, combining Mamba2-style data-dependent scalar decay alpha_t with DeltaNet's corrective rank-one write, and interprets it as test-time SGD on the per-token regression loss 1/2||S k_t - v_t||^2 with adaptive weight decay. Extends the WY chunkwise algorithm to the gated case and builds hybrids with sliding-window/full attention. Evaluated at 1.3B on LM, commonsense, S-NIAH, real-world recall and LongBench.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What memory update rule does it use and what determines the write/erase strength per token?
Eq. 10 [full text, Section 3.1]: scalar decay alpha_t in (0,1) and scalar step beta_t, both data-dependent gates of the current token. Table 1 frames every rule (LA, Mamba2, Longhorn, DeltaNet, GDN) as the closed-form solution of a per-token online objective: a proximity term ||S_t - alpha_t S_{t-1}||_F^2 plus a loss on the current pair (k_t, v_t) only. The paper notes that the retention regulariser becomes problematic "when the state becomes saturated", so alpha_t "relaxes the regularization term" to allow selective forgetting [Section 3.1].

### Goal: What does it report as its limitation on recall / memory capacity at long context?
S-NIAH case study [Section 3.2, Table 2, 1.3B]: on S-NIAH-2 GDN falls from 100.0 (1K) to 92.2 (4K) to 29.6 (8K); on S-NIAH-3 from 86.6 (1K) to 27.6 (4K). DeltaNet without decay collapses on S-NIAH-2 (45.6 at 2K, 14.4 at 8K) due to "memory collision" when the fixed state stores all potentially relevant information; Mamba2 decays too fast (S-NIAH-1 30.4 at 8K). On real-world recall tasks linear recurrent models "show a significant performance gap compared to Transformers" [Section 4, In-context retrieval]. Hybrids improve because attention "reduces the memory management burden on their recurrent components" [Length extrapolation].

### Goal: Does the rule decide what to retain using anything other than recency and the current token?
No. Retention is the product of past alpha's (recency) and the current-key projection; there is no signal from later reads/queries. The decay/delta trade-off is shown as a dilemma: decay hurts retention (S-NIAH-1), no decay causes collision (S-NIAH-2) [Section 3.2].
