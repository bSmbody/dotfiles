# DAMP: Decay-Aware Mixed-Precision Recurrent-State Quantization

## What this paper does
Post-training recurrent-state quantization for GDN/KDA hybrids (Qwen3.6-35B, Kimi-Linear-48B, Kimi-K3). Scores key channels by local quantization error times decay-based error retention over the next H updates, stores the top-K_hi channels per head in FP16 and the rest in INT8 with stochastic rounding, with a fused packed-state kernel in SGLang. At 9.9 bits/value accuracy is close to FP32; state storage -69.1%, TPOT up to -19%.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is precision/capacity allocated across state units, and is the allocation fixed or input-dependent?
Risk score C_u = E_cal[e_{t,u}(1 + sum_j prod_r a_{t+r,u}^2)] from calibration states (36 prompts, 1024-token input, 4096 generated, H=256) [full text, Sections 5.1, 6.1]. "The selected indices ... remain fixed throughout inference" [full text, Section 5.1]. The justification is that slow-decaying channels are stable across tasks (Spearman 0.994/0.999 vs Math) [full text, Section 4.2, Finding 2]. The conclusion calls it "a static mixed-precision layout" [full text, Section 7].
### Goal: What does it report about uniform low-bit failure?
Uniform INT8/FP8 degrade complex reasoning and INT4/NVFP4 collapse it to near zero, which is why a protected FP16 channel subset is needed [abstract; full text, Section 1].
