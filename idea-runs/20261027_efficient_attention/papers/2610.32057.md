# HyScale: Scaling Long-Context Training for Learned Sparse Attention

## What this paper does
A training system for production learned sparse attention (DSA in GLM-5.1, CSA in DeepSeek-V4, QSA in Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, MSA in MiniMax-M3) at 256K-1M tokens. It finds selection (indexer scoring of every previous entry plus top-k) is the bottleneck, at 47% of a GLM-5.1 step at 1M. HyTopK finds exact top-k on the fly without materializing the score table, and selection replay reuses forward indices during recomputation. It adds decoupled context-parallel overlap and locality-aware sparse kernels, for a 2.07x speedup over Megatron-LM on 512 H20 GPUs.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: In native long-context sparse training, how is the indexer trained, and does any signal reach unselected entries?
The indexer learns from its own loss, a KL from the heads-averaged attention p_t over the selected set T_t to Softmax of the indexer scores on T_t. Both sides are detached, "so this loss updates only the indexer and only through the k selected scores", and sparse attention back-propagates only to the selected entries [full text, Section 2.1, Eq 3]. The system relies on this: "nothing reads the scores of the other keys", so the score table is never stored [Section 2.4]. Training sequences run to 1M with k = 2048 (0.2% of scores kept) [Section 2.3]. No dense teacher appears in this training stage.

### Goal: Is there evidence that selections of nearby queries are correlated?
Yes. "Adjacent queries select largely the same keys": in GLM-5.1, two adjacent queries share 71% of their top-2048 keys on average (21 of 78 layers, 128K-192K documents), so their union is only 1.29x one selection [Section 3.4.2]. HyScale's kernels exploit this by processing adjacent-query pairs over the union of their selections.
