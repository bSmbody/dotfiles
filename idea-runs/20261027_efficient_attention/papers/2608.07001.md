# Every Cache Entry Earns Its Place: Global Allocation of Resolution and Coverage for KV Cache Compression

## What this paper does
GraceKV formulates prefill KV compression as a global budget-allocation problem over all layer-KV-head-slot units. Each slot gets a prototype tree whose leaves are token KV entries and internal nodes are merged prototypes; "Add" a root expands coverage, "Split" a node raises resolution, and all candidate actions compete in one priority queue by marginal utility per unit cost. A singleton floor preserves a few high-value exact entries. Training-free, all on GPU; ranks first in 25 of 32 long-context settings and stays robust to 128x compression.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
Marginal utility per cost U(a) = Delta(a)/c(a), with Delta from a weighted distortion reduction of adding or splitting a prototype node [full text, Section 3.4, Eq 12-13]; greedy global allocation after one standard prefill using collected queries, keys, values, head outputs in the question region and token surprisal [Section 3.1, Eq 2-3]. One-shot after prefill; discarded content is merged into prototypes or dropped.

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Limitation: globally optimal allocation is exponential, so it uses greedy allocation plus a singleton floor because "step-wise marginal allocation may underestimate non-smooth gains toward high-value leaves" [Sections 3.4, 5]. Prompt prefix and question suffix are kept intact outside the budget [Section 3.1].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Uses question-region queries available at prefill (query-aware, long-input setting); allocation is set-level across heads/layers but computed once, not during long generation; marginal gains are greedy per node.
