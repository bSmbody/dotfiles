# QEvict: Recoverable Quantized KV Eviction for Attention-Drift-Robust Long-Context Decoding

## What this paper does
Questions two assumptions of KV eviction — that token importance is stable enough for permanent deletion and that a binary keep/evict decision suffices. Manages history as contiguous windows ranked by cumulative attention and assigns each window to full precision, a recoverable low-bit (INT2/INT4) tier that is still attended, or eviction, under a byte budget. Windows can be promoted and demoted between full and quantized tiers; only windows outside both tiers are removed. Evaluated on LongBench, RULER and GSM8K with Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen and Mistral.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
Cumulative window score: summed attention probabilities of the window's tokens over each routing interval, averaged over query heads [full text, Section 4.2, Eq 1]. Candidates (full, quantized, aged recent windows) are ranked; top K_f go to full precision, next K_q to the quantized tier, the rest are evicted [Section 4.3, Eq 4-5]. Full <-> Quantized is reversible; Quantized -> Evicted is permanent [Eq 6].

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Diagnostics with a FullKV reference: binary window eviction discards 64.8% of future attention mass vs 35.7% when an INT2 tier is added [Section 3, Observation II]; importance is "persistent but not static" — the deployed policy shows a 6.18% window revival rate vs 0.98% for the oracle, and demotion is much more frequent than promotion [Observation III]. Limitations: SDPA fallback to expose attention probabilities, dequantization overhead, fixed-size windows [Section 5.1].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Training-free; ranking is by a per-window scalar from past cumulative attention; the hierarchy "requires only an ordering" [Section 4.2]. Recovery is possible only for windows still held in the quantized tier, whose scores keep accumulating because they remain attended.
