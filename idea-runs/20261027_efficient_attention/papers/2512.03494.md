# A Preliminary Study on the Promises and Challenges of Native Top-k Sparse Attention

## What this paper does
Short report showing that exact top-k decoding (each query attends only to its highest-scoring keys) matches or beats full attention on HELMET, LongBench v2 and math benchmarks with Llama-3-8B-ProLong and Qwen3-32B; that SFT with an exact top-k attention kernel (1% ratio) further improves top-k decoding; that downstream accuracy rises with approximate top-k retrieval precision; and that DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp's Lightning Indexer reaches about 60% precision.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is native top-k attention trained, and what makes exact top-k hard to make hardware-efficient?
Training wraps FlashAttention: exact top-k indices and scores are pre-computed and passed into the kernel as a mask [full text, Section 2]. Exact top-k needs the full score matrix, O(N^2) time/space, conflicting with FlashAttention's design of never materializing logits, so practical systems need approximate selectors (ANN, Lightning Indexer) [Section 3].

### Goal: How good are learned approximate selectors, and how does selector precision affect quality?
On HELMET-128K, accuracy improves with retrieval precision p until saturation at a fixed 2048-token window [Section 3, Figure 3]. The Lightning Indexer in DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp averages ~60% precision against exact per-head top-k; it shares one KV set across heads (MQA-style) [Section 3]. The report does not describe how the indexer is trained.
