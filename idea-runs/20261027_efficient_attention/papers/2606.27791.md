# NLL-Guided Full-Attention Layer Selection for Training-Free Sliding-Window Adaptation

## What this paper does
For a Transformer run with sliding-window attention (SWA) during prefill and full attention during decode (SWAA), picks which k layers keep full attention in prefill. Each layer is scored by the drop in teacher-forced NLL on answer tokens when only that layer is switched from SWA to full attention, averaged over 64 long-context calibration examples; top-k layers are kept. On LongMemEval with Qwen3-4B, 1/4 full-attention layers reach 64.6%, matching a 1/2 periodic pattern.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal does it use to choose full-attention layers, and is it computed on the model's own generations?
Delta_l = L_ans(SWA at l) − L_ans(FA at l) with all other layers SWA, i.e., one-layer-at-a-time toggling, mean NLL over reference answer tokens under teacher forcing, "requiring only forward passes without generation" [full text, Section 3.2, Eq 1]. Selection is top-k of independent per-layer scores, no interaction term [Section 3.3]. L+1 forward passes per example, 64 examples of 16K-32K prompts, ~15 minutes on 4 GPUs [Section 3.4]. The answers are reference answers, not the model's own rollouts, and no reasoning/long-CoT task is evaluated; decode stays full attention [Sections 3.1, A.3].

### Goal: What does it report about noise and about whether the signal is long-range specific?
Long-prompt vs short-prompt rankings correlate weakly (Spearman 0.306), and long-prompt Delta-NLL is 85.6x larger, which the authors read as the signal reflecting long-range needs [Section 4.4]. With 16 instead of 64 calibration examples, Jaccard overlap is 0.64 and accuracy drops 2.4 pp [Section 4.6]. On 500 benchmark examples the binomial 95% half-width is about ±4.2 pp, which the authors flag as the scale of end-task noise [Appendix A.4].
