# Preserving Updates in Low-Bit Recurrent State Quantization

## What this paper does
Training-free 4-bit recurrent-state quantization for GDN, GLA, Mamba2 and RetNet. Finds per-step requantization errors add coherently because small updates are lost in rounding (grid spacing fitted to the accumulated state is far larger than one update), and proposes Low-Rank Bypass (buffer small updates in low-rank form, merge periodically) and Bounded Directional Rounding (round reversed elements toward their update). 4.7-5.3x state memory reduction, up to 1.9x decode speedup.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What happens to recurrent-state compression as decode horizon grows, and what limits current methods?
Under 4-bit quantization 68-96% of nonzero update elements are lost per step (median update/grid-spacing ratio 0.02-0.21), and these lost updates carry the coherent error [full text, Section 2.3]. INT4 degradation grows with context length and INT4 hurts recall even at 512-1K tokens (worst on FDA); LRB+BDR narrows the gap to within ~1 point on GDN/GLA [full text, Section 4]. Evaluation goes to 8K PPL; hybrids and joint weight/activation quantization are left to future work [full text, Section 6].

### Goal: What error-persistence model does its allocation use, and does it include delta-rule erasure and cross-step error correlation?
Eq 3 splits ||E_T||^2 into diagonal terms sum a^2 ||R_tau||^2 and off-diagonal terms sum a a <R_tau, R_tau'>; "conventional quantization methods aim to minimize the error of each quantization and therefore account only for these [diagonal] terms". Measured inner products are positive even for steps thousands of tokens apart, and the coherent gain g_T reaches up to 86x at 4K tokens [full text, Section 2.2, Eq 3, Figure 1]. The analysis takes scalar decay A = aI (for GDN the delta write is folded into the written value v-bar) [Section 2.2; Section 3.1 footnote].
### Goal: When is rounding/quantization committed or precision changed (timing), and is that schedule fixed or input-adaptive?
LRB merges the low-rank bypass into the quantized state every m steps, and at a merge holds back elements whose accumulated update is below eps_L times the grid spacing, keeping their rank-k approximation [Section 3.1, Eq 6]. So the merge period is fixed but the per-element release at a merge is magnitude-adaptive. Hybrids with softmax attention are left to future work [Section 6].
