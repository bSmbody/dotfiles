# Tail-Replay: Escaping the Curse of Linear Attention in Prefix Caching for Hybrid LLMs

## What this paper does
A prefix-caching mechanism for GDN-based hybrid LLMs that stores no recurrent-state checkpoints. It caches exact full-attention KV plus each full-attention layer's output hidden states; on a cache hit it rebuilds every linear-attention group's state from zero by replaying the most recent 5-10% of the matched prefix. Evaluated on OLMo-Hybrid-7B, Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.6-27B on LongBench and RULER, with TTFT speedups up to 14.3x at 32K.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it rebuild the recurrent state on a cache hit, and does it store any part of the state?
It stores no state. For each group (one FA layer plus the following linear layers) it initializes S-hat_{m-k} = 0 and applies the GDN recurrence S_i = T_i S_{i-1} + beta_i v_i k_i^T with T_i = alpha_i (I - beta_i k_i k_i^T) over the last k = ceil(r m) cached FA output hiddens [full text, Section 2 Eq 1; Section 3 Eq 2]. Caching FA outputs confines replay error to each group [Section 3]. The transition T_i acts only on the key index of the state.
### Goal: Where does replay lose quality, and how much does the state matter in these hybrids?
Zeroing the linear states without replay collapses RULER from 0.987 to 0.299 (Qwen3.6-27B), 0.960 to 0.415 (Qwen3.5-4B) and 0.812 to 0.215 (OLMo-Hybrid-7B), and LongBench drops 0.426 to 0.313 etc. [Section 4, Table 1]. With replay, retention is 92.8-98.9% on LongBench at r=5% and 93.9-98.1% at r=10%; the worst model is OLMo-Hybrid-7B (LongBench 92.8%/93.9%, RULER 93.1%/96.7%) [Table 1]. Raising replay to 10% "raises TTFT at 32K, where replay becomes the dominant cost" [Section 4, Serving efficiency].
