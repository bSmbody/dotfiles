# SIFt-RLS: Subspace of Information Forgetting Recursive Least Squares

## What this paper does
Adaptive-control paper on forgetting in recursive least squares (RLS). Uniform exponential forgetting multiplies the whole information matrix by lambda each step, so without persistent excitation the covariance of unexcited directions blows up ("covariance windup"). SIFt-RLS forgets only inside the row space of the current regressor block (the "information subspace"), for vector measurements phi_k in R^{p x n}. It first truncates small singular values of the regressor, then discounts only the component of the information matrix parallel to that subspace, then adds the new data. It gives explicit upper and lower covariance bounds that hold with no persistent-excitation assumption, plus stability results.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism of directional (subspace-of-information) forgetting, and why does uniform forgetting fail?
Uniform forgetting R_{k+1} = lambda R_k + phi^T phi "can be interpreted as forgetting uniformly over all directions"; windup "occurs when the information subspace does not contain particular directions over a large number of time steps, while forgetting is uniform" [full text, Section 4 intro / Eq 7]. SIFt decomposes R_k into a part parallel to the row space of the filtered regressor phi_bar and a part orthogonal to it (Definition 5, Eq 14-15), and forgets only the parallel part: R_bar = lambda R^par + R^perp = R - (1-lambda) R phi_bar^T (phi_bar R phi_bar^T)^{-1} phi_bar R [Section 4.2 / Eq 27]. Then R_{k+1} = R_bar + phi_bar^T phi_bar and theta_{k+1} = theta_k + P_{k+1} phi_bar^T (y_bar - phi_bar theta_k) [Section 4.3 / Eq 29-30]. Directions the new data does not touch keep their old information. If p >= n and the block is full rank, SIFt reduces exactly to exponential forgetting [Proposition 10].

### Goal: Is every update low-rank, and what does one step cost?
Yes. The forgetting step on the covariance is a rank-q_k correction, P_bar = P + ((1-lambda)/lambda) phi_bar^T (phi_bar R phi_bar^T)^{-1} phi_bar [Eq 28], and the data step is a rank-q_k Woodbury update, P_{k+1} = P_bar - P_bar phi_bar^T (I + phi_bar P_bar phi_bar^T)^{-1} phi_bar P_bar [Eq 31]. Each costs O(q_k n^2) when ordered as L = phi_bar R first, with q_k <= min(p, n) the number of retained singular values; only q x q matrices are inverted. Both R and P are carried. Round-off asymmetry is fixed by re-symmetrising each step [Section 4.4]. The information-filtering step (compact SVD of phi_k, drop singular values below sqrt(epsilon)) costs O(min(p n^2, p^2 n)) and is "critical to ensure the algorithm is numerically stable" [Section 4.1].

### Goal: What covariance / conditioning guarantees hold, and under which assumptions?
Theorem 3 [Section 5.1]: with no persistent excitation, lambda_min(R_k) >= min{epsilon/(1-lambda), lambda_min(R_0)}, so lambda_max(P_k) <= max{(1-lambda)/epsilon, lambda_max(P_0)}. If regressors are bounded by beta, lambda_max(R_k) <= max{beta/(1-lambda), lambda_max(R_0)}. These bounds do not depend on k (sequence length). Corollary 12 bounds the condition number of the small matrix phi_bar R phi_bar^T that is inverted at each step. The authors note the bounds are "strikingly analogous" to exponential forgetting under persistent excitation with window 1. Conclusion: only constant-rate, uniform forgetting inside the subspace is studied; variable-rate or non-uniform forgetting inside the subspace, and a separate forgetting (e.g. resetting) of the orthogonal complement, are left as future work [Section 7].


### Goal: What does the source say about tuning epsilon and lambda, and which bounds does each part of the proof need?
Tuning [full text, Section 4.4, after Proposition 10]: epsilon "should be small to avoid overly truncating the data". The authors thus concede that the filter also cuts the write. lambda "can be tuned more aggressively (more small)" than in uniform forgetting, "since forgetting only occurs in the information subspace", which is useful when some directions are well excited and others are not. Bounds [Section 5.1 / Theorem 3, Eq 34-37]. The floor on information (Eq 34, lambda_min(R) >= min{epsilon/(1-lambda), lambda_min(R_0)}) is the no-windup bound. The cap (Eq 36, lambda_max(R) <= beta/(1-lambda)) needs bounded regressors and the fact that every written direction is also forgotten. The conditioning of the small inverted matrix (Corollary 12, Eq 38) uses both the floor and the cap. Losing the cap therefore loosens Corollary 12 but leaves Eq 34 untouched.
