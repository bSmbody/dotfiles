
### C5 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [2607.11796] (body read, Sections 3.2, 4.3, 5): the headroom C5 promises to recover is the headroom of a *read mask*. The oracle drops the output terms m = g C_t x_t of modes whose state x was accumulated in full over all past tokens, it holds one mask over a 1024-token window, and the paper says it "does not by itself constitute a compute saving". A policy that actually stores only r units cannot reach that state: a mode it did not keep has lost its past and restarts from new writes. So the "roughly 2x error" gap and the "2-26% recovered" baselines measure a different decision problem than C5's. The headroom of an irreversible-eviction oracle over static masks is unmeasured, and the migration is strongest in exactly the slow, long-horizon modes whose value is their retained past.
  - [2006.16239] (Sections 2, 3, 7): Belady's rule is optimal because an evicted line can be fetched again from a larger memory, so a wrong eviction costs one miss. The recurrent state has no backing store. Eviction destroys content, and a greedy "drop the unit with least future error" teacher is myopic, not the optimal policy, so the "exact oracle" framing is overstated. Parrot also states that no optimal policy is known for multi-level caches, which undercuts the FP16/INT8/drop "cache hierarchy" extension.
  - [2607.11796] (Section 3.1, Eq 1): in Mamba-1, B_t is a dense N-vector, so every token writes every mode. Mamba-2 and GDN writes are dense in the same way. There is no "miss" and no "unit that a new write fills". Admission collapses to re-selecting the top r of all N units at every chunk, so the cache-line analogy adds framing but not a mechanism.
  - [2609.38169] (Section 3.1, Prop 1): in delta-rule layers the error transition (I - beta_t k_t k_t^T) couples key channels. Dropping one GDN key-channel row therefore changes the updates of the kept rows. Per-unit utilities are not additive, so the per-unit ranking that Parrot and KVP rely on ([2602.10238], Prop 1, which requires additivity) has no justification for GDN. The candidate also gives no exact oracle for GDN beyond "one look-ahead roll-out".
  - [2602.04852] (Table 4) and [2608.30386] (Section 3.2): in the hybrids the problem.md scope centres on, static 50% state pruning is already nearly free (Qwen3.5-9B RULER 93.9 to 93.6), and zeroing all linear states moves NIAH by 1 point or less. The quality drop C5 targets (GDN-1.3B NIAH 81.3 to 61.9) occurs in purely recurrent models, which are outside the stated scope. The constraints require state-compression gains to be argued on throughput or prefix-cache cost, which the candidate mentions but does not tie to its mechanism. Per-step gather/scatter and scoring also add overhead to a bandwidth-bound decode step that static masks do not pay.
  - Grounding is otherwise accurate. The Parrot ablation numbers (+9.8 / +3.5 / +16.8), KVP's per-head learned scorer and BASS's near-rank-one, bias-dominated state ([2610.36617], Section 4) are stated correctly.
  - Novelty holds. Searches for "Mamba state pruning", "recurrent state compression", "Gated DeltaNet state compression", "recurrent state eviction", "input-adaptive state pruning", "imitation learning KV cache eviction" and "Belady optimal KV cache eviction" (including runs since 2025-10-28) found no causal, input-adaptive post-training allocator of recurrent-state units. The closest hits were static (BASS, DASC, DRRQR) or about different objects: KVP and KVpop learn eviction for immutable KV tokens; CARVE and ES-SSM change the architecture and train it from scratch; Neural Paging applies a "semantic Belady" to context tokens. [2607.11796] itself names "a learned scheduler" as next work, so concurrent-work risk is high.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The oracle C5 imitates and the headroom it promises come from a read mask over a fully stored state. A memory-saving policy with irreversible eviction (no backing store, unlike Belady) cannot reach that headroom, and the candidate has not measured the headroom it could reach.
- **What a revision must supply**:
  - Redefine the teacher as an irreversible-eviction oracle (per-chunk keep set chosen with look-ahead on the *pruned* trajectory) and state its headroom over static masks. Mamba-1 is a cheap first pilot, since the per-mode instrument can split each state into a retained-content part and a part from new writes.
  - Drop the miss/admission language in favour of per-chunk top-r re-selection.
  - Give a coupling-aware unit definition for GDN, or restrict the method to diagonal and scalar-decay layers.
  - Anchor the payoff in scope: hybrid prefix-cache checkpoint bytes or decode throughput at aggressive budgets (at most 25%), where static masks begin to fail.

### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [0710.3918] Grounding of the source is accurate. CGS keeps a sensor–region incidence graph, a coverage ratio that is positive only above k, and a drowsiness that forbids sleep when any covered region would drop to k. It recomputes greedily, and it beats Randomized Independent Sleeping at matched lifetime (Section III.C Eq 1-3, Section IV.B). But the operating condition is a dense, over-provisioned network: a k-cover of every region exists among the awake sensors, and the scheduler only removes surplus. The source has no rule for which regions to let fall below k when the budget cannot k-cover them all. Its "pessimistic incidence is safe" argument (Section III.D Note 1) also depends on that surplus: over-covering costs only lifetime.
  - Target regime breaks that condition. In the probe setting of [2609.03430] (K = 1024 per head, buffer 64, 8 KV heads on Qwen3-4B), a layer holds about 8K head-entries. At a quorum of k = 3 that is at most about 2.7K positions, against reasoning traces many times longer. So most content units *must* sit below quorum, and choosing which ones is again a per-unit value decision. C1 hands that decision to "leftover budget … to an existing per-token score", which assumes a surplus that does not exist at 1-2K budgets. A pessimistic incidence (missed restatements) here means over-pinning, which uses up the scarce budget, so the CGS safety argument reverses. The mapping needs a budgeted (maximum partial) k-coverage objective with a unit-value term. Without one, "keep every unit at quorum" is not executable.
  - [2609.03430] Measured threshold, grounding checked in the body. The 3% / 60% / 83% / 99% curve is exact-match retrieval for the best single head, "the same two heads", three and all eight (Section 5.2, Fig 4a). The authors warn that exact retrieval floors near 0.03 for a single head and use graded recall R there. On R the cross-head effect is mild: 0.31 together vs 0.10 + 0.16 (Fig 4b, Appendix B Caveats). So part of the "threshold" is the argmax readout. Dealing the fact out token by token across heads, so that no head holds a readable span, barely changes retrieval (0.33 vs 0.39 at equal retained mass). The quantity that matters is retained token-head mass of the fact, not the number of complete copies per head. C1 cites this but still defines a sensor as one (layer, head, position) copy and k as a count of carrier-head copies. The coverage measure should be mass over carrier sites. The knee was also measured only for the *two best* carrier heads on one model. The paper says carrier identity is model-specific and not replicated (Appendix B).
  - [2609.03430] The paper itself argues against the premise. "A value stays usable as long as some heads keep a copy, which is exactly what independent per-head draws maximise." On real MATH500 a shared draw is within 0.3 points of independent draws, so cross-head coverage is not binding on benchmarks (Section 5.2, Appendix D). The residual failure is the once-stated passcode (Table 3), and R-KV already gets 84% there. A quorum rule decides *how many heads* hold a unit, not *which once-stated units matter*. So its leverage on the passcode case depends entirely on the unit detector, which C1 leaves as token identity plus near-duplicate keys, the same signal as R-KV's redundancy term [2505.24133].
  - Novelty holds against the prior work checked. [2610.02235] CORE is position-level with heads fused into one vector and provably diminishing-returns (Appendix B.4). [2606.29563] K-VEC counts unique positions under an additive, equally-informative assumption, at prefill only. [2608.07001] GraceKV allocates globally over slots at prefill only. [2505.24133] R-KV uses a pairwise within-head penalty. Searches run (arXiv, all with a recency pass since 2025-10): "k-coverage KV cache", "cross-head KV cache eviction coverage", "KV cache eviction redundant heads quorum", "set cover KV cache", "submodular KV cache selection", "redundancy-aware KV cache reasoning", "information coverage KV cache eviction decoding", "intermediate result retention KV cache chain-of-thought", "head redundancy KV cache retrieval heads copies". Close hits screened by abstract: 2610.03007 AvoKV-E (per-token read pressure + redundancy + value payload), 2609.22158 StepKV (reasoning steps as retention units but globally ranked scalar scores), 2412.08521 EMS and 2601.13684 HeteroCache (head-wise budgets and merging, not per-unit head multiplicity), 2607.21475 (Poisson-sampled eviction with error certificates). None imposes a per-content-unit k-of-H carrier-head quorum. The closest *suggestion* is [2609.03430] Appendix H: keep the random draw and reserve a few slots per head for a content signal. C1's real delta over that is pinning a quorum of carrier heads instead of all heads. That delta is real but narrow.
  - Contribution type: methodological (an eviction scheduling rule). It passes. Engineering fits the constraints: per-head keep masks only, no new kernel.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: CGS only removes surplus from a network that can k-cover every region, but at 1-2K budgets the cache cannot hold every content unit at quorum, so the method still needs the very per-unit value ranking it set out to replace, and the evidence says the coverage measure should be retained token-head mass at carrier heads, not a count of whole copies.


### C4 verdict

- **Verdict**: revise(focus=novelty)
- **Evidence**:
  - [1812.02353] Source grounding holds. The importance ratio pi/beta, the top-K inclusion multiplier lambda_K = K(1-pi)^(K-1), Boltzmann exploration (top K' deterministic plus sampled rest) and weight capping are all stated correctly [Sections 4.3-5, 6.2]. The candidate also correctly notes that here the propensities are exact because the trainer samples them itself. The source's own assumptions (one rewarded item per page, independent item returns) are not checked, and attention mass is coupled across blocks through the softmax normalizer. The candidate handles this with an IPW estimate of the off-support normalizer, so this is a gap in the argument, not a break in it.
  - [2507.09331] Stated correctly: deterministic terms are computed exactly and the rest is estimated with 1/Q weights, and naively mixing the two biases the estimate [Section 3.3]. The structure is inverted, though. In sampled softmax the item that matters (the positive) is the deterministic term and only the uninformative negatives are sampled. In C4 the item that matters (the missed needle) is exactly the one that has to be sampled. Retrieval practice therefore does not show that this variance regime is benign.
  - [2510.05688] vs [2610.30951] Grounding is misapplied. vAttention's condition is "if the heavy tokens are correctly identified, the residual tail can be approximated with a small sample" [2510.05688, Section 4.1]. C4's target regime is the opposite: the selector has missed a heavy block. OpenAttention measures this regime directly. Behind a practical selector at low reads, random tail sampling is worse than discarding the tail. Its normalizer error grows as tau*CV_T/sqrt(m), and the heaviest unread token is included with probability 0.5% at a 1% read [2610.30951, Section 2.1, Appendix A.2]. So the main risk the candidate names is confirmed in-domain. Averaging over a batch helps only through the selector's parametric generalization across queries, and the candidate does not argue that.
  - [2609.13141, 2606.22874] The motivation is overstated. The candidate claims missed blocks can be fixed only by dense phases that "bring back the O(N^2) cost" growing fastest at 128K-1M. But SpotAttention trains its selector with a dense teacher at only 16K and tracks dense accuracy up to 128K with no length decay [2606.22874, Sections 3, 4.4]. SAS finds sparse scope matches full scope at convergence on MATH500/GPQA/AIME [2609.13141, Section 4.2.2]. SAS also blames its remaining 128K RULER gap on pooled block summaries losing needle information [2609.13141, Appendix B], which is a selector-capacity explanation, not a credit-assignment one. So the gap C4 closes may not be the gap that is actually binding.
  - Missing obvious baseline (no paper found for it). A dense teacher on a random subset of query blocks, run with FlashAttention on gathered rows, gives an exact full-support KL target with no importance-weight variance. At a query fraction of e/N_blocks it costs the same FLOPs as e exploration slots per query block. The candidate must say why spreading noisy per-block exploration across all queries beats exact full rows for a few queries. Otherwise the "sparse-only" claim gives no advantage over this baseline. A related point: the IPW target is unbiased for the full-support (DenseKL) target, and SpotAttention found DenseKL makes the selector "razor-sharp" and miscalibrated compared with SparseKL [2606.22874, Section 5.2]. C4 may inherit that.
  - [2504.12463] Closest out-of-domain prior, not cited. Default MoE and SparseMixer already target "the router gets signal only from the options it selected" in sparse MoE. Default MoE fills the missing outputs with EMA defaults to get a dense router gradient [Sections 3, 4.4]. C4's mechanism, sampled exact feedback with logged propensities, is different. The structural problem is not new, though, and the candidate should place itself against these methods.
  - Novelty search: queries on exploration, bandit, propensity, importance-sampling and off-policy correction for sparse-attention selector or indexer training, with recency, found no paper that trains a sparse-attention selector from propensity-weighted exploration blocks. Recent indexer work (LISA [2607.19358], LatentIndex [2610.04635], LongCat LSA [2608.01662]) uses KL distillation, cross-layer reuse or hierarchical indexing, judged from abstracts only and not read. The mechanism itself looks novel; what is still missing is a case for why it is needed over simpler alternatives.
  - Mapping, transfer assumption and contribution type all pass. Selector = policy, KV blocks = catalog, top-K = page, attention mass on a visited block = reward. The key assumption is named (explored-block attention mass reflects usefulness), and it is the same teacher signal MSA and SAS use. Reusing the arbitrary-index block-sparse kernels plus a weighted fused KL fits the team constraint. It is a training method, so a methodological contribution.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The off-support signal C4 estimates with high-variance importance weights is already available exactly and cheaply from a short-length dense teacher (SpotAttention, 16K to 128K) or from dense attention on a few sampled query rows, and SAS's evidence points to selector capacity, not credit assignment, as the cause of the long-context gap. So the innovator has to show a regime, such as native sparse pretraining with a co-adapting backbone or length-specific long-CoT revisits beyond the training length, where those alternatives fail and propensity-weighted block exploration wins at equal FLOPs.


### C3 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [1605.06065] (Section 3.2, Eq 5-9; Table 2). The source is stated correctly: usage is gamma·u + read weights + write weights, the least-used slot is zeroed and refilled, and the 35.4 -> 62.6% (2nd instance) and 88.4 -> 95.3% (10th) gains are right. But LRUA works because its slots are discrete rows with sharp content addressing. A read weight lands on one slot, and the slot's row *is* the stored content, so "slot i was read" means "association i was read". In a delta-rule state an association k v^T is superposed over all key channels: keys are dense, L2-normalised vectors, not channel-aligned [2605.22791, Eq 10-11; 2606.26560, Eq 8]. Every LM token issues a dense query. So per-channel read mass q_t[i]^2 tells you which *channels* are busy, not which *association* was retrieved. Shielding a hot channel shields every pair with mass on it. The paper itself makes no claim for superposed memories (digest, goal 3). The candidate never says when channel usage separates associations. That holds only if keys and queries are near-sparse or near-orthogonal in the channel basis, and nothing in the cited models gives that.
  - [1605.06065] (Eq 8-9) against the delta rule. The "write into the least-used slot" half of LRUA does not transfer. In LRUA, reads find content by similarity to the row, so content can be relocated to a free slot. In linear attention the write key *is* the retrieval address. Writing a pair along a least-read direction instead of k_t makes it unreachable by the query that needs it. What does transfer is erase pressure: an EDA-style erase address [2606.26560, Eq 8] or decay placed on least-read directions. The candidate should drop the "displace / write into least-used" framing and state that only forgetting is redirected.
  - [1605.06065] (Eq 5) against the candidate's departure. In LRUA a write adds full usage, so a fresh slot is protected until its usage decays. C3 instead gives writes "only a small initial usage", so "a pair that is written and never read becomes the first candidate for eviction, whatever its age". That is exactly the MK-NIAH / passkey needle, written once and first queried at the end. The rule would then evict needles *ahead of* recency order, which is worse on the very benchmarks the candidate cites (MK-NIAH-1 72.6 -> 37.8 [2605.22791]; CARVE 76.8 -> 41.4 [2606.27229]). The "Main risk" names the no-signal half of this, but not that the rule actively hurts.
  - [2006.16239] (Sections 3-4). Parrot needs Belady labels because replacement is a discrete, non-differentiable decision on a fixed trace. In an end-to-end trained linear RNN, backprop from the later query already sends exact "this pair was needed later" credit to the gates that erased it. So the hindsight label ("read again before overwritten?") is a redundant teacher unless the candidate argues an optimisation reason, such as sparse credit over long gaps. It does not. The anticipation component, which the Main risk says the gain "rests on" for single-query tasks, therefore has no stated advantage over current gates.
  - [2106.06295] (Section 3.2, Eq 15-18). Prior work already conditions write signals on reads. The Recurrent Delta Net computes k, v, q and beta from W x_t + R tanh(y_{t-1}), where y_{t-1} is the memory's previous read-out. So "queries only read the state and never change it" (C3's gap framing) is not true of the literature. RDN's feedback, though, is one step and accumulates no per-direction record. It is also sequential (35K vs 63K words/s, no chunk form). C3's accumulated usage trace is a real delta over RDN and over [2606.27229], whose content signal is recomputed per token (digest, goal 1). The candidate must cite RDN and narrow the claim to "persistent, accumulated read usage".
  - Novelty search (arXiv, plus a recency pass since 2025-10-27): "query-aware forgetting linear attention", "read usage decay recurrent state", "least recently used memory linear attention", "usage-based forgetting state space model", "query-dependent decay gated delta", "retrieval-aware memory update linear attention", "read-write memory linear transformer usage", "query-conditioned forget gate", "access frequency retention recurrent memory", "retrieval strengthens memory fast weights", "memory reconsolidation linear attention", "usage gate linear attention", "gated slot attention", "least used slot memory transformer", "read-dependent forgetting". Close hits screened by abstract all change capacity or addressing, not read-informed retention: 2610.15715 NoteBank (an external episodic store archived "before future queries are known"), 2607.07386 Sparse Delta Memory, 2608.12435 MARCH (state anchors), 2610.02816 GSA-2, 2610.34180 FWR (write routing), 2607.21000 Naju (decoupled retention/write gates from input), 2605.22884 Tensor Cache. None accumulates read usage to steer forgetting. The novelty delta survives, but narrowly, and it sits in a crowded month (Oct 2026).
  - Feasibility and kernel. The claim checks out against [2606.27229] (Sections 6.1, 6.3). Query-only usage is a state-independent per-key-channel prefix scan, so the decay can be precomputed and the fla KDA/GDN-2 chunk kernels run unchanged. The hit-weighted variant costs one chunk-boundary matmul, and key-axis gating keeps WY exact. The DPLR "protected subspace" via [2503.14456] (Section 4.1) is stable, but its subspace would need an online top-r estimate of query covariance. That is the same dense or sequential interference problem as gap 2 of this axis, and the candidate does not address it. Scope fit is priority 3 of problem.md. The evaluation (≤1.3B pretraining, MQAR, RULER) does not test the long-CoT claim. It needs a converted or distilled public KDA/GDN hybrid on a re-reading long-CoT probe, which the H200 budget allows. Contribution type: methodological (an update rule). It passes.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The unit LRUA counts usage on (a discrete, content-addressed slot) has no counterpart in a superposed delta-rule state: per-channel read mass from dense queries does not identify which association was read, and the write half cannot move content to least-used directions without breaking its retrieval key. As specified, the rule also evicts written-but-not-yet-read needles first, which hurts the benchmarks it targets.
- **What a revision must supply**:
  - Define the usage unit so that it singles out associations. Either show that per-channel usage separates pairs under the key statistics of KDA/GDN-2 (for example with a probe of channel sparsity in trained keys), or replace it with a read-usage estimate in key space (accumulated q-q^T mass seen through the state, read at chunk boundaries) together with a parallel-friendly way to act on it.
  - Restrict the mechanism to redirecting decay and erase away from heavily read directions. Drop "write into the least-used direction".
  - Give fresh writes LRUA-like initial usage, so recency remains the fallback for unread pairs. Claim gains only where reads recur: interleaved multi-query recall with repeated keys, and long-CoT re-reading of intermediate results. Do not claim single-query NIAH.
  - Either justify the hindsight label against plain backprop credit, or drop it.
  - Cite RDN [2106.06295] and CARVE as the read-feedback priors, and position the contribution as the accumulated trace.

- **Addendum (C4, after reading the three indexer papers above)**: All three are now read. None explores off-support blocks with logged propensities. LongCat LSA [2608.01662] and LatentIndex [2610.04635] use the DSA scheme: a dense KL warmup, then KL normalized over the selected set only. LISA [2607.19358] trains its indexer with a dense full-support KL to a frozen teacher. Its "candidate-set dropout" uniformly subsamples M of the top 1.25M candidates, and the authors state it "does not restore gradients through the hard top-M gate". This is the nearest in-domain use of randomized selection, but it is near-boundary regularization, not propensity-weighted off-support feedback, so C4's mechanism remains unclaimed. LISA does add one more instance of the cheap dense-teacher alternative that the strongest objection names.


### C6 verdict
- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=novelty)`
- **Evidence**:
  - Source grounding holds. [2606.27267] does score candidate sites by propagated forecast-error covariance, using a surrogate TLM linearized around the background ensemble. One propagated ensemble scores every site, selection is greedy, and the paper states that analysis-optimal equals forecast-optimal only in linear systems (Sections 1, 3.1, 4.1, 5.2-5.4). [1011.0686] is stated correctly (T^2 eps vs u T eps, iterative re-collection). The Opal numbers [2609.31947] match its Table 3, though the "33.3 vs 17.1" comparison is at a 400M-token budget.
  - The gap premise is false as written. The candidate claims "every Transformer-to-hybrid selector scores a layer by a teacher-forced, off-policy quantity", but it leaves out two selectors that score placements by the hybrid's own end-task output. PostNAS [2508.15884, Section 2.2] runs beam search over full-attention placements and, for math and retrieval, "choose[s] the one with the highest accuracy", which is a generation outcome. DtR [2601.11667, Section 3.2] swaps layers greedily on task validation metrics, including GSM8K 4-shot generation accuracy, and stops when the score drops below a tolerance. So DtR already reads K off data, and in exactly the way this candidate proposes ("stop when the gain levels off"). [2603.26556, Appendix E] even names PostNAS as the generation-aware end of the selector spectrum. Neither paper targets long CoT or re-collects rollouts, but "outcome on the student's own outputs" and "greedy + data-driven K" are both already taken.
  - What remains new is narrower and is not yet a mechanism: (a) a long-CoT target, (b) DAgger-style re-sampling after each pick, and (c) a cheap surrogate that scores every candidate layer from one set of rollouts. Item (c) is the only part the weather source really contributes, and its mapping is not specified. The candidate says it records "the correction [each layer] would apply, and how much those corrections at earlier steps account for the divergence that builds up later", but never says what plays the role of the ensemble covariance or the TLM. In [2606.27267] an observation acts at one assimilation time and its effect is carried forward by a linear map. A restored attention layer instead acts at every position at once, and the compounding that matters (prefix change through sampled tokens [2609.31947, Section 2]) is discrete and not linear. On a fixed student rollout, "forecast error" collapses to a KL computed on the student's own prefix (on-policy teacher-forced, the DAgger part). Off it, the method needs per-candidate re-generation, which is just brute-force PostNAS/DtR search with a long-CoT metric. At ≤8B on 8×H200 that brute force is affordable, which further shrinks the reason to have the surrogate at all.
  - The key assumption is misattributed and the literature contradicts it. The candidate scores a frozen teacher layer swapped into the current student without per-candidate re-fit, and calls this "the same assumption one-swap scoring makes" [2512.20569]. But [2512.20569, Section 3.2] re-distills every one-swap candidate (100M + 600M tokens) before scoring, precisely to avoid ranking un-adapted swaps. [2511.02651, Figure 2] reports that layer rankings change after only 100 distillation steps. [2601.11667, Section 4.3] attributes ranking failures to layer interactions ("replacing one layer alters the functional role of others").
  - Transfer condition (i) is supported: errors compound along the student's own trajectory, and teacher-forced and generation rankings disagree [2609.31947; 2603.26556, Section 5.1]. Contribution type passes (a selection method). Feasibility within the constraints passes: no new kernel, and the rollouts use existing fla/vLLM.
  - Recency searches since 2025-10 ("hybrid layer selection reasoning", "linearization reasoning model hybrid", "layer selection generation accuracy hybrid", "full attention layer placement search", "hybrid model distillation long reasoning layer") found no long-CoT, on-policy, re-collected allocation method. Nearby work uses answer-token NLL for SWA layer choice [2606.27791, abstract only] and lazy-layer heuristics with QwQ fine-tuning [2410.13846, abstract only]. Neither is read here, and neither is credited or penalized.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Greedy, outcome-on-own-output layer placement with a data-driven stop already exists (PostNAS [2508.15884], DtR [2601.11667]). The only remaining delta is the forecast-ensemble surrogate, which has no specified mapping, because a restored layer acts at every position rather than at one observation time and drift propagates through discrete token branching, not a linear TLM. What is left is "PostNAS/DtR with a long-CoT metric plus re-sampling".
