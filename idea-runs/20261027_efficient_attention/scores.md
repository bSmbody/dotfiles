
### C5 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [2607.11796] (body read, Sections 3.2, 4.3, 5): the headroom C5 promises to recover is the headroom of a *read mask*. The oracle drops the output terms m = g C_t x_t of modes whose state x was accumulated in full over all past tokens, it holds one mask over a 1024-token window, and the paper says it "does not by itself constitute a compute saving". A policy that actually stores only r units cannot reach that state: a mode it did not keep has lost its past and restarts from new writes. So the "roughly 2x error" gap and the "2-26% recovered" baselines measure a different decision problem than C5's. The headroom of an irreversible-eviction oracle over static masks is unmeasured, and the migration is strongest in exactly the slow, long-horizon modes whose value is their retained past.
  - [2006.16239] (Sections 2, 3, 7): Belady's rule is optimal because an evicted line can be fetched again from a larger memory, so a wrong eviction costs one miss. The recurrent state has no backing store. Eviction destroys content, and a greedy "drop the unit with least future error" teacher is myopic, not the optimal policy, so the "exact oracle" framing is overstated. Parrot also states that no optimal policy is known for multi-level caches, which undercuts the FP16/INT8/drop "cache hierarchy" extension.
  - [2607.11796] (Section 3.1, Eq 1): in Mamba-1, B_t is a dense N-vector, so every token writes every mode. Mamba-2 and GDN writes are dense in the same way. There is no "miss" and no "unit that a new write fills". Admission collapses to re-selecting the top r of all N units at every chunk, so the cache-line analogy adds framing but not a mechanism.
  - [2609.38169] (Section 3.1, Prop 1): in delta-rule layers the error transition (I - beta_t k_t k_t^T) couples key channels. Dropping one GDN key-channel row therefore changes the updates of the kept rows. Per-unit utilities are not additive, so the per-unit ranking that Parrot and KVP rely on ([2602.10238], Prop 1, which requires additivity) has no justification for GDN. The candidate also gives no exact oracle for GDN beyond "one look-ahead roll-out".
  - [2602.04852] (Table 4) and [2608.30386] (Section 3.2): in the hybrids the problem.md scope centres on, static 50% state pruning is already nearly free (Qwen3.5-9B RULER 93.9 to 93.6), and zeroing all linear states moves NIAH by 1 point or less. The quality drop C5 targets (GDN-1.3B NIAH 81.3 to 61.9) occurs in purely recurrent models, which are outside the stated scope. The constraints require state-compression gains to be argued on throughput or prefix-cache cost, which the candidate mentions but does not tie to its mechanism. Per-step gather/scatter and scoring also add overhead to a bandwidth-bound decode step that static masks do not pay.
  - Grounding is otherwise accurate. The Parrot ablation numbers (+9.8 / +3.5 / +16.8), KVP's per-head learned scorer and BASS's near-rank-one, bias-dominated state ([2610.36617], Section 4) are stated correctly.
  - Novelty holds. Searches for "Mamba state pruning", "recurrent state compression", "Gated DeltaNet state compression", "recurrent state eviction", "input-adaptive state pruning", "imitation learning KV cache eviction" and "Belady optimal KV cache eviction" (including runs since 2025-10-28) found no causal, input-adaptive post-training allocator of recurrent-state units. The closest hits were static (BASS, DASC, DRRQR) or about different objects: KVP and KVpop learn eviction for immutable KV tokens; CARVE and ES-SSM change the architecture and train it from scratch; Neural Paging applies a "semantic Belady" to context tokens. [2607.11796] itself names "a learned scheduler" as next work, so concurrent-work risk is high.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The oracle C5 imitates and the headroom it promises come from a read mask over a fully stored state. A memory-saving policy with irreversible eviction (no backing store, unlike Belady) cannot reach that headroom, and the candidate has not measured the headroom it could reach.
- **What a revision must supply**:
  - Redefine the teacher as an irreversible-eviction oracle (per-chunk keep set chosen with look-ahead on the *pruned* trajectory) and state its headroom over static masks. Mamba-1 is a cheap first pilot, since the per-mode instrument can split each state into a retained-content part and a part from new writes.
  - Drop the miss/admission language in favour of per-chunk top-r re-selection.
  - Give a coupling-aware unit definition for GDN, or restrict the method to diagonal and scalar-decay layers.
  - Anchor the payoff in scope: hybrid prefix-cache checkpoint bytes or decode throughput at aggressive budgets (at most 25%), where static masks begin to fail.

CGS keeps a sensor–region incidence graph, a coverage ratio that is positive only above k, and a drowsiness that forbids sleep when any covered region would drop to k. It recomputes greedily, and it beats Randomized Independent Sleeping at matched lifetime (Section III.C Eq 1-3, Section IV.B). The source has no rule for which regions to let fall below k when the budget cannot k-cover them all. Its "pessimistic incidence is safe" argument (Section III.D Note 1) also depends on that surplus: over-covering costs only lifetime.

At a quorum of k = 3 that is at most about 2.7K positions, against reasoning traces many times longer. So most content units *must* sit below quorum, and choosing which ones is again a per-unit value decision. C1 hands that decision to "leftover budget … to an existing per-token score", which assumes a surplus that does not exist at 1-2K budgets. A pessimistic incidence (missed restatements) here means over-pinning, which uses up the scarce budget, so the CGS safety argument reverses. The authors warn that exact retrieval floors near 0.03 for a single head and use graded recall R there. On R the cross-head effect is mild: 0.31 together vs 0.10 + 0.16 (Fig 4b, Appendix B Caveats). So part of the "threshold" is the argmax readout. Dealing the fact out token by token across heads, so that no head holds a readable span, barely changes retrieval (0.33 vs 0.39 at equal retained mass). The quantity that matters is retained token-head mass of the fact, not the number of complete copies per head. C1 cites this but still defines a sensor as one (layer, head, position) copy and k as a count of carrier-head copies. The coverage measure should be mass over carrier sites. The knee was also measured only for the *two best* carrier heads on one model. The paper says carrier identity is model-specific and not replicated (Appendix B).

On real MATH500 a shared draw is within 0.3 points of independent draws, so cross-head coverage is not binding on benchmarks (Section 5.2, Appendix D). The residual failure is the once-stated passcode (Table 3), and R-KV already gets 84% there. A quorum rule decides *how many heads* hold a unit, not *which once-stated units matter*. So its leverage on the passcode case depends entirely on the unit detector, which C1 leaves as token identity plus near-duplicate keys, the same signal as R-KV's redundancy term [2505.24133].

[2606.29563] K-VEC counts unique positions under an additive, equally-informative assumption, at prefill only. [2608.07001] GraceKV allocates globally over slots at prefill only. [2505.24133] R-KV uses a pairwise within-head penalty. Searches run (arXiv, all with a recency pass since 2025-10): "k-coverage KV cache", "cross-head KV cache eviction coverage", "KV cache eviction redundant heads quorum", "set cover KV cache", "submodular KV cache selection", "redundancy-aware KV cache reasoning", "information coverage KV cache eviction decoding", "intermediate result retention KV cache chain-of-thought", "head redundancy KV cache retrieval heads copies". C1's real delta over that is pinning a quorum of carrier heads instead of all heads. That delta is real but narrow.
  - Contribution type: methodological (an eviction scheduling rule). It passes. Engineering fits the constraints: per-head keep masks only, no new kernel.

The key assumption is named (explored-block attention mass reflects usefulness), and it is the same teacher signal MSA and SAS use. Reusing the arbitrary-index block-sparse kernels plus a weighted fused KL fits the team constraint. It is a training method, so a methodological contribution.

### C3 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [1605.06065] (Section 3.2, Eq 5-9; Table 2). The source is stated correctly: usage is gamma·u + read weights + write weights, the least-used slot is zeroed and refilled, and the 35.4 -> 62.6% (2nd instance) and 88.4 -> 95.3% (10th) gains are right. But LRUA works because its slots are discrete rows with sharp content addressing. A read weight lands on one slot, and the slot's row *is* the stored content, so "slot i was read" means "association i was read". In a delta-rule state an association k v^T is superposed over all key channels: keys are dense, L2-normalised vectors, not channel-aligned [2605.22791, Eq 10-11; 2606.26560, Eq 8]. Every LM token issues a dense query. So per-channel read mass q_t[i]^2 tells you which *channels* are busy, not which *association* was retrieved. Shielding a hot channel shields every pair with mass on it. The paper itself makes no claim for superposed memories (digest, goal 3). The candidate never says when channel usage separates associations. That holds only if keys and queries are near-sparse or near-orthogonal in the channel basis, and nothing in the cited models gives that.
  - [1605.06065] (Eq 8-9) against the delta rule. The "write into the least-used slot" half of LRUA does not transfer. In LRUA, reads find content by similarity to the row, so content can be relocated to a free slot. In linear attention the write key *is* the retrieval address. Writing a pair along a least-read direction instead of k_t makes it unreachable by the query that needs it. What does transfer is erase pressure: an EDA-style erase address [2606.26560, Eq 8] or decay placed on least-read directions. The candidate should drop the "displace / write into least-used" framing and state that only forgetting is redirected.
  - [1605.06065] (Eq 5) against the candidate's departure. In LRUA a write adds full usage, so a fresh slot is protected until its usage decays. C3 instead gives writes "only a small initial usage", so "a pair that is written and never read becomes the first candidate for eviction, whatever its age". That is exactly the MK-NIAH / passkey needle, written once and first queried at the end. The rule would then evict needles *ahead of* recency order, which is worse on the very benchmarks the candidate cites (MK-NIAH-1 72.6 -> 37.8 [2605.22791]; CARVE 76.8 -> 41.4 [2606.27229]). The "Main risk" names the no-signal half of this, but not that the rule actively hurts.
  - [2006.16239] (Sections 3-4). Parrot needs Belady labels because replacement is a discrete, non-differentiable decision on a fixed trace. In an end-to-end trained linear RNN, backprop from the later query already sends exact "this pair was needed later" credit to the gates that erased it. So the hindsight label ("read again before overwritten?") is a redundant teacher unless the candidate argues an optimisation reason, such as sparse credit over long gaps. It does not. The anticipation component, which the Main risk says the gain "rests on" for single-query tasks, therefore has no stated advantage over current gates.
  - [2106.06295] (Section 3.2, Eq 15-18). Prior work already conditions write signals on reads. The Recurrent Delta Net computes k, v, q and beta from W x_t + R tanh(y_{t-1}), where y_{t-1} is the memory's previous read-out. So "queries only read the state and never change it" (C3's gap framing) is not true of the literature. RDN's feedback, though, is one step and accumulates no per-direction record. It is also sequential (35K vs 63K words/s, no chunk form). C3's accumulated usage trace is a real delta over RDN and over [2606.27229], whose content signal is recomputed per token (digest, goal 1). The candidate must cite RDN and narrow the claim to "persistent, accumulated read usage".
  - Novelty search (arXiv, plus a recency pass since 2025-10-27): "query-aware forgetting linear attention", "read usage decay recurrent state", "least recently used memory linear attention", "usage-based forgetting state space model", "query-dependent decay gated delta", "retrieval-aware memory update linear attention", "read-write memory linear transformer usage", "query-conditioned forget gate", "access frequency retention recurrent memory", "retrieval strengthens memory fast weights", "memory reconsolidation linear attention", "usage gate linear attention", "gated slot attention", "least used slot memory transformer", "read-dependent forgetting". Close hits screened by abstract all change capacity or addressing, not read-informed retention: 2610.15715 NoteBank (an external episodic store archived "before future queries are known"), 2607.07386 Sparse Delta Memory, 2608.12435 MARCH (state anchors), 2610.02816 GSA-2, 2610.34180 FWR (write routing), 2607.21000 Naju (decoupled retention/write gates from input), 2605.22884 Tensor Cache. None accumulates read usage to steer forgetting. The novelty delta survives, but narrowly, and it sits in a crowded month (Oct 2026).
  - Feasibility and kernel. The claim checks out against [2606.27229] (Sections 6.1, 6.3). Query-only usage is a state-independent per-key-channel prefix scan, so the decay can be precomputed and the fla KDA/GDN-2 chunk kernels run unchanged. The hit-weighted variant costs one chunk-boundary matmul, and key-axis gating keeps WY exact. The DPLR "protected subspace" via [2503.14456] (Section 4.1) is stable, but its subspace would need an online top-r estimate of query covariance. That is the same dense or sequential interference problem as gap 2 of this axis, and the candidate does not address it. Scope fit is priority 3 of problem.md. The evaluation (≤1.3B pretraining, MQAR, RULER) does not test the long-CoT claim. It needs a converted or distilled public KDA/GDN hybrid on a re-reading long-CoT probe, which the H200 budget allows. Contribution type: methodological (an update rule). It passes.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The unit LRUA counts usage on (a discrete, content-addressed slot) has no counterpart in a superposed delta-rule state: per-channel read mass from dense queries does not identify which association was read, and the write half cannot move content to least-used directions without breaking its retrieval key. As specified, the rule also evicts written-but-not-yet-read needles first, which hurts the benchmarks it targets.
- **What a revision must supply**:
  - Define the usage unit so that it singles out associations. Either show that per-channel usage separates pairs under the key statistics of KDA/GDN-2 (for example with a probe of channel sparsity in trained keys), or replace it with a read-usage estimate in key space (accumulated q-q^T mass seen through the state, read at chunk boundaries) together with a parallel-friendly way to act on it.

### C2 verdict
- **Verdict**: `reject(reason=collapses to a static state-bits-for-KV-window trade plus existing deferred commitment; knapsack mapping breaks)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [2607.13955] (Sections 1-2, Algorithm 1). The source is mostly stated correctly, with one misstatement that matters. Phase 1 of Symb ends when *a single arriving item of size at least p_safe(r)* appears. It does not end when the reserve totals a threshold, which is how the mapping uses it ("until the reserve reaches a threshold"). The whole competitive structure rests on proportional values (value = size) and on future item sizes being unknown: "a single tiny item forces a dilemma". So the commitment threshold, the regime boundaries in (alpha, beta) and the "prediction" that prices pick the regime have no counterpart. The digest notes that the source's numbers do not transfer to a structured stream. What survives is the generic idea "pay to postpone a decision until evidence arrives".

LazyEviction [2506.15969] evicts every W steps behind a recent window, and BreadthKV [2610.05685] commits every 128 steps behind a 128-token fp16 window. Both are reserve-then-batch-commit schemes. So "postpone irreversible KV commitment until the model's own attention shows which entries matter" is not new. C2's only new element is *where the window's bytes come from*: state precision instead of the KV budget. That is a reallocation knob, not a retention mechanism. The candidate's novelty paragraph does not cite KVpop or DMS.
  - Continuous arrival removes the "loan and repay" structure. In decoding, a new KV block arrives at every step, and each one must be reserved to benefit. So the reserve is a sliding window that never empties, the state stays on loan nearly all the time, and "restore the state's precision" never happens in steady state. Under episodic resets, the tokens written right after a batch commit get a shorter deferral, and the average is a sawtooth. In both cases the scheme reduces to a static configuration: state at b bits permanently, a fixed larger full-precision KV window, and a batched commit every W steps. That is exactly the "stacked recipes with a re-tuned split" baseline the gap says to beat (PACE/LeapQuant state quantization [2610.29545, 2609.38166] plus a BreadthKV-style window). The one genuinely time-varying part, a split that shifts with trace length, needs no credit-line story.
  - [2610.36950] (body read, Section 1; digest appended). The premise number is misapplied. The bf16 KV of Qwen3.5-2B is 12,288 B/token, which is 48 MiB at 4K against a 9.84 MiB state, so the state is about 17% of a 4K request and about 7% at 11K. "Three quarters" holds only after DCKV's 56-60x KV compression, not in C2's regime of fp16 reserves and 2-4-bit commits. The credit line is correspondingly small. Assuming a 16-bit state, dropping it to 8 bits frees about 5 MB, enough to hold roughly 480 tokens at bf16 instead of 2-bit. Dropping it to 4 bits frees about 7.7 MB, roughly 720 tokens, per request across all attention layers. So the method extends an existing 128-256-token deferral window to about 600-1,000 tokens.
  - The value of that extension is small on in-domain evidence. KVpop's ablation of delayed against immediate scoring gains 0.2 points of token accuracy [2607.05061, Section 4.2]. Cheap reuse predictors reach 0.08-0.20 precision, and wake-ups are bursty and often thousands of tokens later [2610.02713, Section 2.2]. LazyEviction's motivating recurrences span intervals that a few hundred extra tokens do not cover in general [2506.15969]. This is C2's own "Main risk", and the literature points to it being realized rather than hypothetical.
  - The cost of the loan is understated. The healing evidence [2609.04098, Section 5.3] measures a one-off impulse from weight/activation noise, which is largely incoherent; the digest caveat says so. During a 4-bit loan, the state *storage* rounding loses 68-96% of nonzero update elements per step, coherently [2610.15709, Section 2.3]. That means the content written into the state during the loan is dropped, not merely noised, and INT4 hurts recall even at 512-1K tokens. Delta erasure [2609.38169] makes the *residual* fade after restoration, but it does not recover what was never written. So the loan's price is not "a few hundred tokens of slightly noisy state"; it is "a few hundred tokens the recurrent half did not record".
  - Transfer conditions (ii) and (iii) are real facts [2506.15969; 2608.05326; 2608.30386]. Contribution type passes (a memory-management method). Feasibility passes: a per-head state-precision switch is modest Triton work, and cumulative-attention scorers need attention probabilities, which FlashAttention does not expose (QEvict falls back to SDPA [2608.05326]). Neither of these rescues the lineage.
  - Novelty search (arXiv, recency since 2025-10-01): "hybrid model KV cache recurrent state joint memory budget", "joint compression KV cache and SSM state", "delayed KV cache eviction decoding window reasoning", "deferred KV quantization recent window full precision", "dynamic memory budget reallocation hybrid linear attention inference", "state quantization free memory for KV cache", "online knapsack KV cache" (0), "KV cache eviction competitive ratio online algorithm" (0), "hybrid linear attention KV cache compression decoding", "recurrent state precision adaptive decoding", "lag window KV eviction reasoning" (0). No method moves bytes between a hybrid's state and its attention KV during decoding, so the gap itself stays open. HeadWiseKV [2609.02029] and GateSight [2610.14754] compress hybrid attention KV with the state untouched. The deferral component is prior art: KVpop [2607.05061], LazyEviction, BreadthKV.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Postponing KV commitment until reuse evidence arrives already exists (KVpop's delayed scoring at a 256-token window exit, LazyEviction, BreadthKV), and since new KV arrives at every decode step the "loan" never repays. C2 therefore reduces to a static trade, permanently lower-bit state for a longer full-precision window, worth a few hundred tokens of deferral (the state is about 17% of a bf16 request at 4K, not three quarters), and the in-domain evidence says such deferral buys about 0.2 points.
- **For the orchestrator**: the gap (one byte budget split across the two memories, with a split that changes with length and task) is still unclaimed. A new intuition should drop the credit-line and online-knapsack framing. It should target an end-to-end, length-dependent split, priced on the cross term [2608.30386] and on the opposite position trends [2609.04098], and compared against the static stacked baseline above.


### C8 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [0805.1661], [0704.2649] The source is stated correctly. In the Noah's Ark objective an edge survives if any leaf below it survives, protection raises survival only partially (a_i to b_i), and utility can be folded into branch lengths (Section 1.2, Eq 2). The HED index discounts each ancestral edge by the chance that all other carriers below it are lost (Theorem III.1), and psi'' = (1 − eps_i) psi_i is the score for actions that cause extinction (Theorem IV.1). One claim is overstated. Greedy is exact only for 0→1 unit-cost NAP, for 0→c_i→1 on ultrametric trees, and for the (1 − x_i)→(1 − kappa x_i) unit-cost case (Section 1.3). C8's survival is a multi-level readability-in-mass curve with a knee: a single carrier head gives about 3%, two give 60% [2609.03430, Fig 4a]. That curve is convex below the knee, so it fits none of those cases. Greedy one-entry-at-a-time eviction is then not exact. It can thin many statements just below the knee before it notices any loss. This is fixable (evict whole head-slices of a statement, or use the clade DP on the two-level tree), but "greedy is exact or near-exact" is not supported.
  - [2506.01939] This is the main problem, the *branch length* mapping. In Qwen3-8B's own long CoT, over half the tokens have entropy below 10^-2. The highest-entropy tokens are connectives and framing words ("wait", "however", "thus", "suppose"), and the lowest-entropy ones include "mathematical expression components" (Section 3, Patterns 1-2). For a self-generated trace, write-time surprise measures sampling randomness at reasoning forks, not new facts. An intermediate result the model computed itself (the Challenge's own failure case) is written with near-zero surprise, so it gets almost no branch length. That makes it the *cheapest* thing to evict, which is the reverse of the stated goal. C8 lists this as a "main risk", but it is structural rather than a risk: every value that is a function of the prompt plus earlier steps is "predictable at write time", yet it can be expensive to recompute once its operands are gone.
  - [2609.03430] The probe that motivates C8 flatters this signal. Its passcode is forced into the trace token by token and the model "cannot restate it" (Section 5.2, Appendix B). Under the model it is a high-surprise, once-stated span, exactly the case write-time surprise handles. A method tuned on that probe can win the passcode table and still lose on natural long-CoT, where the once-stated items that matter are self-computed. The aggregate benchmarks are already saturated by a random draw (Table 2), so they will not expose the failure either.
  - [2609.03430] There is also a smaller mapping mismatch for *species*. The paper finds that retrieval depends on the fact's retained token-head mass and that "the shape of the copies does not matter" (dealt token by token: 0.33 vs 0.39). So partial mass on two statements of the same unit may pool. The NAP noisy-OR, where each statement survives independently, under-states this, and HED's independent-extinction assumption [0704.2649, Section II] does not strictly hold. A conservative approximation is acceptable, but it should be stated.
  - [2404.15574] The tree-edge signal is grounded in the source. The copy-paste criterion (the argmax-attended position holds the emitted token) is the retrieval-head detector, and retrieval heads matter for CoT (Section 2, 4.3). But the detector is restricted to needle tokens there. In free CoT, frequent tokens ("=", "the", digits) produce spurious copy events, and paraphrased restatements produce none. Edge detection therefore needs a span-level rule, for example a run of consecutive copy events, and C8 does not specify one.
  - Novelty holds. [2505.24133] R-KV uses a per-head pairwise key-redundancy penalty. [2610.02235] CORE uses position-level log-det coverage with heads fused, diminishing returns only (Appendix B.4). [2609.22158] StepKV ranks tokens additively by step utility, and reuse raises a step's score (Eq 2-5). [2407.09450] EM-LLM uses surprise to segment and never evicts. Screened by abstract: 2605.24786 CONF-KV uses next-token confidence to set the per-step *budget*, not to value entries. 2610.03007 AvoKV-E combines read pressure, key redundancy and value payload additively. 2610.21286 EGDR gates KV-cache steering by entropy, not eviction. 2610.03135 Page-EntroKV and 2609.03515 InertiaKV use entropy for head pooling or scorer variants. None maximizes expected surviving unit information under a budget. Searches (arXiv, recency-ordered fallback covering 2025-10 onward): "surprisal KV cache eviction", "token surprise KV cache compression", "information-theoretic KV cache eviction reasoning", "irreplaceable tokens KV cache", "expected information loss KV cache eviction", "self-information token importance KV cache", "entropy KV cache eviction", "perplexity KV cache eviction", "copy KV cache reasoning", "redundancy KV cache chain-of-thought", "intermediate results KV cache", "low-confidence tokens KV cache".
  - Contribution type: methodological (a budgeted eviction rule). It passes. Feasibility fits the constraints: per-head keep masks, logits already computed, a small Triton op at most for the copy argmax on a few heads.
  - Suggested direction: keep the NAP/HED objective and the copy-event tree, but redefine branch length as *irreplaceability given the surviving cache*, not as write-time surprise. One option is how predictable the unit's tokens would be if its statements were evicted, estimated cheaply at write time, for example the unit's NLL under a window that excludes its operands, or the attention the unit later receives from downstream steps. Then calibrate on a probe whose once-stated item is self-computed, not forced.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Write-time surprise is the wrong branch length for a model's own trace: self-computed intermediate results are written at near-zero entropy while connectives like "wait" carry the surprise [2506.01939], so the rule would rate exactly the Challenge's once-stated intermediate result as the cheapest thing to evict, and only a forced, high-surprise passcode probe [2609.03430] would hide this.


### C7 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=grounding)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [2608.30386] (body read, Section 5.2 "Composition with state quantization", Table 3, Appendix E.1-E.2): the payoff C7 claims as open, "checkpoints at 25% or less of state bytes", is already met by a plain baseline the candidate never names. SGLang stores the checkpoint's temporal state in FP32. DASC's own appendix quantizes the stored checkpoint once to INT8 at insertion and dequantizes it once on reuse, which gives 3.88x (about 26% of bytes) on its own. Composed with DASC it gives 8.11x (about 12%) on Kimi-Linear, with end-task accuracy equal to Dense within noise (AIME 0.6065 against 0.6215, GPQA 0.6185 against 0.6128, MMLU-Pro 0.6750 against 0.6748). So the gap statement "DASC reaches only 1.10-2.49x on GDN" compares C7 against DASC's selection-only arm, while the paper itself presents selection and quantization as complementary. One-shot checkpoint rounding is also easier than the per-step requantization that [2610.29545] and [2610.15709] already take to about 4-5 bits on GDN hybrids, so pure low-bit storage at about 12% of bytes is a likely, untested baseline.
  - [2609.33477] (Section 1, Section 7.6): verified. On Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.6-27B, SuffixReplay keeps more than 99% of LongBench QA and RULER with no stored state, and even at k=16 it keeps 94-99%. On head-wise GDN, DASC's ranking is not reliably better than a count-matched random mask ([2608.30386], Table 2: "mixed dasc-Random differences"). Both facts back the candidate's own main risk. In the Qwen-class hybrids that its gap statement leans on, the headroom for any content-aware slice sits within a point or two of what replay and quantization already deliver. The case lives only on long-memory models (OLMo-Hybrid-7B 91.4% RULER under replay; KDA), and the candidate says so only in the risk section, not in its payoff.
  - [2412.06464] (Section 3.4, Figure 1): the claim that "readout errors from different channels simply add up" is overstated. GDN applies a per-head output normalization and gate before the output projection, so zero-filling 75% of a head's value channels changes the normalizer applied to the channels it keeps. The key-side-only transition is correct ([2608.30386] Eq 1; [2608.30310] Eq 1), so value rows of the state do evolve independently given the layer inputs. The coupling enters through the norm and, at later tokens, through the changed layer inputs. The closed-form per-channel price is therefore a first-order surrogate, not an exact one. This is a fixable point of statement, not a broken mapping.
  - [1406.5979], [1502.02206] (digests, Sections 2.2-2.5, 3.1-3.2, 5): the borrowed mechanism and its condition are stated accurately. That covers AggreVaTe's cost-to-go regression, its failure when the expert is much better than any learnable policy, LOLS's learned roll-in with mixture roll-out, Theorems 1-3, the absence of any assumption on the reference, and the 1.670 to 0.316 example. The mapping is concrete: trajectory = chain of checkpoints, action = top-r value channels per head, roll-out = resumed forward pass. But the deployable first rung (calibrated future-read weighting times exact stored content) needs no learning to search. The L2S part only trains the per-request correction, and the candidate gives no argument that cross-checkpoint compounding is large enough to need non-myopic credit. The recombination is real, but its share of the gain is unmeasured.
  - [2607.11796] (Sections 4.3, 5) and the C5 verdict: the transfer assumption is named honestly ("irreversible-oracle headroom over static masks at 25% is large ... has not been measured"). It is the right assumption, and the only cited evidence for it is a read-mask upper bound on Mamba-1 at 1024 tokens, which is not the GDN prefix-cache setting.
  - Novelty holds. Searches for "prefix caching hybrid linear attention state checkpoint", "recurrent state checkpoint compression prefix cache", "value channel pruning gated delta", "input-adaptive recurrent state pruning", "learning to search cache eviction", "state checkpoint quantization hybrid prefix cache", plus recency runs since 2025-10-28 ("recurrent state compression hybrid serving", "Mamba state cache compression prefix", "linear attention state quantization prefix cache", "adaptive state pruning per request linear attention"), returned only DASC, Tail-Replay and SuffixReplay. The other hits were exact-checkpoint placement ("Sparse Prefix Caching for Hybrid and Recurrent LLM Serving", abstract only, orthogonal: it changes where exact states are stored, not what is kept), a GLM/LMCache integration fix, and the state quantizers. No work selects value channels per checkpoint from stored content. The contribution is methodological.
  - Feasibility is fine under the constraints: a gather/scatter into DASC-style ragged buffers, fla kernels unchanged, and cheap roll-outs on 8xH200.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The stated payoff ("checkpoints at 25% or less of state bytes") is already reached near-losslessly by one-shot INT8 storage of the FP32 checkpoint (3.88x alone, 8.11x composed with DASC [2608.30386]), and in the Qwen-class GDN hybrids the candidate cites, replay already keeps more than 99% of quality. So the candidate has not located a regime where a content-selected slice beats the obvious equal-byte quantization baseline.
- **What a revision must supply**:
  - Re-anchor the payoff against equal-byte quantization. Either compose the slice with INT8/INT4 storage and target the regime beyond what pure low-bit one-shot storage reaches (for example 6% of FP32 bytes or less), or argue why a 25% slice beats INT8 at equal bytes.
  - Name the target models where replay and quantization leave headroom (OLMo-Hybrid-7B, KDA hybrids, long multi-turn chains), instead of anchoring the gap on Qwen-class GDN.
  - State the per-channel price as a first-order surrogate that accounts for the per-head output norm, for example by renormalizing kept channels or storing the head norm statistic.
  - Say what learning to search adds over the greedy first rung, that is, the cross-checkpoint compounding it captures, and how a pilot measures it.


### C10 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=grounding)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [1304.2460] The source is stated correctly. Thompson's variance ratio, the "rare and patchy" condition, about 2x for tight clusters, 20-50% for diffuse ones, and gains shrinking as hits become common all match [Sections 4-7]. The source also bounds the payoff: in the favourable case ACS is about a 2x efficiency gain over a non-adaptive sample. So C10's contribution over C4 is at most a constant-factor detection gain, and only if the premise holds.
  - [2004.02708] Mostly correct, with one overreach. PoSA's exact unbiasedness and closed-form joint inclusion probabilities rely on a 1-D visiting order in which a positive forces inclusion of the *next* unit only, so "only consecutive pairs become dependent" [Section 3, Eq 3-8]. CPoSA fixes only a *minimum* size. A hard maximum is left as future work "because truncation complicates unbiasedness" [Sections 4, 6]. C10 uses a 2-D neighbourhood (adjacent KV blocks *and* adjacent query blocks) under a fixed per-step budget. That case falls outside what the source proves, so "every probed pair carries its inclusion probability, so the loss is design-unbiased" is asserted, not inherited. The candidate partly hedges this by leaving "unbiasedness vs detection" to the report_writer. But without the weights, what remains of ACS is "also try the neighbours of a found miss", which is a generic local-expansion heuristic.
  - [2512.03494] This contradicts the "rare" half of the premise under C10's own hit definition. A hit is "a block outside the selected set whose exact mass exceeds the weakest selected block", which is a top-K set-membership error. DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp's trained Lightning Indexer has only about 60% precision against exact top-k, averaged over *all* decoding steps, and end-to-end quality stays high [Section 3, Definition 2]. So a typical query has a large fraction of its set as misses, and most of them do no harm. Hits by this definition are common at every query, which is the regime where ACS gains vanish [1304.2460, Section 5]. The budget would go to benign rank swaps near the boundary.
  - [2606.08446] The rarity evidence the candidate cites measures something else. Sparrow's skew is in per-token *output* acceptance rate (most tokens >0.999), measured with heuristic Quest-style block top-k [Section 3.1, Appendix D]. That is rarity of *harmful* misses, not of set-membership misses. Supporting "rare" needs a harm-based hit condition, such as explored mass above a fraction of the selected mass, and C10 does not use one.
  - [2610.22985] The "clustered over keys" evidence is misapplied. Prism's locality is in *rank*: misses fall "between the original and expanded boundaries" of the proposal ranking [Section 3, Figs 2-3]. It does not show locality in key *position*, and C10's neighbourhood is positional ("the adjacent KV blocks for the same query"). Rank locality favours sampling or expanding by indexer rank, which C4's initial screen and a top-ρK pool already do. It does not favour ACS's spatial expansion. [2610.27438] MLIPS (abstract only, not read) makes the same point: broad shared pools give "substantially higher coverage" than final selections. [2502.11089] NSA's clustering over adjacent keys is qualitative, from a dense model's attention map [Section 6.2]. It is also the reason selection is already done in contiguous blocks, so it says nothing about whether *misses* cluster across neighbouring blocks.
  - [2610.32057] "Clustered over queries" is only partly supported. Adjacent queries share 71% of their top-2048 selections [Section 3.4.2], which is correlation of *selections*, not of misses. HyScale's pair kernel already computes over the union of adjacent queries' selections (1.29x), so the cheapest query-neighbour probe already exists as a deterministic by-product. The regime claim itself holds: the indexer KL is detached and "only through the k selected scores", and no dense teacher appears in the sparse stage at up to 1M tokens [Sections 2.1, 2.3]. The equal-FLOP arithmetic in (b) is also right (2 slots vs 16,384 blocks per row ≈ 1/8,000).
  - The equal-FLOP case against dense rows comes down to the unmeasured assumption. Harmful misses that matter are deep-tail. For those, a uniform-floor screen is a non-adaptive grid sample, like dense rows, so per FLOP it detects them no better. ACS adds value only *after* a hit, and only if deep-tail harmful misses come in position or query patches. None of the cited papers measures this for a trained indexer. The candidate names it as the key assumption and main risk. The problem is that the in-domain evidence above points the other way: membership misses are common, and their locality is in rank. [2610.30951] still applies to the screen: behind a practical selector at 1% reads, the heaviest unread token has 0.5% inclusion probability under random tail sampling [Appendix A.2]. The 2x ACS multiplier does not change that order of magnitude.
  - Regime positioning is now correct and is a real improvement over C4. Frozen-backbone plug-ins are conceded to the short dense teacher [2606.22874, Section 4.4]. Sparse-rollout RL is conceded because dense log-probs come nearly free [2606.08446, Section 4.5]. Native co-adapting sparse training is correctly identified as having no off-support signal [2610.32057, 2606.13392, Section 3.2]. Routing absorption [2603.02227] is relevant in both directions, though. Under co-adaptation the backbone can move information to where the selector already looks, which may shrink the harmful-miss population C10 hunts. The candidate does not discuss this.
  - Novelty holds for the mechanism. Searches: "adaptive cluster sampling attention", "sparse attention indexer exploration training", "lightning indexer recall training unselected tokens", "sparse attention selector training off-support signal", "neighbor expansion sparse attention top-k training", "indexer KL selected tokens sparse attention training long context", plus `--since 2025-10-01`: "DeepSeek sparse attention indexer training", "indexer recall sparse attention", "trainable sparse attention recall missed tokens", "stochastic top-k sparse attention training exploration", "sparse attention indexer distillation dense teacher sampled queries", "importance sampling sparse attention training gradient". Close hits were screened by abstract, not read. 2606.09079 FlashMemory-LSA trains its indexer backbone-free as a dual encoder. 2605.07363 MISA routes indexer heads and re-ranks an enlarged candidate set without training. 2610.31911 LazyFlash uses importance sampling to sparsify the *backward* of dense attention. 2610.27438 MLIPS uses training-free pool sharing. None trains a sparse-attention indexer from adaptively sampled off-support feedback. The positioning against Default MoE / SparseMixer [2504.12463, Sections 3, 4.4] and SAS [2609.13141, Section 4.2.2, Appendix B] is accurate.
  - The mapping is explicit and the remaining checks pass. Population = (query block, KV block) grid, hit = membership miss, initial sample = C4 slots, neighbourhood = adjacent keys and queries, budget = CPoSA. It is a training method, so a methodological contribution. Feasibility fits: arbitrary index lists in existing block-sparse kernels plus a logits-only gather and a weighted loss is modest Triton work, and native sparse pretraining of ≤3B models at 64K-256K fits 8×H200. One small mismatch: DSA/CSA select *tokens* (k = 2048), not 64-token blocks, so "adjacent KV block" needs a definition at token granularity for the production systems named as the regime.
  - What a revision must do. (1) Redefine a hit as a *harmful* miss, for example explored block mass ≥ τ times the selected mass or a top-p mass criterion, not "beats the weakest selected block". (2) Ground the *clustering of harmful misses in key position and in query position* for a trained indexer, with a paper that measures it. If no such evidence exists, propose a cheap pilot that measures it with dense rows on a DSA-style model, and make the method conditional on it. (3) State which estimator stays unbiased under a 2-D neighbourhood and a hard budget, or drop the unbiasedness claim and argue detection per FLOP against an indexer-rank-stratified screen, not against dense rows. If (2) cannot be grounded, the C4/C10 lineage has no regime left and should be abandoned.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: ACS pays only when hits are rare and spatially patchy, yet under C10's own hit definition a trained production indexer misses about 40% of exact top-k at every decoding step, mostly harmlessly [2512.03494], and the cited "clustering" of misses is locality in rank [2610.22985], not in key or query position, so the neighbourhood expansion that distinguishes C10 from C4 has no grounded population to exploit.
