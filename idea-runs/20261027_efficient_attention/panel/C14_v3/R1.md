# R1 review: C14 v3, "Keep the Attention Layers That Fix the Student's Own Mistakes"

## Summary
The paper converts Qwen3-4B (thinking) into a Gated DeltaNet hybrid and chooses which 6 of 36 layers keep exact attention. It does this by greedy forward selection inside a frozen-teacher supernet. Each layer is scored by the fraction of high-KL "first divergence" positions on the student's own wrong long-CoT rollouts (filtered by a teacher-vs-student continuation success gap) at which switching that layer (or a pair) back to exact attention brings next-token reverse KL below a threshold. Mixer-only on-policy distillation runs between commits, and the chosen layout is then shipped with Opal's joint OPD recipe. If the predictions land at threshold, the paper shows four things. Ours-6 beats uniform-6 by 5 pp and sits at the 90th percentile of 16 random layouts. It is non-inferior to Opal 9/36 at a 3 pp margin. At one pre-registered aggressive byte budget, ours-6 plus its best compressor beats Opal 9/36 plus its best compressor by 5 pp. And it beats a random-commit-order control by 5 pp.

## Strengths
- The question matters and is posed in the right units. In a GDN hybrid the full-attention layers carry the KV that grows with long CoT. Counting growing-KV bytes rather than layers, and putting layer count on the same frontier as FP8/INT4/eviction, is the right framing. The v3 design correctly notes that FP8 alone puts Opal 9/36 (4.5 L-eq) below six bf16 layers. Most layer-selection papers in this area (KL-guided [2512.20569], NLL-guided [2606.27791], ConSA [2606.18056], HydraHead [2606.20097]) never make this comparison.
- I found no prior or concurrent paper that picks a hybrid's full-attention layers from the trained student's own long-CoT rollouts. Across my searches, every selector I read uses one of the following: teacher-forced or generic text ([2512.20569], [2606.27791], [2604.19877]), synthetic retrieval ([2606.20097], [2410.10819]), attention patterns on reference answers ([2410.13846]), or aggregate on-policy statistics for depth pruning ([2610.25679]). The on-policy, position-localized signal is a real (if narrow) delta.
- The experimental hygiene is unusually good for this subfield:
  - Opal's recipe with matched GPU-hours for the uniform arms, plus a reproduction tolerance against the published 52.5/40.4 [2609.31947].
  - A fresh 190-problem pool, with AIME'24 used only for reproduction.
  - Hierarchical bootstrap over pipeline seeds, Holm correction, an honest MDE of about 5 pp, and dev-only selection of B-dagger and of the compressor knobs.
  - Two kill gates that address the most likely failure: on-policy training routing around allocation, as SWARR shows for pure SWA [2606.11634].
- A rounds-only control is included because, in LOOP's ablation, iteration rather than the score carried most of the gain (about 7 pp versus 2 pp) [2610.25679]. That is the right instinct.

## Weaknesses
1. **[major] The C2 memory headline is compared only against layer-uniform compressors. Training-free layer-adaptive KV compression is the direct counterpart of "drop 3 of 9 layers", and it is missing.** Every listed compressor (FP8, INT4, PM-KVQ, R-KV, BreadthKV, decode-adapted GateSight) spreads the byte budget uniformly over Opal's nine layers; HeadWiseKV is per-head but static. Ours-6 is a non-uniform allocation of bytes across layers: zero for three layers, full for six. So C2 mixes up two things: "fewer exact layers" and "non-uniform per-layer budget". Existing methods already allocate per-layer budgets non-uniformly:
   - per-layer mixed precision searched end-to-end on math [2502.04420];
   - per-layer and per-head decode-time eviction budgets for long-CoT reasoning models, which beat uniform R-KV by about 10 pp on AIME24 at tight budgets [2606.11164];
   - training-free conversion of whole layers to sink+window streaming attention [2410.13846];
   - the same at head level [2410.10819].

   Applied to the shipped Opal 9/36, any of these can give three layers a constant sink+window cache, which is near zero growing bytes, for free. If that matches ours-6 at B-dagger, the whole allocation pipeline (up to 1.5 extra shipping runs) buys nothing for memory. The C2 margin is also partly arithmetic. Given bf16 non-inferiority, a 6-layer model compresses each layer 1.5x less at equal bytes. B-dagger is defined as the budget where Opal's frontier has already dropped 5 pp, so it sits on the steepest part of the curve. At B >= 2 L-eq the paper only claims "not more than 3 pp worse", so at the FP8/INT4 budgets practitioners actually use, the memory lever shows no advantage.
2. **[major] The novel component, failure localization, is never confirmed against the strongest simple alternative.** C3 compares against a random commit order. Given C1 and the 90th-percentile result, that is almost implied, because random commit order is close to a random layout. The baselines that test whether localizing to failure positions matters are only "reported as estimates, not claims", with a point-estimate bar of 3 pp, below the 5.1 pp MDE:
   - greedy whole-trace on-policy KL, with the same rounds;
   - LOOP-style aggregate scores;
   - DtR-style greedy dev accuracy.

   The teacher-forced selectors (KL-guided [2512.20569], FlashMorph, NLL-guided, Super Apriel) get one seed and no statistics. So the paper cannot statistically support its title: neither "on-policy beats off-policy selection" nor "localized beats aggregate" is a confirmatory claim. A reviewer who knows the layer-selection literature will expect selected layers to beat uniform; [2606.27791] and [2410.13846] already show this at low ratios. C1 is therefore the least surprising of the claims.
3. **[major] Learned or trained KV compression is excluded even though matched GPU-hours make it the natural competitor.** The Opal arms get their surplus budget as extra OPD. A specialist would instead spend that budget on DMS-style learned delayed eviction (4x in about 300 steps [2506.05345]) or KVpop-style learned scorers [2607.05061] on Opal 9/36, and then compare at matched bytes. Deferring DMS "to a later tier" leaves out the competitor most likely to erase C2.
4. **[major] Bytes per generated token is the wrong memory metric when accuracy changes trace length.** Peak and total KV equal bytes per token times trace length. A weaker or more compressed model often generates longer traces and loops more ([2512.12008] reports this for low eviction budgets). C2 should be reported in peak KV bytes per problem and in solved problems per GPU-second. A per-token frontier can flatter whichever arm produces longer traces.
5. **[major] The paper skips the most obvious allocation-only experiment: ours-9 versus Opal 9/36.** v3 drops K = 7-9. Allocation quality at Opal's own budget is the cleanest test that allocation, not count, matters. It would also give the paper a direct "better Opal" result. As designed, every comparison with Opal changes both the layer count and the choice of layers.
6. **[minor] The link between the search regime and the shipped model is weak at threshold.** The search regime (frozen shared weights, mixer-only) and the shipped model (joint OPD) agree only at Spearman >= 0.5 over 16 allocations, and that estimate has a very wide CI. The KL-repair to answer-repair link is Spearman >= 0.3. Both are weak, so the selection is "noisy but better than chance". Jaccard >= 0.6 across seeds says the same.
7. **[minor] The breadth claims are thin.** On MiMo, "same sign, >= 3 pp" at 3 seeds is not significant, and the paper requires a per-checkpoint search. LiveCodeBench "no more than 2 pp below uniform" is a non-inferiority claim against the weakest arm. Mining uses math prompts only, so the allocation may be task-specific, which [2610.18104] would predict.
8. **[minor] The systems payoff is small.** Going from 9 to 6 layers gives under 33% decode-throughput gain, because weights and GDN state stay the same. Memory is measured in vLLM only for FP8; for the other compressors and the head-level arms it is computed from retained bytes.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- **LightTransfer [2410.13846]** is uncited and the closest prior on two of the three pillars. It converts a Transformer into a layer-level hybrid by selecting "lazy" layers for streaming attention. On o1-like long reasoning, it pre-selects layers on question+answer text and then fine-tunes, keeping AIME24 with 50% streaming layers on QwQ-STILL-32B. It also shows that layer removal stacks with SnapKV on the remaining layers (Appendix C.2), and it compares against SqueezeAttention's layer-wise budgets. So "nobody has shown that cutting layers saves anything that compressing the same layers' KV does not" is overstated. That result exists qualitatively, for dense-to-SWA hybrids. LightTransfer's selection is off-policy and attention-pattern based, has no failure localization, and has no matched-bytes frontier, so the on-policy selection and the matched-bytes test remain new.
- **DuoAttention [2410.10819]**: optimization-based selection of full-KV versus streaming heads, combined with 4-bit KV. It is the head-level training-free counterpart and should be a compressor baseline on Opal 9/36's attention layers.
- **ReasonAlloc [2606.11164]** and **KVTuner [2502.04420]**: layer-wise non-uniform decode-time eviction budgets for long-CoT, and layer-wise mixed precision. These are the continuous versions of the layer-count lever (see W1).
- **LOOP [2610.25679]**: the same loop structure (select, short OPD, re-select from on-policy rollouts), applied to depth pruning. The proposal cites it and controls for it, but the control is not confirmatory.
- **KL-guided selection [2512.20569]**: the same per-layer restore-and-measure-KL primitive, on generic text. OFL is essentially this primitive moved to on-policy failure positions, thresholded and iterated.
- **Opal [2609.31947]**, **Super Apriel [2604.19877]**, **DtR [2601.11667]**, **HydraHead [2606.20097]**, **ConSA [2606.18056]**, **GateSight [2610.14754]**, **HeadWiseKV [2609.02029]**: cited correctly. I found no concurrent paper (searches with --since 2025-10-29) that allocates hybrid full attention from on-policy failure positions.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- **Layer-adaptive training-free compression on shipped Opal 9/36, at every budget, entering C2.** Three variants:
  - ReasonAlloc-style per-layer and per-head decode budgets [2606.11164];
  - KVTuner-style per-layer precision [2502.04420];
  - "3 of 9 layers set to sink+window", with the 3 chosen by DuoAttention gates [2410.10819], by LightTransfer lazy ratio [2410.13846], or by OFL's own coverage scores applied to Opal's layers.
- DMS-style learned eviction on Opal 9/36, trained with the surplus GPU-hours [2506.05345].
- Ours-9 versus Opal 9/36 (uniform), at 5 seeds.
- Greedy whole-trace on-policy KL and KL-guided [2512.20569] at 5 seeds, inside the confirmatory family.
- Uniform-6 and best-random-6 plus best compressor at B-dagger, as a confirmatory split of C2 into a layer-count part and an allocation part.
- Peak KV bytes per problem, mean generated length per arm and per budget, and accuracy per GPU-second.
- A LightTransfer-style lazy-ratio selector run through the same shipping stage.

## Questions for the authors
1. At B-dagger, how much of the C2 gap does uniform-6 plus its best compressor already close? Is the memory result about K = 6 or about OFL?
2. If three of Opal's nine layers are converted to sink+window at decode time (training-free, chosen by your own coverage score), where does that model fall on the frontier relative to ours-6?
3. How do mean trace lengths differ between ours-6 and Opal 9/36 under compression at B-dagger, and does C2 survive when memory is measured as peak KV per problem?
4. What fraction of committed layers coincide with the top layers of the teacher-forced KL-guided selector? If Jaccard is high, what does on-policy localization add?
5. Repair tests use only next-token KL at the first divergence. How often does a repair at the event position fail to change the eventual answer (the 300-event check)? Does the coverage ranking change if a 1-continuation answer flip is used instead?

Three variants: three of its nine layers set to sink+window (chosen by DuoAttention-style gates or by OFL's own coverage score), ReasonAlloc-style per-layer decode budgets, and KVTuner-style per-layer precision. Then "fewer layers beats compression" is tested against the compressor that can already zero out layers.
