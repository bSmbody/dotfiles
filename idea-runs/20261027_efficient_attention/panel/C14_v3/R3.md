# R3 (experimentalist) review: C14 v3, "Keep the Attention Layers That Fix the Student's Own Mistakes"

## Summary
The paper converts Qwen3-4B (thinking) into a Gated DeltaNet hybrid and picks which 6 of 36 layers keep full attention. It does this with a greedy loop run in a supernet whose shared weights are frozen at teacher values: train only the mixers on-policy, collect first-high-KL positions on wrong rollouts that pass an answer-flip filter, count how many of those positions each single layer or pair repairs when switched back to exact attention, commit the best one, and repeat. The shipped models are then put on an accuracy-versus-growing-KV-bytes frontier with seven training-free compressors against Opal's 9/36. Assuming every prediction lands exactly at its threshold, the paper would show three things: ours-6 is 5 pp above uniform-6 and 5 pp above a random-commit-order control (both Holm-significant on 190 recent competition problems); ours-6 is non-inferior to Opal 9/36 in bf16 within 3 pp; and ours-6 beats Opal 9/36 by 5 pp at one dev-chosen budget of about 1 L-eq or below, while trailing it by up to 3 pp at other budgets.

## Strengths
- The statistics are well above the norm for this subarea. The test pool is 190 problems from 2025-2026 competitions, with AIME'24 kept only for the reproduction check. Scoring is avg@32 with 5 pipeline seeds on the headline arms, analysis uses a hierarchical bootstrap with Holm over a pre-registered family of three tests, and every choice is made on a separate dev pool. The power analysis honestly concludes that the design detects 5 pp and not 3 pp, and the confirmatory claims are sized to match. Opal itself reports neither seeds nor sample counts for its 30-problem AIME numbers [2609.31947], so this alone is a real contribution to how the area evaluates.
- The baselines are fair in the ways that usually fail. Uniform and Opal layouts are trained with Opal's own pipeline, with OPD extended to matched total GPU-hours (allocation cost included). The Opal rerun must land within 3 pp of the published AIME numbers before any comparison is labelled "versus Opal". Opal's AIME is still rising at the end of its OPD budget [2609.31947], so the extension favours the baselines, which is the right direction.
- Moving the headline from layer count to matched bytes answers the obvious objection. FP8 on Opal's 9 layers (4.5 L-eq) is already smaller than 6 bf16 layers, and the proposal says so itself. Strong long-CoT compressors are included: BreadthKV [2610.05685], R-KV [2505.24133], PM-KVQ-style, and hybrid-specific ones. Each compressor's knob is chosen end to end on dev, following BreadthKV's finding that offline proxies pick the wrong setting.
- Cheap kill gates come first. G0 (allocation spread in the frozen supernet, plus compression headroom) and G1 (spread after joint OPD) respond directly to the SWARR result that on-policy RL lets pure SWA nearly match full attention on math [2606.11634]. That is exactly the scenario that would make allocation irrelevant.
- The rounds-only control (C3) answers the LOOP lesson that iterating carried about 7 pp and the better score about 2 pp [2610.25679]. Without it, the paper would be uninterpretable.

## Weaknesses
1. [major] The ablation for the claimed mechanism, localization at failure positions, is not part of the confirmatory design. C3 compares against a random commit order, which only shows that some on-policy greedy signal beats chance. The natural control for "localize to failure positions" is greedy whole-trace on-policy KL with the same rounds. The same goes for the LOOP-style aggregate score, on-policy gates and DtR-style greedy dev accuracy. These run at 3 seeds, are predicted at only "point estimate ≥ 3 pp", and are explicitly "estimates, not claims". With SE around 2-2.5 pp at 3 seeds, a 3 pp point estimate is not distinguishable from zero. At threshold, then, the paper cannot claim that failure localization beats the obvious aggregate on-policy signals. That is the claim in the title, and the one a reviewer who knows LOOP [2610.25679], DtR [2601.11667] and KL-guided selection [2512.20569] will check first.
2. [major] The method is not shown to beat cheap random search. At threshold, ours-6 sits at the 90th percentile of 16 random 6-layer allocations, so 1-2 random layouts tie or beat it. G1 already trains 16 random 6-layer allocations for 250M tokens as part of the pipeline, so best-of-G1-random chosen on dev is close to free. The proposal ships best-of-16 at 3 seeds, but only as a secondary arm. The paper needs a random-search baseline at matched allocation compute: N random layouts, with N set so that N × short-OPD cost equals the measured OFL cost of at most 1.5 shipping runs. It also needs a confirmatory test against that baseline. Otherwise "ours ≈ best random" is the likely reading.
3. [major] The memory win sits in an extreme, narrow regime, and it is mostly a layer-count effect. G0 requires compression to cost ≥3 pp only at ≤1 L-eq, so B-dagger is about 1 or 0.5 L-eq. That means compressing Opal's cache 9-18x, past where KVpop and R-KV still retain most dense accuracy [2607.05061][2505.24133]. At threshold the frontier may sit up to 3 pp below Opal at the budgets practitioners actually use (FP8 at 4.5 L-eq; 2 L-eq). There is also a deeper problem. "Six layers compressed 6x beat nine layers compressed 9x" follows from the convexity of compression loss for any 6-layer layout that is near Opal in bf16. C2 compares ours-6 with Opal-9 and does not separate OFL from K-reduction. The uniform-6 frontier is computed but sits outside the confirmatory family, so the paper cannot say whether the C2 gain needs OFL at all.
4. [major] The byte matching is under-specified for eviction methods, and it depends on length. The budget is "growing-KV bytes per generated token". For R-KV, HeadWiseKV and GateSight-decode with a token cap, bytes per token fall as the trace gets longer. Compressed and hybrid models also produce longer, sometimes looping traces under tight budgets [2512.12008]. Knobs and B-dagger are chosen on a dev pool decoded at 16K but tested at 32K, so a budget that matches at 16K will not match at 32K. The paper must define the budget as peak, or length-integrated, KV bytes at the realized trace length. It must report the generated-length distribution per arm, and it must choose knobs at the test generation length.
5. [major] At threshold, the breadth results read as mildly negative and the paper has to absorb them. On LiveCodeBench, ours-6 can sit 2 pp below uniform-6, which suggests a math-specific allocation. At 64K the gap to Opal can widen by 3 pp, and multi-key NIAH/RULER can be 3 pp below Opal. Head-level selection [2606.20097][2606.18056] can beat ours-6 by up to 3 pp. MiMo-7B-RL only needs "same sign, point ≥3 pp" at 3 seeds, which is not a replication. A reviewer will add these up: a 4B math-tuned allocation that trades retrieval, length and code robustness for a cache cut that matters only at extreme budgets.
6. [major] The budget does not obviously fit. There are about 90 shipping-run equivalents of 1B-token OPD with rollouts up to 32K, run on 8×H200 as about two parallel pipelines, and per-run GPU-hours are unknown (Opal does not report them [2609.31947]). Even at an optimistic 1-2 days per run on 4 GPUs, that is 45-90 days of training before the allocation rounds, G1, and a frontier evaluation of 4 families × 7 compressors × 5 budgets × knobs. On dev alone that is several billion decode tokens, mostly through reference implementations that are not in vLLM. For one new PhD student, 4-6 months is tight. The pre-stated cut order protects the headline, but it removes the teacher-forced and head-level comparisons, which are exactly the ones a skeptical reviewer wants.
7. [minor] Most mechanism ablations run at 1 seed: no answer-flip filter, one-shot without re-collection, event position, placement sampling, frozen versus joint shared weights, and heuristic layouts. With a 1.5 pp seed SD and a 1.7 pp SE for even the 5-seed contrasts, 1-seed shipped comparisons are not diagnostic. Where an ablation changes the six layers, report its effect at the allocation level (Jaccard, and the coverage trajectory). Ship it at 3 seeds only when it does change them.
8. [minor] Frozen-versus-shipped Spearman ≥0.5 is a weak bar for a search that runs entirely in the frozen supernet. At ρ = 0.5 a large share of the ranking does not transfer, and the paper cannot diagnose why after joint training.
9. [minor] Systems evidence is thin. Peak memory and throughput are measured only for bf16 and FP8. For every other compressor, which includes the regime where C2 is won, they are computed from retained bytes. Weights (about 8 GB) and GDN state do not shrink, so the paper should report max batch size or throughput at fixed GPU memory at 32K and 128K. Today's evaluation stops at 32K and 64K, while the memory argument is made at 128K.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- Opal [2609.31947]: same conversion recipe, models and 9/36 reference. It never varies the ratio, never compresses the retained KV, and reports no seeds. This work builds directly on it, and the overlap is acknowledged.
- LOOP [2610.25679]: the same select → short on-policy distillation → re-select loop with on-policy scores, but for depth pruning, with aggregate per-layer statistics and a hand-set K. It is the closest methodological precedent, and its ablation (iteration about 7 pp, score about 2 pp) is why weakness 1 matters.
- DtR [2601.11667], Super Apriel [2604.19877], KL-guided selection [2512.20569], FlashMorph [2606.30562], DASH [2605.20936], ConSA [2606.18056], HydraHead [2606.20097]: off-policy, teacher-forced or probe-based layer and head selection for hybrids. None uses the student's own long-CoT rollouts or localizes to failure positions. DtR and Super Apriel already show that greedy, outcome-driven or interaction-aware placement beats static scores, so "greedy beats uniform" is not new. The new parts are the on-policy repair signal and the matched-bytes framing.
- 2609.02986 gives a rule-based, layer-specific allocation (no full attention in the first and last layers, denser in the middle). That supports the proposal's heuristic baselines, which deserve more than 1 seed.
- KV compression for long CoT: BreadthKV [2610.05685], KVpop [2607.05061], R-KV [2505.24133], the decode-eviction benchmark [2512.12008]. These are the competing lever. None compares layer-count reduction against compression at matched bytes.
- SWARR [2606.11634] and 2606.15378: evidence that on-policy training or enough training routes around the choice of efficient-attention design. This is the main threat to G1.
- My searches (12 queries, 8 of them with --since 2025-10-29: hybrid layer selection, on-policy distillation, Gated DeltaNet, KV compression of hybrids, layer importance on student rollouts) found no paper that allocates hybrid full attention from on-policy failure positions, and none that puts hybrid layer count and KV compression on one matched-bytes frontier. Novelty against prior work holds. In a crowded 2026 subarea (more than 10 selectors in 12 months), reviewers will judge it on whether the effect is clearly above the simple alternatives.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- A confirmatory test (5 seeds) against greedy whole-trace on-policy KL with the same rounds. This is the direct ablation of localization, and it should replace or join C3.
- Random search at matched allocation compute: best-of-N random, chosen on dev, with N × short-OPD cost equal to the measured OFL cost, at 5 seeds.
- A C2-prime test: uniform-6 + best compressor versus Opal-9 + best compressor at B-dagger, plus ours-6 versus uniform-6 at B-dagger. Together they separate the K-reduction gain from the OFL gain on the frontier.
- A frontier with eviction budgets matched by peak or length-integrated bytes at the realized length, per-arm length distributions and loop or derail rates, and knobs chosen at 32K.
- Max batch size or throughput at fixed memory in vLLM at 32K and 128K. For the compressors that matter at B-dagger, measure peak memory rather than computing it.
- Heuristic layouts (contiguous middle, skip first and last, 2609.02986-style) at 3 seeds, not 1.
- Event mining on a mixed math and code prompt pool, run as a planned arm rather than a fallback, given the threshold LiveCodeBench outcome.

## Questions for the authors
1. Which of C1-C3 survives if greedy whole-trace on-policy KL replaces random commit order as the control? What is the predicted gap?
2. What measured OFL cost (in shipping runs) does the pilot give, and how many random allocations does that buy at G1's 250M-token cost? Would best-of-that-many beat ours?
3. How are bytes counted for fixed-cap eviction methods when trace lengths differ across arms and between the 16K dev and 32K test decodes?
4. At B-dagger, what absolute accuracy do Opal-9 and ours-6 reach? A 5 pp gap between two badly degraded models is a different claim from a 5 pp gap near bf16 accuracy.
5. Is the uniform-6 frontier within 3 pp of ours-6 at B-dagger? If so, what does OFL add to the memory claim?
6. What is the measured wall-clock time of one 1B-token OPD shipping run on 4 H200s, and does the 90-run plan fit in 4-6 months with evaluation included?

## Rating
- rating: 3 (borderline reject)
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.30. This is my estimate that, in reality, an OFL-chosen 6-layer hybrid beats uniform-6 by a significant margin after matched on-policy training and stays within 3 pp of Opal 9/36. SWARR and the convergence results make it quite likely that on-policy training washes out allocation.
- one_point_fix: Add 5-seed greedy whole-trace on-policy KL with the same rounds, and best-of-N random at matched allocation compute, to the confirmatory family (replacing the random-commit-order control). The paper can then claim that localizing to failure positions is what selects better layers, not merely that a greedy on-policy loop beats chance.
