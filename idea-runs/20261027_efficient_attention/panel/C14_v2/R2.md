# R2 (generalist) review of C14 v2: "Fewer Full-Attention Layers from the Failures On-Policy Training Leaves Behind"

## Summary
The paper converts Qwen3-4B (thinking) into a Gated DeltaNet hybrid. It picks which layers keep full attention with a greedy loop over a teacher-anchored supernet. The loop mines the first high-KL token in each of the student's own wrong long-CoT traces, then finds a 1-minimal set of layers whose switch back to exact attention brings the KL at that token under a threshold. It commits the layer or group of at most 3 layers that fixes the most such events, retrains the mixers on-policy, and repeats until coverage falls below 5%. If every prediction lands exactly at its threshold, the paper would show four things:
- random allocations at K = 4 or 6 differ by at least 5 pp AIME after joint OPD;
- the method stops at K in {4, 5, 6};
- the shipped hybrid is non-inferior to Opal's 9/36 model at matched GPU-hours (CI lower bound just above -2 pp), with a growing KV cache about a third smaller;
- it beats the uniform interleave at the same K, on-policy differentiable gates, greedy dev-accuracy allocation and greedy whole-trace KL by about 3 pp AIME avg@32, with CIs just clear of zero, on one model family and one domain.

## Strengths
- **A real question, with a gate first.** Proposals in this crowded area usually assume that allocation matters. Asking first whether it still matters after on-policy training is the right move, and it is motivated by SWARR, where RL makes pure SWA nearly match full attention on math [2606.11634]. The random-spread gate is a finding that holds up even without the method, and would be cited on its own.
- **A crisp core idea.** Choose the full-attention layers that repair the failures the trained student still makes on its own traces. Of the many conversion selectors read here, none scores on the hybrid's own long rollouts: KL on DCLM [2512.20569], passkey gates [2606.30562], KL+cost at 4K [2605.20936], LM-loss L0 masks [2606.18056], teacher-trace likelihood [2604.19877], 128-token teacher drafts [2505.22135], and NIAH causal patching for heads [2606.20097].
- **The comparison discipline is unusually good:**
  - headline claims are made on the shipped, jointly trained model rather than the frozen supernet;
  - every method gets matched total GPU-hours, with the whole allocation run charged to the method;
  - the strong baselines are on-policy versions of the competitors (OPD-trained gates, DtR-style greedy accuracy [2601.11667], greedy on-policy KL);
  - there is an ablation that isolates repair-set reduction from event localization.
- **The kill conditions are honest.** They include "uniform at the same K is already non-inferior to Opal, so the reduction is free and not due to allocation", which blocks the most common way such papers overclaim.

## Weaknesses
1. **[major] The payoff is small next to the machinery, and the problem framing hides cheaper ways to get the same saving.**
   - At threshold, the deliverable is 9 to 6 full-attention layers in a 4B hybrid that is already about 4x lighter than full attention at 128K [2609.31947]. That is about a third less growing KV, bought with a supernet, mixer-only OPD rounds, 8-vs-8 continuation filtering on up-to-32K prefixes, per-event backward elimination and a 9-round greedy loop.
   - The same third of FA KV can be removed with FP8 KV storage, or with eviction or codec methods that target exactly these layers in hybrids [2610.14754][2609.02029][2610.36950], all orthogonal and nearly free.
   - A generalist reader will ask why the lever is "fewer layers" rather than "compressed layers". The proposal never compares against, or composes with, these methods at matched KV bytes. Without that, the significance argument for NeurIPS rests on a 33% saving of one memory term.
2. **[major] At threshold, the statistics are thin for the claims made.**
   - Every superiority claim is "+3 pp with CI lower bound > 0": AIME 2024+2025 (60 problems), avg@32, 3 pipeline seeds, one base model.
   - Non-inferiority means a lower bound just above -2 pp, so the point estimate may itself be negative.
   - The MiMo replication has one seed, and there is no second domain.
   - A reader who has seen this year's 10+ hybrid layer-selection papers will see a single-model, single-benchmark, barely-significant result. Stability is at threshold too: K_stop varies by 2 layers across the knob grid, which is the entire {4, 5, 6} range. That undercuts the "K from data" claim.
3. **[major] The novelty in mechanism is narrower than the vocabulary suggests.**
   - Stripped of ddmin, PMA and "verified repair set" language, the method is greedy layer restoration scored by a KL drop at selected token positions.
   - That is close to one-swap KL-guided selection [2512.20569] and to causal-patching unit selection [2606.20097]. The differences are where the positions come from (the on-policy student's wrong traces) and iterating with retraining.
   - The proposal's own ablation predicts that event-localized KL without reduction is only 2 pp worse. So the part that is truly new (on-policy failure localization) and the part that carries the name (minimal repair sets, verified coverage, monotonicity) are separated by the smallest margin in the paper.
   - At threshold, the paper's honest message is "on-policy failure positions are a better selection signal". That is a good message, but a simpler one than the title promises.
4. **[major] The search space and the shipped model differ.**
   - Allocation is searched in a supernet whose MLPs and exact paths are frozen at teacher values. The shipped model then trains all weights jointly, which changes what each layer does.
   - The proposal measures the gap between the frozen and shipped models but cannot diagnose it.
   - If the predictions hold, this is a correlation the reader must take on trust. If the allocation transfers only partly, the 3 pp edge is what absorbs the loss.
5. **[minor] Matched GPU-hours may hide a large diagnosis cost.**
   - Answer-flip filtering runs 16 continuations from each candidate prefix. Prefixes can be up to 32K tokens, and benign forks are rejected only after those continuations run.
   - At 1,000 kept events per round and up to 9 rounds, this can plausibly reach billions of generated tokens, comparable to or larger than Opal's entire 2B-token OPD budget.
   - The cost is "to be measured". If it is large, the baselines receive several times more OPD, which is fair, but the method then becomes an offline research tool rather than something practitioners adopt.
6. **[minor] Transfer is unclear.**
   - Events are mined only on math prompts, and AIME is the only load-bearing metric. Layer-ordering theory says the best allocation is task-specific [2610.18104], so a math-mined 6-layer allocation could hurt code, agentic or retrieval-heavy long generation.
   - The retrieval check (within 5 pp on NIAH and RULER at 32K, at threshold a 5 pp drop) is a loose bound for a memory paper.
7. **[minor] The story is hard to retell.** The title, the six-module pipeline and the eight pillars make the paper hard to retell without the authors present. The memorable sentence ("keep the attention layers that fix your student's own mistakes") should lead, and the ddmin and monotonicity material belongs in an appendix unless the violation-rate result changes a decision.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- **Opal [2609.31947]** is the direct reference. It fixes 9/36 by rule and never varies the ratio. The proposal correctly builds on it, but the contribution is framed as a delta on a one-month-old preprint, which is fragile if Opal's own follow-up varies the ratio.
- **KL-guided layer selection [2512.20569]** restores single layers and scores them by KL drop after brief re-distillation. Mechanistically, this is the closest ancestor; the difference is off-policy DCLM text versus on-policy failure tokens.
- **HydraHead [2606.20097]** (read this run) chooses FA units by causal activation and path patching on NIAH counterfactuals. At head granularity it already reaches a 7:1 LA:FA ratio that matches a 3:1 layer-wise hybrid on RULER. The idea of a K-reduction through causal unit selection is therefore not new. HydraHead is off-policy, short-benchmark and retrieval-only, with no long CoT, so the long-CoT, on-policy angle here remains distinct. Head-level allocation is a competing way to cut FA KV that the proposal does not address.
- **ConSA [2606.18056], FlashMorph [2606.30562] and DASH [2605.20936]** learn allocations under a budget with gates. The proposal reruns them with the OPD objective as baselines, which handles them well.
- **DtR [2601.11667] and Super Apriel [2604.19877]** are outcome-driven and supernet-based placement methods; they are adapted or credited appropriately.
- **SWARR [2606.11634]** is the main threat to the premise, since on-policy training may make allocation irrelevant. The gate addresses it.
- **Hybrid KV compression [2610.14754][2609.02029][2610.36950]** shrinks the same FA-layer cache by other means. These are not discussed as alternatives or complements.
- Recency searches since 2025-10-28 found no paper that selects hybrid full-attention layers from the on-policy student's long-CoT failures. The core angle appears unclaimed as of 2026-10-28.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- Opal 9/36 with KV compression on its 9 FA layers (FP8 KV, and one eviction method such as [2610.14754] or a per-head residency method [2609.02029]) at the same KV bytes as K_stop. Also the composition of the two, ours plus the same compression, to show the gains add.
- Opal's own dedicated pipeline at 6/36 uniform, not only uniform inside the supernet. Otherwise "beats uniform at K" may partly reflect a weaker supernet route to uniform.
- Head-level allocation at matched KV bytes (ConSA head-wise [2606.18056] or HydraHead-style causal head selection [2606.20097]), since the claim is about the fewest FA KV bytes, not the fewest layers.
- At least one non-math long-generation task (for example, code with long thinking) to test whether a math-mined allocation transfers.
- A second model family with 3 seeds, or at least the headline comparison on MiMo-7B with 3 seeds.
- A power analysis for detecting 3 pp on 60 AIME problems with avg@32 and 3 seeds. Also add a harder pool, such as HMMT or AIME 2026 if it is decontaminated, to reduce reliance on 60 items.
- Reported diagnosis cost in generated tokens and GPU-hours per round, and an accuracy-versus-allocation-budget curve showing what the method costs relative to just training Opal longer.

## Questions for the authors
1. If FP8 KV storage on Opal 9/36 gives a larger memory saving at no accuracy loss, what is the argument that removing three layers is the lever a practitioner should pull? Do the two compose?
2. At threshold, event-localized KL is only 2 pp behind. Would you lead the paper with that simpler method, and keep repair-set reduction only if the violation rate changes the chosen layers?
3. Are the chosen layers interpretable? For example, do they coincide with the layers whose attention reaches far back at the event positions, and are they stable across Qwen3-4B and MiMo? A memorable "which layers and why" result would carry the paper further than another +3 pp.
4. How many generated tokens does event mining with the 8-vs-8 filter consume per round, including rejected benign forks? What fraction of the matched GPU-hour budget does it take?
5. How much does the frozen-supernet ranking of allocations agree with the shipped-model ranking (Spearman over the random allocations in the gate)?

## Rating
- rating: 4 (borderline accept). At threshold this is a rigorous, well-controlled study with a clear and apparently unclaimed angle: allocation by on-policy long-CoT failures, with an allocation-matters gate. The payoff is a modest 33% FA-KV cut, shown on one model and one 60-problem benchmark, with margins that are just significant. It also lacks comparison with cheaper ways to remove the same memory.
- confidence: 3
- p_core: 0.3. This is the probability that, run as written, the shipped K <= 6 model is non-inferior to Opal 9/36 and beats uniform at the same K by at least 3 pp. SWARR-style routing-around and the frozen-to-shipped transfer gap are the main risks.
- one_point_fix: Add the matched-KV-bytes comparison and composition against Opal 9/36 with FA-layer KV compression (FP8 plus one hybrid eviction method). Then lead with "on-policy failure positions pick better layers" as the simple core idea, and show that the K-reduction adds to compression rather than duplicating it.
