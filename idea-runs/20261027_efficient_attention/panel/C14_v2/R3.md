# R3 (experimentalist) review: C14 v2, "Fewer Full-Attention Layers from the Failures On-Policy Training Leaves Behind"

## Summary
The paper converts Qwen3-4B (thinking) into a Gated DeltaNet hybrid. In a teacher-anchored supernet (frozen exact attention plus a trainable GDN mixer in every layer), it picks which layers keep full attention by repeating four steps: mine failure positions on the student's own wrong long-CoT traces, shrink each failure to a 1-minimal set of layers whose restoration brings next-token KL back under tau, greedily commit the layer or group with the highest verified coverage, then retrain mixers on-policy. It stops once no candidate repairs at least 5% of residual events. Taking every prediction exactly at its threshold, the resulting paper shows four things: allocation still matters after joint OPD (random spread of 5 pp, twice the seed range); a shipped K_stop = 6 hybrid is non-inferior to a re-run Opal 9/36 on AIME'24+'25 avg@32 (CI lower bound -2 pp) with a third less growing KV; it beats uniform-at-6 and three on-policy allocators by 3 pp each and event-localized KL by 2 pp; and it costs up to 5 pp of multi-key NIAH/RULER at 32K.

## Strengths
- The experiment order is right. The allocation-matters gate runs first, at the shipped-model level, with a stated kill. This takes SWARR's finding that on-policy training routes around missing long-range access [2606.11634] seriously, which no prior hybrid selector does.
- The baselines are strong and on-policy, not strawmen. They include OPD-trained differentiable gates with an exact-K Lagrangian (FlashMorph/DASH/ConSA-style [2606.30562][2605.20936][2606.18056]), DtR's greedy end-task criterion moved to long-CoT dev accuracy [2601.11667], and greedy whole-trace on-policy KL. All share the supernet, the shipping stage and the GPU-hour budget. Teacher-forced selectors are also included [2512.20569][2606.27791][2604.19877].
- The mechanism ablation is the right one. Event-localized KL without reduction, at 3 seeds, separates "where it fails" from "which layer set fixes it". Inferred vs verified coverage and one-shot vs re-collected allocation are scored on final accuracy, not on diagnosis cost.
- Headline claims sit on the shipped (jointly trained) model at matched total GPU-hours, with the full allocation run charged to every K taken from it. The frozen-versus-shipped gap is reported per allocation.
- The load-bearing benchmark is chosen for headroom: AIME has 21-23 pp of pass@1 headroom to the teacher, while MATH-500 is near ceiling and carries no claim [2609.31947]. Using avg@32 and a paired bootstrap is the right direction.
- Diagnostics come with their own kills: KL-repair vs answer-repair agreement, monotonicity-violation rate, and stopping-knob stability.

## Weaknesses
1. [major] **The headline saving is not compared against the obvious ways to cut the same bytes.** At threshold the paper's practical result is a growing KV cache one third smaller (9 to 6 full-attention layers), at a cost of up to 2 pp on AIME and up to 5 pp on 32K retrieval. A reviewer who works on long-CoT memory will point out that larger savings on exactly those 9 layers already exist with little reasoning loss: learned delayed eviction retrofits 4x compression on reasoning models in a few hundred steps [2506.05345], R-KV reports near-full AIME/MATH-500 at 10-34% cache [2505.24133], and long-CoT KV quantization goes progressively to 4 or 2 bits [2505.18610]. The paper never puts Opal 9/36 plus one of these at the same growing-KV bytes as K = 6, and never shows that K-reduction composes with them. Without an accuracy versus KV-bytes Pareto plot that includes those points, "33% smaller cache" reads as a weak operating point rather than a contribution. Throughput is measured but not argued: going from 9 to 6 attention layers saves KV reads only, so the decode-speed gain at 32K will be well below 33%.
2. [major] **At threshold, the effect sizes sit at the edge of what the design can resolve.** Everything rests on 60 AIME problems from one 4B model. A 3 pp gain over uniform at K* is smaller than the 5 pp spread of random allocations at that same K. The paper never shows where its allocation falls within the random distribution, and with 5 single-seed draws it cannot. The gate compares the range of 5 draws with the range of 3 draws. Under no allocation effect that ratio is already about 1.4 in expectation, so "at least 2x" is a weak test and passes by chance often. The main CI bootstraps over problems with seeds pooled. That leaves out pipeline-seed variance, which is the dominant variance for 30-day training pipelines, and with 3 seeds it can hardly be estimated. The hierarchical bootstrap is only a robustness check. At least six pairwise claims are made at K* and K_stop with no multiplicity control.
3. [major] **Test-set reuse in design decisions.** K* is the K whose AIME random spread is largest, and that same AIME set then carries the main comparison at K*. This is mild selection on the test set. Draw K* from a dev pool (the DtR baseline already has one) or fix it in advance.
4. [major] **"K from data" is fragile at threshold.** The stability prediction allows K_stop to move by 2 layers across s_min/tau/kappa and seeds, with Jaccard 0.6. With K_stop = 6 at the default, some knob settings give K = 7 or 8. The headline "K <= 6" then holds only at the default knobs, and the full trajectory to K_max = 9 shows the stopping point is a tuning choice. A reviewer will read the paper's real result as "a better allocation at a hand-chosen K = 6", which is a smaller claim.
5. [major] **Compute is underbudgeted where it hurts.** The answer-flip filter runs 8 teacher and 8 student continuations of up to 32K tokens from every candidate prefix. To yield 1,000 events per round, over up to 9 rounds, per seed, for ours and for the event-localized-KL ablation, this likely costs several billion generated tokens per allocation run, more than the 1B-token shipping stage. That cost is missing from the 40-shipping-run Tier-1 plan. Matched GPU-hours then hands Opal 9/36 and uniform a large OPD extension, which is fair but raises the bar for non-inferiority. Opal's AIME was still rising at the end of 2B tokens [2609.31947]. The proposal needs a pilot-measured cost per round before committing to the seed plan.
6. [major] **The reference baseline is a reproduction.** Non-inferiority to a re-run Opal 9/36 is cheap if the re-run is weak. The paper should require the reproduction to come within a stated tolerance of Opal's published 52.5/40.4 pass@1 [2609.31947] before the margin means anything. It should also state whether "uniform at K = 6" is trained with Opal's dedicated recipe and an extended OPD at matched GPU-hours, or only through the supernet plus 1B shipping. Only the first rules out "the gain comes from extra compute".
7. [major] **Scope is narrow for the claim.** One primary model (Qwen3-4B), a single-seed MiMo-7B replication, math-only allocation and evaluation, generation up to 32K, whole-layer granularity. Head-level allocation is reported to beat layer-level [2606.18056], and the paper itself frames per-layer KV as the cost. A NeurIPS reviewer will ask whether an allocation mined on math hurts code or general chat. At threshold, 32K retrieval already drops 5 pp.
8. [minor] **The repair predicate is a single-position KL test in a frozen supernet.** The agreement analysis at threshold (Spearman 0.3, success gain 0.25) is weak evidence that the predicate tracks outcomes in the shipped model, since nothing is diagnosed after joint OPD. The ablation "shared weights trained during allocation" is listed but not linked to a prediction.
9. [minor] **No signal from iterating alone.** In an analogous on-policy layer-selection loop, iterating (select, OPD, re-select) carried about 7 pp and the better score about 2 pp [2610.25679]. The whole-trace-KL and DtR baselines share the rounds, which is good. Still, the paper should report "random commit order + same rounds" to measure how much the rounds alone buy.
10. [minor] **AIME'24/'25 may be contaminated** in Qwen3's training data. AIME 2026, HMMT 2025/2026 and BeyondAIME-style sets would both reduce that risk and roughly double the problem count.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- Opal [2609.31947]: same conversion, same OPD recipe, same model; fixed 9/36 and never varies the ratio. This is the direct reference, and the proposal positions against it correctly.
- Super Apriel [2604.19877]: frozen-shared-weight supernet, placement scored by teacher-trace likelihood on MATH500/AIME with an interaction surrogate, no on-policy stage. The supernet design is borrowed. The novelty is the on-policy, per-failure signal.
- DtR [2601.11667]: greedy outcome-driven replacement with a tolerance stop, i.e. already a "K from data" rule. It has no long-CoT or on-policy component, and is correctly included as a baseline.
- FlashMorph [2606.30562], DASH [2605.20936], ConSA [2606.18056]: joint gates or masks under a budget. Their objectives are retrained on OPD here as the strongest baseline, which is fair.
- KL-guided [2512.20569], HALO [2601.22156], GenDistill [2603.26556], RAD [2505.22135], NLL-guided [2606.27791]: teacher-forced or teacher-side selectors with hand-set budgets. None uses student long-CoT rollouts.
- LOOP [2610.25679] (concurrent, October 2026): interleaves one-layer-at-a-time structural selection with short on-policy distillation and scores remaining layers from the recovery rollouts (Block Influence on student trajectories plus OPD-loss gate gradients). It targets depth pruning, not hybrid allocation, and it does not localize failures or run per-failure restoration tests. A reviewer will still cite it as the same "select, OPD, re-select from on-policy signals" loop, and its ablation suggests iteration does most of the work. It should be discussed and its scoring rule added as a cheap baseline.
- SWARR [2606.11634]: the reason the gate exists. On-policy training can make allocation matter less.
- Long-CoT KV compression [2506.05345][2505.24133][2505.18610] and hybrid-specific KV compression [2610.14754][2609.02029]: competing ways to cut the same growing cache. None of them is a novelty overlap, but they matter for significance (Weakness 1).
- I found no paper that picks hybrid full-attention layers from per-failure repair sets on the student's own long-CoT traces, or that tests whether allocation matters after on-policy training. The narrow novelty claim survives my searches, including recency searches since 2025-10-28.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- **Matched-memory Pareto.** Opal 9/36 plus delayed-eviction or quantized KV (DMS-style [2506.05345], R-KV [2505.24133], FP8/INT4 KV) at the growing-KV bytes of K = 6 and K = 4. Also ours at K_stop plus the same compression, to show the two compose. This is the comparison that decides significance.
- **The null distribution at K*.** At least 10-20 random allocations, ideally 2 seeds each, with ours reported as a percentile. Also the best random allocation shipped at 3 seeds.
- **Uniform at K* and K_stop through Opal's dedicated recipe** at matched GPU-hours, not only through the supernet.
- **Rounds without the signal.** The same diagnose-free rounds with a random or LOOP-style [2610.25679] commit order, so the value of iterating is separated from the value of repair sets.
- **Heuristic layouts.** A contiguous middle-block layout, which ConSA's learned masks favour [2606.18056], and a "last-layers" layout. Both are cheap, and reviewers will ask for them.
- **A larger and fresher evaluation pool.** AIME 2026 and HMMT added to the 60 problems. The seed-level hierarchical bootstrap should be the primary CI, with a Holm correction across the baseline comparisons.
- **A second model at 3 seeds** (MiMo-7B, or Qwen3-8B), and one out-of-domain long-generation task such as LiveCodeBench, so the allocation is not shown to be math-specific only by omission.
- **Measured compute.** Per-round event-mining and diagnosis GPU-hours from the pilot, and the decode throughput gain from 9 to 6 layers at 32K/128K at realistic batch sizes.

## Questions for the authors
1. What growing-KV accuracy does Opal 9/36 reach with an off-the-shelf long-CoT KV compressor at the bytes of K = 6, and does your K_stop model still compress as well on top?
2. Is "uniform at K" trained with Opal's dedicated recipe and an extended OPD at matched GPU-hours? If not, what fraction of the 3 pp gain could come from training budget rather than placement?
3. Where does your allocation rank among random allocations at K*, and how many random allocations would you need to say it is above the 90th percentile?
4. How many continuation tokens does one round of event mining cost at 32K prefixes, and what is the total allocation-run GPU-hour figure relative to one shipping run?
5. Since K* is chosen from AIME spread, why not pick it from the dev pool already used by the DtR baseline?
6. With K_stop allowed to move by 2 layers across knobs, would you present the headline as "K = 6 chosen" or as "K from data"? Which knob settings give K > 6?
7. What tolerance to Opal's published AIME numbers will your reproduction of 9/36 have to meet?

## Rating
- rating: 3 (borderline reject)
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.3
- one_point_fix: Make the headline an accuracy versus growing-KV-bytes Pareto comparison that includes Opal 9/36 with an off-the-shelf long-CoT KV compressor (delayed eviction or 4-bit KV) at the bytes of K = 4 and K = 6, together with ours plus the same compressor. Then show that the K-reduction is either better than compression at matched memory or composes with it.

Rationale: if every prediction lands exactly at threshold, the paper is careful and well controlled. But it delivers a one-third cut of a cache that existing compressors already cut by 3-10x. That cut costs up to 2 pp of AIME and 5 pp of 32K retrieval, on 60 problems from one 4B model, and a 3 pp gain sits inside the random-allocation spread. A reviewer who has seen this year's layer-selection and KV-compression papers will say the mechanism is interesting but its significance is unproven without the matched-memory comparison. With that comparison, and a gain that holds, the paper moves to 4.
