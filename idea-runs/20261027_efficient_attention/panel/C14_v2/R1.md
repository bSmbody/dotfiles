# R1 review: C14 v2 "Fewer Full-Attention Layers from the Failures On-Policy Training Leaves Behind"

## Summary
The paper converts Qwen3-4B (thinking) into a Gated DeltaNet hybrid and picks which layers keep full attention, and how many, as follows. It first builds a supernet in which every layer has a frozen copy of the teacher's attention plus a trainable GDN mixer, so switching every layer to exact attention gives back the teacher. In that supernet it mines the first high-KL token of each wrong student rollout, then runs an activation-restoration test (switch extra layers to exact attention, prefill the prefix, check whether next-token KL falls below tau). Backward elimination shrinks each event's repair set to a 1-minimal one, and layers are committed greedily by verified coverage until coverage drops below 5%. Assuming every prediction lands exactly at its threshold, the shipped model (after joint OPD) keeps K_stop of 4 to 6 full-attention layers and is non-inferior to Opal's 9/36 on AIME 24+25 avg@32. The 95% CI lower bound is just above -2 pp, so the point estimate is roughly parity. It beats the uniform interleave at the same K and three on-policy allocators by 3 pp each (CI > 0), beats an event-localized-KL ablation by 2 pp, and stays within 5 pp of Opal on multi-key NIAH/RULER at 32K, with 33% or more less growing KV.

## Strengths
- The problem is real and placed well. Opal [2609.31947] fixes 9/36 by rule and never varies it, and every published allocator scores layers on teacher-forced text, probes or short teacher drafts: KL-guided [2512.20569], FlashMorph [2606.30562], DASH [2605.20936], ConSA [2606.18056], NLL-guided [2606.27791], HALO [2601.22156], RAD [2505.22135], Super Apriel [2604.19877]. None of them uses the trained hybrid's own long-CoT rollouts. In my search I found no paper, prior or concurrent, that selects layers from on-policy residual failures.
- The allocation-matters gate runs first and is the most valuable scientific content. SWARR [2606.11634] shows that on-policy RL lets a model route around missing long-range access. 2606.15378 shows that hybrid design choices converge with enough training. So "does placement still matter after OPD at K = 4-6?" is an open and non-obvious question. A positive answer, with a 5 pp random spread and twice the seed noise, would be useful to the community whatever one thinks of the method.
- The baselines are unusually strong for this sub-area. Gates retrained with the OPD objective under an exact-K hard-concrete budget, DtR-style greedy selection by long-CoT dev accuracy [2601.11667], and greedy whole-trace on-policy KL are exactly the cheap alternatives a specialist would name. They get matched GPU-hours and 3 seeds, and Opal's own pipeline is extended to equal compute.
- The headline is measured in the right place: the jointly trained shipped model, not the frozen supernet. The frozen-versus-shipped gap is also reported.
- The engineering is honest. Existing kernels are reused (FLA, FlashAttention, vLLM), the theory claims nothing about greedy optimality, and the 1-minimality claim is narrow and correct.

## Weaknesses
1. [major] The practical gain is modest and is not compared against the obvious ways to save the same bytes. Going from 9 to 6 full-attention layers cuts the growing KV from 25% to 16.7% of a full Transformer's. Opal already captures the 4x reduction at 128K [2609.31947]. The paper claims memory, yet it never compares against Opal 9/36 with its 9 layers' KV compressed to the same byte budget: FP8/4-bit KV quantization for long-CoT [2505.18610], decode-time heavy-hitter eviction, which works on reasoning models [2512.12008], or hybrid-specific KV eviction [2610.14754]. FP8 KV alone halves the cache at a near-negligible accuracy cost on most models. A reviewer will say the 33% could have been had with no allocation search. The authors can answer "orthogonal, they stack", but that has to be shown (K_stop + FP8 vs 9/36 + FP8), not asserted.
2. [major] At threshold, every pillar is marginal, and together they make a thin paper:
   - The headline is "parity" with a CI that admits a -2 pp loss on a 60-problem benchmark.
   - The gain over uniform is 3 pp with CI barely above 0.
   - Retrieval may be up to 5 pp worse on multi-key NIAH/RULER at 32K, which a long-context reader will notice.
   - KL-repair versus answer-repair correlation is only 0.3, so the predicate is a weak proxy for outcomes.
   - K_stop may vary by 2 layers across knobs and seeds, i.e. anywhere in 4-6, with layer-set Jaccard 0.6. "K is set by the data" is then only loosely true: the knobs decide whether you save 33% or 56%.
   - The non-monotonicity rate is 5%, so verified coverage changes little.

   Nothing fails, but nothing is decisive.
3. [major] Allocation is searched in one regime and shipped in another. Events, repair sets and coverage are all computed in a mixer-only supernet with MLPs and attention frozen at teacher values, and with GDN mixers trained under randomly sampled placements. Joint OPD then retrains everything for 1B tokens. Taking the results as given, the method still works. But the paper cannot explain why. Do the repaired layers matter because of what the frozen supernet lacks, or because of what the shipped model needs? The ablations never test the obvious cheaper story: any allocation that avoids the worst few layers, or that matches ConSA-style "contiguous middle blocks" [2606.18056], does as well.
4. [major] The scope is one model, one domain and one length. Results come from Qwen3-4B with 3 seeds and MiMo-7B-RL with 1 seed; allocation and evaluation are math only, generation is capped at 32K, and AIME 24/25 is the only load-bearing metric. The output of the method is an allocation for one checkpoint. Super Apriel [2604.19877] and the layer-ordering theory [2610.18104] already say allocations are task-specific, so a math-tuned allocation may cost code, agentic or 128K-generation quality, and none of those is tested. The retrieval-within-5pp prediction already hints at this.
5. [minor] The mechanism is less new than the wording suggests. Strip away the vocabulary ("verified repair sets", "ddmin") and the method is activation patching / causal restoration of whole layers [2307.15771] at one token per failure, plus one-at-a-time backward elimination, plus greedy set cover. These are standard tools. The novelty is applying them on-policy to hybrid allocation, and that is a fair but incremental step from Opal + DtR [2601.11667] + Jet-Nemotron's outcome-based placement search [2508.15884].
6. [minor] Going below 1:3 is not new in itself. Jet-Nemotron keeps 2 full-attention layers (+2 SWA) of 28 [2508.15884]. KL-guided selection and FlashMorph run at 1:8 / 6:1 [2512.20569][2606.30562]. 2606.15378 reports that full-attention density "can be safely reduced once enough full-attention layers are available". The new part is doing it for long-CoT after OPD, and the framing should say so.
7. [minor] The statistics are borderline for the claims. On 60 AIME problems, the per-problem paired bootstrap pools 3 seeds, so seed variance is folded into problem means. The hierarchical bootstrap should be primary, not a robustness check. K* is chosen post hoc from the gate (largest spread), which is legitimate but should be pre-registered as written. There are also several 3 pp superiority tests without multiplicity correction.
8. [minor] Cost. 1,000 events per round × up to ~36 restoration prefills of prefixes up to 32K × up to 9 rounds, plus 8-vs-8 continuation filtering, is substantial. Matched GPU-hours absorbs this into the comparison, but the paper should report what fraction of the budget went to diagnosis rather than training, since that decides whether practitioners adopt it.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- Opal [2609.31947]: same base models, same OPD recipe, fixed 9/36. This paper is a direct follow-up that varies Opal's untouched knob. No overlap on allocation.
- DtR [2601.11667]: greedy replacement by end-task validation score with a tolerance stop. This is the closest algorithmic ancestor (greedy outcome-driven, K chosen by a stop rule), and the paper includes it as a baseline adapted to long-CoT after OPD.
- Jet-Nemotron / PostNAS [2508.15884]: once-for-all supernet with frozen MLPs, and placement chosen by the subnetwork's own task accuracy (math/retrieval). That is outcome-based, but short few-shot, with a fixed budget and no re-collection.
- Super Apriel [2604.19877]: frozen-shared-weight supernet, placement by likelihood of teacher AIME/MATH traces with an interaction surrogate. Same supernet design; off-policy scoring; RL named as future work. It is the closest concurrent "reasoning-aware placement" work.
- FlashMorph [2606.30562], DASH [2605.20936], ConSA [2606.18056]: budgeted joint gate/mask search on teacher-forced or passkey signals. Their on-policy versions are the paper's strongest baseline.
- KL-guided layer selection [2512.20569], HALO [2601.22156], NLL-guided [2606.27791], RAD [2505.22135], GenDistill [2603.26556]: single-layer or beam scores on fixed text, probes or 128-token teacher drafts. They do not overlap on the on-policy signal.
- SWARR [2606.11634] and Rethinking efficient attention in hybrids [2606.15378] (read this run): both suggest that with enough (on-policy or pretraining) training, architecture differences shrink. This is the main prior reason to expect the gate might fail, and the paper should discuss 2606.15378.
- Systematic hybrid ratio sweep [2507.06457]: recall saturates near 3:1 at small scale, with no reasoning evaluation.
- Recency searches (since 2025-10-28) on hybrid distillation, layer selection, on-policy distillation of linear attention, hybrid reasoning attention ratio and layer restoration found no paper that allocates full-attention layers from a hybrid student's own long-CoT failures. HydraHead (2606.20097, abstract only, not read) and Switch Attention (2603.26380, abstract only, not read) work at head or token granularity. I found no direct overlap; the novelty claim holds as of today.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- A matched-growing-KV-bytes comparison: Opal 9/36 with FP8 (and 4-bit) KV, or decode-time eviction on its 9 layers [2505.18610][2512.12008][2610.14754], at the byte budget of K_stop. Then the stacked version: K_stop + the same compression vs 9/36 + compression. Without this, the memory claim is unanchored.
- SWA-for-some-layers variants at the same growing-cache bytes. For example, keep 6 exact layers and turn 3 of Opal's 9 into SWA-4K instead of GDN, as Jet-Nemotron mixes full and SWA [2508.15884]. That is the cheap alternative a hybrid practitioner would try first.
- Opal-style direct training at K = 6 with simple structural priors: middle-block contiguous placement (ConSA's finding [2606.18056]) and "uniform but skip the first/last layers". If these close the 3 pp, the event machinery is not needed.
- Head-level allocation, or keeping more KV heads in fewer layers, at matched bytes. The proposal declares this out of scope, but specialists will ask, given ConSA's head-wise > layer-wise result [2606.18056].
- Out-of-domain checks on the shipped K_stop model: at least one code benchmark (LiveCodeBench) and generation beyond 32K (e.g. 64K AIME/HMMT traces). This shows that a math-mined allocation does not overfit.
- Transfer: does Qwen3-4B's K_stop layer set, applied to MiMo-7B or a second Qwen size, beat uniform? If yes, the method gives reusable knowledge. If not, it is a per-checkpoint search.
- A diagnosis-cost breakdown, and a "same GPU-hours spent on more OPD for uniform K = 6" control. The proposal partly covers this through matched GPU-hours; it should be shown as its own line.

## Questions for the authors
1. Which layers end up in A_{K_stop}, and how do they compare with Opal's every-fourth set and with ConSA/NLL-guided layer rankings? Is the gain over uniform due to a few specific "look-up" layers, i.e. could a human read the answer off one diagnostic plot?
2. How much of the 3 pp over uniform survives if uniform at K = 6 gets the GPU-hours you spend on event mining and diagnosis as extra joint OPD?
3. Given K_stop varying over {4, 5, 6} across knobs and seeds, which K do you recommend a practitioner ship, and with what expected AIME and memory?
4. Why is the repair predicate a single-token KL rather than the 8-continuation success gain you already compute for 300 events? With a correlation of only 0.3, would an answer-based predicate at lower event counts do better at the same cost?
5. How does joint OPD change the ranking of allocations? Report the Spearman correlation between frozen-supernet accuracy and shipped accuracy across the 10 random allocations of the gate.
6. What is the end-to-end decoding throughput and peak-memory gain of K = 6 over 9/36 at batch sizes you would actually serve, given that weights and GDN states do not shrink?

## Rating
- rating: 4 (borderline accept)
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.2. This is my own estimate that the gate passes AND K <= 6 is non-inferior to Opal 9/36 AND the method beats all three strong on-policy allocators by 3 pp when actually run. The gate alone I put near 0.5. Beating DtR-style greedy dev-accuracy and on-policy gates by 3 pp each on 60 AIME problems is the least likely link.
- one_point_fix: Add a matched-growing-KV-bytes comparison against Opal 9/36 with its 9 layers' KV compressed (FP8 / 4-bit quantization or decode-time eviction) to K_stop's byte budget, and show that K_stop plus the same compression still beats 9/36 plus compression. Without it, the 33% memory claim can be dismissed as something a free KV-quantization switch already delivers.
