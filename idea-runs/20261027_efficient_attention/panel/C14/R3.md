# R3 (experimentalist) — C14: Verified Repair Sets for full-attention allocation in hybrid reasoning models

## Summary
The proposal converts Qwen3-4B (and MiMo-7B-RL) into a GDN hybrid inside a supernet. All shared weights and the exact-attention paths stay frozen at teacher values, so the all-exact configuration is the teacher. It then mines first-KL-spike positions on the student's own wrong-answer long-CoT rollouts and uses ddmin (with PMA-gated skips) to shrink, per event, the set of layers that must be exact for the next-token KL at that position to fall below tau. It greedily commits the layer or small group with the highest verified coverage, continues mixer-only OPD, re-collects rollouts, and stops when coverage or repair-set size thresholds are hit. The claimed insight is that the allocation (and K) should be chosen against the failures left behind by on-policy training, not from teacher-forced proxies. The predicted evidence is:
- small, recurring repair sets;
- repaired events flip answers more often than random high-KL positions;
- accuracy above single-layer and whole-trace on-policy baselines just beyond benchmark noise at matched K;
- smaller long-bin degradation than teacher-forced selectors;
- a stopping rule near the K-sweep knee;
- the ranking survives joint OPD, with no retrieval regression.

## Strengths
- The question is right for the scope. Opal shows OPD recovers long-CoT reasoning but keeps every fourth layer by rule [2609.31947]. Every selector I checked scores layers on teacher-forced text, synthetic probes or short benchmarks: DCLM KL [2512.20569], passkey gates [2606.30562], reference-answer NLL [2606.27791], commonsense sensitivity [2604.22050], teacher-trace likelihood [2604.19877]. None scores on the hybrid's own long rollouts.
- The teacher-anchored supernet is a clean design choice. Making the all-exact configuration exactly the teacher gives each reduction a valid starting point, and it is a measured improvement over Super Apriel's approximately-teacher all-FA placement [2604.19877].
- The two required simple on-policy baselines are the right controls. One is a single-layer swap score at the same events, the other greedy whole-trace on-policy KL. They separate "on-policy data" from "repair-set machinery", which is exactly what a skeptic would ask for.
- The ablation list is unusually complete: answer-flip filter on/off, one-shot vs re-diagnosis, PMA vs plain ddmin, uniform vs targeted placement sampling, frozen vs joint training during allocation, and sensitivity to tau, the unclear band and kappa.
- The benchmarks are standard (AIME 24/25, MATH-500, GPQA-D, NIAH, RULER), and accuracy is broken down by generated length. Cost is reported in GPU-hours, with prefill and generation separated.
- There are explicit kill criteria and a pilot gate before any allocation claim.

## Weaknesses
- [major] **The load-bearing comparison sits at the noise floor, and the statistical protocol is underspecified.**
  - Prediction 3 only promises a gap just outside the bootstrap CI, citing a ±4.2 pp half-width for 500 examples [2606.27791].
  - On MATH-500 Opal's hybrid is already at 92-93.8% (thinking) [2609.31947]. A >4 pp gain over a comparable allocation is close to impossible there, so the claim effectively rests on AIME.
  - AIME has 30 problems per year, so single-sample pass@1 noise is far larger than ±4 pp. The proposal does not say how many samples per problem (avg@16/32?), how many pipeline seeds, or what "across seeds" means. A seed here could be the allocation run (stochastic rollouts, ddmin orderings), the OPD run, or the evaluation sampling.
  - At threshold, the resulting paper shows a margin a reviewer can call within run-to-run variance of a multi-round, multi-day pipeline that is run perhaps once.
- [major] **Compute fairness against baselines is not stated.**
  - The method gets 5-9 commit rounds, each with OPD, fresh rollouts, about 3K events, 8 answer-flip regenerations of up to 32K tokens per event, and 5-30B diagnosis prefill tokens.
  - It is not said whether the "same mixer-only OPD budget" for baselines includes the method's extra OPD between rounds. Nor is it said whether baselines receive the diagnosis GPU-hours as extra OPD.
  - The Opal baseline is trained here inside a placement-sampling supernet rather than as a dedicated fixed-allocation student. That can handicap it relative to Opal's own recipe.
  - The joint-OPD finishing stage partly fixes this, but only if its budget is large and identical. A reviewer will ask for a total-GPU-hour-matched comparison against the original Opal pipeline at 9/36.
- [major] **At matched K the efficiency story vanishes.** KV memory and throughput depend only on K. Every falsifiable prediction is framed as accuracy at matched K, or as the stopping rule "near the knee". So the paper as predicted shows no memory saving over Opal's 9/36, which is the scope's actual object ("what history to keep").
  - The formal goal mentions "minimize K at matched accuracy", but no prediction or kill condition tests it.
  - A paper whose best case is about 1 AIME point at the same cache size, for several extra GPU-days, has a weak cost-benefit case next to NLL-guided selection (about 15 min on 4 GPUs [2606.27791]).
- [major] **The strongest simple outcome-driven baseline is missing or ambiguous.**
  - DtR greedily replaces blocks by end-task validation score with a tolerance stop that sets K from data [2601.11667]. The proposal lists it, and also "PostNAS-style beam search by task accuracy", but does not say these are run on long-CoT dev accuracy from the same supernet after the same OPD.
  - With compute "not binding", greedy allocation directly by AIME/MATH dev accuracy on student rollouts is feasible. It is the natural upper bound for "on-policy allocation". If it matches the repair-set method, the event-localization machinery is unnecessary.
- [major] **The repair predicate is a local proxy, and the proposal's own core assumption is unverified.**
  - psi_e tests next-token reverse KL at one position on a fixed prefix. That is a teacher-forced, one-position criterion on a student prefix: essentially activation patching.
  - It does not test that the trajectory recovers. Answer flip is only measured post hoc (prediction 2), and the event filter uses k=4 continuations per side, so success rates can only take the values {0, .25, .5, .75, 1} and delta=0.5 is very coarse.
  - Pillar 2 at threshold means "markedly more than random" with no stated magnitude. A reviewer can argue the method optimizes a criterion only weakly tied to accuracy.
- [major] **Feasibility in 4-6 months for a new PhD student.** The pipeline has these parts, none off the shelf:
  - a two-path supernet at 4B and 7B;
  - Opal-style OPD with rollout-length doubling to 32K;
  - event mining with regenerations;
  - ddmin + PMA + revisit-to-fixpoint;
  - 5-9 retrain rounds;
  - 10 baseline allocations, each through the identical OPD and joint-OPD finishing stage;
  - 11 ablations, many of which need the full pipeline rerun.

  Compute is fine, but engineering and wall-clock are not. The likely outcome is a 4B-only paper with single seeds and a trimmed ablation set, which aggravates the first weakness.
- [minor] **Prediction 5 ("lands near the knee") has no operational definition** of the knee or of "substantially". DtR already sets K by a tolerance stop [2601.11667], so this is a weaker novelty claim than it reads.
- [minor] **Prediction 6 at threshold means the ranking is merely preserved after joint OPD.** That is consistent with the gap shrinking to a fraction of a point on the shipped model. Report the gap before and after joint OPD with CIs.
- [minor] The PMA/ddmin ablations measure diagnosis cost vs repair-set size, not final accuracy. They read as software-engineering detail that a NeurIPS reviewer will see as apparatus rather than insight.
- [minor] Allocation is driven by math prompts only. There is no out-of-domain long-generation check (code, e.g. LiveCodeBench) to show the chosen layers are not overfit to the event distribution.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2609.31947] Opal — on-policy distillation to Qwen3-4B / MiMo-7B GDN hybrids at a fixed 9/36 allocation, with the same models, recipe and benchmarks. It does not kill novelty, since it never chooses the allocation. It is the base this work extends, so the delta is allocation alone.
- [2604.19877] Super Apriel — frozen-shared-weight supernet with placement search on reasoning (likelihood of teacher traces on MATH500/AIME), modeling interactions with an order ≤3 surrogate and solving by DP. It does not kill novelty, because it is off-policy, scores whole placements and has no per-event localization. But the supernet, the sampling regime and "reasoning-scored placement" are already there, which narrows the contribution to the on-policy diagnosis loop.
- [2601.11667] DtR — greedy replacement driven by the hybrid's own end-task output, with a data-set K via tolerance stop, and evidence that static scores lose to interaction-aware greedy search. Partial overlap on "K from data" and "interactions matter". No long-CoT, so no kill.
- [2512.20569] KL-guided layer selection, [2606.30562] FlashMorph, [2606.27791] NLL-guided selection — teacher-forced selectors with hand-set K. These are baselines, not killers.
- [2604.22050] LayerBoost (Qwen3-4B) — per-layer removal sensitivity on commonsense benchmarks plus a keep/revert search, with K fixed at 33%. Its limitations section concedes the signal may miss long-form reasoning. Not a killer; it should be cited and is a cheap baseline.
- [2609.02986] Implicit Hybrids — a head-level, analysis-derived fixed allocation rule, pretrained from scratch at 380M, with no conversion or long-CoT evaluation. Not overlapping in mechanism.
- My searches, including recency searches since 2025-10-28, found no work that chooses hybrid allocation from per-failure repair tests on on-policy rollouts. Novelty of the mechanism holds. Novelty of the problem framing ("on-policy allocation") is thinner: one obvious variant, greedy on-policy KL, is already among the proposal's own baselines.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- **Total-GPU-hour-matched baselines.** Opal's original fixed-allocation pipeline (not the supernet version) at 9/36, given extra OPD tokens equal to the method's diagnosis plus inter-round OPD cost.
- **Greedy or beam allocation by long-CoT dev accuracy** (AIME/MATH held-out pass rate on student rollouts) on the same supernet after the same OPD: DtR's criterion applied to the target task.
- **A K-reduction experiment.** The smallest K at which each allocation method matches Opal-9/36 accuracy after joint OPD, with the KV-memory and throughput gain that follows.
- **Statistics.**
  - AIME avg@32 (or at least avg@16) per problem; paired bootstrap over problems and samples.
  - At least 3 seeds of the full allocation pipeline, reporting Jaccard stability of the chosen layer set and the accuracy spread.
  - The same seeds for the strongest two baselines.
- **Random-allocation distribution, not a single draw.** Accuracy of 5+ random K-subsets at matched K, to show how much allocation matters at all after joint OPD. If the spread is small, every comparison is moot.
- **Correlation analysis.** Per-event repair success (psi_e) against trajectory-level answer recovery, giving the predicate's validity as a proxy.
- **An out-of-domain long-generation benchmark** (code reasoning) and RULER/NIAH beyond 32K, to check that a math-driven allocation generalizes.

## Questions for the authors
1. What exactly is a "seed" in Prediction 3? How many full allocation-pipeline runs and how many samples per AIME problem will be used, and what is the resulting CI half-width on AIME?
2. Do baselines receive the same total GPU-hours (including your diagnosis and inter-round OPD), or only the same final OPD budget? Is the Opal baseline trained as a dedicated 9/36 student or inside the placement-sampling supernet?
3. Is the PostNAS/DtR-style baseline scored on long-CoT accuracy of student rollouts? If not, why is that not the main competitor?
4. After joint OPD, what is the expected spread across random allocations at K=9? If it is around 1-2 AIME points, what is the maximum attainable gain from any allocation method?
5. Will you commit to a falsifiable prediction about reducing K at matched accuracy? Otherwise the memory benefit claimed in the motivation is never tested.
6. How often does psi_e = PASS at the event position coincide with a recovered final answer when regenerating? Is the repair test predictive at all of trajectory outcome?
