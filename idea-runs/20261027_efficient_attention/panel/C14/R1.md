# R1 review of C14: Verified Repair Sets for full-attention allocation in hybrid reasoning models

## Summary
The submission converts Qwen3-4B (thinking) into a GDN/full-attention hybrid inside a supernet in which every weight except the linear mixers stays frozen at teacher values. It then grows the set of exact-attention layers greedily. At each round it takes wrong-answer rollouts of the current hybrid, keeps the first high-KL token whose prefix the teacher solves more often than the student, uses ddmin to shrink the set of layers that must be restored to exact attention before next-token KL at that token falls below tau, and commits whichever layer or small group passes the most of these "repair tests". Mixer-only on-policy distillation (OPD) runs between rounds, and a coverage threshold picks K. The insight it claims is that allocation should target the failures left after on-policy training, on the student's own prefixes, rather than teacher-forced proxies. The predicted evidence is modest: residual failures have small, recurring repair sets; repairs flip answers more often than random high-KL positions; the method beats two simple on-policy allocators and teacher-forced selectors at matched K by just over benchmark noise; the stopping rule lands near the knee; the ranking survives joint OPD; and retrieval does not regress.

## Strengths
- The gap is real and well located. Every published selector for Transformer-to-hybrid conversion scores layers on teacher-forced text or synthetic probes: one-swap DCLM KL [2512.20569], passkey gates [2606.30562], KL+cost at 4K [2605.20936], perplexity beam search [2603.26556], teacher-trace likelihood [2604.19877], answer NLL [2606.27791], LM loss with L0 masks [2606.18056]. The current best long-CoT conversion keeps every fourth layer by rule [2609.31947]. Super Apriel itself names RL/FA-supervised placement as future work [2604.19877]. Picking the allocation from the hybrid's own long-CoT failures is the obvious next question in this subfield, and nobody has published it yet.
- The teacher-anchored supernet is a clean design choice. Because all-exact equals the teacher exactly, every reduction starts from a passing state, and the method rests on executed tests rather than a fitted surrogate. This avoids the circularity of placement-specialized supernets that Super Apriel warns about [2604.19877].
- The baseline list is unusually honest. It includes the two cheap on-policy allocators that a specialist would ask for first (single-layer on-policy swap score, greedy whole-trace on-policy KL), plus random allocation at matched K. It also gives every baseline the same finishing joint-OPD stage, and it reports cost in GPU-hours.
- The pilot has kill criteria (repair-set size, recurrence, answer-flip vs. random positions), so the paper reports evidence on whether residual long-CoT failures are localized at all. That diagnostic is useful even if the allocation gains are small.
- Choosing K from data is a contribution in its own right. HALO uses L/4 [2601.22156], RAD uses 50% [2505.22135], and the rest use user-set ratios.

## Weaknesses
- [major] At the stated thresholds the headline gain is marginal and the evidence is weak. Prediction 3 only requires beating the simple on-policy allocators by more than noise, quoted as ±4.2 pp for 500 items, which is MATH-500. AIME 2024/2025 have 30 problems each, so a single pass@1 run carries a CI of roughly ±15–17 pp. A gain "just beyond noise" is therefore barely significant on MATH-500 and not significant on AIME, the benchmarks where long-CoT drift matters most. Prediction 4 only asks for "smaller drop on the longest bins" and prediction 6 only that the ranking "is preserved". So the paper that results shows a few-point MATH-500 gain from a pipeline that is much more complex and more expensive than greedy whole-trace on-policy KL. A reviewer who has seen ten layer-selection papers this year will read that as an engineering increment.
- [major] It is not clear which component produces the gain, and the simplest on-policy explanation is not ruled out. The repair test is still a teacher-forced next-token KL, only computed on student-generated prefixes. Greedy whole-trace on-policy KL uses the same supernet and rollouts and optimizes nearly the same quantity, summed over positions instead of taken at the first spike. If the predicted margin is at threshold, the per-event ddmin/PMA machinery adds roughly "localization at one token" over that baseline. The paper needs an ablation that keeps the event positions and drops ddmin: score candidate sets by summed KL at the filtered event positions only. That would show whether the gain comes from repair sets or from event filtering. In addition, the on-policy versions of the existing differentiable selectors (FlashMorph gates [2606.30562], DASH logits [2605.20936], ConSA L0 masks [2606.18056] trained with the OPD reverse-KL objective on student rollouts) are missing. They are the natural "cheap on-policy" competitors, and they also model layer interactions jointly.
- [major] The software-engineering framing is heavier than the mechanism needs. With at most 36 candidate layers and median repair sets the pilot predicts to be small, ddmin with revisit-to-fixpoint amounts to backward elimination with caching, and PMA-gated skipping saves time on a search that is already cheap next to OPD. Monotonicity-violation logs, unclear bands and three orderings add hyperparameters (kappa, tau, band, delta, k, s_min, m), and the ablation list has to cover all of them. Reviewers will see the vocabulary as dressing on activation patching. The DAgger grounding is decorative too: the u·T·epsilon bound does not justify greedy layer commitment.
- [major] The search runs on a model that does not ship. Allocation is chosen in a frozen-MLP, frozen-exact-path supernet trained with placement sampling across sizes. The deployed model is then jointly trained with OPD (Opal Stage 3 [2609.31947]), which changes MLPs and exact layers, so the residual failures being repaired belong to a weaker, differently trained model. Prediction 6 at threshold says the ranking is preserved but the margin may shrink to almost nothing. A reader cannot tell whether the method found better layers or found layers that compensate for the frozen supernet's specific deficits.
- [minor] The event filter is noisy. Teacher and student success rates come from k=4 continuations each, with delta=0.5, on 32K-token prefixes. That costs a lot of generation per candidate and still gives a coarse estimate, and the answer-flip test compares against matched random high-KL positions, not against later high-KL positions in the same trace. The "first spike" heuristic assumes the first large disagreement is causal. In long CoT the decisive error is often a later retrieval of an earlier value, which is exactly the illustrative scenario in the proposal.
- [minor] The scope is narrow for a NeurIPS allocation paper. Allocation is whole-layer only, while head-level hybrids (HydraHead, Head-wise Hybrid) and head-wise ConSA [2606.18056] report clear gains over layer-wise allocation at the same budget. There is one linear mixer (GDN), math/science prompts only, and generation up to 32K. The KV-cache argument is strongest at 64K–128K, where retained layers dominate memory, yet nothing is evaluated there.
- [minor] The total cost is large and not compared with spending the same compute on weights. Supernet warm-up, mixer-only OPD at up to 32K across sampled placements, 5–9 diagnosis rounds and 5–30B prefill tokens add up. Baselines get the same OPD budget but not the diagnosis GPU-hours. There should be a control in which Opal's every-fourth-layer model gets the diagnosis GPU-hours as extra OPD.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2604.19877] Super Apriel: frozen-shared-weight supernet, placement search scored on MATH500/AIME reasoning traces, explicit layer-interaction surrogate solved by DP. It overlaps on supernet design and on reasoning-driven placement. It does not kill novelty, because its score is the likelihood of teacher traces, it has no on-policy stage and no per-failure localization, and it names RL-based placement as future work. Reviewers will still treat C14 as "Super Apriel + on-policy scoring", so the delta has to be shown empirically, with Super Apriel scoring applied to student rollouts as an ablation.
- [2609.31947] Opal: same base models, same OPD recipe, fixed every-fourth-layer allocation. C14 is built directly on it. No novelty conflict, but beating it at matched K is the main bar, and Opal already recovers 83–93% of teacher math, so headroom is limited after joint OPD.
- [2512.20569] KL-guided layer selection: one-swap restoration plus brief re-distillation, scored by KL. It is the teacher-forced, single-layer ancestor of the repair test. Its mechanism is close; C14 differs in using on-policy prefixes and joint sets. Not a kill.
- [2606.30562] FlashMorph / [2605.20936] DASH / [2606.18056] ConSA: joint, differentiable allocation under a budget, using passkey hidden-state loss, KL+cost and LM loss respectively. They already handle layer interactions jointly, which weakens C14's "static single-layer scores miss interactions" motivation. Not a kill, but trained with an on-policy loss they are the missing strong baseline.
- [2505.22135] RAD: picks layers to replace using a generation-based signal, the self-speculative acceptance/throughput of the teacher with layers skipped, optimized by BO over whole configurations. It is the closest earlier use of "the model's own generations" for layer selection. But the draft is the teacher, not the trained hybrid, generations are 128 tokens, selection happens once before distillation and the ratio is fixed at 50%. Partial overlap with the "generation-aware selection" claim; C14 should cite it and narrow its "every criterion is teacher-forced" sentence.
- [2508.15884] PostNAS / [2601.11667] DtR: supernet or drop-in blocks searched by task accuracy (an outcome signal) with a given budget or a tolerance stop. DtR's tolerance stop overlaps with "K from data". Not a kill.
- [2606.27791] NLL-guided selection: single-layer toggles scored by answer NLL on Qwen3-4B. A teacher-forced single-swap analogue. Not a kill.
- [2610.18104] Layer-ordering theory: shows the optimal hybrid ordering is task-specific and that containing a motif (TS, TST) decides performance. It supports C14's premise that allocation should be chosen against the target task and that layers act as sets. It is analysis only, so no overlap.
- Net: I found no paper that chooses hybrid allocation from on-policy residual failures, whether through per-event repair sets or through whole-trace on-policy scoring. The novelty is real but incremental: a new selection signal in a crowded design space, not a new capability.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- On-policy differentiable allocation: FlashMorph-, DASH- or ConSA-style gates or logits trained with the same OPD reverse-KL on student rollouts under a budget penalty, in the same supernet. This is the strongest cheap alternative, and it is missing.
- Super Apriel scoring applied to student rollouts (on-policy likelihood plus the same interaction surrogate). It separates "on-policy data" from "repair-set search".
- Event-localized KL without ddmin: score candidates by KL at the filtered event positions only. It separates localization from the set-reduction machinery.
- A compute-matched control: Opal every-fourth-layer and the best teacher-forced selector given the diagnosis GPU-hours as extra OPD.
- Low-variance AIME: avg@32 or avg@64 per problem with seeds, rather than pass@1 against a ±4.2 pp threshold borrowed from a 500-item set.
- Allocation stability: Jaccard of selected sets across seeds, prompt pools and orderings. If the selected layers change with the seed, the method is fitting noise.
- Evaluation at 64K–128K generation or context, where retained-layer KV dominates memory, plus head-level allocation as an extension or explicit comparison (HydraHead / head-wise ConSA).
- Transfer of the chosen allocation to a non-math domain (code, agentic long traces), to test whether reasoning-driven allocation is domain-specific.

## Questions for the authors
1. With predictions at threshold, how large is the gap to greedy whole-trace on-policy KL on AIME under avg@k evaluation, and is it significant across seeds? If it is not, what is left of the repair-set contribution?
2. How often do repaired events actually change the final answer under regeneration, compared with repairing the second or third high-KL position in the same trace instead of the first?
3. How much does the allocation change between the frozen supernet and a supernet in which the MLPs are also trained (your ablation 7)? If the two differ substantially, which one predicts the ranking after joint OPD?
4. Is the stopping rule stable to s_min and m, or does K move by more than one or two layers across reasonable settings?
5. What is the measured wall-clock split between supernet OPD, event mining (k=4 continuations from 32K prefixes), and ddmin diagnosis?
6. Do selected layers cluster (e.g., contiguous middle blocks as in [2606.18056]) or spread out, and do they overlap with retrieval-head-dense layers?
