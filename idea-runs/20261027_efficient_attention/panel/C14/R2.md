# R2 (generalist) review of C14: Verified Repair Sets

## Summary
The paper converts Qwen3-4B (and MiMo-7B-RL) into Gated DeltaNet hybrids inside a supernet. In that supernet the shared and exact-attention weights are frozen at the teacher's values, so the "all exact" setting is the teacher itself. Only the linear mixers are trained, with Opal-style on-policy distillation. The method then picks which layers keep full attention, and how many, from the failures the student still makes on its own long-CoT rollouts. Each failure is the position of the first sharp teacher/student KL spike on a wrong-answer trace, kept only if teacher continuations from there succeed more often. Each failure is shrunk by delta debugging (ddmin with PMA-gated skips) to a 1-minimal set of layers whose restoration brings next-token KL back under tau. The layers that repair the most events in direct tests are committed one at a time, with retraining and fresh rollouts between commits. The claimed insight is that allocation should be judged against the failures that on-policy training leaves behind, not against teacher-forced proxies on fixed text. The predicted evidence, with every prediction landing at its threshold:
- Repair sets are median at most about a third of the candidate layers, with some layer group fixing more than 10% of events.
- Repaired events flip answers more often than random high-KL positions.
- At matched K the method beats single-swap and greedy whole-trace on-policy baselines just beyond about ±4.2 pp benchmark noise.
- Its layer sets differ from teacher-forced selections and do slightly better on the longest traces.
- The stopping rule lands near the knee of a K sweep.
- The ranking survives joint OPD, and retrieval does not regress.

## Strengths
- The problem is real and timely. Converting Transformers to hybrids for reasoning is an active area at NeurIPS. In a hybrid, the KV cache of the retained full-attention layers is the only memory that grows with length. Opal itself still fixes allocation by rule (every fourth layer) [2609.31947]. Every selector I checked scores layers on teacher-forced text, synthetic probes or short benchmarks [2512.20569][2606.30562][2606.27791][2604.22050][2604.19877].
- The headline idea is crisp and easy to remember: choose the architecture against the failures the trained student still makes, on its own prefixes. SWARR's finding that on-policy RL changes the conclusion one draws from conversion plus SFT about an architecture [2606.11634] supports this premise from the outside.
- Freezing the exact paths and shared weights makes the all-exact supernet equal to the teacher. This is a neat design trick: every reduction starts from a passing state by construction, and the repair predicate is deterministic. It is a real improvement over Super Apriel's "close to the teacher" all-FA placement [2604.19877].
- The proposal is honest about its weak points. It has a pilot kill criterion and an answer-flip filter against benign forks. Cost is reported in GPU-hours. The baselines include the two simple on-policy ones (single-swap on-policy score, greedy whole-trace on-policy KL), which are the right tests of whether the ddmin machinery earns its keep.
- Measuring the frozen and jointly trained models and asking whether the ranking survives targets the right question.

## Weaknesses
- [major] **At threshold, the gain in the model that actually ships is close to zero.**
  - The predictions that matter for deployment are #6 (the ranking survives joint OPD) and #4 (long-length bins are "better" at equal K). At threshold both hold with a margin that is only just above nothing.
  - The one prediction with a stated margin (#3, beyond about ±4.2 pp) is measured in the frozen, mixer-only supernet. That is a training regime nobody ships.
  - There is outside evidence that on-policy training lets a model route around the limits of its architecture. With RL and no full-attention layer at all, pure sliding-window attention gets within 0.4 pp of full attention at 1.5B and within 1 pp at 4B on math, because the policy learns more local trajectories [2606.11634]. Opal's every-fourth-layer rule already recovers 83-93% of the teacher [2609.31947].
  - So the most likely paper is "a large gain in a frozen supernet that mostly disappears after the training you would actually run". A generalist reader will ask why the allocation matters at all.
- [major] **The evaluation has little statistical power where the headroom is.**
  - MATH-500 in thinking mode is already 92-93.8% for Opal's 9/36 hybrid [2609.31947], so there is not room for a gain beyond ±4.2 pp at K=9.
  - AIME 2024/2025 has 30 problems per year. Unless the authors average over many samples (avg@32 or more), the bootstrap CI is wider than any plausible allocation effect.
  - Prediction #3 could therefore land at threshold only at small K or in the frozen model, which is not where the paper's story lives. The proposal does not say which K values or how many samples per problem carry the claim.
- [major] **The method's own thresholds do not fit together.**
  - The pilot passes as long as the median repair set is at most one third of the candidate layers (about 12 of 36). The allocation loop stops as soon as the median repair set exceeds 4 layers.
  - So at the pilot threshold, the loop stops in round 1 with almost nothing committed.
  - Either repair sets are in fact small, and the kill criterion is too lax to be informative, or "localized failures" (pillar 1) is not what the pilot measures.
  - Relatedly, "K from data" swaps one hand-set knob (K) for three (s_min = 5%, m = 4, tau with its unclear band). The claim that K is set from data needs a sensitivity analysis showing K is stable across these settings, not just one point near the knee.
- [major] **The method is heavy relative to the claimed delta, and the subfield is crowded.**
  - In one year: KL-guided selection [2512.20569], FlashMorph [2606.30562], DASH, HALO, DtR [2601.11667], NLL-guided selection [2606.27791], LayerBoost [2604.22050], Super Apriel [2604.19877]. A reviewer who has seen these will read this as "another layer selector, now with an on-policy loop and ddmin".
  - The machinery is large: 5-9 rounds of OPD at up to 32K rollouts, 5-30B prefill tokens of diagnosis, 3 orderings per event, PMA confidence, a revisit fixpoint and an answer-flip filter with 8 continuations per event.
  - If the win over greedy whole-trace on-policy KL is only just past noise in the frozen model, the simpler baseline will be what people adopt. The ddmin/PMA layer will read as vocabulary imported from software engineering rather than a needed mechanism.
- [major] **The insight is diluted by mechanism layering.**
  - The memorable claim is "allocate on on-policy residual failures". The paper's identity is "verified repair sets via delta debugging".
  - These are separable, and the evaluation (the ablation against greedy whole-trace on-policy KL) could show that the first carries everything.
  - The story only holds together if the joint, non-monotone repair sets are shown to matter. That needs the monotonicity-violation rate to be substantial, and the proposal does not predict a value for it.
- [minor] **Layer-level granularity may look dated.**
  - Head-level hybrids match a 3:1 layer-wise hybrid's long-context performance at 7:1 (HydraHead, abstract only, not read).
  - Task-specific ordering theory suggests the right allocation depends strongly on the workload [2610.18104].
  - Whole-layer allocation for one math-heavy workload on two models of 4B and 7B is a narrow empirical base.
- [minor] **The repair predicate is next-token KL at one position.**
  - That is still a local, teacher-relative signal, now on student prefixes. The answer-flip check only filters events; it is not the repair criterion. The paper should show that "KL repaired" and "answer repaired" agree.
- [minor] **Training cost is not compared at matched compute.**
  - The diagnosis compute could instead be spent on more OPD for Opal's fixed allocation. Without a compute-matched comparison, the cost-benefit claim is open.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2609.31947] Opal: same models, same on-policy reverse-KL training, same benchmarks. It fixes allocation at every fourth layer. This is the base the paper builds on, and it does not kill novelty, since Opal never uses the on-policy signal for allocation. It also sets the ceiling on headroom (83-93% of the teacher already).
- [2604.19877] Super Apriel: a supernet with frozen shared weights, placement scoring on math reasoning traces, and an interaction surrogate. It overlaps on the supernet design and the reasoning-targeted placement. Its scores are teacher-forced likelihood of teacher traces, with no on-policy stage. It does not kill novelty, but it narrows it to "on-policy plus per-event repair sets". It also names RL-with-FA-supervision for placement as future work, so a follow-up from that group is plausible concurrent work.
- [2601.11667] DtR: greedy outcome-driven replacement with a tolerance stop, which already sets K from data by a whole-task tolerance and already shows that static scores miss interactions. It overlaps the "K from data" and "interactions" arguments. It does not kill novelty (short few-shot tasks, no on-policy stage, no per-failure localization).
- [2512.20569], [2606.30562], [2606.27791], [2604.22050]: teacher-forced or benchmark-ablation layer selectors (DCLM KL, passkey gates, answer NLL, commonsense ablation) with hand-set budgets and no long-CoT evaluation. LayerBoost explicitly lists long-form reasoning as a blind spot. No kill; these are the baselines.
- [2606.11634] SWARR: no allocation method, so no overlap in mechanism. It weakens the significance case: on-policy RL lets even zero-full-attention SWA models nearly match full attention on math, which suggests allocation differences shrink after on-policy training.
- [2610.18104] Layer-ordering theory: no method overlap. It supports the premise that the best allocation depends on the task.
- [2307.15771] Hydra effect: the motivation for non-monotone repair. Not a competitor.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- **Does allocation matter after the training that ships?** The spread of accuracy across several random allocations at the same K after joint OPD. If random allocations land within noise of each other, the problem does not matter at that K. This is the cheapest test of significance and it is missing.
- **A K sweep of Opal's uniform interleave with joint OPD** (K = 3, 4, 6, 9, 12) as the reference curve. A uniform-interleave curve is the natural "knee" baseline that the stopping rule must beat.
- **A compute-matched Opal**: the diagnosis plus inter-round OPD GPU-hours spent instead on more OPD of the fixed every-fourth-layer allocation.
- **A SWA or zero-full-attention on-policy control** in the spirit of [2606.11634], to show how much of the gap any allocation can still close.
- **AIME with avg@k for large k** (at least 32) and per-length-bin CIs. The headline comparison should be at small K (4-6 of 36), where headroom exists.
- **A reported monotonicity-violation rate**, plus a direct comparison of "always trust monotonicity" (a plain hitting set) against ddmin on final accuracy, not just on repair-set size.
- **Agreement between KL repair and answer repair** on a sample of events.
- **A head-level selector baseline** (e.g., retrieval-head selection [2602.11374]) at matched KV memory, since the cost is memory, not layer count.

## Questions for the authors
1. With A starting empty, how many OPD rounds at what rollout length does a full allocation take, and what is the total GPU-hour cost compared with Opal's 2B-token OPD?
2. How do you reconcile the pilot pass threshold (median at most L/3) with the stopping rule (median above 4 means stop)? What median repair-set size do you actually expect?
3. At which K do you expect the frozen-model gain over greedy whole-trace on-policy KL to exceed noise, and on which benchmark, given that MATH-500 is near ceiling?
4. If joint OPD shrinks the gap between allocations to about 1 pp, what is the paper's claim?
5. How sensitive is the chosen K to s_min, m and tau? Does K move by more than 2 layers across reasonable settings?
6. Do the selected layers transfer from Qwen3-4B to MiMo-7B in relative depth, or must the whole loop be rerun per model?
