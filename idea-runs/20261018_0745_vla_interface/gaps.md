
## Axis: language sub-task grounding mismatch between controller and pretrained VLA

### What has been done
Query trail: "VLA language instruction paraphrase robustness", "rephrasing instructions vision-language-action", "hierarchical VLM VLA language subtask", "planner alignment low-level policy", "VLM planner VLA executable subtask", "instruction optimization VLA prompt", "test-time instruction VLA", "VLA instruction grounding referring expression", plus a recency pass since 2025-10 ("language steering VLA", "VLA command translator", "executability VLA instruction", "VLM controller frozen VLA").

- **Static, offline-distilled rewriting (policy frozen, once per episode).** [2610.10526] scores phrasings of 8 evidence tasks by rollouts (36 per phrase) or a gripper-error proxy (r=0.54 with success), has an LLM distill 10–20 rules, and rewrites each instruction once before the robot moves; the applier does not see the image. It transfers zero-shot to unseen SIMPLER tasks (+16–27% relative), but its own data show context-dependent effects no static rule fits ("purple" +61 on one task, −30 on another), and on π0.5/LIBERO out-of-finetune tasks it leaves success unchanged though oracle phrasing reaches 66 vs 52.
- **Learned translators trained on rollout reward.** [2607.04517] trains a Qwen3.5-9B command policy q(ũ | o_0, u) with GRPO on sparse task success; it emits one colour/shape/position-grounded command fixed for the whole rollout. It costs 76,800 rollouts (83 h on 2×H100) for π0, and gains shrink where distractors, wrong object grounding or long horizons dominate. [2606.31958] (SARL) learns a per-step semantic Q(s, ℓ) by TD over VLM-proposed commands. It decomposes long-horizon LIBERO-10 tasks and picks executable phrasings (spatial moves instead of naming an OOD "hammer"), but it is learned per task from 60–100 online episodes. [2606.12299] distills rollout-evaluated local perturbations of narrated language sequences into a closed-loop per-step feedback policy, with a conformal improvement head ψ(o_0, ℓ) that falls back to the original instruction (refusing 43–58% of cases in simulation).
- **Hierarchical planner–policy alignment.** [2510.14968] segments demonstrations so sub-task intervals visually match the low-level policy's training intervals (DP over retrieval similarity). The sub-task text is still generated free-form by Gemini, so only granularity is aligned, not phrasing, and no policy feedback is used.
- **Test-time ensembling over phrasings.** [2610.19438] executes the medoid action over K paraphrases; it never helps (paraphrases are non-exchangeable conditionals with their own biases). Paraphrase action disagreement is nonetheless the only failure signal informative from the first replan (risk-set AUROC ~0.6), and it is used only as a monitor.
- **Diagnostics of what the VLA can be told.** [2610.17746] shows that attribute/spatial grounding-dependent references are relatively cheap for π0.5/π0-FAST/Cosmos3-Edge. Undecomposed events cost the most, and even fully resolved atomic commands depend on invocation state: resetting to home before each sub-task gives +11.8 for π0.5. [2610.06235] shows that an in-scene target substitution in the instruction redirects π0.5/GR00T in only 0.5–13% of trials (86.5–98.3% go to the scene-associated original target), with instruction tokens contributing ~26% (LIBERO) down to ~2% (RoboTwin) of the action expert's input.

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Controller-side methods learn which sub-task phrasings a frozen VLA will execute only from outcome-level task success of complete rollouts: GRPO over one command per episode, per-task TD, Monte-Carlo improvement estimates, or rollout-scored rule evidence. This outcome-only signal leaves the executability of a given phrasing at a given state unobserved until an episode ends. The method assumption challenged is that executability must be learned from closed-loop episode reward rather than from the VLA's own immediate response to a candidate command.
  - **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2607.04517] — GRPO-trained grounding translator; comes closest to a reusable controller that knows the VLA's executable vocabulary, but needs 76,800 rollouts / 83 h on 2×H100 for π0, and its credit attaches to the whole command over the whole rollout. [2606.31958] — semantic Q-learning over prompts; assigns per-step credit by TD, but per task from 60–100 online episodes, so nothing transfers to unseen tasks. [2606.12299] — rollout-estimated Δ distilled by rejection fine-tuning; local search in one training environment. [2610.10526] — offline rule distillation; its only non-rollout signal (gripper error against ground-truth demos) correlates at r=0.54 and is blamed for its weak in-finetune rulebooks. [2610.19438] — the one method that reads the VLA's pre-execution response to phrasings (action disagreement across paraphrases). That signal is informative from the first replan, but the method uses it only to aggregate actions, which fails, and never to choose what the controller says. The shared deficiency across the five read papers is that the executability signal is either sparse episode reward or a demo-dependent proxy.
  - **Methodological deficiency:** Credit-assignment flaw. A per-command decision is credited with a delayed, binary episode outcome that mixes language misgrounding with motor and perception failures. [2607.04517] Sec. 4.5 notes that group-relative reward gives no signal between commands that alias to the same behaviour, and Sec. 6.2 notes that distractors and execution errors swamp the language effect on RL4VLA. [2606.31958] cannot transfer its Q to new tasks. None of the methods exploits the immediate, observation-conditioned action response of the VLA, available in one forward pass before execution, as a dense per-command learning signal.
  - **Why it matters:** A team with 24 GB GPUs and a ≤40 GPU-day budget cannot pay tens of thousands of rollouts per VLA–benchmark pair. Without a cheap per-command signal, a small controller cannot be adapted to a new pretrained VLA's language interface, so the frontier model stays in the loop. A dense, task-transferable executability signal would let a controller learn a VLA's executable command space cheaply and reuse it on unseen tasks.
  - **Evidence route:** On held-out tasks and phrasings (e.g. LIBERO-Goal/LIBERO-Para, SimplerEnv), compare success against the number of VLA rollouts consumed to train the controller, versus a GRPO translator and a rollout-distilled rulebook. Also report the within-task correlation between the new signal and per-phrase rollout success, against the r=0.54 demo-based proxy, without ground-truth demos.
- **Gap:** Transferable controller-to-VLA translators treat whether a sub-task command will be executed as a property of the phrasing (static rules) or of the initial observation with one command per episode (q(ũ | o_0, u); improvement head ψ(o_0, ℓ)). In long-horizon sub-task sequences, though, the same correct atomic command succeeds or fails depending on the current invocation state and on whether the scene prior overrides the named target. The only state-conditioned method learns this per task. The method assumption challenged is that executability can be represented without conditioning on the state at which each sub-task is issued.
  - **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2607.04517] — conditions on o_0 only and holds one command for the full rollout; limits stated for long-horizon and distractor cases. [2606.12299] — closed-loop per-step feedback beats open-loop rewriting (75.0 vs 71.3), but its steer-or-not decision ψ uses only o_0, it refuses 43–58% of cases, and on refusal it can only revert to the original instruction. [2606.31958] — state-conditioned Q(s, ℓ) solves long-horizon LIBERO-10 tasks, but it is learned per task and has no transfer. [2610.10526] — rules fixed per phrase; its "purple" +61/−30 evidence shows context dependence it cannot express. [2510.14968] — aligns sub-task boundaries to training data but not the text or the state at which each sub-task is issued. Evidence that state matters: [2610.17746] (home-reset before each atomic command: +11.8 for π0.5) and [2610.06235] (86.5–98.3% of trials ignore an in-scene target swap).
  - **Methodological deficiency:** Representation limitation. The executability model has the wrong arguments: phrase-only (rules) or (o_0, instruction) (Grounder, ψ), rather than (sub-task command, current observation/proprioceptive state). It therefore cannot predict, at each controller call, that a correct command will fail from this state, or that no wording will redirect a scene-prior-dominated VLA ([2610.06235] Sec. 4.1, 6.1). It has no way to say what to do instead beyond reverting to the original instruction ([2606.12299] Sec. 4.1, 5.3). SARL shows that state conditioning helps, but its per-task Q is not a transferable representation ([2606.31958] Sec. 6).
  - **Why it matters:** Long-horizon and OOD benchmarks issue many sub-tasks from states produced by earlier sub-tasks. This is exactly where [2610.17746] finds a residual execution gap after the language is fully resolved, and where [2607.04517] reports its gains vanish. A controller that knows, per state, whether its sub-task command is executable could re-phrase, re-pose or change the cue only when needed, turning the challenge's "controllers do not know which phrasings a VLA can execute" into a predictable, per-call decision.
  - **Evidence route:** On multi-step suites (LIBERO-10/LIBERO-Long, CALVIN-style chains), measure per-sub-task success conditioned on invocation state, and compare per-call executability prediction accuracy (AUROC versus realized sub-task success) for state-conditioned and o_0-only or phrase-only predictors. Then report end-task success and refusal/fallback rates against [2606.12299]-style o_0 gating and episode-level rewriting on held-out tasks.


## Axis: non-language intermediate representations (points, traces, goal images) for conditioning pretrained VLAs

### What has been done
Query trail: "VLA point conditioning", "trajectory sketch conditioned policy", "goal image conditioned VLA", "visual prompting VLA", "hierarchical VLA trace", "RT-Trajectory", "HAMSTER hierarchical", plus a recency pass since 2025-06 ("training-free visual prompt VLA", "frozen VLA visual prompt", "VLA point prompt zero-shot", "goal image pretrained VLA", "VLM planner points VLA", "pointing interface VLA", "Steerable Policies", "LoHo-Manip", "IA-VLA").

- **Input-side cue, VLA retrained to read it.** [2603.22003] draws a crosshair and a placement box on an extra overhead image. It trains the whole VLA with a key-frame coordinate-classification grounding loss (46 h on 8×H200). Without that loss the prompt is "perceived but not associated with action" (53.8 → 49.4), and a bare point is treated "as visual perturbation" (47.3). [2412.10345] overlays the robot's own CoTracker history traces and needs 5 more epochs on 150k annotated trajectories; fine-tuning on the same data without traces gives no gain. [2608.28108] compares RGB box/fade rendering with additive mask-channel fusion before or after the vision encoder, on π0 with matched steps. All of them need two-stage full fine-tuning, and single-stage training erases the zero-shot gain. [2609.11308] trains a flow-matching Action Model on language plus optional grasp, place and move points, with hint dropout, noise and temporal jitter. [2602.13193] writes points and traces as pixel coordinates inside the language command and retrains OpenVLA/π0.5 on ~2M synthetic commands. [2502.05485] fine-tunes a 13B VLM to emit RDP-simplified 2D paths and trains 3D low-level policies from scratch on path overlays.
- **Input-side cue, light injection into a mostly frozen VLA.** [2609.07006] generates one goal image per episode and adds ZeroConv(F_goal − F_obs) to the observation embedding, training only the projector, the zero-conv and the action head for one epoch. Cross-attention injection collapses to ~0, and goal-only without language gives 0%.
- **Input-side cue, training-free on a frozen VLA.** [2512.20014] tints the tracked instance mask (α=0.5) and rewrites "my X" to "the <tint-colour> X". Its gains depend on that coupling: mask-only gives 5.2–7.3% SR, against 62.7–83.5% for mask+rewrite.
- **Output-side steering of a frozen generative action head.** [2609.38989] maps a target-centroid heatmap to a diagonal affine modulation of π0.5's flow velocity at every integration step. The adapter (0.02% of parameters) is trained with spatial-shift and residual contrastive losses on the same 80 demos. [2610.10534] uses a 0.77B translator trained on 500 same-robot play trajectories to turn points and traces into an action draft, then blends it into the base policy's denoising at a level γ that rises with chunk index.
- **Controller choosing which cue to send.** [2602.13193] lets Gemini choose the command style (point, trace, motion, sub-task) in context, re-queried every 20 steps.

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Methods that condition a pretrained VLA on points, traces, masks or goal images do so by inserting the cue into the VLA's input pathway: pixel overlay, extra image, mask channel, coordinate text or a zero-conv feature residual. They fine-tune the backbone on demonstrations in which the cue sits on the demonstrated target. This leaves the cue-to-action-target binding as a learned co-occurrence rather than an explicit mechanism. The challenged assumption is that a cue entering through perception will bind to the action target once enough cue-annotated demonstrations are seen. On frozen or lightly tuned VLAs, the cue is used only when it is re-expressed through a language token the VLA already grounds. Under unseen targets and layouts, fine-tuned readers fall back to the scene prior and grasp the distractor.
  - **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2512.20014] — training-free mask tint on a frozen VLA. It comes closest to cue-conditioning without training, but works only through a colour word: mask-only collapses to 5.2–7.3% SR, and point, trace-arrow and numbered-marker prompts stay at 28–36% against 83.5% (App. E.1, E.4). [2603.22003] — full retraining plus an auxiliary grounding loss. Without the loss the cue is "perceived but not associated with action". Under the strongest shift (unseen object + novel language), its π0.5 re-implementation in [2609.38989] reaches 4.2% and 8.3%. [2608.28108] — four input-fusion variants under matched training. Spatial-ZS gains stay single-digit for three of four methods because the policy "select[s] a visually identical distractor bowl despite valid masks", and single-stage training reduces Object-ZS gains to ~0. [2609.07006] — zero-conv goal-image injection. Goal-only gives 0%, and the method "enhances rather than creates" capability. [2602.13193] and [2412.10345] need full retraining on large relabelled corpora, and [2602.13193] states the approach cannot be applied to narrow datasets such as LIBERO. The deficiency repeats across six read papers: the cue is read only as far as backbone training on cue-target co-occurrence makes it salient. The one route that sidesteps input reading ([2609.38989], output-side) is a learned adapter tied to its own 80 demos. Its absolute LIBERO-PRO Object success is 15.5% against 10.5% for the base, so it does not close this gap.
  - **Methodological deficiency:** Wrong inductive bias / missing mechanism. Input-level injection gives no mechanism that ties the cue's image location to the action target, so the VLA's existing language-to-object attention decides whether the cue matters. [2609.38989] Sec. 1 says this directly: "after being encoded with the rest of the visual observation, the prompt is only implicitly represented, without a direct mechanism that encourages the generated trajectory to align with the System 2 guidance". The digests show the consequence. A frozen VLA follows a tint only when a colour word routes its language grounding to it ([2512.20014] Table 14). Fine-tuned readers learn cue-reading that does not transfer to new spatial layouts without a two-stage curriculum ([2608.28108] Sec. IV-B1–B2). A light goal-image injection cannot act without the language channel ([2609.07006] Table 6).
  - **Why it matters:** The problem setting fixes the VLA and forbids large-scale retraining. Every read method that reliably uses a non-language cue either retrains the VLA on 10⁵–10⁶ annotated samples on 8×H100/H200-class hardware, or restricts the cue to "which instance" by translating it back into a colour word. Today a 24 GB budget therefore cannot use points, traces or goal images to fix the challenge's failure ("second mug from the left" → nearest mug). Closing the gap would let a small controller pass where to act to an unmodified VLA. That would give a test of whether pretrained VLAs carry a reusable spatial target representation that the cue can address.
  - **Evidence route:** On a frozen or LoRA-only π0/π0.5 or OpenVLA-OFT checkpoint, under target swaps and layout shifts (LIBERO-PRO/LIBERO-Plus Object and Spatial, SimplerEnv variant aggregation, distractor-lookalike scenes), measure the fraction of episodes whose first contact lands on the cued object. Do this for point, trace and goal-image cues, and compare against mask-tint+colour-word, overlay-plus-grounding-loss and zero-conv injection baselines at matched training compute. Report cue-following when the cue contradicts the scene prior, the regime where [2608.28108] and [2609.38989] show input-side readers fail.
- **Gap:** Output-side methods steer a frozen diffusion or flow-matching VLA by converting a point or trace into a modulation of the action-generation process: an affine velocity modulation in [2609.38989], a noise-level blend of an action draft in [2610.10534]. They set how strongly the cue overrides the VLA's own action prior with a single global, state-independent strength (a constant learned magnitude under one regulariser weight, or a fixed γ schedule indexed by chunk count). The challenged assumption is that one open-loop strength fits a whole rollout. It over-modulates near contact, where the prior should dominate, and under-steers when the prior heads to the wrong object. Neither method has any signal for when to hand control back.
  - **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2609.38989] — cue-conditioned affine steering of π0.5's velocity. Its own failure analysis says "the steering strength is applied consistently throughout the rollout, which can over-modulate … overshooting or unstable grasping" (App. D). Its λ_reg sweep peaks at a single setting (11.5/15.5/12.5/10.5 SR) because one global trade-off between over- and under-steering is being tuned. [2610.10534] — Progressive Biased Denoising with γ^m = min(k^0 + mΔk, N)/N. The hand-off schedule depends only on chunk index, and its denoising-dynamics study shows the denoising level is a non-linear control knob (Fig. 8). It relies on a human to decide when to re-prompt. [2502.05485] — the low-level policy "is not explicitly constrained to strictly follow the predicted trajectory", and 72% of RVT-2 failures are adherence failures. [2512.20014] — fixed tint opacity with a saliency–occlusion trade-off (App. E.4). In all four, cue authority is a fixed hyperparameter, never computed online from the policy's behaviour.
  - **Methodological deficiency:** Credit-assignment flaw between cue and prior. The steering magnitude is never conditioned on whether the frozen VLA's own proposed action chunk already agrees with the cue. That agreement is available before execution in both methods: [2609.38989] has the unsteered velocity v^τ, and [2610.10534] has the γ=1 base action. Correction strength therefore cannot be assigned to the steps where the prior disagrees with the cue (target selection) and withdrawn where it agrees or where precise contact needs the prior. The documented failures are overshoot and unstable grasps ([2609.38989] App. D) and the non-linear, schedule-dependent hand-off ([2610.10534] Sec. IV Q3).
  - **Why it matters:** Output-side steering is the only read route in which a frozen VLA follows a spatial cue under unseen object and novel language (66.7% against 4.2% for input prompting in [2609.38989] Table 2). It is also the cheapest to build on 24 GB GPUs. It is capped by the arbitration problem, though: gains vanish in simulation (15.5% on LIBERO-PRO) and contact-rich phases are where it breaks. Per-step cue/prior arbitration would turn a demo-specific trick into a controller-driven interface. A cheap controller could then point once, and the VLA would follow only as far as it needs correcting.
  - **Evidence route:** On frozen π0/π0.5 in LIBERO-PRO/LIBERO-Plus and SimplerEnv with lookalike distractors, log the per-step divergence between the unsteered and steered action chunks alongside outcomes. Compare (i) cued-target selection rate and (ii) grasp/placement failure rate after correct targeting against constant-strength affine steering and fixed-schedule noise blending at their best global settings. A closing method should improve both at once, rather than trading one for the other along a single λ or γ sweep as the read papers do.
