### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=novelty)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [2602.12281] (CoVer-VLA, Feb 2026, not cited by C1 or gaps.md) already treats the frozen VLA as a listener whose immediate response picks what to say: at every chunk boundary it samples M chunks from π0/π0.5 under each of K VLM rephrasings, scores them with a verifier, and makes the best-scoring rephrase the active prompt (Sec. 4.1, Alg. 2), with no rollouts or episode outcomes. This is in-domain sample-and-rerank RSA. It falsifies Gap 1's premise, as worded, that no method learns executability "from the VLA's own immediate response to a candidate command", and C1's novelty paragraph has to be rewritten against it.
  - What C1 still adds over [2602.12281] is real but has to become the headline. (a) The listener score is target-referenced: the controller knows the referent, and the score is whether the predicted reach lands on it. CoVer's verifier instead scores alignment with the original *language* (Sec. 4.2), so it must itself resolve "second mug from the left" and inherits the same grounding problem. Its training costs about 1.3×10^20 FLOPs, 3.8× π0 (App. 8.6), which is out of budget here. (b) Amortization: C1 emits one command, while CoVer pays K×M samples per chunk (~453 ms for 16 candidates, Sec. 5.6). (c) C1 issues per-sub-task controller commands, while CoVer has one boot-time rephrase set per episode. Point (b) alone is a thin delta. A 2027 panel will read C1 as "CoVer with a geometric verifier, distilled" unless (a) is shown to fix the wrong-referent case that a language-conditioned verifier cannot.
  - The source grounding checks out for [2609.14207] and [2006.00418]. The process listener beats the outcome listener ([2609.14207] App. C.3.1 control). The amortized speaker on a graded L0 matches sample-rerank RSA, and REINFORCE on binary success fails ([2006.00418] Results). C1 states both correctly.
  - The mapping of [2110.05422] is wrong. That paper's safeguard is a population of independently trained listeners, and it reports that a *dropout* population (stochasticity within one model) does not close the train/held-out gap. C1's "population" is several flow/diffusion samples from the same VLA, which is the within-model case that failed, and those samples share the model's biases (cf. [2610.19438]'s shared-bias point). They give a graded score, which is legitimate as the [2006.00418] L0 probability, but not the anti-drift safeguard. A real population would be several checkpoints (e.g. π0 / π0.5 / a LoRA variant).
  - The key transfer assumption is stated and partly supported. [2610.06235] shows that the committed approach largely decides which object is manipulated, and that action deviation alone is not target-directed, which is the right caveat. The same paper shows that in scene-prior-dominated layouts no in-scene rename redirects π0.5 (0.5–13%). There the speaker's reward is flat across all commands, and C1's correct fallback is "flag non-executable", not a better phrase.
  - Contribution type passes: a trained controller-side speaker with a new reward source is a methodological contribution.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Choosing the command from the frozen VLA's pre-execution chunks at every step is already done by CoVer-VLA [2602.12281], so C1 survives only if it is re-anchored on what that work cannot do (a language-free, referent-referenced listener score that a cheap amortized speaker learns at forward-pass cost) and its listener-population safeguard is re-mapped to independent checkpoints.
