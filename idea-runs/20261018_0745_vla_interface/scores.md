### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=novelty)`
- **Evidence**:
  - [2602.12281] (CoVer-VLA, Feb 2026, not cited by C1 or gaps.md) already treats the frozen VLA as a listener whose immediate response picks what to say: at every chunk boundary it samples M chunks from π0/π0.5 under each of K VLM rephrasings, scores them with a verifier, and makes the best-scoring rephrase the active prompt (Sec. 4.1, Alg. 2), with no rollouts or episode outcomes. This is in-domain sample-and-rerank RSA. It falsifies Gap 1's premise, as worded, that no method learns executability "from the VLA's own immediate response to a candidate command", and C1's novelty paragraph has to be rewritten against it.
  - What C1 still adds over [2602.12281] is real but has to become the headline. (a) The listener score is target-referenced: the controller knows the referent, and the score is whether the predicted reach lands on it. CoVer's verifier instead scores alignment with the original *language* (Sec. 4.2), so it must itself resolve "second mug from the left" and inherits the same grounding problem. Its training costs about 1.3×10^20 FLOPs, 3.8× π0 (App. 8.6), which is out of budget here. (b) Amortization: C1 emits one command, while CoVer pays K×M samples per chunk (~453 ms for 16 candidates, Sec. 5.6). (c) C1 issues per-sub-task controller commands, while CoVer has one boot-time rephrase set per episode. Point (b) alone is a thin delta. A 2027 panel will read C1 as "CoVer with a geometric verifier, distilled" unless (a) is shown to fix the wrong-referent case that a language-conditioned verifier cannot.
  - The source grounding checks out for [2609.14207] and [2006.00418]. The process listener beats the outcome listener ([2609.14207] App. C.3.1 control). The amortized speaker on a graded L0 matches sample-rerank RSA, and REINFORCE on binary success fails ([2006.00418] Results). C1 states both correctly.
  - The mapping of [2110.05422] is wrong. That paper's safeguard is a population of independently trained listeners, and it reports that a *dropout* population (stochasticity within one model) does not close the train/held-out gap. C1's "population" is several flow/diffusion samples from the same VLA, which is the within-model case that failed, and those samples share the model's biases (cf. [2610.19438]'s shared-bias point). They give a graded score, which is legitimate as the [2006.00418] L0 probability, but not the anti-drift safeguard. A real population would be several checkpoints (e.g. π0 / π0.5 / a LoRA variant).
  - The key transfer assumption is stated and partly supported. [2610.06235] shows that the committed approach largely decides which object is manipulated, and that action deviation alone is not target-directed, which is the right caveat. The same paper shows that in scene-prior-dominated layouts no in-scene rename redirects π0.5 (0.5–13%). There the speaker's reward is flat across all commands, and C1's correct fallback is "flag non-executable", not a better phrase.
  - Contribution type passes: a trained controller-side speaker with a new reward source is a methodological contribution.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Choosing the command from the frozen VLA's pre-execution chunks at every step is already done by CoVer-VLA [2602.12281], so C1 survives only if it is re-anchored on what that work cannot do (a language-free, referent-referenced listener score that a cheap amortized speaker learns at forward-pass cost) and its listener-population safeguard is re-mapped to independent checkpoints.


### C2 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=coherence)`
- **Evidence**:
  - The CoVer delta is now stated correctly. [2602.12281] scores every (rephrase, chunk) pair against the *original* instruction through frozen SigLIP2 encoders. None of its tasks separates identical instances by position or ordinal (App. 8.1), and its F1 of 0.772 is measured against random actions, not wrong-referent ones (App. 8.5.1). A referent-referenced, language-free score is therefore a real difference. Recency searches (since 2025-10: rational speech acts robot, VLA relational/spatial referring, rephrase VLA, VLA prompt optimization, frozen VLA predicted action verifier) found no work that trains a speaker on a frozen VLA's referent readout. The nearest hits, TTT-VLA latent prompts, Q-DIG red-teaming prompts and RefGuard's scene-graph grounding, do something else.
  - The ReGuide grounding checks out. [2609.38616] integrates the unexecuted chunk into an endpoint (Eq. 2) and tests it against approach boxes with a demo-calibrated radius (Eq. 3). It uses the mismatch only to trigger scripted transport and never changes the instruction (App. B.3). Relational instructions are explicitly untested (App. E). C2's readout and its "words, not motion" delta are accurate. The readout's commitment radius, however, is calibrated from demo end-effector and object poses, "which not every released policy provides" (App. E). C2 needs the same calibration for every checkpoint in its listener population.
  - The source mappings are now right. [2006.00418] trains the amortized speaker on a fixed L0's graded referent probability, and REINFORCE on binary success fails. C2's within-model chunk samples serve exactly as that graded L0. [2110.05422] counts as a population only separately trained listeners; dropout does not close the gap. C2 now uses independent checkpoints, which is the correct mapping. Caveat: that paper tests n ∈ {10, 20, 30}, and populations of 2–5 are untested (App. B, Sec. 5). With the 3–4 public checkpoints per benchmark that C2 can obtain, protection against drift is a hope, not something the source shows.
  - **The headline case contradicts the mechanism.** C2's headline is "same-category lookalike instances that only a relation or ordinal tells apart". Suppose the instances really are lookalikes. Then the only words that pick out the pointed one are relational or ordinal, and those are exactly the words frozen VLAs fail to ground ([2610.12696] Sec. V-B; [2310.19785] for the CLIP-family encoders inside them). The referent score is then flat across the speaker's candidates. Suppose instead they differ in an attribute the instruction did not use (the challenge's "red mug"). Then the hard step, resolving "second from the left", has been handed to the pointer, and what is left is turning a point into a name. The proposal never says which of these two regimes its 15-point claim lives in.
  - **The baselines the claim must beat are missing.** In the second regime two cheap point-to-words routes already exist, and C2 commits to no margin over either.
    - (a) A literal speaker: a VLM names the pointed object directly. [2610.12696] shows that replacing a relational or ordinal instruction with a direct naming of the same target lifts π0.5 / MolmoAct2 / GR00T-N1.7 by 28.3–40.0 points on matched scenes (Sec. V-B). This is the S0 baseline in [2006.00418], against which the amortized speaker must be shown to add value.
    - (b) VAP-from-point [2512.20014], which manufactures a distinguishing attribute for true lookalikes. It tints the pointed instance and says "the <tint> cup", reaching 62.7–83.5% on frozen π0/OpenVLA-class policies against 5.2–7.3% for the mask alone (App. E.1). This is the training-free answer to C2's own headline case, and C2 cites it only as motivation.
  - **The separating experiment is information-mismatched.** C2's score gets the resolved point (simulator pose, or a pointer at test time). The CoVer-style verifier gets only the unresolved instruction. A 15-point gap would therefore partly measure the pointer, not the scoring rule. A fair version gives CoVer the same resolved information, for example by scoring against the literal speaker's direct naming. At test time the pointer is also the bottleneck that C2 assumes away: RoboPoint puts only 49.8% of points inside the target on lookalike references ([2406.10721] Table 2).
  - **Significance.** The committed effect is an intended-instance *commitment rate* on custom distractor layouts, a proxy. The problem's bar asks for a committed effect on a standard benchmark's success. The listed main risk also stands: in-scene target swaps redirect LIBERO-finetuned π0.5/GR00T in only 0.5–13% of trials ([2610.06235] Sec. 4.1). This forces the evaluation onto SimplerEnv-class Bridge/Fractal checkpoints, where CoVer already reports +31 OOD.
  - The other checks pass. Contribution type: a trained, referent-scored controller-side speaker is a method. Conceptual mapping: speaker, literal listener, interpretation, L0 and population are all specified. Key assumption: stated.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The headline case, lookalikes told apart only by relation, leaves the speaker no VLA-groundable words. Once distinguishing attributes exist, the referent resolution sits in the pointer and the job reduces to turning a point into words. Training-free baselines already do that: direct naming gives +28–40 points [2610.12696], and VAP's tint plus colour word reaches 62.7–83.5% on lookalikes [2512.20014]. C2 commits to a margin only over CoVer, which never sees the point. It must re-scope its headline regime and commit to an information-matched gain over the literal speaker and VAP-from-point, in task success on a standard benchmark.
