# Encoded but Not in Control: Revealing the Grounding Gap in Vision-Language Robot Policies

## What this paper does
Scene-preserving instruction interventions (in-scene target substitution, random nouns, unrelated sentences) on π0.5, GR00T N1.7 and two world-action models in LIBERO-Object, RoboTwin 2.0 and real scenes, plus layerwise action lens, linear probes, attention-contribution analysis, UMAP and shared semi-NMF, to test whether language actually selects the target.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism maps the controller/user instruction to phrasing the frozen VLA executes, and at what granularity (per episode / per sub-task / per step)?
Not a method; a diagnostic. Interventions change the instruction once per episode while holding the scene fixed [full text, Sec. 4.1, App. B].

### Goal: What signal tells the method whether a phrasing is executable, and what does acquiring it cost?
Internal signals: the instructed target is linearly decodable from representations [full text, Sec. 5.3]; instruction tokens account for only 25.6–27.9% (LIBERO), ~1.7% (RoboTwin) and 9.7% (real) of attention-weighted contribution to π0.5's action expert [full text, Sec. 6.1].

### Goal: What limitation does it report regarding state/context-dependence or cases where no phrasing works?
When the instruction names a different visible object, 86.5–98.3% of LIBERO-Object trials still go to the original scene-associated target vs 0.5–13.0% to the instructed one [full text, Sec. 4.1]. Target-token attention can stay on the original object [full text, Sec. 6.2]; fine-tuning reorganizes instruction representations around scene identity [full text, Sec. 6.3–6.4]. So in scenes with a strong scene prior, rewording the target cannot redirect the policy.
