# Scaling Verification Can Be More Effective than Scaling Policy Learning for Vision-Language-Action Alignment

## What this paper does
CoVer-VLA: at boot time a VLM writes K rephrasings of the user instruction from the initial image. At every chunk boundary a frozen π0/π0.5 samples M action chunks per rephrasing, and a contrastive vision-language-action verifier (SigLIP2 image-text fusion + transformer action encoder, InfoNCE on OXE data with rephrase augmentation, 20M samples, 1B params) scores every (rephrase, chunk) pair against the ORIGINAL instruction. The rephrase with the highest mean score becomes the active prompt for that chunk and its best chunk is executed. Gains: SIMPLER +16 ID / +31 OOD under red-teaming instructions; PolaRiS π0.5 +13.9 progress / +9.3 SR.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What does CoVer-VLA select, at what granularity, and from which signal?
It selects the high-level prompt AND the action chunk, re-deciding at every chunk boundary (closed loop), from the frozen VLA's pre-execution action samples under each rephrasing: k* = argmax_k (1/M) Σ_j V(o_t, h_t, l', a'_{k,j}) [full text, Sec. 4.1, Sec. 4.3, Alg. 2]. No environment rollouts or episode outcomes are used to choose the prompt. The paper frames this explicitly as "treating the instruction itself as a decision variable … optimization in language space" [full text, Sec. 4.1].

### Goal: What does the verifier score, and what does it cost to obtain?
The verifier scores semantic alignment of (observation, original instruction) with (action history, action chunk), learned contrastively from successful demonstrations with in-batch negatives; no failure labels and no explicit target/referent [full text, Sec. 4.2, Alg. 1]. So the verifier must itself ground the original language. Training cost is about 1.3×10^20 FLOPs, 3.8× the π0 base-policy training estimate [full text, App. 8.6, Table 7].

### Goal: Does it amortize the prompt choice, and what is the test-time cost?
No amortization: every chunk evaluates K×M samples plus the verifier; 16 candidates take ~453 ms (~2.2 Hz) with batching and KV caching [full text, Sec. 5.6]. Rephrasings are generated once per episode at boot time because "user intent is typically consistent throughout an episode" [full text, Sec. 4.3]; there is no sub-task decomposition or controller-issued sub-task commands.

### Goal: What limits or failure cases are reported regarding wrong-object grounding?
Real-world baselines "often fail to identify the correct object" while CoVer-VLA "consistently shows the correct intention" [full text, Sec. 5.5]. Random VLM rephrasings without verification are inconsistent (78% on one task, 1% on another) [full text, Sec. 5.4]. Absolute PolaRiS success stays low (13.1% average) [full text, Table 1].
