# Learning What to Say to Your VLA: Mostly Harmless Vision Language Action Model Steering

## What this paper does
Learns a closed-loop language feedback policy (LFP, Qwen3-VL-4B + LoRA) that emits a language string at every replanning step to steer a frozen VLA (π0.5-LIBERO in sim, π0.5-DROID on a Franka). Three phases: (1) narrated fine-tuning on VLM captions of 50 base-VLA rollout videos per task; (2) interactive search — an LLM produces N=16 trajectory-level perturbations of a seed language sequence, rollouts estimate improvement Δ over the original instruction, best sequences are distilled by rejection fine-tuning; (3) an improvement head ψ(o_0, ℓ_task) regresses Δ and is conformally calibrated so the system steers only when improvement is predicted, else falls back to the original instruction. Evaluated on LIBERO-OOD visual/semantic perturbations and hardware tasks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism maps the controller/user instruction to phrasing the frozen VLA executes, and at what granularity (per episode / per sub-task / per step)?
Per replanning step: ℓ_t ~ π(· | o_t; ℓ_task), passed to the VLA for the next action chunk [full text, Sec. 3]. Closed-loop trajectory-level search beats an open-loop single static rewrite (75.0 vs 71.3 SR) and phrase-level per-step perturbation (70.4) [full text, Sec. 5.4 Table 2]; closed-loop feedback produces recovery after mid-task scene changes.

### Goal: What signal tells the method whether a phrasing is executable, and what does acquiring it cost?
Monte-Carlo estimates of episode-level improvement Δ from closed-loop rollouts of each candidate sequence vs the original instruction [full text, Sec. 4, Eq. 2, Eq. 4]. Search is local around narrations within the training environment (Visual-Viewpoint) [full text, Sec. 5.1]. The improvement head ψ conditions only on the initial observation o_0 and task text, not on later states [full text, Eq. 6].

### Goal: What limitation does it report regarding state/context-dependence or cases where no phrasing works?
Notes that the VLA may ignore language or lack the skill, so steering can harm; RFT on small data degrades OOD [full text, Sec. 4.1]. The calibrated system refuses steering on 43–58% of simulation cases (refusal rate 43.42% uncalibrated, 57.66% conformal) [full text, Sec. 5.3 table]. Limitations: "language is not always the best way to exert control authority over the VLA"; future work on editing observation/latents/actions; search is local paraphrasing around a fixed sequence [full text, Sec. 6].
