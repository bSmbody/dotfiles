# Steerable Vision-Language-Action Policies for Embodied Reasoning and Hierarchical Control

## What this paper does
Trains VLAs (OpenVLA, π0.5) on Bridge with task labels randomly replaced by synthetic steering commands of many styles: sub-tasks, atomic motions, gripper-trace pixel lists, points and combinations, about 2M commands from 38k labels via Molmo, SAM2, DETR and Gemini. Two controllers drive them: a fine-tuned embodied-reasoning VLM, and an off-the-shelf Gemini 3.0 that picks the command and its abstraction level in context from the history of observations and commands.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What non-language representation is used and how is it injected into the VLA?
Points and traces are written as pixel coordinates inside the language command (e.g. "grasp at [x,y]", "move from [x1,y1] to [x2,y2]") [full text, Sec. IV-A].
### Goal: How much VLA training does reading the cue require, and what happens without it?
Full BC retraining on the relabelled data: OpenVLA for 80k steps at batch 256 on 8×H100; π0.5 for 30k steps on 8×H200 [App. F-A]. Steerability "comes from the diversity of behaviors within its data", and the method "cannot easily be applied to LIBERO" because of LIBERO's narrow, unimodal demos [App. E-D].
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report for cue adherence or OOD generalization?
With a human oracle, trace and point commands dominate semantic generalization (OOD objects), atomic motions dominate spatial-relation tasks, and no single style dominates [Sec. VI-A]. Most failures stem from "the high-level VLM's limited grasp of the low-level Steerable Policy's affordances". This is a pragmatics problem: the controller must infer which command and abstraction the VLA will execute in the current state, and in-context discovery misses subtle cases [App. E-D]. The in-context controller is a frontier VLM re-queried every 20 steps [Sec. V-B].
