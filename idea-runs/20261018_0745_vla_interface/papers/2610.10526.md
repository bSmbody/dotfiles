# Rephrase Before You Act: Characterizing and Mitigating Language Sensitivity in Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
Characterizes phrasing sensitivity of frozen π0 (SIMPLER/Bridge) and π0.5 (LIBERO) with statistically tested single-edit swings and an oracle phrase search, then reduces it without touching the policy: many phrasings of 8 evidence tasks are scored by rollouts (plus a gripper-error proxy for non-simulatable tasks), an LLM distills the 5,453 scored pairs into 10–20 natural-language rephrasing rules, and at deployment a VLM applier rewrites each incoming instruction once per episode under the rules. Gains of 16–27% relative on 12 held-out SIMPLER tasks; on LIBERO in-finetune 93.6→97.8%, out-of-finetune unchanged.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism maps the controller/user instruction to phrasing the frozen VLA executes, and at what granularity (per episode / per sub-task / per step)?
A static rulebook R distilled offline by a multi-agent LLM panel; rewrite f_R applied once per episode before the first action, and reused across all layouts and repetitions [full text, Sec. IV-A, V-B, V-C]. The applier does not receive the image; it gets the instruction, R, and a Gemini reasoning trace produced from the phrase and the scene image [full text, Sec. V-C]. No per-sub-task or per-step rewriting; single-instruction tasks only.

### Goal: What signal tells the method whether a phrasing is executable, and what does acquiring it cost?
Episode-level rollout success per (task, phrase): 36 rollouts per phrase for simulatable tasks (72 for characterization); for 208 non-simulatable Bridge tasks a proxy, gripper-dimension error of predicted action chunk vs ground truth over 64 frames, correlating with rollout success at Pearson r=0.54 within task [full text, Sec. V-A1]. Conclusion attributes underperformance of in-finetune rulebooks possibly to this weak proxy [full text, Sec. VII].

### Goal: What limitation does it report regarding state/context-dependence or cases where no phrasing works?
Some swings do not transfer: adding "purple" to eggplant gains 61 points on one task and costs 30 on another, "so no static rule can satisfy both" [full text, Sec. IV-C]. A prompt requiring each rule to hold across all tasks produced a rulebook that gained nothing [full text, Sec. VI-F]. On π0.5/LIBERO out-of-finetune tasks rulebooks leave success unchanged (p>0.4) although oracle phrase search shows headroom (52→66) [full text, Sec. IV-D Fig. 7, VI-F]. Sets where an ambiguous noun matched several scene objects were removed manually as scene-ambiguity rather than phrasing [full text, Sec. IV-B].
