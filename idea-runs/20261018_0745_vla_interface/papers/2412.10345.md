# TraceVLA: Visual Trace Prompting Enhances Spatial-Temporal Awareness for Generalist Robotic Policies

## What this paper does
Overlays CoTracker point traces of the robot's recent motion (M=5 active points, window N=6) on the current frame. The traced image and the original image, separated by a special token, are fed to a VLA. OpenVLA-7B and a newly pretrained Phi3-V 4B VLA are fine-tuned for five epochs on ~150k trace-annotated Bridge/RT-1 trajectories. Training uses trace dropout. Evaluated on SimplerEnv, LIBERO and a WidowX robot.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What non-language representation is used and how is it injected into the VLA?
Historical 2D point traces drawn on the RGB frame. They encode the policy's own recent motion, not a controller command. The un-annotated frame is also input, so overlays do not hide objects [full text, Sec. 3.1–3.2].
### Goal: How much VLA training does reading the cue require, and what happens without it?
Five further epochs on 150k annotated trajectories. Pretraining the Phi3 variant used 32 H100s [Sec. 3.3]. Fine-tuning on the same data without traces gives +1.1 (OpenVLA) and −0.3 (Phi3), so the gain needs trace-aware training [Sec. 4.3, Fig. 7]. Text-coordinate traces give +2.4 against +8.8 for visual traces, at ~150 extra tokens [Sec. 4.3, Fig. 8].
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report for cue adherence or OOD generalization?
Trace length trades history against occlusion (N=12 clutters the view) and is dataset-specific [Sec. 4.3, Fig. 9]. The stated limitations are training memory and inference cost [Sec. 5]. The representation is self-generated history, so it carries no controller intent.
