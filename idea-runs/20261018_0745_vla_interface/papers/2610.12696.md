# Spa-Bench: A Real-Robot Benchmark for Spatial Grounding in Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
A real-robot (SO-101) diagnostic benchmark of six pick-and-place tasks whose target is fixed by an attribute (object state, relative size), a relation (relational placement, referential description) or scene structure (ordinal position, counting). It fine-tunes five VLAs (π0.5, MolmoAct2, GR00T-N1.7 ×2, VLA-0) on the same 1,200 demos and compares ID against withheld (OOD) concept–argument cells. Matched-scene diagnostics replace the spatial instruction with a direct instruction that names the target, and paraphrase both. Not a method.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: When a relational or ordinal referent is resolved externally and the target is named directly, how much does a VLA's success change on the same scenes?
On 120 matched OOD scenes per policy, success rises from 60.8% to 89.2% (MolmoAct2, +28.3), from 45.0% to 83.3% (π0.5, +38.3) and from 41.7% to 81.7% (GR00T-N1.7 F-LLM, +40.0) when the spatial instruction (e.g. "the crushed soda can closest to the airplane toy") is replaced by a direct one ("Move the crushed Coke can into the bowl") [full text, Sec. III-D, Sec. V-B]. The direct instruction is written by the authors (an oracle naming), and the target has a unique name in these scenes; identical-lookalike instances with no distinguishing name are not the test case [full text, Sec. III-D, Fig. 4].
### Goal: How sensitive are spatial versus direct instructions to rewording?
On a 60-scene subset, paraphrasing lowers spatial-instruction success for all three policies (e.g. 65.0→50.0 MolmoAct2, 48.3→41.7 π0.5). Its effect on direct instructions is model-dependent: 81.7→70.0 for GR00T-N1.7 F-LLM, unchanged at 86.7 and 90.0 for π0.5 and MolmoAct2 [full text, Sec. V-B, Fig. 7]. So wording still matters for some VLAs even after the referent is resolved, but most of the gain comes from naming the target directly.
