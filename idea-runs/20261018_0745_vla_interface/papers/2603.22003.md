# VP-VLA: Visual Prompting as an Interface for Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
A dual-system framework: a "System 2" VLM planner (Qwen3-VL-4B), triggered by gripper-state change events, picks the current sub-task, target object and target location. SAM3 grounds them into a crosshair (object centroid) and a bounding box (placement region), drawn on a separate copy of the overhead image. The "System 1" VLA (QwenOFT: OpenVLA-OFT with a Qwen3-VL-4B backbone) is fully trained on (language, observation, visual-prompt image). An auxiliary key-frame grounding loss (cross-entropy over 1000 coordinate bins) makes the VLM backbone predict the prompted coordinates. Evaluated on RoboCasa-GR1 tabletop, SimplerEnv and a real robot.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What non-language representation is used and how is it injected into the VLA?
Crosshair C∈R² for pick targets and bounding box B∈R⁴ for placement, overlaid on an extra overhead image I_vp fed alongside o_t: a_t = π(l, o_t, I_vp) [full text, Sec. 3.2–3.3, Eq. 5]. The planner is re-invoked only when the gripper state changes [Sec. 3.2, Eq. 2].
### Goal: How much VLA training does reading the cue require, and what happens without it?
The VLA is trained end-to-end on prompt-annotated OXE/RoboCasa data (lr 1e-5 VLM, 1e-4 action head), plus the grounding loss λ=0.1 on key frames only. Training on the full dataset took ~46 h on 8×H200 [Sec. 4.1, App. B]. Without the grounding loss, RoboCasa average drops from 53.8 to 49.4: "the model may perceive the visual prompts but fail to consistently associate them with action generation". A point instead of a crosshair gives 47.3: the policy treats it "as visual perturbation". Direct overlay on the main RGB gives 50.8 [Sec. 4.5, Table 3]. There is no frozen-VLA variant.
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report for cue adherence or OOD generalization?
Its stated limitation is the bandwidth of 2D boxes and crosshairs [App. A]. The ablations show that the cue is read only to the extent that the auxiliary loss forces the backbone to align with it, and that prompt geometry changes whether the cue registers as signal or noise [Sec. 4.5].
