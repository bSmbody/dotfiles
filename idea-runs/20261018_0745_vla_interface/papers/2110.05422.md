# Calibrate your listeners! Robust communication-based training for pragmatic speakers

## What this paper does
Shows that speakers trained to be informative to a single fixed neural listener over a large (GPT-2 BPE) vocabulary generalise to new games with that listener but drop to chance with held-out listeners and stop using domain words (semantic drift). Diagnoses the cause as poor uncertainty calibration of the listener on novel utterances and fixes it by training the speaker against a population of listeners (ensembles; dropout works less well).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Why does a speaker trained against a fixed listener drift or overfit, and what fixes it?
Large-vocabulary speakers reach 98/94% with the training listener but 37/36% with held-out listeners, with 1% token overlap with ShapeWorld captions [full text, Sec. 3, Table 1]. The hypothesis is that the listener makes high-certainty predictions on novel sentences, which the speaker exploits [full text, Sec. 4, Abstract]. An ensemble-based listener population closes the train/held-out listener gap as population size grows (n up to 30) and raises domain-token overlap to 48.4%, while a dropout population does not close the gap [full text, Sec. 5]. Calibrated-population speakers produce utterances closer in GloVe space to ground-truth captions than single-listener speakers [full text, Sec. 5, Table 3].
