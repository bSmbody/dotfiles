# RoboPrompt: Intuitive Robot Policy Steering with Sparse Human Input

## What this paper does
Steers pretrained diffusion or flow-matching policies (Diffusion Policy, π0.5, FastWAM) with sparse human inputs (a target point, a drawn 2D trace, or a coarse directional text) without fine-tuning them for steerability. Phase I: a 0.77B Evo-1 VLA trained on 500 same-robot play trajectories, with hindsight prompt labels, turns the prompt into a 6D action draft. Phase II, Progressive Biased Denoising: the base policy re-noises the draft to level γ^m and denoises it. Noise-Augmented Flow Reversal Steering (NA-FRS) smooths this, and γ rises over successive chunks. Steered rollouts filtered by time-weighted optimal transport feed DAgger.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What non-language representation is used and how is it injected into the VLA?
Point and trace prompts are overlaid on the camera image, plus a prompt-only image, and given to the separate Phase I translator. They reach the base policy only as an action draft injected in noise space [full text, Sec. III-A, III-B].
### Goal: How much VLA training does reading the cue require, and what happens without it?
The base policy is untouched. The translator needs 500 play trajectories on the same platform [Sec. III-A]. The steering-vs-prior balance comes from the denoising level: γ=0 returns the draft and γ=1 the unsteered policy [Sec. III-B].
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report for cue adherence or OOD generalization?
The balance follows a fixed, hand-set schedule, k^m = min(k^0 + mΔk, N), increasing with the chunk index regardless of state [Sec. III-B]. Action change is highly non-uniform across denoising levels, so "naively increasing the denoising level does not provide a linear control knob" [Sec. IV Q3, Fig. 8]. Prompt alignment (>95%) is scored only on the prompted direction [Sec. IV Q1]. The steering source is a human in the loop who decides when to intervene [Sec. IV Q4]. Dexterous hands are unsupported [Sec. V].
