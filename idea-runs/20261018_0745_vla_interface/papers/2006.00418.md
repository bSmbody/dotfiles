# Learning to refer informatively by amortizing pragmatic reasoning

## What this paper does
Compares speakers for reference games (ShapeWorld, Colors in Context): a naive captioner, a contextual captioner, full and sample-rerank Rational Speech Acts (RSA) speakers that query a fixed internal neural listener L0, an amortized RSA speaker trained to minimise listener surprisal of the target, and a REINFORCE speaker trained on binary communicative success. The amortized speaker matches the reranking RSA speaker's accuracy at captioner speed and generalises to held-out colour/shape combinations; the REINFORCE speaker fails to learn.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism by which a speaker learns to be informative to a fixed listener, and what does the listener provide?
A fixed, pre-trained listener L0(t | I, u) gives a graded probability that utterance u picks out target t among the referents [full text, Eq. 4]. RSA chooses u maximising log L0(t|I,u) − C(u); with unbounded utterances it samples M candidates from the naive speaker and reranks them (S_RSA-SRR) [full text, Eq. 5]. The amortized speaker S_RSA-Am has the captioner's architecture but is trained on −log L0(t|I,û) + C(û) for its own samples, through Gumbel-softmax straight-through; no alternative utterances are needed at train or test time, and utterances are never compared to human language [full text, Eq. 6–7]. Because L0 is introspectable, the speaker receives "subtle word-by-word supervision" [full text, Sec. "Learning to produce informative utterances"].

### Goal: How does learning from the listener's graded interpretation compare with learning from binary communicative success?
S_RL is architecturally identical but gets +1/0 from the argmax of the same listener and is trained by REINFORCE. It performs on par with the naive speaker on ShapeWorld and "is unable to learn" on Colors, while S_RSA-Am is on par with sample-rerank on ShapeWorld and highest (94%) on Colors [full text, Results "Efficacy"]. The authors read this as: the RL speaker "was forced to learn from the weaker signal of communicative success or failure"; "an internal model of the listener greatly helps communication-based training" [full text, Conclusion]. Amortized speakers adapt length to context difficulty and come closest to full RSA on held-out combinations; the contextual captioner fails there, suggesting memorisation [full text, "Concision", "Generalization"].

### Goal: What does the method cost at test time and what are its limits?
Sample-rerank and full RSA must evaluate the listener across many utterances and are much slower; the amortized speaker needs one sample [full text, "Inference Speed"]. Limits: language drift towards unconventional utterances (lower per-word LM probability) [full text, "Conventionality"]; highly novel situations may need reverting from amortized to explicit reasoning [full text, Conclusion].
