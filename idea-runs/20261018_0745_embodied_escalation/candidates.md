### C1 — Uplift triggers: learn the frontier call's treatment effect with an X-learner over free cheap rollouts and a few randomized calls

- **Intuition**: Treat a frontier call as a scarce medical-style "treatment" and train the escalation trigger as an uplift model in the style of an X-learner. Abundant, free cheap-VLA rollouts give the "no-call" outcome at every state. A few hundred randomized frontier calls give "called" outcomes, and the difference between each one and the no-call value at the same state is that call's imputed effect. The trigger regresses that imputed effect. It then stays silent on states the cheap policy will finish anyway ("sure things") and on states already past the point of no return ("lost causes"), and fires only on states the helper can still save ("persuadables").

- **Gap it attacks**: Axis "failure prediction signals for cheap VLA policies", Gap 1, with Gap 2 of the same axis as the binding constraint. AEGIS, SAFE, TDQC, Foresight and ReAP regress the cheap policy's own failure probability [2606.06660, 2506.09937, 2604.20472, 2606.23085, 2610.16309]. That score stays high after the recoverability horizon, where the helper's advantage has already collapsed. RESCUE puts that horizon a median 34 steps before the visible drop and finds that real monitors alarm after it in most episodes [2610.13516]. So a risk threshold wastes calls on lost states and fires late on savable ones. AEGIS recovers only about a third of the always-strong headroom, and its edge vanishes in the hard tercile [2606.06660]. The only helper-conditioned estimators today use dense Monte-Carlo branching of the helper (RESCUE, 128K branches [2610.13516]) or abundant backup rollouts (VAPS [2610.01397]), which is unaffordable when each helper rollout is a billed frontier call. Closing the gap would make the problem's "confidently grasps the wrong object" state the place the trigger fires, before the grasp, and spend a small call budget only where it changes the outcome.

- **Source inspiration**: Heterogeneous-treatment-effect estimation and uplift modeling from causal ML and marketing. The X-learner [1706.03461] estimates the conditional treatment effect when one arm is huge and the other tiny. It learns the complex control response from the large control arm. It then subtracts that fitted response from each scarce treated outcome to impute a per-unit effect, and regresses only that residual effect. So the few treated units are spent on the effect, which is often zero or simple, rather than on the full outcome surface. The authors prove that this reaches the effect's own (faster) learning rate when control data are abundant, and show large gains in a 1%-treated simulation where the effect is a simple indicator inside a complex 20-dimensional response [1706.03461, Sections "X-learner", "Smoothness conditions", Appendix A.1]. Fernández-Loría and Provost [2104.04103] say exactly when targeting by outcome risk, the non-causal proxy every VLA trigger uses, is good enough: when risk and effect are correlated. They also say when it breaks: when the treatment can harm some units, or when effect and outcome decouple. They argue that the targeting rule should be judged by decision regret, which randomized data let one estimate directly [2104.04103, Sections 3.1, 3.3]. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts. A causal-inference estimator built for cheap administrative control data and expensive field treatments is carried over to cheap-VLA rollouts and expensive frontier calls.

- **How it maps to this problem**: A "unit" is a state at a chunk boundary of a cheap-VLA episode. "Control" means letting the cheap VLA continue, and "treatment" means one frontier call at that state (the helper plans or takes over for a fixed span, then hands back), with eventual task success as the outcome. The control response function is the cheap policy's own success value. It is learned from free simulated rollouts with the TD calibration of TDQC [2604.20472] on frozen VLA features, the same features AEGIS probes [2606.06660], and it can be sharpened at any specific state by restoring the simulator checkpoint and rolling the cheap policy out again for free, as RESCUE does [2610.13516]. The treated arm comes from a budgeted data-collection phase. In each training episode the frontier model is called once, at a chunk boundary drawn at random with a known probability that may be skewed toward moderately risky states. Because the call times are randomized by design, the propensity is known and there is no confounding. Each called episode's outcome minus the control value at the call state is an imputed helper advantage. A small head on the same VLA features regresses it, and it is trained to rank states for the call/no-call decision (the regret view of [2104.04103]) rather than to estimate the effect exactly. At deployment the cheap VLA runs, and the trigger calls the frontier model when the predicted advantage, not the risk, clears the price of a call or the remaining per-episode cap. A lost state has a high risk and a low control value, but it gets a near-zero imputed advantage, because the called outcomes there also fail. So it never pays for a call.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**: The X-learner's two preconditions plausibly hold here. (i) The design is extremely unbalanced. Cheap-VLA rollouts in LIBERO-style simulation cost only GPU time, and RESCUE shows checkpoint-restore branching of the cheap policy at scale on LIBERO [2610.13516]. Frontier calls are the scarce arm, so the first-stage error is nearly zero, which is the regime where [1706.03461] proves the fast rate. (ii) The effect is structurally simpler than the outcome surface. RESCUE finds most failures are unrecoverable under any intervention, and recoverability is confined to a window before a horizon [2610.13516], so the helper's advantage is zero on most states and nonzero in a narrow band. That is the "CATE is often zero" structure the X-learner exploits. [2104.04103] predicts that risk targeting breaks exactly when effects decouple from outcomes or can be negative. Both are documented in-domain: risk stays high past the horizon [2610.13516], and well-timed subgoal-rewrite calls lowered LIBERO success from 8/10 to 4/10 on one task [2610.21160]. VAPS independently confirms that conditioning on the backup's own outcome, not the nominal policy's, is what makes switching work [2610.01397]. Labels for the scarce arm fit the budget. RouteLLM bought ~120K judged pairwise labels for ~US$700 [2406.18665], and here a few hundred to ~2,000 single frontier calls, each paired with free cheap continuations, are within the ~US$1,000 cap. A local stronger VLA (π0.5, as in AEGIS [2606.06660]) can stand in as the helper while the pipeline is debugged. Key assumption: the frozen-VLA features that predict cheap-policy risk also carry the information that separates "still savable" from "already lost" (e.g. a wrong-object grasp in progress vs. an object already in the drawer), so a small effect head can learn the band from hundreds of noisy binary outcomes.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: AEGIS [2606.06660] is the closest. It is a weak-to-strong VLA cascade gated by a probe trained on the weak policy's eventual failure, with no helper outcomes in its target. C1 trains the gate on the imputed helper effect, using randomized helper calls and the cheap-policy value as the control arm. RouteLLM-style routers [2406.18665] do learn a helper-conditioned win probability, but for single-turn queries with both models labelled on every query, and with no sequential timing or point of no return. RESCUE [2610.13516] and VAPS [2610.01397] estimate helper-conditioned value by dense helper rollouts and train no sparse-label trigger.

- **Main risk**: With only hundreds of binary called outcomes, the imputed effects may be too noisy for the effect head to out-rank the high-signal risk score. This is the failure mode [2104.04103] reports, where an outcome model targets as well as an uplift model. In that case the trigger would collapse back to risk thresholding, gaining nothing at small call budgets.


### C2 — Grounded surrogate uplift: free stand-in helpers map where a call can help, billed frontier calls only fit the frontier-minus-surrogate correction

- **Intuition**: Most of what decides a frontier call's value can be measured for free in simulation: is the state still savable at all, and how much would a correct instruction rescue it? Branching cheap stand-in helpers from saved checkpoints answers both. Those stand-ins are a privileged oracle that issues the correct subgoal, a local open VLM, and a scripted recovery. So learn this "surrogate uplift" densely at no API cost, and spend billed frontier calls only on a small correction: how far the real frontier model's uplift falls from the stand-ins', and only on states where the stand-ins say help can still matter.

- **Gap it attacks**: Axis "failure prediction signals for cheap VLA policies", Gap 1: triggers regress the cheap policy's failure risk, not the helper's advantage. Gap 2 of the same axis is the binding constraint: helper-conditioned labels need dense, billed helper rollouts. The concrete failures run both ways. Risk triggers (AEGIS, SAFE, TDQC [2606.06660, 2506.09937, 2604.20472]) spend calls on states RESCUE shows are already lost [2610.13516]. Every helper-conditioned learner, in turn, queries its helper on-policy or on every training input: Ask4Help [2211.09960], When2Ask [2306.03604], UniIntervene++ [2610.03620], CTD [2604.14251]. That is free when the helper is a NavMesh oracle, a local controller or an LLM classifier. With a billed frontier model it costs more calls to train than the trigger saves. C1's billed-only uplift learner does better, but scatters its randomized calls across all states, and only a few dozen of a few hundred land in the narrow savable band. Closing the gap gives a helper-conditioned trigger whose training cost, counted in frontier calls, stays below its deployment savings. The trigger then beats both risk thresholding and always-call on total frontier calls.

- **If improving prior candidate**: Fixes C1's novelty weakness. With a simulator-exact control arm, C1's X-learner reduces to baseline-subtracted advantage regression on a few dozen informative binary outcomes, and helper-value triggers and causal partial-feedback routing already exist. What changes: (1) The claim is now about label cost: total (training plus deployment) frontier calls, not "a trigger that regresses helper value". (2) The free simulator is used twice. Beyond the no-call value, it also gives a dense surrogate-helper uplift, and billed calls fit only the frontier-minus-surrogate residual. (3) Billed calls are placed with known probability only inside the band the surrogates mark as savable. Each one is paired with same-seed surrogate branches from the same checkpoint, which act as a control variate. (4) It commits to an effect size (below).

- **Source inspiration**: The idea combines two mechanisms. (a) *Experimental grounding* from causal inference [1810.11646]. Learn a biased but low-variance effect function from a large sample. Then use a small randomized experiment with known propensities to fit only a low-capacity correction, the true effect minus the biased one, and add it back. This works because the large sample carries the effect's complex shape. The scarce unbiased data are spent only on the correction, which is assumed parametric. Consistency then holds at the rate of the large-sample fit plus a parametric 1/n term [1810.11646, Section 4, Theorem 1], and if the big-sample effect is already right, the correction is learned as zero and does no harm [Section 3]. The authors name as open the case where "interventions are not directly comparable, but … the difference can be modeled" [Section 6]. That is this case: the large sample uses a stand-in helper, the experiment uses the real one. (b) *Multifidelity control variates* from multifidelity Monte Carlo RL [2206.05165]. Each expensive high-fidelity return gets a low-fidelity twin, generated from the same states in a cheap generative simulator. The twin serves as a control variate, and the cheap simulator's mean comes from many extra rollouts. The estimate stays unbiased, while both its variance and the number of expensive samples needed shrink by (1 − ρ²), where ρ is the correlation of the paired returns. The gain vanishes when the two fidelities decorrelate [2206.05165, Sections 3.2–3.3, 4.1]. C1's X-learner [1706.03461] stays as the first layer, supplying the free no-call arm. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts. Data fusion from causal inference and pairing from multifidelity estimation are carried over to learning when to call a billed embodied helper.

- **How it maps to this problem**: A unit is a chunk-boundary state of a cheap-VLA episode with a saved simulator checkpoint, as in RESCUE [2610.13516]. There are three arms, and two of them are free.
  - *Control* (free, as in C1): the cheap VLA continues.
  - *Stand-in treatments* (free): branch from the same checkpoint through the same call channel the frontier will use. That channel is "issue a corrected next subgoal to the executor", the rewrite channel of [2610.21160]. Three stand-ins give the answer: a privileged oracle that reads the simulator's goal predicates and issues the correct subgoal; a local open VLM that proposes its own subgoal (the Qwen-7B rewrite helper of [2610.21160]); and scripted Recover [2610.13516]. Their success minus the control value is a dense surrogate uplift at every state. This plays the role of the large biased sample.
  - *Real treatment* (billed): a frontier call at a few states. This plays the role of the small randomized experiment.

  The oracle stand-in carries most of the information. When the frontier can only change what the executor is told, it can do little better than telling it the correct subgoal, so oracle uplift is an approximate ceiling on frontier uplift. Two kinds of state therefore get zero predicted value without a single billed call: sure things, which the cheap policy finishes anyway, and lost causes, such as an object already in the wrong drawer. The local VLM gives the floor, a plausible but wrong subgoal, including the harmful calls seen in [2610.21160]. The frontier's uplift lies in this bracket. The correction to learn is roughly where in the bracket it falls, which should depend on a few semantic factors such as object or instruction ambiguity, not on the full physics of recoverability.

  Billed calls go to states drawn with known probability from the band where oracle uplift is positive. Each billed call is matched by stand-in branches restored from the same checkpoint with the same simulator seed. The frontier-minus-surrogate difference then cancels the shared physical noise, as the paired twin does in [2206.05165]. A low-capacity head fits these residuals. At deployment, one small head on frozen cheap-VLA features (where AEGIS probes [2606.06660]) is distilled to predict surrogate uplift plus correction without branching. The frontier is called when that prediction clears the price of a call or the remaining cap.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**: The three conditions of [1810.11646] plausibly hold.
  - *The big-sample effect is learned well.* The surrogate arm is dense and free. RESCUE ran 128K checkpoint-restored branches on LIBERO with local policies [2610.13516], so dense stand-in branching fits the GPU budget.
  - *Overlap.* The experiment samples the same states the surrogate arm covers, by construction.
  - *The correction is simple.* The hard part of the effect is the recoverability horizon, which RESCUE shows depends on the physical state and the intervention available (14/66 failures recoverable under Continue vs 34/66 with Recover) [2610.13516]. Stand-ins that drive the same executor from the same state share it. The frontier's extra skill is semantic: choosing the right object or subgoal.

  The condition of [2206.05165] is a high paired correlation, and it is plausible here. The frontier branch and the oracle branch run the same executor from the same state and seed. Whenever the two issue the same subgoal their outcomes are identical, so the residual is non-zero only where the frontier errs, and ρ can be measured from the first few dozen billed calls. The label count then drops for three compounding reasons: every billed call lands inside the band (C1's calls mostly did not), per-label variance falls by (1 − ρ²), and the target is a low-capacity correction rather than the full effect. The baseline costs are high. CTD's helper-benefit probe, trained with expert labels on every training input, reaches Spearman 0.33–0.57 [2604.14251]. That shows helper benefit is learnable from latents but label-hungry. RouteLLM bought ~120K judged pairs for ~US$700 [2406.18665], which ~50K-token Astra calls (9.53M tokens over 181 calls [2609.34261]) cannot match here.

  Key assumption: the frontier is used through an instruction channel (plan or correct a subgoal for a local executor), so the oracle subgoal bounds its uplift. A full take-over channel would break this ceiling.

  Committed effect size (falsifiable), on LIBERO-Long with a SmolVLA-class cheap policy and GPT-6 Astra issuing subgoal corrections:
  - (i) With ≤ 300 billed training calls, the trigger ranks held-out frontier uplift (Spearman against frontier-branched audit states) at least as well as C1's frontier-only X-learner trained on 1,200 billed calls, a ≥ 4× label saving.
  - (ii) Counting training calls, it matches the success of an AEGIS-style risk-thresholded trigger with ≤ 50% of that trigger's total frontier calls over the 500-episode LIBERO-Long evaluation. It also recovers ≥ 60% of the always-call success gain over cheap-only with ≤ 10% of always-call's frontier calls.
  - (iii) It spends zero billed calls, by construction, on states whose oracle uplift is zero.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: Ask4Help [2211.09960] is the closest. It learns help-seeking against a free oracle expert and then deploys with other experts, with no correction, so it is effectively a surrogate-only trigger. C2 keeps the free oracle signal but corrects it to the real billed helper with a small randomized experiment. When2Ask [2306.03604] and UniIntervene++ [2610.03620] learn helper values on-policy with free helpers. CTD [2604.14251] and Causal LLM Routing [2505.16037] learn helper benefit in single-shot routing, from expert labels on every input or from logged single-model outcomes. C1 learns from billed outcomes alone. None fuses a dense free stand-in-helper effect with a billed experiment that fits only the correction, paired same-seed branches serving as control variates, or reports training-plus-deployment helper calls.

- **Main risk**: The frontier's uplift may not be a simple correction of the stand-in ladder. It may win through things the oracle-subgoal channel does not express (phrasing the executor responds to better, multi-step reordering), or its errors may be idiosyncratic per state. Either way the correction would be complex and need as many billed calls as C1.


### C3 — Pay only for the frontier's choice: free branching prices every subgoal on the menu, billed calls are actively sampled to learn which one the frontier would pick

- **Intuition**: When the frontier helps by choosing the next subgoal for a cheap executor, the simulator can measure for free what every candidate subgoal would be worth from the current state. The only thing a billed call reveals is which candidate the frontier picks. So spend frontier calls only to learn that choice, and only where it can change the outcome. Use the local VLM's choice as a free prediction, and collect frontier labels, with known probabilities, where the menu's candidate values differ and the local VLM is unsure. This is active statistical inference, applied to learning a call trigger.

- **Gap it attacks**: Axis "failure prediction signals for cheap VLA policies", Gap 1. Triggers regress the cheap policy's failure risk [2606.06660, 2506.09937, 2604.20472], which stays high on states RESCUE shows are already lost [2610.13516]. The binding constraint is Gap 2: helper-conditioned labels come from dense helper branching, with the billed model in every branch (RoboAware, K = 20 coding-agent suffixes per root, no training-call count [2610.11480]), or from free helpers (Ask4Help, When2Ask, UniIntervene++ [2211.09960, 2306.03604, 2610.03620]). Closing it gives a trigger that ranks states by the value of the frontier's answer, at a training cost in frontier calls well below its deployment savings. The problem's "confidently grasps the wrong object at stage 2" state is exactly where some subgoal on the menu is worth much more than the cheap stack's own choice.

- **If improving prior candidate**: Fixes C2's mapping error, as the C2 verdict directs. (1) Drops the multifidelity pairing. Given the frontier's subgoal, executor outcomes branch for free, so physical outcome noise is not what the billed calls buy. The billed quantity is now the frontier's categorical choice. (2) The canonical oracle subgoal is no longer used as a ceiling. The ceiling is the best value over an enumerated subgoal menu, measured by free branching. (3) Calls are placed with active-inference probabilities instead of in an "oracle band". States where all menu values are equal (sure things, lost causes, and states where no subgoal changes anything) provably need no label. (4) Two premises are named as gates and checked before training calls are spent: that the executor follows subgoals, and that the frontier has a semantic edge. (5) The label-efficiency claim is restated against a C1 that also re-branches each frontier answer for free. RoboAware is added to the novelty comparison.

- **Source inspiration**: *Active statistical inference* [2403.03208]. A cheap model predicts every unlabeled point. Gold labels are bought with a known probability set by the model's uncertainty. The estimate is prediction plus an inverse-probability-weighted correction (label minus prediction) on the bought points, and it stays unbiased for any model and any sampling rule. Its variance depends only on the prediction error where labels were skipped. So a point whose prediction is exactly right needs sampling probability zero at no variance cost [Section 4, Eq 1–2]. The same construction applies to fitting a model (M-estimation, GLMs), and a sequential version learns the uncertainty from collected labels [Sections 5–6]. Mixing with a uniform rule guards against wrongly confident uncertainty [Section 7.2]. It saved 70–85% of the label budget against classical sampling, and 20–60% against uniform prediction-powered sampling [Section 8]. Prediction-powered data fusion [2610.12332] gives the CATE-learner version: it keeps the small sample's unbiased target under any bias in the large sample, with no parametric model of the gap (DR/R-Fusion, 0.52–0.76 of trial-only risk). That removes the parametric-correction assumption [1810.11646] that C2 relied on. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts. Survey and proteomics label-budget inference is carried over to learning when to buy a frontier model's answer.

- **How it maps to this problem**:
  - *Channel and menu.* The frontier is used through one channel. It picks the next subgoal for the cheap executor from a menu built from the task's BDDL. The menu holds the unsatisfied goal atoms of the instruction plus the scene's other object-to-region relations, all phrased the way the executor was trained, plus "keep going". LIBERO goals are conjunctions of checkable predicates over a listed object and region set [2306.03310], so the menu is finite and each atom can be checked in simulation. A closed vocabulary also removes the off-vocabulary commands that sank GPT-4o as a high-level policy [2502.19417] and the phrasing effects seen in [2610.21160]. HSE uses the same closed-vocabulary decomposer [2610.15425].
  - *Free layer: what each answer is worth.* At chunk-boundary checkpoints, branch the executor once for every menu item, over several seeds, and once for the cheap stack's own continuation. The uplift of a subgoal at a state is its success rate minus the default's. RESCUE-scale checkpoint branching is affordable on LIBERO with local policies [2610.13516]. The within-channel ceiling at a state is the best uplift on the menu, and it is exact for this channel. Two quantities follow with no API spend:
    - the *spread*: how much the menu values differ at that state;
    - the *local prediction*: the uplift expected from the local VLM's choice distribution.
    The local VLM can be the cheap stack's own subtask head, as in π0.5, whose action expert conditions only on the predicted subtask [2504.16054].
  - *Billed layer: which answer the frontier gives.* A billed label is one frontier query on a stored state (images, instruction, menu) that returns its pick. No robot rollout is needed, because that pick's uplift is already in the free table. Labels are bought with known probability that grows with the spread times the local VLM's uncertainty or disagreement, mixed with uniform sampling, as in [2403.03208]. Where the spread is zero, the frontier's answer cannot change the value, so the label is skipped with no loss. A low-capacity head learns how the frontier's choice distribution departs from the local one. It is fitted with the prediction-powered / DR-Fusion loss [2403.03208, 2610.12332], with the local choice as the free prediction.
  - *Deployment.* Two heads on frozen executor features are distilled offline: menu uplift (free targets) and the frontier-choice correction (billed targets). The predicted value of a call is the expected uplift of the frontier's pick. The trigger fires when this clears the call's price or the remaining cap. Lost states and sure things have a flat menu, so they score zero by construction.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**: The active-inference condition is that prediction errors are concentrated, so most points need no label [2403.03208]. That holds here for two structural reasons. First, the residual between the frontier's and the local choice's value can never exceed the menu spread, and RESCUE finds most failures unrecoverable under any intervention, with recoverability confined to a window [2610.13516], so the spread is zero on most states. Second, inside the window the residual is non-zero only where the two choosers disagree. Each billed label is an exact categorical answer, and its value is read from the free table, so a billed call no longer carries outcome noise.

  *Label-efficiency claim, restated against a C1 that also re-branches each frontier answer for free.* That C1 learns the exact value of the frontier's one pick at each called state. It still has to learn where value is zero from billed calls, and it cannot use the local chooser as a predictor. C3's saving therefore has two measurable factors:
  - the share of C1's risk-skewed call states that have zero spread, which is free to measure before any spend;
  - the active-over-uniform PPI factor within the band, which is 1.3–2.5× in [2403.03208] and about 1.3–1.9× in [2610.12332].

  *Gate 1, the executor follows subgoals (zero API).* LIBERO suite-trained VLAs often ignore a changed target and execute the memorized task [2510.13626, Findings 7–8]. In [2610.21160], instruction rewrites hurt at every dose, and atomic executors given the correct subtask fail from handoff states: π0.5 reaches 1.6% ordered success under oracle HSE [2610.15425]. So the executor is trained on per-subtask instructions, which makes the subgoal string causal for the action, as in π0.5 and Hi Robot ("executes nearly flawlessly" under human commands [2504.16054, 2502.19417]). Concretely, a SmolVLA or π0.5 LoRA is trained on LIBERO-90 single-step demos plus LIBERO-Long demos cut at the times their BDDL atoms become true, each segment labelled with its atom. LIBERO-90 and LIBERO-Long come from the same task pool [2306.03310]. The cut segments start from real handoff states, which addresses the start-state gap that PRTI's start-state broadening closes in [2610.15425]. The gate passes if, from logged failure states, distractor atoms are achieved when commanded and the menu ceiling lifts LIBERO-Long success by at least 15 points over the cheap stack. Otherwise stop.

  *Gate 2, the frontier has a semantic edge (free, then at most 100 calls).* In π0.5, zero-shot GPT-4 choosing from a label list was the worst high-level policy [2504.16054], and GPT-4o lost to Hi Robot's trained high level [2502.19417]. The setting is therefore LIBERO-Long under LIBERO-Plus commonsense and reasoning-chain instruction rewrites [2510.13626], plus perturbed layouts that cause wrong-object grasps. The gate first checks for free that the local chooser leaves at least 10 success points of in-band regret below the ceiling. Then a pilot of at most 100 calls on stored frames must show the frontier closing at least half of that regret. Otherwise stop before spending on training calls.

  *Committed effect sizes* (SmolVLA-class executor, GPT-6 Astra on the menu channel):
  - (i) At matched held-out decision regret, at least 3× fewer billed training calls than C1-with-free-rebranching, provided at least 50% of C1's call states have zero spread, a condition reported up front.
  - (ii) Counting training calls, the success of an AEGIS-style risk trigger with at most 50% of its total frontier calls over 500 LIBERO-Long-perturbed episodes.
  - (iii) At least 60% of the always-call gain over the cheap stack with at most 10% of always-call's frontier calls.
  - (iv) Zero billed calls on zero-spread states, which is exact by construction.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: RoboAware [2610.11480] is the closest. It learns helper-conditioned values from state-locked branching restored from simulator snapshots on LIBERO-Pro, but every branch calls the billed coding agent, it reports no training-call cost, and it chooses a code family, never whether to call. C3 branches only the free executor and bills only a categorical answer. HSE [2610.15425] measures planner versus executor errors with oracle queues but trains no trigger. π0.5 and Hi Robot [2504.16054, 2502.19417] run a high level at every step with no budget. C1 bills full outcomes, and C2 bills a residual return. No prior work combines a free per-subgoal value table with actively sampled, known-probability frontier choice labels to learn a call trigger.

- **Main risk**: The frontier may not out-choose a robot-tuned local planner even on perturbed instructions. GPT-4 and GPT-4o lost to in-domain high levels in [2504.16054, 2502.19417]. Gate 2 then fails, and the trigger correctly learns never to call. That is honest, but it is not the decisive cost/performance result the significance bar asks for.
