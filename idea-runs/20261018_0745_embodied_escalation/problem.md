# Budgeted escalation from a cheap VLA to a frontier controller

## Problem definition
A cheap embodied stack (an open VLA such as π0.5 or SmolVLA, optionally with a small open VLM) controls a robot by default, and an expensive frontier model (e.g. GPT-6 Astra) can be called at any step to plan, correct or take over. Given a task instruction, the observation stream and a budget on frontier calls (number, tokens or dollars), decide when to call the frontier model and what to ask it, so that task success approaches that of always using the frontier controller at a small fraction of its cost. Input: instruction, observations, budget. Output: actions plus a call schedule. Evaluation: success rate vs number of frontier calls / API cost on long-horizon simulated manipulation benchmarks.

## Challenge
Frontier VLMs used as robot controllers are strong at long-horizon reasoning, recovery and novel instructions, but every call is slow and expensive, and calling at every step is impractical. Cheap VLAs are fast but fail on unseen task compositions, recover poorly and give no reliable signal of when they are about to fail: their action entropy, chunk variance or VLM-judged progress are poorly calibrated to task failure, and failures often become irrecoverable a few steps after the moment help would have mattered. Existing hybrids call the big model on a fixed schedule, at sub-task boundaries, or after a failure is detected, which either wastes calls or calls too late. Concrete failure: on a 5-stage LIBERO-Long-style task the VLA confidently grasps the wrong object at stage 2; a fixed-interval planner call arrives after the grasp, and a post-failure detector fires only once the object is already in the drawer.

## Constraints
- **Venue and deadline**: CVPR 2027 main track (embodied / robot learning track). Paper deadline 2026-11-16 AoE; today is 2026-10-18, so every experiment must finish by about 2026-11-09.
- **Compute**: at most 2–4 consumer GPUs with 24 GB each (RTX 3090 / 4090), shared cluster, single-GPU jobs; the whole paper ≤ 40 GPU-days. Open VLAs that fit: π0 / π0.5 (~3B), SmolVLA (~0.5B), OpenVLA-OFT (7B, inference or LoRA), GR00T-class (~3B); open VLMs up to ~8B for inference or LoRA. No large-scale VLA pre-training.
- **Frontier-model access**: API access to one frontier model (e.g. GPT-6 Astra) as a teacher, reference controller or occasional helper; total API spend for the paper must stay modest (assume about US$1,000; the per-call cost must be measured, not assumed). The point of the work is to need the frontier model less, not more.
- **Robots and data**: no robot hardware is assumed. Evaluation in public simulation benchmarks (e.g. LIBERO, SimplerEnv, CALVIN, RoboCasa, ManiSkill, BEHAVIOR/Habitat-style embodied agent suites) with public checkpoints and datasets.
- **Team**: a small group experienced with diffusion transformers and RL post-training; new to robotics, so engineering risk counts against an idea.
- **Literature**: no cutoff — search up to today. arXiv work from the last 12 months counts as prior or concurrent work; frontier-model-as-controller work from Sept–Oct 2026 is the immediate context.
- **Significance bar**: a simulated CVPR panel will judge the result. Thin deltas over 2026 work and directional-only predictions have been rejected before; prefer a contribution that gives a new capability or a decisive cost/performance change, with a committed effect size on a standard benchmark.
