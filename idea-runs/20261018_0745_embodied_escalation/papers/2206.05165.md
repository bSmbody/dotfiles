# Multifidelity Reinforcement Learning with Control Variates

## What this paper does
Khairy and Balaprakash study RL when a control task can be simulated at a cheap, inaccurate low fidelity and an expensive, accurate high fidelity. Their MFMCRL estimator replays each high-fidelity episode's state-action sequence in a generative low-fidelity simulator, and uses the low-fidelity return as a control variate for the high-fidelity Monte Carlo return, with the low-fidelity mean estimated from many extra cheap rollouts. The estimator stays unbiased while its variance shrinks by (1 − ρ²), where ρ is the correlation between paired low- and high-fidelity returns. Theory bounds the high-fidelity sample size needed for policy evaluation and greedy-action selection; experiments on random MDPs and NAS-Bench-201.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the multifidelity control-variate mechanism, and what coupling between low- and high-fidelity samples does it require?
Eq 7-10 [full text, Section 3.2]: Q̂_MFMC = Q̂_hi + α*(Q_lo − Q̂_lo), where Q̂_lo is computed on low-fidelity trajectories obtained by mapping each high-fidelity trajectory through T and replaying it in the generative low-fidelity simulator, Q_lo is the low-fidelity mean estimated from additional cheap rollouts, and α* = Cov/Var is estimated from the paired samples. The required coupling is pairing: each high-fidelity return has a low-fidelity twin generated from the same states and actions, so their noise is correlated (Section 3.1: Σ_lo is generative and can start at any state-action pair; the state map T is many-to-one). Variance becomes (1 − ρ²)·Var[Q̂_hi] [Eq 9].

### Goal: How large is the gain and when does it vanish?
Theorem 1 [Section 3.3.1]: the high-fidelity sample size needed for a given accuracy scales with (1 − ρ²); Theorem 2: the probability of picking the correct greedy action rises toward 1 as |ρ| → 1 [Section 3.3.2]. Empirically, at a fixed budget of 10K high-fidelity episodes MFMCRL beats standard MC control on random MDPs, with gains growing with the number of low-fidelity rollouts per pair and with low-fidelity SNR; at SNR −10 dB "there is no benefit", because the fidelities are too weakly correlated [Section 4.1, Fig 2]. NAS-Bench-201 shows the same with 10-epoch accuracy as the low-fidelity reward, including a case where the low-fidelity state space and dynamics differ [Section 4.2]. Limitation: tabular MC only; function approximation is future work [Section 5].
