# When Replanning Becomes the Bottleneck: Budgeted Replanning for Embodied Agents

## What this paper does
BRACE is a controller for LLM replanning in embodied agents (Habitat, RoboFactory, AirSim, small real-robot study): it decides whether a trigger becomes a planner call, chooses a replanning mode, and assigns a per-call token budget B_t and latency SLO_t; E-RECAP prunes the accumulated replanning context with a learned token-utility predictor. Reports 62–92% fewer tokens per call and far lower SLO violation rates.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism decides when to call the expensive model, and does the decision depend on the remaining call budget or remaining horizon?
Triggers (periodic, failure-driven, hazard-driven) pass a stability gate u_t = 1[τ_t ∧ Δ_t ≥ δ ∧ κ_t ≥ ω] (cooldown and commit counters), with failure-aware overrides that relax gates after repeated failures; then (m_t, B_t, SLO_t) = f(τ_t, σ_t) where σ_t holds counters (steps since last call, since last plan change, consecutive failures) [full text, Section 3.2, Eq 1–2]. Budgets are per call (tokens, latency), not an episode-level call budget; no remaining-budget or horizon term.

### Goal: What signal estimates the value of a call, and does it model how much the expensive model's answer will help (not just whether the cheap policy is failing)?
No value estimate: the trigger is an externally supplied flag and the controller is rule-based on counters. The paper frames itself as "a budgeted replanning systems framework with explicit controller/accounting semantics rather than a theorem-level analysis" [Section 5]. Gains are reported "in settings where task success is already saturated", i.e. the mechanism targets cost/latency per call, not which calls change success [Abstract, Section 4.3].

### Goal: What does the paper report as the limitation of its call-allocation mechanism?
Conclusion lists as open "richer controller policies (e.g., risk-aware budget allocation and failure-conditioned overrides)" [Section 6].
