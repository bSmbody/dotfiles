# Calibrate-Then-Delegate: Safety Monitoring with Risk and Budget Guarantees via Model Cascades

## What this paper does
CTD is a two-stage cascade for LLM safety monitoring: a cheap latent probe scores every input and an expensive expert LLM classifier is called on selected inputs. Instead of delegating on probe uncertainty, CTD trains a delegation-value (DV) probe on the same activations to predict how much the expert's score improves on the probe's for the true label, and calibrates a threshold on it with Learn-then-Test plus Pareto filtering, giving finite-sample guarantees on either the expert-call rate or the cascade's error. Four safety datasets, a strong (Gemma-3-27B) and a weak (Llama-3.2-1B) expert.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the delegation trigger learn the expert's benefit rather than the cheap model's uncertainty, and how is it labelled?
Yes. DV is v(x,y) = P_expert(y|x) − P_probe(y|x) [full text, Section "Delegation-Value Probes", Eq 6], a helper-conditioned target; the DV probe is trained by squared loss on targets "computed offline from probe and expert outputs" on the whole dev split [Sections "Delegation-Value Probes", "Experimental Setup"], i.e. the expert is run on every training input. There is no budget on training-time expert calls and no sample-efficient estimator. The paper's motivation is that uncertainty "is entirely agnostic to the expert's performance" [Section "Calibrate-Then-Delegate"].

### Goal: What setting does it cover, and how well does the DV probe rank expert benefit?
Single-shot classification of independent inputs; no sequential timing, no state that becomes unrecoverable, and the expert's output overwrites a score rather than steering a trajectory [Sections "Problem Definition", "Calibrate-Then-Delegate"]. The DV probe reaches Spearman 0.33 (strong expert) and 0.57 (weak expert) with the true DV, MSE 0.14/0.16, and selects higher-value top-k sets than uncertainty [Section "Delegation Value Probe Performance"]. With the weak expert, whose average effect is negative in every group, CTD still finds positive-value subsets, while uncertainty top-k keeps negative-value subsets on two datasets [Section "Analysis Across Groups"].
