# Selective Critique for Cost-Aware LLM Agents in Long-Horizon Decision Making

## What this paper does
SAG gates calls to a critic LLM in text-based long-horizon agents (ALFWorld etc.): the actor scores admissible actions, a gate S_t = normalized entropy + 1[top-2 log-margin < δ] invokes the critic when S_t > τ, and successful (possibly critic-corrected) trajectories are periodically SFT'd into the actor so reliance on critique falls over time. ALFWorld success 24.6% → 78.4% at a ReAct-comparable token budget.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism decides when to call the expensive model, and does the decision depend on the remaining call budget or remaining horizon?
A stationary per-step threshold g_t = 1[S_t > τ], "τ is chosen as a monotone function of the critic cost c" [full text, Section 3.3, Eq 3–4]. Lemma 4.1 gives the Bayes-optimal rule 1[VoI(z_t) > c] for the single binary step decision [Section 4.1]; no remaining budget or horizon state.

### Goal: What signal estimates the value of a call, and does it model how much the expensive model's answer will help (not just whether the cheap policy is failing)?
VoI is approximated as ρ·γ·Pr(err_t | z_t), with ρ (probability critique corrects a wrong actor) and γ (gain of a correction) treated as constants, so the gate reduces to thresholding the actor's error risk via entropy/margin [Section 4.2, Eq 7]. The value of the critic's answer is not estimated per state. Actions are from a discrete admissible set, which is what makes entropy/margin computable.

### Goal: What does the paper report as the limitation of its call-allocation mechanism?
"our current gating mechanism relies on ambiguity signals derived from the actor's action distribution; alternative uncertainty or value-estimation signals may further improve critique allocation"; fixed critic architecture [Appendix A, Limitations].
