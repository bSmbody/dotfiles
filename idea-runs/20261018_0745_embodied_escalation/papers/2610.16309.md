# ReAP: From Visual Failure Recognition to Action-Conditioned Risk Prediction for Runtime Failure Monitoring

## What this paper does
ReAP is a two-stage runtime monitor. Stage 1 trains an observation-only detector (frozen DINOv2 patch tokens, Perceiver latent queries, linear projection to z_t, risk head) on successful and failed rollouts. Stage 2 freezes it and trains an action-conditioned latent transition model that predicts z_{t+H} from z_t and the policy's proposed chunk; the frozen head scores the predicted latent as future risk. Detector and predictor thresholds are calibrated separately on successful rollouts and OR-fused. Evaluated on four simulated policy-benchmark settings (e.g. OpenVLA and pi0 on LIBERO-10) and real robots.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal/mechanism does it use to decide when to intervene, and what supervision or labels does that signal rely on?
Risk targets from episode outcomes only: y_{i,t}=0 for successes and y_{i,t}=t/(T_i-1) for failures, a linear ramp to 1 at the end of a failed rollout, trained with class-weighted BCE [full text, Section 3, Eq 1]. Predictor trained with MSE to the stop-gradient future latent plus future-risk BCE [Section 4.2, Eq 2]. Alarm b_t = detector OR predictor crossing time-varying thresholds [Section 4.3, Eq 3].

### Goal: How early do failure alarms fire relative to the point where help can still change the outcome?
Measured against VLM (Qwen3.5-9B) annotated failure onset, i.e. visible failure: ReAP mean normalized lead +0.081 (OpenVLA) and +0.045 (pi0) of episode length, while baselines with higher bACC alarm after onset [Appendix D.3, Table 14]. Binary vs ramp label ablation: binary labels give much later alarms (SAFE-MLP T-det 0.925 vs 0.641; ReAP detector 0.946 vs 0.450) [Appendix D.2, Table 13], showing the timing of alarms is set by the label heuristic.

### Goal: Does good failure-prediction quality translate into intervention value, and what limitation does the paper report?
No intervention is evaluated; success is bTWA/bACC/T-det. The action-conditioned branch only evaluates the nominal policy's proposed chunk, so it estimates the risk of continuing, not the benefit of any alternative controller [Sections 3, 4.2].
