# Exploring the Limits of Combinatorial Generalization in Embodied Manipulation

## What this paper does
The paper introduces RoboCombine, a RoboTwin benchmark of 19 unseen two-stage compositions of atomic skills (380 episodes per policy). It also introduces HSE, a diagnostic interface that runs an instruction-conditioned atomic VLA under a subtask queue (from an LLM decomposer or ground truth) and a handoff cue (predicted or oracle). It finds that a start-state gap at handoffs is the bottleneck, and proposes PRTI, which post-trains atomic skills from broadened start states.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Given the correct subtask instruction, do atomic VLAs execute it from mid-task handoff states?
Often not. Under oracle-queue, oracle-cue HSE, overall ordered success is 19.7% for Motus, 6.1% for FastWAM, 3.2% for LingBot-VLA and 1.6% for π0.5 [full text, Section 4.1, Table 1]. "The correct interface alone is not sufficient … the downstream atomic skill must start from a handoff state rather than from the canonical start distribution seen during isolated atomic training" [Section 4.1]. Conversion is pair-specific: the same target skill succeeds or fails depending on the preceding skill [Fig 4]. Broadening start states with PRTI raises oracle-HSE ordered success to 18.16%, against 4.21% under random perturbation and 9.47% under a robot-reset oracle [Abstract, Section 5.2].

### Goal: How is the subtask chosen, and does the chooser matter?
An LLM decomposer is given "the closed atomic vocabulary Z" and must output "an ordered list of valid atomic task names". Non-task tokens are discarded, so the output stays within the vocabulary [Appendix C.2]. For Motus, Ordered-HSE (true queue, predicted cue) gives 17.4% vs 19.7% Oracle, with no significant difference (p ≈ 0.44) [Section 4.1]. On these explicit "s1, then s2" commands the queue is easy, and the executor dominates.

### Goal: What does full-instruction training do on unseen compositions?
It reaches 40.0% ordered success on covered composition set A but 15.0% and 4.4% on sets B and C (7.7% averaged), i.e. memorization of sequence templates [Section 4.1].
