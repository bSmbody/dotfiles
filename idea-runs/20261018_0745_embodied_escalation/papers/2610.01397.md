# Continue, Abort, or Fall: Viability-Aware Policy Selection (VAPS) for Safe Humanoid Acrobatics

## What this paper does
VAPS protects humanoid flips by switching among three RL specialist policies (nominal tracking, abort-and-land, protective fall). For the nominal and abort policies it trains separate policy-conditioned receding-horizon viability predictors, V_i^H(s_t)=Pr(no fall within H steps | s_t, pi_i), from simulated rollouts of each policy started at the states where that policy is entered at deployment, with a discounted cross-entropy loss. At run time it executes the least-sacrificial policy still predicted viable. Validated on Unitree G1 and LimX Oli in simulation and on hardware.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal/mechanism does it use to decide when to intervene, and what supervision or labels does that signal rely on?
Per-policy viability predictors f_theta_i on the last N observations, labels y_{t,i}^H = 1[time-to-fall > H] from each policy's own simulated rollouts under domain randomisation and disturbances [full text, Section III-B, Eq 5-10]. "Rollouts start from the states at which that policy is actually entered at deployment. Matching training states to the states the predictor is queried on affected the switching decision more than architecture, history length, and sensing combined" [Section III-B]. Switching rule is a viability-thresholded hierarchy [Section III-C, Eq 11].

### Goal: How early do failure alarms fire relative to the point where help can still change the outcome?
The nominal threshold trades false alarms against lead time; tau_nom=0.2 at <=1% false-alarm rate; a higher threshold hands off earlier, "giving the abort more time to recover" [Section III-C]. The core claim is that "whether a state is safe cannot be decided from the state alone. It depends on what the robot does next and on how much time remains to save it" [Section V].

### Goal: Does good failure-prediction quality translate into intervention value, and what limitation does the paper report?
Yes in its setting, because the backup's own viability is predicted, not just the nominal policy's failure; monolithic networks and distilled students "learn how to fall but not when to give up the task" [Section V]. The method needs cheap, abundant simulated rollouts of every backup policy from deployment handoff states; backups are small RL policies and the failure predicate is a physical fall over a short horizon H [Sections III-A, III-B]. Extension beyond acrobatics is left open [Section V].
