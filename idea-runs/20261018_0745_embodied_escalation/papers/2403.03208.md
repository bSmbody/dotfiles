# Active Statistical Inference

## What this paper does
Zrnic and Candès propose active inference: under a budget on how many labels can be collected, a machine-learning model's predictions stand in for unlabeled points, and labels are collected with a known probability π(x) that grows with the model's uncertainty at x. The estimator is AIPW-style: prediction plus inverse-probability-weighted residual (label minus prediction) on sampled points. It is unbiased for any model and any π, and it covers means and general convex M-estimation (GLMs). Prediction-powered inference (PPI) is the special case with uniform π. Experiments on surveys, census and AlphaFold proteomics show large label-budget savings.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism, and why does it save labels?
Collect label Y_i with probability π(X_i) and estimate with f(X_i) + (Y_i − f(X_i))·ξ_i/π(X_i) [full text, Section 4, Eq 1]. It is unbiased for every π. Its variance is (1/n)(Var(Y) + E[(Y − f(X))²(1/π(X) − 1)]) [Eq 2]. So labels are needed only where the prediction is wrong: "for instances for which the predictor is correct, i.e. f(X)=Y, we would ideally like to set π(X)=0 as this incurs no additional variance" [Section 4]. The rule sets π ∝ u(x), an estimate of |Y − f(X)|, rescaled to the budget [Section 4]. For classification with probabilities p(x), u(x) = K/(K−1)·(1 − max_i p_i(x)) [Eq 3].

### Goal: Does it extend beyond means to fitting a model, and what keeps it stable?
Yes. For convex M-estimation the loss is L^π(θ) = (1/n)Σ[ℓ_θ(X_i, f(X_i)) + (ℓ_θ(X_i, Y_i) − ℓ_θ(X_i, f(X_i)))·ξ_i/π(X_i)], which is unbiased for the population loss, with a CLT that gives valid intervals [Section 5, Eq 6, Theorem 1]. A sequential version lets u(x) be learned from the labels collected so far [Section 6]. Because an uncertainty estimate that wrongly reads near zero blows up the variance, π is mixed with a uniform rule, π^(τ) = (1 − τ)π + τ·n_b/n, with τ tuned on held-out data or set to 0.5 [Section 7.2].

### Goal: How large are the savings, and over what baselines?
At matched interval width it saves over 85% (Biden approval) and over 70% (Trump approval) of the classical i.i.d. label budget, and about 25% over uniform PPI [Section 8.1]. On census data it saves about 80% over classical inference and over 60% over PPI [Section 8.2]. On AlphaFold proteomics it saves over 75% over classical and 20–25% over PPI [Section 8.3]. Fine-tuning the model on the collected labels adds 20–45% further savings [Sections 8.4–8.5]. The gain over PPI therefore comes from placing labels where the model errs, and it is largest when errors are concentrated.
