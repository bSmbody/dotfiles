# Reward-DAgger: Robot-Gated Interactive Imitation Learning with General-Purpose Progress-Based Reward Models

## What this paper does
Reward-DAgger gates human takeover in interactive imitation learning with a policy-agnostic signal: a general-purpose VLM progress/reward model phi_t=f_psi(o_<=t, l). A short-window gate fires on a sharp progress drop (Spearman correlation below tau_s and max drop >= delta_min); a long-window gate fires on a plateau (correlation below tau_l). Human corrections are aggregated and the policy is fine-tuned over rounds with the gate fixed. Evaluated on four simulated and four real tasks with diffusion policies and VLAs.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal/mechanism does it use to decide when to intervene, and what supervision or labels does that signal rely on?
The gate g_t = g^s_t OR g^l_t over windows of predicted progress [full text, Section III-A, Eq 2-4]; no failure labels are trained, the reward model is pretrained, and the gate configuration (L_s, tau_s, delta_min, L_l, tau_l) is selected on source tasks and transferred [Sections I, III-A].

### Goal: How early do failure alarms fire relative to the point where help can still change the outcome?
By construction after evidence: a drop requires progress already lost, a plateau requires a sustained stall, so repeated failed grasps "may persist for several seconds before triggering" [Section V, Limitations].

### Goal: Does good failure-prediction quality translate into intervention value, and what limitation does the paper report?
It reports downstream policy improvement and return on human effort, but the authors state the framework "is reactive rather than preemptive, requesting human assistance only after evidence of a failure emerges, which limits its applicability ... when failures are difficult to recover from", and suggest predictive world models as the fix [Section V].
