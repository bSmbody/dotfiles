# AEGIS: A Backup Reflex for Physical AI

## What this paper does
AEGIS is a cascade controller: a weak VLA (SmolVLA, 450M) drives by default and a two-layer MLP probe on its frozen action-expert activations (layer 15, mean-pooled over the chunk) predicts eventual trajectory failure. When the split-conformal threshold is crossed (after an early-harm gate t>=0.2T and under a per-episode fire cap), control hands to a stronger separate policy (pi0.5, 4.14B) at the next chunk boundary, held for >=3 chunks with hysteretic de-escalation. A pre-registered four-arm factorial (weak-only, targeted, budget-matched blind, rate-matched random) on LIBERO-Spatial isolates the value of trigger timing.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal/mechanism does it use to decide when to intervene, and what supervision or labels does that signal rely on?
The trigger is the probe output g(h_t) trained with class-weighted BCE against the eventual weak-policy trajectory outcome y (fail=1), only on early steps t<=0.3T, split at trajectory level [full text, Section 3.1]. Thresholds come from split-conformal quantiles of scores on would-not-fail weak trajectories (alpha=0.10), optionally per difficulty stratum [Section 3.2]. Chunk-overlap delta, STAC variance and flow-sampling MAD are logged as complements; token entropy and MC-dropout sit near chance and are not used [Section 3.1]. The label never involves the strong policy: it is P(weak policy fails), not whether the switch would rescue.

### Goal: How early do failure alarms fire relative to the point where help can still change the outcome?
Early-window AUROC (first 30% of steps) is 0.764 [Abstract]. The paper shows a late-detection miss where the gate fires only past 0.8T [Section 3.3, Fig 6]. In the HARD tercile the targeted-vs-blind contrast interval touches zero, which the authors attribute to little recoverable margin even for the strong policy [Section 5, confirmatory result].

### Goal: Does good failure-prediction quality translate into intervention value, and what limitation does the paper report?
Partly: targeted escalation recovers 10.1% of weak-only failures vs 4.6% blind and 5.1% random at matched 38% strong duty cycle, recover-to-disrupt ratio 6.5 [Section 5]. But it captures about a third of the available headroom: always-strong recovers 31.9% of the same pool and HELM-style or GR00T arms that run the strong policy from the first flagged step reach 15.5% [Section 5]. The 38% duty cycle is high for an expensive helper, and the replicate-resampled B-C gap shrinks to a median 2.5 pp [Section 5.1]. Cost is a parameter-count schematic, not measured [Section 3.3]. The escalation target is another local VLA, not a planner-level frontier model.
