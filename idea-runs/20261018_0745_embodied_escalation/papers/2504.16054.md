# π0.5: a Vision-Language-Action Model with Open-World Generalization

## What this paper does
π0.5 co-trains one VLA on multi-robot data, high-level subtask annotations, verbal instructions and web data. At inference it first predicts a text subtask from the overall prompt and the images, then generates actions with a flow-matching action expert conditioned only on that subtask. It evaluates generalization to unseen homes and ablates the co-training recipe and the source of the high-level subtask.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Is the low-level action policy conditioned on a subtask instruction that another planner could supply?
Yes. The model factorizes π(a, ℓ̂ | o, ℓ) = π(a | o, ℓ̂)·π(ℓ̂ | o, ℓ), "where the action distribution does not depend on ℓ, only on ℓ̂" [full text, Section IV-A]. Ablations swap in other high-level policies while keeping "the full π0.5 low-level inference process": the model's own HL, an expert human HL "oracle", and GPT-4 [Section V-E]. Because subtask prediction is trained into the same model, its own text head gives a probability over candidate subtask strings for free.

### Goal: Does a frontier model as the subtask chooser beat the in-domain high-level policy?
No, in their setting. GPT-4 was "prompt[ed] with a description of the task and a list of the most used labels to choose from", and "the zero-shot GPT-4 ablation attains the worst performance". The full π0.5 HL beats even the human-HL oracle [Section V-E, Fig 13]. Per task, π0.5 beats GPT-4 HL on Items-in-Drawer and Dishes-in-Sink, and the choice of HL matters little on the shorter, less semantic tasks [Appendix A-D, Fig 17]. So a frontier model's subtask choice is not automatically better than a robot-tuned local one. Its edge has to be shown in the target setting.
