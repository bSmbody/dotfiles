# RESCUE: Is This Alarm Worth Acting On? Counterfactual Evaluation of VLA Runtime Monitors

## What this paper does
RESCUE evaluates existing VLA runtime monitors as intervention rules rather than as failure classifiers. It saves full simulator+policy checkpoints at action-chunk boundaries of OpenVLA-OFT and pi0.5 rollouts on LIBERO-Spatial/LIBERO-Plus, restores them, and runs seeded counterfactual branches under a declared intervention repertoire (Continue, scripted Recover; stop-and-reset analytically). This yields a state-intervention success probability Q^succ(s_t,a), a recoverability horizon (latest checkpoint whose best intervention clears tau), and a cost-to-completion decision value. 1,562 trunk episodes, 128,340 branches. It trains no new detector.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal/mechanism does it use to decide when to intervene, and what supervision or labels does that signal rely on?
It does not propose a trigger; it measures what triggers should target. Recoverability is defined per state-intervention pair: V_A(s_t)=max_a Q^succ(s_t,a), estimated by restoring s_t and running K=4 (expanded to 16) branches with a lower Jeffreys quantile [full text, Section III-A, Eq 1-6]. Monitors evaluated are steps-since-progress, action-jerk, pi0.5 sampling disagreement, and a SAFE-style MLP/LSTM on OpenVLA-OFT hidden states, each thresholded at a nominal 5% false-alarm rate [Section IV-A]. All of these score failure of the nominal policy; none conditions on the intervention.

### Goal: How early do failure alarms fire relative to the point where help can still change the outcome?
Late. Under Continue, every real monitor alarms after the recoverability horizon in more than half of the episodes where both are defined; SAFE-style timing rates fall inside the random-alarm interval [Section IV-E]. On OFT/LIBERO-Plus the median horizon is step 44 of 220, a median 7 steps after the first grasp attempt and 34 steps before the visible object drop [Section IV-B]; steps-since-progress alarms at mean step 133 on failures [Table I]. Only 14/66 failures are ever recoverable under Continue vs 34/66 under Continue-or-Recover, so recoverability depends on which intervention is available, and under Recover it is non-monotone in time on 8/20 episodes [Sections IV-B, IV-E].

### Goal: Does good failure-prediction quality translate into intervention value, and what limitation does the paper report?
No. Detection/timing metrics admit real monitors in 61/75 verdicts, decision value against never-intervening admits 0/15 [Table II]. Even an idealised immediate detector can afford only a 2.0-3.3% false-alarm rate under stop-and-reset [Section IV-C]. Routing the same alarm to Recover instead of reset cuts excess cost 3.1-13.4x, showing that alarm value depends on the triggered intervention [Section IV-D]. Limitations: only Continue and one scripted Recover; no learned or stronger-policy repertoire; MuJoCo/LIBERO only; all failures are 220-step timeouts, which inflates episode-level AUROC (a pure length score gets AUROC 1.000) [Sections IV-E, V].
