# When to Ask for Help: A Decision-Theoretic Account and Measurement Standard for Slow-Module Scheduling in Dual-System Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
Formalizes slow-module invocation in dual-system VLAs as minimizing E[w_fail·1{fail} + c_slow·Σ r_t], defines a Value of Intervention VoI(s) = E[Δ(s)] with Δ = Q*(s) − Q_f(s) (value of one slow correction then optimal continuation minus fast-only value), shows fixed-ratio (FiS), fixed-budget (Hume) and history-gated (Jev) schedules are degenerate VoI rules, derives a renewal-optimal periodic interval under state-independent collapse, proposes a threshold router p̂·μ̂ > c_slow, a leak-free four-measure protocol, and a schedule compiler. Closed-loop tests on LIBERO (OpenVLA-7B fast, Qwen2.5-VL-7B slow, ρ = 1.53) and analysis of 929 RoboICL GPT-6 Astra traces.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism decides when to call the expensive model, and does the decision depend on the remaining call budget or remaining horizon?
A per-step threshold: query iff p̂(s_t)·μ̂(s_t) > c_slow [full text, Section 3.5, Eq 10]. The budget enters only as a Lagrangian per-step price: "Consider the Lagrangian of (1) with the budget as a per-step cost c_slow … up to continuation-value terms absorbed into Δ by dynamic programming" [full text, Appendix A]. Neither the remaining number of calls nor the remaining horizon is a state variable of the router; budget matching is done by sweeping τ offline [Section 3.5 (i)]. The renewal result (Prop 4) gives a periodic optimum with interval sqrt(2c_slow/(w_fail π)) under state-independent collapse. The full online router (iii), a two-head MLP on fast-policy features, is "validated in this paper only through its two operating proxies" [Section 3.5].

### Goal: What signal estimates the value of a call, and does it model how much the expensive model's answer will help (not just whether the cheap policy is failing)?
VoI factorizes as magnitude × collapse probability; "the three source systems implicitly fix one of the two" [Section 3.2]. In the experiments the magnitude head μ̂ is not instantiated: proxies are a calibrated uncertainty threshold (synthetic) and logistic regression over schedule features (traces). The closed-loop LIBERO test shows this missing term is decisive: "the adaptive trigger allocates its queries exactly as the threshold rule predicts, yet a subgoal-rewrite channel cannot convert them into success" (pooled 43% vs fast-only 58%); on task 3 the fast policy solves 8/10, periodic rewrite 2/10, adaptive 4/10 despite the detector correctly seeing stalls (159 queries) — "a query that returns a plausible-but-wrong subgoal is worse than no query" [Section 5.10, Table 9]. Only the revert actuator (undo a rewrite that fails to restore progress) has zero net downside (54/90 vs 52/90). Leak-free early-warning AUC on traces is 0.62 vs 0.93 post hoc [Section 5.4].

### Goal: What does the paper report as the limitation of its call-allocation mechanism?
"ρ prices a query but the channel decides whether the answer can land" [Section 6, Mid-continuum boundary case]; remaining work lists online-router closure, live gate replacement on RoboICL, and "Mixture routing … over intervention types" as untested [Section 5.11]. State-uncertainty features were unavailable in the traces [Section 6].

### Goal: Does any closed-loop arm separate wrong subgoals from the executor's inability to follow a rewritten instruction (e.g. an oracle or correct-subgoal arm)?
No. The rewrite channel replaces the instruction the LIBERO-fine-tuned OpenVLA-7B conditions on until the next query [full text, Section 5.10]; arms vary trigger, dose, cleaning, screening and an undo, but none issues a ground-truth subgoal, so the harm cannot be attributed. The paper reads it as "a query that returns a plausible-but-wrong subgoal is worse than no query" [Section 5.10]. The harm is large and has no dose at which it helps: rewrite arms at periods 50/25/10 score 36/90, 39/90, 34/90 vs fast-only 52/90 [Section 5.10, "The dose axis"]. On LIBERO-Goal the periodic rewrite collapses 48/100 → 6/100 (43 vs 1 discordant pairs), while the revert arm matches fast-only [Section 5.10, "The second suite"]. Cleaning a meta prefix from the reply recovered 10 points on the periodic arm, so phrasing alone moves outcomes [Section 5.10]. Per-task rewrite harm does not track fast-policy competence (Spearman 0.32, p=0.18) [Section 5.10].
