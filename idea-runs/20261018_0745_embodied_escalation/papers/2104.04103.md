# Causal Decision Making and Causal Effect Estimation Are Not the Same… and Why It Matters

## What this paper does
A perspective paper (Fernández-Loría and Provost) separating causal effect estimation (estimand τ(x)) from causal decision making (estimand a*(x) = 1(τ(x) > threshold)). It argues that models optimized for treatment assignment (regret / ranking) can beat effect-MSE models, that confounding hurts decisions less than estimates, and that a non-causal outcome-prediction model can sometimes target as well as an uplift model, giving the conditions under which this proxy works and when it fails. Illustrated with the Criteo randomized uplift dataset.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: When does targeting by outcome risk (a non-causal score) work, and when does it fail compared with targeting by treatment effect?
Outcome prediction is a reasonable proxy when [full text, Section 3.3, numbered list]: (1) outcomes and effects are correlated; (2) outcomes are easier to estimate than effects, e.g. when data for one treatment arm are limited by experimentation cost or the signal-to-noise ratio is higher for outcomes; (3) predictions are only used to rank. On Criteo, a tree predicting conversion without the ad targeted at least as well as a causal tree, because "features predictive of conversions are also predictive of treatment effects" and conversions have higher SNR [Section 3.3, Fig 3]. It fails "in customer retention applications where modeling negative effects is important" [Section 3.3, citing Ascarza 2018 and Radcliffe and Surry 2011], i.e. when the treatment can harm some units or when effect and outcome decouple.

### Goal: What objective should a learned treatment-assignment rule optimize?
Regret E[Y(a*(X)) − Y(â(X))] rather than effect MSE [Section 3.1, Eq 3-4]; a model with larger effect-MSE can make better decisions if it ranks/signs correctly [Fig 1]. With randomized data the targeting value of any scoring rule can be estimated directly as E[D]·E[Y|T=1,D=1] + (1−E[D])·E[Y|T=0,D=0] [Section 3.3, Eq 5]. Under budget constraints, targeting by largest CATE may not be optimal when costs vary; the threshold can come from a budget or cost/benefit matrix [Section 2, footnote 12].
