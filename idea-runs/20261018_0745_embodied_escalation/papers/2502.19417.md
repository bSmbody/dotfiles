# Hi Robot: Open-Ended Instruction Following with Hierarchical Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
Hi Robot splits control into a high-level VLM that maps an open-ended prompt and the images to a short atomic command ℓ̂, and a low-level π0-style VLA that executes ℓ̂. The low level is trained on demonstrations segmented into 1–3 s skills with skill labels. The high level is trained on these labels plus synthetic user interactions. It is evaluated on real single-arm, bimanual and mobile robots against GPT-4o as the high level and a flat VLA.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does a low-level VLA trained on segmented per-skill labels actually execute supplied subtask commands?
Yes. The low-level policy p_lo(A | I, ℓ̂, q) is trained on D_labeled ∪ D_demo, where D_labeled comes from segmenting demonstrations into short labelled skills [full text, Sections 4.1, 4.3]. "With human high-level instructions, the low-level policy executes nearly flawlessly, showing that failures stem more from reasoning than actuation" [Section 5.3, finding (4)].

### Goal: How does a frontier VLM as the high-level chooser compare with the trained local high level?
It is worse. GPT-4o "frequently loses context once physical interaction begins, issuing nonsensical commands (e.g., 'pick up bermuda triangle')" and issues commands such as picking up new objects while the gripper is occupied. Hi Robot has substantially higher instruction accuracy and task progress [Section 5.3, findings (1)–(2)]. Part of the frontier's failure is commands outside the executor's vocabulary.
