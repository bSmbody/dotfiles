# RouteLLM: Learning to Route LLMs with Preference Data

## What this paper does
Learns a binary router between a strong (expensive) and a weak (cheap) LLM. A win-prediction model estimates P(strong model's answer beats the weak model's | query) from pairwise preference labels (Chatbot Arena battles, augmented with MMLU golden labels and ~120K GPT-4-judge comparisons costing ~US$700), and the query goes to the strong model when this probability exceeds a cost threshold α. Router parameterizations: similarity-weighted ranking, matrix factorization, BERT and causal-LLM classifiers.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the router's training target depend on the expensive model's counterfactual benefit, and how are its labels obtained?
Yes, the target is helper-conditioned: P_θ(win_s | q), fit by maximum likelihood on comparisons of the strong and weak responses to the *same* query [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 1-2]. Labels are paired: both models answer every training query (Arena battles, or responses generated from both models then judged) [Section 4.1, 4.1.1]. Label sparsity is named as the key challenge and is handled by tiering models and buying more judged pairs, not by a sample-efficient estimator [Section 4.1].

### Goal: What setting does it cover and what does it not cover?
Single-turn, one decision per query, with the whole query handed to one model [Section 3.1, Eq 3]. There is no notion of when within a sequential episode to switch, no state that becomes unrecoverable, and no reuse of the weak model's own outcomes to reduce the number of strong-model labels; the authors list multi-model routing and distribution shift as limitations [Section 6].
