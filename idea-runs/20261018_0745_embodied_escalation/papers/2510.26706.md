# Budgeted Multiple-Expert Deferral

## What this paper does
DeSalvo, Mohri, Mohri and Zhong study how to train a learning-to-defer router without querying every expert on every training example. The ground-truth label y is already known; what costs money is running an expert to learn its cost c_k(x, y). Building on IWAL, the method samples one expert per example, queries its cost with a known probability set by disagreement within the current version space, and fits the router on an importance-weighted, unbiased surrogate loss. It gives generalization and label-complexity bounds (square-root, or logarithmic, in T in the realizable case) for two-stage and single-stage deferral, and experiments on ten classification datasets.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is bought during training, and how is the query decision made?
Only the expert's output (its cost c_k(x_t, y_t)) is bought; (x, y) pairs are already labelled: "labels are already known, and the core challenge is to decide which experts to query" [full text, Section 1]. Per round, an expert k is drawn with probability q_t,k, and its cost is queried with probability p_t,k; queried examples are stored with weight 1/(q p), so the empirical surrogate loss is unbiased [Section 3, Algorithm 1]. p_t,k is the largest surrogate-loss gap for expert k between two routers still in the version space [Section 4, Algorithm 2], so examples on which all surviving routers agree are not queried.

### Goal: Does it use a free predictor of the expert's output, or skip points where the expert's answer cannot change the loss?
No free predictor of the expert's output is used (no prediction-powered control variate); savings come only from IWAL-style disagreement sampling over the router's version space [Sections 3–4, 6]. Points are skipped when the surviving routers agree on that expert's loss, not by a value-of-answer bound.

### Goal: How large are the savings, and what extension is left open?
Same system accuracy as standard deferral (which queries all n_e experts per example) with at most one query per example; reductions generally to 35–40% of queries on binary datasets and below 30% on multi-class, growing with the number of experts [Section 7, Results]. The conclusion names "integration with reinforcement learning for sequential or interactive decision-making" as future work [Section 8].
