# FailureSpot: Label-Efficient Timestamp-Level Failure Detection for Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
FailureSpot targets timestamp-level VLA failure detection. It argues SAFE's trajectory-level labels mark normal pre-failure steps of failed rollouts as failures. It pretrains an MLP/LSTM on VLA internal representations with soft pseudo-labels built from action-chunk signals (overlap inconsistency, full-chunk and executed action magnitudes, with EMAs, percentile-normalised), then uses entropy-based active learning to request dense human timestamp labels for the most uncertain trajectories, and thresholds with functional CP. Evaluated on three VLA policies.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal/mechanism does it use to decide when to intervene, and what supervision or labels does that signal rely on?
p_{i,t}=f_theta(h_{i,t}) on VLA hidden states [full text, Method: Problem Setting]. Weak labels: s = [c+E(c)] + eta1[r+E(r)] + eta2[r_exec+E(r_exec)], clipped to [0,1] [Method: Pretraining, Eq 1-3]. Human labels mark a timestamp as failure "when the robot no longer makes meaningful progress toward the task objective" (missed target, stuck, idle, erratic motion) [Method: Timestamp-level failure annotation].

### Goal: How early do failure alarms fire relative to the point where help can still change the outcome?
Its reference is the annotated failure onset (first no-progress timestamp); at the best-bal-acc alpha it alarms before the average ground-truth onset [Evaluation: Accuracy and Failure-Onset Time]. Onset is defined by observable lack of progress, not by whether any intervention could still succeed.

### Goal: Does good failure-prediction quality translate into intervention value, and what limitation does the paper report?
Not evaluated; using the detector for intervention and recovery is stated as future work [Conclusion]. Dense onset annotation is the costly ingredient the method tries to economise [Introduction].
