# Ask4Help: Learning to Leverage an Expert for Embodied Tasks

## What this paper does
Ask4Help augments a frozen embodied agent (RoboTHOR ObjectNav, iTHOR rearrangement) with a small MLP+GRU policy that, at every step, chooses whether the agent or an expert acts. It reads the frozen agent's recurrent belief state, is trained with DD-PPO on a reward that penalises task failure heavily and expert help lightly, and is conditioned on an embedded reward configuration so one policy covers many help-cost preferences. The expert in training is the simulator's NavMesh shortest-path oracle; noisy-oracle and human experts are used only at test time.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What target does the help trigger learn — the cheap agent's failure risk or the value of the expert's help — and how is it supervised?
The value of help. The Ask4Help policy is an RL policy over {Agent, Expert} whose return is r_fail·1{fail} + r_init_ask·1{first ask} + r_step_ask per expert step [full text, Sections 3.2, 4.3.2], so its learned action values compare the outcome with the expert acting against the outcome with the frozen agent acting: a helper-conditioned target, not a failure probability. Baselines it beats are a random helper and a "model confusion" top-2 margin rule [Section 4.2].

### Goal: How many expert interventions does training consume, and is the expert cheap?
Training runs DD-PPO for 15 million steps with the NavMesh oracle as the expert [Section 4.3.2], i.e. the expert is a free simulator oracle queried on-policy as often as the RL exploration asks. The paper does not attempt sample-efficient learning under a budget on training-time expert queries; human/noisy experts appear only at inference [Section 4.3.2].
