### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=novelty)`
- **Evidence**:
  - Grounding holds. [1706.03461] is stated correctly: impute D̃1 = Y1 − μ̂0(X) on the scarce treated arm and regress it. The fast-rate argument needs m ≫ n and a CATE simpler than μ0, and Simulation 1 (1% treated, an indicator CATE in 20 dimensions) is the regime C1 invokes. [2104.04103] is also stated correctly: risk targeting works when outcome and effect correlate and outcomes have higher SNR, it fails when effects can be negative or decouple, and the rule should be judged by regret. The in-domain facts check out against the digests: AEGIS's label is weak-only failure, 10.1% vs 31.9% headroom, and the hard-tercile null [2606.06660]; RESCUE's horizon is a median 34 steps before the drop and monitors alarm after it [2610.13516]; the rewrite channel took a task from 8/10 to 4/10 [2610.21160]; VAPS conditions on the backup [2610.01397].
  - Novelty, missing embodied priors. "No trigger regresses the helper effect" is false outside VLA monitoring. Ask4Help [2211.09960] trains a {agent, expert} policy on a frozen embodied agent's belief state with RL, using a large failure penalty and a small help penalty, so it learns assisted-vs-unassisted value, not failure risk. When2Ask [2306.03604] learns Ask/Not-Ask with PPO on task return with the LLM planner inside the environment. UniIntervene++ [2610.03620] learns SMDP Double-DQN values over {RL policy, two assist controllers} with per-call costs on a real robot. C1 cites none of them. Its real delta is narrower: all three treat helper calls as free during training (a NavMesh oracle for 15M steps, an on-policy LLM, local unbilled controllers), and C1 learns the same target from a few hundred billed calls.
  - Novelty, missing routing prior. Causal LLM Routing [2505.16037] already brings causal effect estimation to model routing. It learns from partial feedback (one model per query), imputes counterfactual utility with a doubly robust estimator (meta-learners named as alternatives) and trains for regret. C1's claim that routers need "both models labelled on every query" is out of date. So the cross-domain jump of "uplift/CATE to call routing" is already taken, and C1's remaining differences are in-domain: a sequential call time, a point of no return, and a simulator that supplies the control arm for free.
  - Thinness risk. When the simulator gives μ0(s) almost exactly by checkpoint-restore branching (C1's own plan, following [2610.13516]), the X-learner reduces to regressing the called outcome minus the cheap policy's value at the call state. That is a one-step, baseline-subtracted advantage from a randomized single-action logging policy, i.e. a contextual bandit with a known baseline. A CVPR panel will read this as "advantage regression with V^cheap as baseline" unless C1 states clearly what the causal framing adds beyond the RL priors above. Candidates are the randomized, known-propensity design, which removes the confounding that on-policy RL triggers inherit, and the label-cost accounting.
  - Mapping and transfer assumption pass. The roles are explicit: unit = chunk-boundary state, control = continue the cheap VLA, treatment = one fixed-channel frontier call then hand-back, μ0 = TD/branched V^cheap, τ = imputed helper advantage. The key assumption is named (frozen-VLA features separate savable from lost) and is plausible given AEGIS's early-window AUROC of 0.764 [2606.06660]. The binding risk is effective sample size, not the mapping. τ is non-zero only in cheap-policy failures, and there only inside the pre-horizon window (14/66 failures recoverable under Continue in [2610.13516]). A strong cheap VLA rarely fails on LIBERO, so a few hundred binary called outcomes may contain only a few dozen informative ones, and C1 already concedes the [2104.04103] collapse to risk ranking. A ~50K-token Astra call [2609.34261] also makes a "take over for a span" treatment several calls per unit.
  - Contribution type passes: a new trigger-learning method (estimator plus data-collection design), not analysis or a benchmark.
  - Suggested refinement for the innovator, under novelty: (a) reposition the claim against [2211.09960], [2306.03604], [2610.03620] and [2505.16037] as "helper-conditioned triggers at a training-label cost below the deployment savings". (b) Give the estimator a mechanism the RL and routing priors lack, aimed at the sparse-band problem. For example, use the free simulator twice: learn μ0 and also a free surrogate uplift (a local π0.5 helper or scripted Recover branches, as in [2610.13516]) as the effect-head prior or covariate, and spend the billed calls only on the frontier-minus-surrogate residual. That stacks the X-learner logic a second time and gives the paper a falsifiable label-efficiency claim. Then commit to an effect size: training-plus-deployment frontier calls needed to match a risk-thresholded AEGIS-style trigger.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Learning when to call a helper from its counterfactual value, and causal partial-feedback routing, both already exist ([2211.09960], [2306.03604], [2610.03620]; [2505.16037]), and with a simulator-exact control arm C1's X-learner reduces to baseline-subtracted advantage regression on a few dozen informative binary outcomes, so its delta is a label-efficiency claim that is not yet backed by a mechanism stronger than what those priors would do with a budget.
