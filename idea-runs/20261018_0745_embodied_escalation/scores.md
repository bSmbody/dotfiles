### C1 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=novelty)`
- **Evidence**:
  - Grounding holds. [1706.03461] is stated correctly: impute D̃1 = Y1 − μ̂0(X) on the scarce treated arm and regress it. The fast-rate argument needs m ≫ n and a CATE simpler than μ0, and Simulation 1 (1% treated, an indicator CATE in 20 dimensions) is the regime C1 invokes. [2104.04103] is also stated correctly: risk targeting works when outcome and effect correlate and outcomes have higher SNR, it fails when effects can be negative or decouple, and the rule should be judged by regret. The in-domain facts check out against the digests: AEGIS's label is weak-only failure, 10.1% vs 31.9% headroom, and the hard-tercile null [2606.06660]; RESCUE's horizon is a median 34 steps before the drop and monitors alarm after it [2610.13516]; the rewrite channel took a task from 8/10 to 4/10 [2610.21160]; VAPS conditions on the backup [2610.01397].
  - Novelty, missing embodied priors. "No trigger regresses the helper effect" is false outside VLA monitoring. Ask4Help [2211.09960] trains a {agent, expert} policy on a frozen embodied agent's belief state with RL, using a large failure penalty and a small help penalty, so it learns assisted-vs-unassisted value, not failure risk. When2Ask [2306.03604] learns Ask/Not-Ask with PPO on task return with the LLM planner inside the environment. UniIntervene++ [2610.03620] learns SMDP Double-DQN values over {RL policy, two assist controllers} with per-call costs on a real robot. C1 cites none of them. Its real delta is narrower: all three treat helper calls as free during training (a NavMesh oracle for 15M steps, an on-policy LLM, local unbilled controllers), and C1 learns the same target from a few hundred billed calls.
  - Novelty, missing routing prior. Causal LLM Routing [2505.16037] already brings causal effect estimation to model routing. It learns from partial feedback (one model per query), imputes counterfactual utility with a doubly robust estimator (meta-learners named as alternatives) and trains for regret. C1's claim that routers need "both models labelled on every query" is out of date. So the cross-domain jump of "uplift/CATE to call routing" is already taken, and C1's remaining differences are in-domain: a sequential call time, a point of no return, and a simulator that supplies the control arm for free.
  - Thinness risk. When the simulator gives μ0(s) almost exactly by checkpoint-restore branching (C1's own plan, following [2610.13516]), the X-learner reduces to regressing the called outcome minus the cheap policy's value at the call state. That is a one-step, baseline-subtracted advantage from a randomized single-action logging policy, i.e. a contextual bandit with a known baseline. A CVPR panel will read this as "advantage regression with V^cheap as baseline" unless C1 states clearly what the causal framing adds beyond the RL priors above. Candidates are the randomized, known-propensity design, which removes the confounding that on-policy RL triggers inherit, and the label-cost accounting.
  - Mapping and transfer assumption pass. The roles are explicit: unit = chunk-boundary state, control = continue the cheap VLA, treatment = one fixed-channel frontier call then hand-back, μ0 = TD/branched V^cheap, τ = imputed helper advantage. The key assumption is named (frozen-VLA features separate savable from lost) and is plausible given AEGIS's early-window AUROC of 0.764 [2606.06660]. The binding risk is effective sample size, not the mapping. τ is non-zero only in cheap-policy failures, and there only inside the pre-horizon window (14/66 failures recoverable under Continue in [2610.13516]). A strong cheap VLA rarely fails on LIBERO, so a few hundred binary called outcomes may contain only a few dozen informative ones, and C1 already concedes the [2104.04103] collapse to risk ranking. A ~50K-token Astra call [2609.34261] also makes a "take over for a span" treatment several calls per unit.
  - Contribution type passes: a new trigger-learning method (estimator plus data-collection design), not analysis or a benchmark.
  - Suggested refinement for the innovator, under novelty: (a) reposition the claim against [2211.09960], [2306.03604], [2610.03620] and [2505.16037] as "helper-conditioned triggers at a training-label cost below the deployment savings". (b) Give the estimator a mechanism the RL and routing priors lack, aimed at the sparse-band problem. For example, use the free simulator twice: learn μ0 and also a free surrogate uplift (a local π0.5 helper or scripted Recover branches, as in [2610.13516]) as the effect-head prior or covariate, and spend the billed calls only on the frontier-minus-surrogate residual. That stacks the X-learner logic a second time and gives the paper a falsifiable label-efficiency claim. Then commit to an effect size: training-plus-deployment frontier calls needed to match a risk-thresholded AEGIS-style trigger.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: Learning when to call a helper from its counterfactual value, and causal partial-feedback routing, both already exist ([2211.09960], [2306.03604], [2610.03620]; [2505.16037]), and with a simulator-exact control arm C1's X-learner reduces to baseline-subtracted advantage regression on a few dozen informative binary outcomes, so its delta is a label-efficiency claim that is not yet backed by a mechanism stronger than what those priors would do with a budget.


### C2 verdict

- **Verdict**: `revise(focus=mapping)`
- **Evidence**:
  - Grounding mostly holds. [1810.11646] is stated correctly. The method fits ω̂ on the big sample, then fits only a parametric correction η = τ − ω on the randomized sample, with target q(X)Y; the rate is O_p(r(n) + 1/n); it learns η ≈ 0 when ω is unbiased; and Section 6 names non-comparable interventions as open. [2206.05165] is also stated correctly as a mechanism: paired low-fidelity twins, a (1 − ρ²) reduction in variance and in high-fidelity samples, and no gain once the fidelities decorrelate. The in-domain facts check out against the digests: RESCUE's 14/66 vs 34/66 [2610.13516], CTD's Spearman 0.33–0.57 with the expert run on every input [2604.14251], and Astra's 9.53M tokens over 181 calls [2609.34261].
  - Mapping error in source (b). In MFMC [2206.05165] the scarce item is the high-fidelity return, so pairing saves expensive samples. In C2 the scarce item is the frontier's answer: one subgoal string per billed call. Once that string is in hand, its executor outcome can be branched over any number of seeds from the same checkpoint at no API cost. Physical outcome noise is therefore not scarce, and the (1 − ρ²) cut in billed labels does not apply. The only billed-label noise left is which answer the frontier gives at s. This makes the "three compounding reasons" double-count. It also makes committed effect (i), ≥4× over C1 at 1,200 calls, a comparison against a strawman unless C1 also gets free re-branching of each frontier answer. C2's own observation points to the correct mapping: "whenever the two issue the same subgoal their outcomes are identical". The correction to learn is the frontier's choice of subgoal, not a residual return.
  - Mapping error in the "ceiling". The canonical oracle subgoal is not an upper bound on frontier uplift through the instruction channel, because a different subgoal, order or phrasing can suit this executor better. [2610.21160] shows phrasing alone moves outcomes: stripping a meta prefix from the reply recovered 10 points on the periodic arm. So committed effect (iii), zero calls on zero-oracle-uplift states, can drop savable states. A real within-channel ceiling is max over g of U(s, g), taken over an enumerated subgoal set. LIBERO's BDDL goal predicates make that set finite (objects × target relations), and each U(s, g) costs only free branches.
  - The key transfer assumption is unstated and contradicted by the only closed-loop evidence. C2 assumes a correct subgoal sent to the cheap VLA produces uplift on a non-trivial band of states. In [2610.21160], replacing the instruction of a LIBERO-fine-tuned VLA hurt at every dose (36/90, 39/90, 34/90 vs fast-only 52/90). On LIBERO-Goal it collapsed success from 48/100 to 6/100. No arm sent a ground-truth subgoal, so "wrong subgoal" cannot be told apart from "executor cannot follow subgoal text". If the second holds, the oracle band is empty and there is nothing for either layer to learn. This is fixable at zero API cost. Choose an executor trained on per-subtask instructions: LIBERO-10 tasks are compositions of LIBERO-90-style single-step tasks, or the team can LoRA on segmented demos. Then make the measured size of the positive-ceiling band a week-1 go/no-go.
  - Second unstated assumption: the frontier has a semantic edge that matters here. On LIBERO-Long the full instruction is given and decomposes trivially, so the local 7B stand-in may already pick the right subgoal. Then frontier-minus-surrogate is ≈ 0, which makes C2's own trigger look good but leaves the frontier unnecessary. That fails the problem's premise and the significance bar. C2 needs a setting where the local VLM's subgoal choice measurably trails the frontier's, such as novel compositions or perturbed instructions. It should state that gap as an assumption.
  - Novelty. The delta over Ask4Help [2211.09960] (free oracle, no correction), CTD [2604.14251] (expert on every input) and C1 is real, but a closer in-domain prior is missing. RoboAware [2610.11480] already learns helper-conditioned values from state-locked counterfactual branches restored from simulator snapshots on LIBERO-Pro, RoboSuite and RoboTwin. It calls the billed frontier coding agent (Claude Opus 4.8 / GPT-5.5) in every branch, K = 20 suffix simulations per root, and reports no training-call cost. So checkpoint-branching for helper values is taken. C2's delta is the stand-in fusion plus training-call accounting, and RoboAware is good evidence for the "dense billed branching" failure C2 attacks. It chooses a code family, not whether to call, so it is not a duplicate.
  - The cross-domain source has moved on. Prediction-powered data fusion [2610.12332] keeps the randomized sample's CATE target unbiased under any large-sample bias, without a parametric model of the discrepancy, which [1810.11646] requires. That answers C2's stated main risk that the correction is not simple. The same paper also calibrates expectations: its DR/R-fusion learners reach 0.52–0.76 of trial-only CATE risk, roughly a 1.3–1.9× label saving. The rest of C2's ≥4× would have to come from in-band placement and must be argued separately.
  - Contribution type passes: a new trigger-learning method (free stand-in uplift plus a billed correction, with a placement design), not analysis or a benchmark.
  - Suggested refinement for the innovator, under mapping. (a) Re-map the billed quantity to the frontier's answer. Enumerate the channel's subgoal set G(s) from BDDL predicates. Branch every g for free to get U(s, g). Spend billed calls only on learning the frontier's answer distribution p_F(g | s), or its agreement with argmax_g U, inside the band where max_g U > 0. Predicted call value is then Σ_g p_F(g | s) U(s, g) − 0. Each billed call gives an exact categorical label, which is where a defensible label saving comes from. Drop or demote MFMC. (b) Use max_g U as the ceiling and the local VLM's p_L(g | s) as the stand-in, with the correction p_F − p_L fitted by a [1810.11646]- or [2610.12332]-style fusion. (c) Name and gate the executor-follows-subgoal assumption and the frontier-semantic-edge assumption, each measurable before any API spend. (d) Re-state effect (i) against a C1 that also re-branches answers for free, and add RoboAware [2610.11480] to the novelty paragraph.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: C2 borrows MFMC pairing for a quantity that is not scarce: given the frontier's subgoal string, outcomes branch for free, so the only billed uncertainty is which subgoal the frontier picks. Its oracle "ceiling" is not an upper bound, and the method rests on an unstated premise, that a LIBERO-trained cheap VLA executes subgoal instructions, which the only closed-loop evidence contradicts (instruction rewrites took LIBERO-Goal from 48/100 to 6/100 in [2610.21160]).
