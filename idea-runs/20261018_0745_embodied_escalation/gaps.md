
## Axis: allocating a limited budget of frontier-model calls during embodied execution

### What has been done
- **Per-step thresholding of a cheap-policy uncertainty score, with the budget as a Lagrangian price.** [2610.21160] casts slow-module invocation as min E[w_fail·1{fail} + c_slow·Σr_t] and queries iff p̂(collapse)·μ̂(gain) > c_slow; the budget is turned into a constant per-step cost, with "continuation-value terms absorbed into Δ" (Appendix A), and budget matching is done by sweeping τ. It shows fixed-ratio, fixed-budget and history-gated schedules are degenerate cases, and that under state-independent collapse the optimum is periodic. [2610.05025] (TUD) maximizes E[SR] − λE[N_G], calling a co-trained generalist when the running mean of the specialist's predictive action-plan dispersion exceeds an online percentile threshold, with a Δ_max cap. [2610.07335] (SAG, text agents) gates a critic on actor entropy plus a top-2 margin, with τ a monotone function of critic cost and VoI ≈ ρ·γ·Pr(err) for constant ρ, γ.
- **Learned help/failure classifiers with no budget variable.** [2510.01389] (INSIGHT) trains temporal transformers on π0-FAST token-uncertainty sequences to emit per-step help triggers. Trigger frequency is only measured afterwards: strong labels fire early and over-intervene, weak labels fire late or miss.
- **Rule-based controllers that cut calls or cost per call.** [2608.01428] (BRACE) gates externally supplied triggers with cooldown/commit counters and sets per-call token/latency budgets; it lists "risk-aware budget allocation" as open. [2609.34261] (RoboICL) calls GPT-6 Astra on a fixed chunk horizon H(T); an optional Jev gate reuses Astra's plan suffix without looking at RGB, which cuts calls by 33–48% but changes success unevenly across tasks (3/5→2/5, 4/5→5/5). [2610.01939] (PyRUA-Lean) keeps the frontier model in charge and cuts calls by moving retries into code cells under a hard per-episode call cap.

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Under a hard per-episode cap on frontier calls in multi-stage manipulation (e.g. a 5-stage LIBERO-Long task), prior allocators decide each call with a fixed threshold on the current-step score, using the budget only as a fixed Lagrangian price (c_slow, λ or a percentile). That rule assumes a call is worth the same whenever it is made, so whether to spend now or keep calls for later stages is never optimized, and neither the calls left nor the time left before the cheap policy's error becomes uncorrectable enters the decision.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2610.21160] comes closest. It derives the call rule from a budgeted objective, but via a Lagrangian per-step cost with continuation value absorbed into a one-shot correction gap Δ, and its renewal optimum assumes state-independent collapse. [2610.05025] thresholds plan-dispersion against an online percentile, and its stated limitation is that p "does not directly optimize for a specific target operating point". [2610.07335] uses a stationary τ tied to cost. [2608.01428] uses counters and cooldowns and names risk-aware budget allocation as open. [2609.34261] and [2610.01939] use fixed horizons or hard caps; under the cap, the tool-calling baseline in [2610.01939] ran out of LLM calls in 52 of the 96 instances that only PyRUA-Lean solved. Every one of these methods uses a memoryless rule: score now vs. a fixed threshold. Query trail: "slow-module scheduling", "dual-system VLA", "budgeted replanning", "help triggers VLA", "intervention budget", "value of intervention", "call budget VLM", "deferral robot policy", "RoboICL", "GPT-6 Astra" (recency checked to 2026-10). None of these turned up a budget-state- or deadline-aware allocator for embodied execution.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Algorithmic limitation (a myopic, stationary threshold policy). Collapsing the hard budget into a constant shadow price is exact only when the value of a call doesn't depend on timing. [2610.21160]'s own renewal analysis shows this holds only for state-independent collapse. Embodied failures break it: the gain from help decays to zero once an error becomes uncorrectable, and failure is concentrated in a few stages. The scores these thresholds use also rise late. TUD's dispersion separates failed from successful rollouts only "in the final ~20 steps" [2610.05025], and INSIGHT's labels force a choice between early-but-over-calling and late-or-missed triggers [2510.01389]. So a fixed threshold either spends the cap early or fires after the point of no return. No read method has a state variable for calls left, or an estimate of how long a detected deviation stays correctable.
- **Why it matters:** Groups who use an API frontier model as a rare helper are blocked. With ρ ≈ 10³ ([2610.21160]'s RoboICL measurement) they can afford only a handful of calls per episode, so one wasted or late call is a large share of the budget. Closing the gap would make a success-vs-calls curve achievable at a specified budget, rather than an operating point found after the fact by sweeping thresholds. It would also show whether "call before the point of no return" can be learned from rollouts.
- **Evidence route:** At matched hard caps (e.g. K = 1, 2, 4 calls per episode) on LIBERO-Long or CALVIN multi-stage tasks with a frozen cheap VLA and a fixed frontier helper, compare success against threshold-swept baselines (VoI threshold, TUD-style dispersion, INSIGHT-style classifier, fixed interval). The gap is closed if the new method (a) wins at small K, where the baselines' curves drop off, (b) leaves fewer episodes with the budget used up before the failing stage, and (c) places a larger share of calls in the window before the logged irrecoverability step, measured by replaying the oracle frontier controller from saved states.

- **Gap:** Prior call allocators score a call by how likely the cheap policy is to fail, and treat the frontier call's benefit as a constant (SAG's constant ρ·γ; TUD's single refresh action; [2610.21160]'s magnitude head μ̂, which is specified but never trained). That assumption breaks in embodied execution, because the same correctly timed call can help or hurt depending on the state and on what the frontier model is asked to do (rewrite the subgoal, rank candidate actions, revert, take over).
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2610.21160] factorizes VoI into collapse probability × gain magnitude, but runs only proxies of the probability term. Its closed-loop LIBERO arms show the consequence: the adaptive trigger allocated calls "exactly as the threshold rule predicts" yet subgoal-rewrite calls lowered pooled success (43% vs 58% fast-only). On one task, success fell from 8/10 to 4/10 even though the detector correctly flagged stalls, while a ranking channel and a revert actuator did not hurt. It lists "mixture routing over intervention types" as untested. [2610.07335] reduces VoI to ρ·γ·Pr(err) with ρ, γ constant and lists value-estimation signals as future work. [2609.34261]'s Jev gate cut Astra calls but moved success in opposite directions on two tasks, because it judges only whether replanning is unnecessary. [2610.05025] and [2510.01389] model only staleness or failure likelihood of the cheap policy. All five methods condition the call decision on the cheap policy's state alone, never on the counterfactual effect of the frontier's answer.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Training-objective mismatch / missing mechanism. The allocators are trained or calibrated on failure labels (collapse, no-progress, episode success). The quantity the budgeted objective actually needs is the counterfactual gain Q(s, call type) − Q_f(s) for each kind of frontier intervention. Estimating that gain needs paired continuations, with and without each call type, from the same state. None of the read methods gathers such data or learns that estimate. So a probability-only allocator spends budget on states where the cheap policy is failing but the available frontier channel cannot fix, or makes things worse, as in [2610.21160] Table 9.
- **Why it matters:** The problem asks both when to call and what to ask. Without a gain model for each call type, a better failure detector can lower success, as the LIBERO rewrite arms showed, and budget comparisons mix up schedule quality with channel quality. A method that learns which intervention a state can use would let a few frontier calls go where they actually change the outcome. It would also show which call types (plan, correct, take over) are worth paying for.
- **Evidence route:** From saved mid-episode states, run each frontier call type and the no-call continuation to get measured gains per state and call type. Then check two things: (a) the learned allocator's predicted gain ranks these measured gains better than failure-probability scores do, and (b) in closed loop at a matched call budget, it beats a probability-only allocator using the same detector. The test passes only if the method avoids the harmful-call states, i.e. it does not lose to the cheap policy alone on any task, as the rewrite arms did on that task.


## Axis: failure prediction signals for cheap VLA policies (when to ask for help)

### What has been done
- **Probes on frozen VLA internals, trained on the eventual outcome of the nominal policy.** SAFE [2506.09937] fits a 1-2 layer MLP/LSTM on last-layer VLA embeddings, with every step of a failed rollout labelled as failure, and alarms when the score leaves a functional-conformal band fitted on successful rollouts. AEGIS [2606.06660] uses the same kind of probe (SmolVLA action-expert layer 15, BCE on the eventual weak-policy failure, early-window steps only) as an escalation trigger to a stronger VLA (pi0.5), with a split-conformal threshold, an early-harm gate and a per-episode fire cap. TDQC [2604.20472] shows that the minimiser of the sequential Brier score is the nominal policy's value function V^pi and trains it with a TD loss on hidden states or on black-box token probabilities.
- **Action-conditioned or world-model predictors of the nominal policy's future.** Foresight [2606.23085] scores V-JEPA 2-AC latents predicted from the nominal policy's next chunk with a causal Transformer, trained on trajectory labels with early-detection weighting. ReAP [2610.16309] learns a DINOv2/Perceiver failure space with a linear-ramp risk target t/(T-1) on failures, plus a latent transition model that scores the predicted risk of executing the proposed chunk.
- **Weakly or densely supervised onset localisation.** FailureSpot [2609.04277] pretrains on pseudo-labels built from action-chunk inconsistency and magnitude, then actively requests human onset labels, where onset means "no meaningful progress". ReAP [2610.16309] shows that the label shape sets alarm timing: binary labels push T-det from 0.45 to 0.95.
- **Policy-agnostic progress gates.** Reward-DAgger [2610.04054] fires human takeover on drops or plateaus in a VLM progress estimate, and states that this is reactive.
- **Intervention-conditioned value.** RESCUE [2610.13516] estimates Q^succ(s_t, a) for each intervention by counterfactual branching from restored simulator checkpoints, and finds that monitors chosen by AUROC or timing are decision-negative (0/15 verdicts) and alarm after the recoverability horizon in more than half of episodes. VAPS [2610.01397], in humanoid acrobatics, trains a separate viability predictor for each backup policy from that backup's own rollouts and switches to the least-sacrificial backup still predicted viable.

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Escalation triggers for cheap VLAs (AEGIS, SAFE, TDQC, Foresight, ReAP) learn the failure probability of the cheap policy, 1-V^pi_cheap(h_t), from its own rollout outcomes and treat that as the call-for-help score. But the decision of when to call the frontier controller in long-horizon LIBERO-style manipulation depends on the helper's advantage, V^{switch@t}(h_t) - V^pi_cheap(h_t). That advantage collapses once the recoverability horizon passes, while the cheap policy's failure probability stays high or keeps rising, so thresholding failure probability spends calls on states that are already lost and fires late on states that could still be saved.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2606.06660] AEGIS is the closest: a weak-to-strong VLA cascade gated by a probe trained on the weak policy's eventual failure. It recovers 10.1% of weak-only failures at a 38% strong-policy duty cycle, against 31.9% for always-strong and 15.5% for arms that start the strong policy from the first flagged step, and its targeted-vs-blind edge vanishes in the hard tercile, where little recoverable margin remains. [2610.13516] RESCUE shows directly that recoverability is a property of the state-intervention pair: 14/66 failures are recoverable under Continue vs 34/66 with a Recover option, and Recover is non-monotone in time on 8/20 episodes. It also finds that every real monitor (including a SAFE-style probe) alarms after the horizon in most episodes and has no positive decision value, but it trains no trigger. [2604.20472] TDQC estimates V^pi_cheap exactly, on-policy, with TD bootstrapping along the cheap policy's own transitions, and so carries no information about the switched policy. [2506.09937] SAFE, [2606.23085] Foresight and [2610.16309] ReAP condition only on the nominal policy's features or proposed chunk. [2609.04277] FailureSpot and ReAP time alarms against visible no-progress onset, while RESCUE puts the horizon a median 34 steps before the visible drop. [2610.04054] Reward-DAgger is reactive by design. [2610.01397] VAPS does condition viability on the backup policy, but only for cheap RL backups in acrobatics. Query trail: "failure prediction vision-language-action", "VLA uncertainty calibration", "robot policy failure detection", "robot-gated interactive imitation", "runtime monitoring generalist robot policy", "failure detection VLA", "intervention value robot policy", "escalation VLA", "help-seeking VLA" (the last four with --since 2025-10-01). None of the VLA papers found trains a trigger on a helper-conditioned target.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Training-objective mismatch. Every VLA trigger read regresses onto the outcome of the nominal policy: a BCE target on the eventual weak-policy failure in AEGIS and SAFE, the TD fixed point V^pi in TDQC, and ramp or onset labels in ReAP and FailureSpot. The minimiser of that objective is P(cheap policy fails | h_t), which is the same high value at a still-recoverable state and at an unrecoverable one. RESCUE's finding that recoverability depends on the intervention and is non-monotone means the quantity that should gate a call is not a monotone function of this score, so no threshold or conformal band on it can separate "call now" from "too late, don't waste a call".
- **Why it matters:** Budgeted escalation to a frontier model only pays off if calls land inside the window where the helper changes the outcome. A trigger that ranks states by the helper's marginal value, rather than by cheap-policy risk, would let a small call budget recover a large share of the always-frontier headroom. AEGIS leaves about two-thirds of it on the table at 38% duty. Such a trigger would also turn the problem's "VLA confidently grasps the wrong object" failure into a detectable event before the grasp, instead of after it.
- **Evidence route:** On LIBERO-Long/LIBERO-Plus with a fixed cheap VLA and helper, compare the recovered-task rate per helper call (and per API dollar) of a trigger against AEGIS/SAFE/TDQC-style triggers at matched call budgets, using AEGIS's budget-matched-blind and rate-matched-random controls. Then measure, with RESCUE-style checkpoint branching, the fraction of calls placed before the recoverability horizon and the fraction spent on already-unrecoverable states.
- **Gap:** The only estimators of a helper-conditioned value that exist are Monte-Carlo branching of the helper from restored states (RESCUE, 4-16 branches per checkpoint, 128,340 branches in total) and per-backup viability predictors trained on dense rollouts of that backup from its deployment handoff states (VAPS). Both assume that helper rollouts are nearly free. When the helper is a frontier model billed per call under a roughly US$1,000 budget, labels for a helper-conditioned target cannot be collected densely over the states the cheap VLA visits, and no method learns the helper's advantage from a sparse set of helper interventions.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2610.01397] VAPS shows that a policy-conditioned predictor is what makes switching work. Matching training states to deployment handoff states "affected the switching decision more than architecture, history length, and sensing combined", and monolithic or distilled alternatives fail. But each predictor is fit on abundant simulated rollouts of a small RL backup. [2610.13516] RESCUE estimates Q^succ(s_t, a) only by repeated branching from every checkpoint, with an expansion step for borderline checkpoints. [2606.06660] AEGIS sidesteps the issue by training on weak-only outcomes and running the strong policy (a local 4B VLA) on 38% of steps. [2604.20472] TDQC's TD estimator is strictly on-policy for the nominal policy, so it cannot produce the switched policy's value from off-policy data. The VLA monitors [2506.09937], [2606.23085] and [2610.16309] never involve a helper. Same query trail as the previous gap.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Method-scalability limit of the label source. Helper-conditioned value targets are obtained only by on-policy Monte-Carlo rollouts of the helper at (nearly) every candidate handoff state, and the number of expensive helper calls needed for those labels grows with the number of cheap-policy states, checkpoints and branches. No estimator in the read work shares statistical strength across states, or reuses cheap-policy rollouts (TD on V^pi_cheap), to infer the helper's advantage from a few helper interventions.
- **Why it matters:** This is what currently forces the choice between a trigger with the wrong target (cheap-policy failure, which is cheap to label) and a trigger with the right target that costs more frontier calls to train than it saves at deployment. Closing it would make advantage-aware escalation affordable for API-priced controllers and allow honest cost accounting that includes training-time helper calls.
- **Evidence route:** Report the total frontier calls spent on training plus deployment against success. Show that a helper-advantage trigger learned from a small number of helper interventions (for example a few hundred) matches the ranking quality of a RESCUE-style densely branched oracle advantage on held-out checkpoints, for example by Spearman correlation with the branched estimate, and that its end-to-end success per total API dollar beats outcome-trained triggers.
