# ROI-Gated SAHI: Content-Adaptive Slicing-Based Inference for Efficient Object Detection

## What this paper does
ROI-Gated SAHI (2026-08) is a training-free inference scheme that restricts SAHI's sliced, high-resolution detection to regions of interest. A small pretrained YOLOv8n run at 416 px proposes boxes; these are NMS-merged, enlarged by a 15% margin, and their total area ratio R decides the route. If R < 0.40, only tiles inside the ROIs are processed by YOLOv8s; otherwise full SAHI runs. Proposer and refiner detections are merged by NMS. It is evaluated on COCO128 and a three-image case study.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (reviewer C3) What decides which slices are processed, and how do accuracy and cost compare with full slicing?
The gate is class-agnostic and placed where the coarse detector already found objects: ROIs are the proposer's boxes after NMS and a 15% margin, tiles of 640×640 with 50–75% overlap are cut only inside them, and a fallback to full SAHI fires when ROI coverage R ≥ 0.40 [full text, Sec III-B Stages A–E, Sec III-D, Eq 5–7]. On COCO128, static gating is slower than full SAHI (speed ratio 0.88, 298 vs 264 ms) and less accurate (mAP@0.5 0.6602 vs 0.7569); the adaptive fallback brings the average speed ratio only to 1.02× [Sec IV-B, Sec IV-E, Table I]. Speed-ups (up to 6.90×) appear only in sparse scenes, and in moderate and dense regimes gating is slower than full slicing [Sec IV-C, Sec IV-E]. The accuracy loss is attributed to the proposer: "Missed regions at the proposal stage cannot be recovered in the later stages" [Sec IV-E, Limitations].
