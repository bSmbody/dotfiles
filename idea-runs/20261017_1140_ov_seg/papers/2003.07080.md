# PS-RCNN: Detecting Secondary Human Instances in a Crowd via Primary Object Suppression

## What this paper does
PS-RCNN (ICME 2020) detects people in crowds in two rounds on one shared backbone. A primary R-CNN detects the unoccluded or slightly occluded people. Their human-shaped (or predicted instance) masks are then used to erase them from the feature maps, so that the weak cues of heavily occluded people "stand out", and a secondary R-CNN, trained only on the instances the primary missed, detects the rest. The union of the two outputs raises AP by 2.92 and recall by 4.49 points on CrowdHuman, with similar gains on WiderPerson. Both R-CNN heads are trained.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (innovator C3) How does it remove already-detected instances before looking again, and why does that reveal missed ones?
After the primary module detects G_d, "a human-shaped binary mask is covered at the location ... of each detected BBox in G_d on feature maps", so "the visual cues for G_d should be invisible" to the secondary module, which is trained with only the missed instances G_m as targets; the final output is the union [full text, Sec 3.1, Fig 2]. The rationale is that occluded instances have weak cues and are otherwise treated as duplicates of the dominant overlapping instance [Sec 1]. Recall of instances with visibility V ≤ 0.5 rises from 0.82 to 0.92, and the secondary module yields many high-score detections rather than low-confidence leftovers [Sec 4.2, Fig 3]. Masking the input image instead of the feature maps gives nearly the same result (AP 87.86 vs 87.94, recall 95.40 vs 95.11) [Sec 4.2, Table 3].

### Goal: (innovator C3) What goes wrong with the masking, and what else is needed for the weak instances?
Mask quality is the stated failure mode: "An imperfect mask which leaves parts of a primary instance uncovered will lead to plenty of duplicate detection results in the S-RCNN detection stage, bringing about lots of false positives"; predicted instance masks therefore beat hand-crafted human-shaped ones [Sec 3.3, Table 2]. Suppression alone helps little when the remaining cues are too weak: without the High Resolution RoI Align, which reads the finest feature level, recall improves by only 2.05 points, against 3.15 with it and 4.49 with instance masks as well [Sec 4.2, Table 2]. The method is supervised; nothing is shown for a frozen model.
