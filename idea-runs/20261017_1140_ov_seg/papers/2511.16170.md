# Target Refocusing via Attention Redistribution for Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation: An Explainability Perspective

## What this paper does
RF-CLIP (AAAI 2026), training-free CLIP OVSS. Finds "distraction tokens" in layers 7-12 that receive high attention from every query, caused by massive activations in a few fixed "distraction dimensions" shared across datasets. In every layer it (a) flags distraction tokens by a threshold tau = 5/d on their maximum normalised weight in those dimensions, (b) finds "defocused" target tokens by a 2-way normalised cut on k-k attention, (c) moves a fraction beta of attention mass from distraction to defocused tokens and replaces distraction-dimension entries with 3x3 neighbour means. The final layer uses layer-averaged k-k attention.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use to suppress spatial noise in CLIP patch tokens, at which layer(s), and on which quantity?
It acts in every layer, mainly on attention weights: attention to distraction tokens is scaled by (1-beta) and the freed budget is given to defocused tokens in proportion to their original weights, keeping each row normalised [full text, Approach, Eqs. 9-12]. Token content is touched only for distraction tokens and only in the distraction dimensions, which are replaced by the 3x3 neighbourhood mean [full text, Eq. 13]. Defocused tokens are the positive side of the Fiedler vector of k-k attention (foreground/background bipartition) [full text, Eqs. 7-8]. Final prediction replaces last-layer q-k with layer-averaged k-k attention [full text, "Dense prediction"]. Redistribution in all layers beats partial-layer variants [full text, Appendix C, Table 7].

### Goal: What does it identify as the source of the noise, and is it sparse?
Tokens with massive weights in a few data-agnostic "distraction dimensions" (e.g. 4, 162, 474) become distraction tokens; attention weight grows exponentially with that weight [full text, "How do distraction tokens surface?", Fig. 3]. The authors argue the attention these tokens absorb comes not only from CLS but also from local foreground patches, so they act as proxies for foreground targets [full text, Appendix A, Table 6].

### Goal: What happens when the distraction tokens are removed outright, and what are the method's limiting assumptions?
Removal collapses the model: with a k-k baseline (32.1 avg), -inf masking gives 1.4 avg mIoU and clamping (low-pass) 4.2, while 3x3 mean filtering gives 34.2; "direct elimination disrupts the topological structure of CLIP's high-dimensional space, inducing model collapse" [full text, Table 1]. Hence the design only redistributes mass. Fixed constants: tau = 5/d chosen by sweeping across the eight benchmarks, a binary foreground/background graph cut, 3x3 neighbourhoods [full text, Experiments "Threshold tau analysis", "Graph cut threshold analysis"].
