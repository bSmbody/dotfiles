# An Analysis of Scale Invariance in Object Detection – SNIP

## What this paper does
SNIP (CVPR 2018) analyses why detectors struggle with extreme scale variation and shows that CNNs are not robust to scale changes between pre-training and test. It proposes Scale Normalization for Image Pyramids: train and test on an image pyramid, but at each resolution only use (back-propagate, and at inference keep) object instances whose size falls in a valid range for that resolution, so every object is handled at a scale close to the pre-training resolution. It improves COCO detection, especially for small objects.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (innovator C2) How does SNIP decide which scale is responsible for which object, at training and at inference, and why?
At each resolution i, an RoI whose area falls inside a range [s_i, e_i] is valid and the rest are invalid; invalid ground truths are ignored in training (anchors overlapping them by >0.3 get no gradient) [full text, Sec 6, Fig 6]. "During inference, we generate proposals using RPN for each resolution and classify them independently at each resolution ... we do not select detections ... which fall outside a specified range at each resolution", then rescale and merge with NMS [Sec 6, Fig 6 caption]. The reason: "at a high resolution (like 1400x2000) large objects are hard to classify and at a low resolution (like 480x800) small objects are hard to classify", and at extreme resolutions "the receptive field of the network is not sufficient" for large objects, while multi-scale training without the restriction degrades large-object performance [Sec 5–7]. The ranges overlap by 40 px between adjacent resolutions and the inference ranges are set on a held-out minival split [Sec 7].
