# Vision Transformers Don't Need Trained Registers

## What this paper does
Mechanistic study of high-norm outlier tokens in ViTs (OpenCLIP, DINOv2), NeurIPS 2025 spotlight. Finds a sparse set of MLP "register neurons" (e.g. 5-10 in OpenCLIP B/16 layer 6) whose activations create the outliers and the attention sinks. Training-free fix: append an extra token and move the register-neuron activations into it at test time ("test-time register"), or zero the neurons and inject a constant per-head attention bias (k', v'). Gives clean CLS attention maps and keeps classification accuracy. A mathematical model ties register neurons to no-op attention and implicit attention bias.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism creates the sink/outlier tokens in CLIP, and what does the training-free remedy change?
Outliers appear after the MLP of layer 6 in OpenCLIP B/16, followed by attention sinks; a few register neurons activate on all outlier positions and causally set their location [full text, Section 3.1-3.2, Figs. 2-4]. The remedy edits activations at every layer: for each register neuron, the maximum activation is copied into an appended zero-initialised token and zeroed elsewhere [full text, Section 4]. A variant zeroes the neurons and adds constant k', v' (mean key/value of the test-time register over 1000 images) to every attention head; this removes high-norm outliers from the residual stream in all layers [full text, Section 6.2; Appendix A.2, Fig. 8].

### Goal: Is the sink functionally necessary, i.e. what happens if it is simply removed?
Yes. Zeroing the 10 register neurons drops OpenCLIP B/16 IN1k zero-shot accuracy from 70.4% to 55.6% (random neurons: 69.3 +/- 1.1); "these outliers are essential to the model's internal computations and must appear within a token" [full text, Section 4; Section 6.2(2)]. Relocation or the constant attention bias restores 71.3% [full text, Table 7]. The analytical model shows a register neuron makes a token an attention sink that turns attention into a no-op, equivalent to explicit attention bias terms [full text, Section 6.1, Proposition and Corollary].

### Goal: How much does removing the sink help dense prediction, and was text-based open-vocabulary segmentation evaluated?
Dense gains are small: linear-probe ADE20K mIoU for OpenCLIP B/16 goes 40.1 -> 40.3 (DINOv2-L 48.3 -> 49.1) [full text, Section 5.1, Table 2]. Zero-shot segmentation is evaluated only by binarising the CLS attention map on ImageNet-segmentation (foreground/background), not by patch-text similarity on OVSS benchmarks [full text, Section 5.2]. Limitations: only neurons are addressed, not attention layers or positional encodings, and test-time registers are not fully equivalent to learned ones [full text, Section 7].
