# EOVSAM: Efficient Open-Vocabulary Segmentation with SAM 3 in One Pass

## What this paper does
EOVSAM (2026-08) removes prompt conditioning from SAM 3's fusion encoder and detector decoder, turning it into a single-pass mask generator on a frozen C-RADIOv4-H backbone distilled from SigLIP 2, DINOv3 and SAM 3. It adds a trained "Attentional Aggregation" classifier that uses both SAM 3 and SigLIP 2 text embeddings. It is trained on COCO Panoptic (20k iterations, 2 RTX 3090s), so it is not training-free. It reports gains over vanilla SAM 3 on A-150, A-847, PC-59, PC-459 and PAS-20, and speed-ups of up to 338x.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (panel R2, C3) How sensitive is SAM 3-style open-vocabulary segmentation accuracy to input resolution?
Table 3 runs one checkpoint at several input scales (longer side). From 512 to 1152 px, A-150 mIoU goes 37.0 to 39.0, A-847 15.4 to 16.6, PC-59 59.1 to 60.9, PC-459 18.8 to 20.4 and PAS-20 96.1 to 97.0 [full text, Results "Inference Efficiency", Table 3]. Vanilla SAM 3 at 1008 px scores 37.8 on A-150. Resolution therefore moves mIoU by about 1–2 points across a 2.25x scale range on these mostly low-resolution benchmarks. The authors frame this as accuracy "maintained" at lower resolution [Sec "Inference Efficiency"].

### Goal: (panel R2, C3) Does it evaluate Cityscapes or small objects, and is it training-free?
It is not training-free: the decoder and aggregation are trained on COCO Panoptic with the backbone and text encoders frozen [Experimental Setup, Implementation details]. Semantic evaluation covers ADE20K, Pascal Context and Pascal VOC only; there is no Cityscapes, no high-resolution benchmark and no small-object analysis [Experimental Setup, Datasets].

# EOVSAM: Efficient Open-Vocabulary Segmentation with SAM 3 in One Pass

## What this paper does
EOVSAM (August 2026) removes prompt conditioning from SAM 3 so that its decoder acts as a prompt-free mask generator, and adds an Attentional Aggregation head that classifies masks against text embeddings in one pass. It is trained on COCO Panoptic (20k iterations on 2 RTX 3090s) with a frozen C-RADIOv4-H backbone distilled from SigLIP 2, DINOv3 and SAM 3. It reports open-vocabulary semantic segmentation on A-150, A-847, PC-59, PC-459 and PAS-20 and panoptic results on ADE20K, with up to 338x speed-ups over vanilla SAM 3.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (reviewer R3, C3) Is EOVSAM training-free, and is it a fair baseline for a training-free SAM 3 OVSS plug-in on the eight standard benchmarks?
No. It is trained on COCO Panoptic with AdamW for 20,000 iterations, crop 1152 [full text, Implementation details], and it uses a RADIO backbone rather than SAM 3's Perception Encoder [Implementation details]. It evaluates on the trained-OVS protocol (A-847, PC-459, A-150, PC-59, PAS-20), not on the eight training-free benchmarks (no Cityscapes, no COCO-Object/Stuff as test) [Datasets]. It is context for the efficiency argument, not a baseline that a training-free paper must beat.

### Goal: (reviewer R3, C3) How does accuracy change with input resolution, as evidence on whether resolution is the bottleneck on low-resolution benchmarks?
Table 3, one checkpoint at several inference scales: A-150 mIoU 37.0 (512), 37.9 (576), 38.4 (768), 39.0 (1152); PC-59 59.1, 59.6, 59.9, 60.9; PAS-20 96.1, 96.4, 96.8, 97.0 [Results, Table 3]. Raising the longer side from 512 to 1152 (2.25x linear) therefore buys about 2 mIoU on ADE20K and 1.8 on PC-59, and FPS falls from 8.76 to 3.77 on A-150 on an RTX 3090. Vanilla SAM 3 is listed at 37.8 A-150 mIoU and 0.03 FPS on the same card [Table 3]. On these low-resolution benchmarks, resolution matters but moderately; the paper does not test inputs above 1152 or crops.
