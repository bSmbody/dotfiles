# FOCUS: Internal MLLM Representations for Efficient Fine-Grained Visual Question Answering

## What this paper does
FOCUS (NeurIPS 2025) is a training-free visual-cropping method for fine-grained VQA. It extracts the target object named in the question, builds an object relevance map from cosine similarity between that object's cached text tokens and the cached visual value tokens (aggregated across layers), proposes regions of interest around top-k anchor points, re-ranks them by asking the MLLM whether the object exists in each crop, and answers the question from the best crop. It improves LLaVA-1.5 by 42% and LLaVA-OneVision by 17% on average across V*Bench and HRBench-4K/8K, matching ZoomEye at 3-6.5x less compute.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How does FOCUS choose which image region to re-query at higher resolution, and what signal triggers it?
Trigger is the question: an in-context prompt extracts the target object(s), and for each a relevance map A is computed as value-value cosine similarity between target text tokens and visual tokens, rolled out over layers and multiplied across the object's sub-tokens (Eq 1-3) [full text, Sec 3.2]. Regions are proposed at the top-k highest-scoring anchor points with a minimum distance, grown from s_min to s_max until mean relevance falls below a threshold, NMS-filtered, then re-ranked by the MLLM's existence confidence on each crop [Sec 3.3]. Selection is therefore "where the named target scores highest", for one known target class at a time.

### Goal: Does it handle dense, many-class labelling, and what limitations does it report?
No: the output is an answer to one question; for single-instance questions only the top-confidence ROI per target is used, and for multi-instance questions ROIs above a confidence threshold [Sec 3.3]. There is no per-location label map and no competition among many candidate classes. Stated limitations: dependence on the resolution of the relevance map, so on very high-resolution (8K) inputs small objects are hard to localise (suggests sliding-window relevance maps), and inherited weak spatial reasoning [Sec 4.6].

### Goal: What evidence does it give that re-querying a CLIP-style vision encoder on a zoomed crop recovers detail that the global view misses?
Global-view MLLMs downscale inputs to the encoder's base resolution (336 for CLIP in LLaVA-1.5) and "perform poorly" on small details; even global-local models that see all local crops "struggle to identify the few visual tokens that are relevant" among many [Sec 1, Sec 3.1]. Passing only the selected crop raises average accuracy by 42% (LLaVA-1.5) and 17% (LLaVA-OneVision) over vanilla on three fine-grained VQA sets [Sec 1]. This supports that selective re-encoding, rather than uniform tiling, is what recovers small details.
