# PEARL: Geometry Aligns Semantics for Training-Free Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation

## What this paper does
PEARL (CVPR 2026) is CLIP-only and two-step: an orthogonal Procrustes rotation of keys toward queries inside the last self-attention block, then a text-aware Laplacian propagation that refines class logits on a pooled grid by a confidence-weighted quadratic solve, where data trust grows with peak probability and agreement with a text-derived class-similarity graph, and neighbour links are gated by image gradients and text consistency. Reports SOTA among CLIP-only training-free methods on eight benchmarks.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (axis: small objects and stuff classes) What mechanism does it use that affects small or thin objects, and at what resolution/scale is the class decision made?
PEARL (a) rotates keys toward the query subspace in CLIP's last attention block with an orthogonal Procrustes map [full text, Sec 3.2], then (b) refines the upsampled logits on a pooled H_g×W_g grid by a text-aware Laplacian [Sec 3.3]. The quadratic objective is ½Σρ_i||F_i − Z_i||² + (τ/2)Σ a_ij||F_i − F_j||² (Eq 12), where the data trust is ρ_i = (max_c p_i(c))²(1+u_i) and u_i = p_iᵀGp_i agreement with a text-similarity class graph G (Eq 9-10); edges a_ij combine grayscale gradient and text gate (Eq 11). Low-peak-confidence nodes (typically minority classes at mixed nodes) thus get the least trust and are pulled toward neighbours. The solution is bilinearly upsampled and argmax'd (Eq 14).

### Goal: (axis: small objects and stuff classes) What mechanism does it use for stuff classes / large regions / many-class benchmarks, and what global context or class-competition handling does it apply?
Text relations decide which neighbouring labels may reinforce each other (G from TTᵀ) [Sec 3.3]. Claims "coherent masks for small objects, stable coverage for 'stuff' regions" [Sec 1, 5].

### Goal: (axis: small objects and stuff classes) What does it report as its limitation or failure on small objects or stuff classes, and which knobs are set per dataset?
Grid size is dataset-specific: 224 for Cityscapes ("prevents oversmoothing of thin structures") and 80 otherwise [Sec 4.1]; the grid-size sweep shows City peaks at 224, V21/V20 at ~80, ADE at 64 [Sec 4.3, Fig 4a]. On ADE, "generic CLIP prompts sometimes under-specify rare 'stuff' categories, which limits zero-shot matching" [Sec 4.2]. Stated limitations: dependence on prompts/label names, low-contrast boundaries, "the grid size trades detail for cost", motivating "adaptive grids" [Sec 5].
