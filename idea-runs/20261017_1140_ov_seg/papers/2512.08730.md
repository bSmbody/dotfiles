# SegEarth-OV3: Exploring SAM 3 for Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation in Remote Sensing Images

## What this paper does
SegEarth-OV3 applies frozen SAM 3 to training-free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, mainly of remote-sensing images, and extends it to change detection and 3D point clouds. It fuses SAM 3's instance (Transformer decoder) output with its semantic-head output by a pixelwise maximum, and uses the presence score to suppress categories absent from the image. It reports results on 20 remote-sensing datasets and on general benchmarks (VOC20 96.8, COCO-Stuff 42.8, Cityscapes 69.7 mIoU), far above CLIP-based training-free methods.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (innovator C2) How does it handle input resolution and small targets, and what does it say about where SAM 3 fails on them?
"Input images are resized to 1008×1008" with per-dataset adjustment of the background threshold and the decoder confidence threshold [full text, Sec 4.1]. Its motivating observation is that the Transformer decoder delineates countable objects but fragments amorphous regions, "while the semantic head provides dense, global coverage but often blurs the boundaries of small targets or misses them entirely" [Sec 3.2; Fig 1]. Max-fusion of the two heads is its remedy; it does not re-encode any region at higher zoom [Sec 3.2].

### Goal: (innovator C2) How is the presence score used, and is it ever evaluated per crop or window?
Presence-guided filtering suppresses categories whose presence score is low, because a large global vocabulary queried against a small local view causes hallucinated categories [Sec 3.2, "Presence-Guided Filtering"]. On very large images (>10k×10k) it runs sliding-window inference and applies this filtering "patch-by-patch", which "effectively eliminates localized false positives" [Sec 4.2, Fig 4]. Per-window presence is therefore used only to remove categories inside uniformly tiled windows, never to decide where to look more closely or to add a missing category.
