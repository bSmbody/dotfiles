# Improving Visual Discriminability of CLIP for Training-Free Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation

## What this paper does
LHT-CLIP, training-free CLIP OVSS. Layer-wise analysis: visual discriminability (same-class patch-pair AUC) follows an inverted U, dropping over the last 2 layers before the final one in ViT-B/16 and the last 7 in ViT-L/14, while text alignment saturates. Attributes the drop to sparse high-norm abnormal tokens that attract attention from all positions and behave like near-constant bias vectors. Three fixes: abnormal token replacement (Hoyer sparsity threshold, 3x3 neighbour mean) at the penultimate layer; spatial-semantic reweighting (upweight residual, downweight MSA/FFN in late layers); selective head enhancement (similarity from top discriminative heads as soft pseudo-mask).

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism does it use to suppress spatial noise in CLIP patch tokens, at which layer(s), and on which quantity?
(1) ATR: tokens whose Hoyer sparsity exceeds tau are replaced by the mean of their non-abnormal 8 neighbours, only at layer L-1 [full text, Section 3.1, Eq. 6]. (2) SSR: in the last few blocks (layers 10-11 in B/16, 17-23 in L/14) the block is reweighted as X^ = (1+alpha)X + (1-alpha)MSA(LN(X)) and likewise for the FFN, a single scalar per block for all tokens and channels [full text, Section 3.2, Eqs. 7-8]. (3) SHE: average features of top-k heads ranked by visual-discriminability scores, threshold their similarity at beta, and use it to re-aggregate X^(L-1) [full text, Section 3.3]. Hyperparameters are chosen on 1,000 training samples from Context, ADE and COCO-Stuff [full text, Appendix A.3.1].

### Goal: What does it identify as the source of the noise, and what do the abnormal tokens encode?
Abnormal tokens emerge in middle layers and persist at the same positions; nearly all tokens attend to them, which makes tokens similar and lowers discriminability [full text, Section 2.2, Figs. 2-3]. Their cosine similarity with CLS rises with depth, and across positions, layers and samples they are almost identical (cosine > 0.98), so the authors read them as bias components offsetting global mean features that aid text alignment [full text, Section 2.2, Fig. 4].

### Goal: What limitation does it report for removing abnormal tokens earlier or for last-layer refinements?
"Applying this strategy before the penultimate layer leads to a performance drop, likely due to the removal of inherent biases encoded in abnormal tokens", so ATR is applied only at L-1 [full text, Section 3.1]. It also states "anomalous tokens appeared in earlier layers already degrade visual discriminability, limiting the effectiveness of final-layer refinements", and that final-layer methods "inevitably inherit the global semantic alignment bias of the preceding layers" [full text, Section 3.2]. On SigLIP, ClearCLIP's last-block surgery performs worse than vanilla SigLIP, attributed to disrupting the AttentionPool head's input [full text, Appendix A.3.2].
