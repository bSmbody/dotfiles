# SegEarth-OV: Towards Training-Free Open-Vocabulary Segmentation for Remote Sensing Images

## What this paper does
SegEarth-OV adapts training-free OVSS to remote sensing, where small targets and stuff dominate. SimFeatUp, a single JBU upsampler with a larger kernel trained once on unlabeled images with an extra image-reconstruction loss, upsamples CLIP's last-block input features; and a fixed fraction of the [CLS] token is subtracted from all patch tokens to remove the global bias that the image's dominant class imposes on local tokens. Evaluated on 17 remote-sensing datasets plus Context59, COCO-Stuff and Cityscapes.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (axis: small objects and stuff classes) What mechanism does it use that affects small or thin objects, and at what resolution/scale is the class decision made?
SegEarth-OV upsamples CLIP features with SimFeatUp, a single parameterised JBU (11×11 kernel, range kernel from an HR-image guidance feature, spatial Gaussian kernel) trained once on unlabeled images with FeatUp's reconstruction loss plus an image-reconstruction loss through a small "content retention net" [full text, Sec 4.1, Eq 4-8]. The motivation for the extra loss: with FeatUp "a small building in the original image is present in the LR prediction but disappears in the HR prediction" [Sec 4.1, Fig 4]. It upsamples the input to the last block (Proj(X)), and the base model uses q-q+k-k+v-v attention without FFN/residual [Sec 4.1, 5.2]. Upsampled features are kernel-weighted combinations of LR features.

### Goal: (axis: small objects and stuff classes) What mechanism does it use for stuff classes / large regions / many-class benchmarks, and what global context or class-competition handling does it apply?
"Global bias": the [CLS] token, which encodes the class covering most of the image (building in Fig 5), is highly similar to patches of other classes (roads, pavements) — the dominant region contaminates local tokens [Sec 4.2]. Fix: Ô = O[1:] − λ·O[0] with one λ=0.3 for all datasets and all patches (Eq 9, Sec 5.2). Remote sensing is stuff-dominated (grassland, roads, forests) [Sec 1].

### Goal: (axis: small objects and stuff classes) What does it report as its limitation or failure on small objects or stuff classes, and which knobs are set per dataset?
Road extraction stays poor (best IoU 35.4) — elongated shapes are hard training-free [Sec 5.3]. Small-area building classes benefit from larger inputs (896 px), supporting that spatial detail is essential [Sec 5.3]. On natural images SimFeatUp adds +1.2 to SCLIP/ClearCLIP on Context59/Stuff/City [Table 6]. Global-bias subtraction gives +1.3 average [Sec 5.4].
