# Promptable Concept Segmentation from Above: Evaluating SAM 3's Zero-Shot and One-Shot Capabilities in Remote Sensing

## What this paper does
An empirical study (July 2026) of frozen SAM 3 on remote-sensing scene classification (AID), open-vocabulary detection (DIOR) and instance segmentation (iSAID), in zero-shot and one-shot settings. It repurposes SAM 3's presence head as a standalone image classifier, compares five prompt configurations (text, box, text+box, each with or without an oracle negative filter that removes absent classes), and analyses per-class failures.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (reviewer R3, C3) Does SAM 3's presence head behave like a local "is the object here" detector or like a scene-level prior, and how does it fail?
The authors read presence as scene-level: the presence head "evaluates the global existence of the target concept across the entire scene embedding", whereas the localisation heads verify it in sub-regions; as an AID scene classifier presence alone gets 37.26% accuracy, localisation 28.25%, and their product 38.70% [full text, Sec IV-A1]. Without oracle filtering, text prompts hallucinate on structurally similar elements, e.g. windmills detected as airplanes on DIOR and grass detected as a ground track field on iSAID; an oracle negative filter recovers 1.5–2 points [Sec IV-B2, Sec IV-C2]. They conclude that presence false positives are real but that false negatives from the domain gap dominate [Sec IV-B2, IV-C2]. They also state SAM 3 is "constrained by sub-pixel resolution limits" [Abstract]. The study never edits or masks the input, so it does not test how presence responds once the visible instances are removed.
