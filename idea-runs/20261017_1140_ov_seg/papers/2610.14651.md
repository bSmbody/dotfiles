# TAG: Calibrating SAM3 for Universal Image Segmentation without Dense Supervision

## What this paper does
TAG (2026-10, under review) diagnoses why frozen SAM 3 underperforms on downstream semantic, instance and panoptic benchmarks and fixes it with weak supervision. Its oracle studies on ADE20K, Cityscapes and iSAID argue that the bottleneck is concept recognition (which classes are present, and which label a mask gets), not mask quality. It then trains a 3.1M-parameter image-conditioned low-rank "Anchor Residual Pathway" on the text anchors, with all SAM 3 weights frozen, using image-level labels (TAG-I) or MLLM-produced labels (TAG-V), a BCE plus soft-ranking presence objective, and confidence-weighted distillation. It is not training-free.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (panel R2, C3) What does TAG find about frozen SAM 3's errors on Cityscapes: how many ground-truth components fail, and are the failures recognition errors or geometry errors?
Two failure modes are named: inter-class confusion (a well-delineated mask gets a neighbouring class) and under-activation, where "the confidence of the correct concept falls below the filtering threshold, causing the model to suppress the entire object as background" [full text, Sec 1, Sec 2.2]. Object-level statistics use 8-connected GT foreground components of at least 20 px, and a component "fails" when under half its pixels get the correct class, the same criterion C3 uses for "missed" [App D.5.1]. On Cityscapes val, 19,225 of 46,490 components fail (41%, about 38 failed components per image); ADE20K has 16,281 of 40,740 failed [App D.5.1, Table 13]. Failures cover nearly the whole object: the mean mislabelled fraction of a failed object is 94.5% on Cityscapes, usually dominated by one wrong label or background [App D.5.1, Fig 12]. Recognised objects are well delineated (median mask IoU 71.7% on Cityscapes) [App D.5.2, Fig 13]. Oracle interventions that keep SAM 3's raw masks but fix categories or presence gates beat oracle GT geometry on every benchmark [Sec 2.2, Fig 2b]. These numbers are for frozen SAM 3 with SegEarth-OV3-style presence-weighted fusion, not ActiveSAM, and they are not broken down by component size.

### Goal: (panel R2, C3) Does TAG treat resolution or small objects as a cause, and at what input does it run SAM 3?
No. Each view is resized to 1008x1008 and features are computed once per view [App C.1 "Image preparation"]. The paper attributes errors to anchors lying too close to a neighbouring class or too far from their own class [Sec 3], not to resolution, and runs no resolution or small-object analysis. Small objects appear only in qualitative iSAID figures and in MLLM prompts [App, Fig 16; App F].
