# From Pixels to Concepts: Do Segmentation Models Understand What They Segment? (CAFE)

## What this paper does
CAFE (2026-05) is a benchmark of 2,146 counterfactually edited images (from COCO, SA-Co/Gold and LVIS) that tests whether promptable segmenters, including SAM 3, ground the queried concept or follow misleading cues. Each sample pairs a valid positive prompt with a misleading negative prompt under one of three edits: superficial mimicry (surface repainted), context conflict (surroundings swapped) and ontological conflict (material re-rendered). It reports cgF1, IL_MCC, pmF1 and false-positive rates, and a GPT-based SAM 3 verification agent.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (reviewer R1, C3) Does SAM 3's presence decision follow the target's own appearance or its surrounding context, and is removal of the target (with context kept) tested?
Context conflict keeps the target and swaps its surroundings so that a contextually plausible wrong concept is suggested (skateboarder in snow, misleading prompt "snowboarder") [full text, Sec 3.1]. SAM 3 is fairly robust there: IL_MCC 0.857 on CC, against -0.241 on ontological conflict; the authors conclude that "SAM3's image-level presence head enhances robustness on SM and CC, where misleading cues stem from surface appearance or context, but is insufficient for OC" [Sec 5.1-5.2]. Most false positives are target-aligned, i.e. driven by the edited region rather than elsewhere [Sec 5.1]. The benchmark never removes or hides the target while keeping the context, and it covers a single edited target per image; "co-occurrence with unedited instances of the same or related concepts" is explicitly untested [Sec 7]. So it gives indirect evidence that SAM 3 presence is not dominated by scene context, but no measurement of presence after the class's own instances are masked.
