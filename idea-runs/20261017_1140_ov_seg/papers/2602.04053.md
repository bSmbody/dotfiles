# Seeing Through Clutter: Structured 3D Scene Reconstruction via Iterative Object Removal

## What this paper does
Seeing Through Clutter (3DV 2026) reconstructs a structured 3D scene from one image by taking the scene apart object by object. At each step a frozen VLM (GPT-4o) names the closest unoccluded object, Grounded-SAM with HQ-SAM segments it, and an inpainter (Flux Kontext or a removal model) erases it. The loop repeats until the VLM finds no object left, and each removed object is then fitted in 3D. The method needs no task-specific training. A segmentation study on an ADE20K subset shows that iterative removal improves object segmentation over a single-pass ablation.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (panel R2, C3) Is the second look made by the same frozen model(s) on the image with found objects removed, and what decides when to stop?
Yes. All models are frozen, and there is "no task-specific training" [full text, Abstract]. After each removal the same VLM is asked again on the edited image; "if the VLM no longer identifies any objects within the scene, the inpainting process stops", or when Grounded-SAM detects nothing [Sec 3.1]. This is a training-free erase-and-re-ask loop with frozen recognisers, but it is class-agnostic, removes one object (plus objects it supports) per iteration, and works on whole images; it does not zoom or gate where to look.

### Goal: (panel R2, C3) How is removal done, and what failure modes after removal are reported?
Removal is done in pixel space by inpainting, with dilated masks, crops to the dilated mask, and VLM-generated positive and negative inpainting prompts that steer away from re-inserting objects [Sec 3.1 "Foreground Removal"]. The stated failures are inpainting "artifacts and ghost objects", and text-guided editing sometimes failing "to remove any object when the category provided by the VLM is not precise" [Sec 4.2, Limitations]. No context-answering rate is measured.

### Goal: (panel R2, C3) What segmentation evidence on ADE20K does it give, and does it target small or missed objects?
On an ADE20K subset, things IoU rises from 0.45 without removal to 0.51 with Kontext removal, and things+stuff IoU from 0.35 to 0.44 [Sec 4.2, Table 2]. The gain is attributed to "discovering and segmenting occluded or overlapping objects", not to small objects, and resolution is not studied [Sec 4.2].
