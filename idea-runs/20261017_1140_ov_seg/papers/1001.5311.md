# Distilled Sensing: Adaptive Sampling for Sparse Detection and Estimation

## What this paper does
Haupt, Castro and Nowak (math.ST / IT) study detecting and localizing a sparse non-negative vector observed in Gaussian noise when measurements can be taken sequentially and their precision allocated adaptively under a fixed total budget. They propose Distilled Sensing (DS): measure all components crudely, discard those whose observation falls below the null level, re-measure only the survivors with the remaining budget, and repeat. They prove that DS localizes signals whose amplitude grows arbitrarily slowly and detects signals of constant amplitude, whereas any non-adaptive single-measurement thresholding needs amplitude of order sqrt(log p). Simulations confirm a gain of roughly log p in SNR.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What is the Distilled Sensing procedure: what is measured at each stage, what rule decides which components are re-measured, and how is the precision budget split across stages?
Measurement model y_{i,j} = x_i + gamma_{i,j}^{-1/2} w_{i,j}, where gamma_{i,j} is the precision spent on component i at step j (e.g. proportional to samples averaged or exposure time), with total budget sum gamma_{i,j} <= R(p); the non-adaptive baseline is one measurement per component with gamma = 1 [full text, Sec I, Eq 1-2]. Algorithm 1: start with all indices I_1; at step j spread budget R_j evenly over the current set I_j, observe, and keep I_{j+1} = {i in I_j : y_{i,j} > 0}, i.e. reject only components whose observation is below the null median (zero) [Sec III, Alg 1]. Because roughly half of the null components are dropped per step, a per-step budget slightly above half of the previous one makes the precision per surviving component grow exponentially, while the total number of measurements is only about 2p [Sec III]. The final support estimate thresholds the last-step observations of survivors [Thm III.1].

### Goal: Why does DS detect weaker signals than a single non-adaptive measurement, and how large is the gain?
Non-adaptive coordinate-wise thresholding can localize p^{1-beta} non-zeros only if their amplitude is sqrt(2 r log p) with r > beta (Thm II.3); detection likewise needs amplitude of order sqrt(log p) (Thm II.4), because the largest of p null observations grows like sqrt(2 log p) [Sec II, Sec V]. DS separates two tasks: a crude threshold at 0 that almost never discards a true signal (Lemma IV.2: with mu >= 2 sigma, a fraction at least 1 - sigma/(mu sqrt(2 pi)) of signal components survive) and halves the nulls (Lemma IV.1, Hoeffding) — "Reject the Nulls, Retain the Signal" [Sec IV-A]. Precision is thereby concentrated on survivors. Thm III.1: localization succeeds if mu(p) grows arbitrarily slowly; detection succeeds if mu exceeds a constant max{sqrt(4/c1), 2 sqrt(2/c_k)} [Sec III]. Simulations (p = 2^14, 128 non-zeros, k = 6) show DS succeeds at SNR 8 where non-adaptive sensing is poor, and at SNR 2 still recovers about 20% of components at FDP 0.05; NDR is also much less dependent on p [Sec V, Fig 1-2]. Authors summarise the gain as roughly log p in SNR [Sec VI].

### Goal: What assumptions does DS rely on (signal sign, sparsity, independence of re-measurements, budget schedule), and what practical settings do the authors recommend?
(i) Non-negative signal components (both signs handled by running the procedure twice with -y) [Sec III]. (ii) Sparsity: p^{1-beta} non-zeros, extended down to very small counts; the halving argument requires nulls to dominate [Sec III, Sec VI]. (iii) Each step's noise w_{i,j} is i.i.d. and independent of earlier steps, so a re-measurement is a fresh look whose precision is set by gamma [Eq 1]. (iv) Budget schedule R_{j+1}/R_j >= delta > 1/2 with R_1 and R_k fixed fractions of p; number of steps k = ceil(log2 log p) + 2 [Thm III.1]. Practical rule of thumb: R_{j+1}/R_j = 0.75 for intermediate steps and equal precision in the first and last steps, because the first step controls missed signals (NDP) and the last controls false discoveries (FDP); this "provides good performance over the full SNR range" without tuning to the signal [Sec V]. Extensions to adaptive linear measurements are mentioned [Sec VI].
