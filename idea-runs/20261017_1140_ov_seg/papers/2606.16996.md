# ActiveSAM: Fast and Accurate Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation with Frozen SAM 3

## What this paper does
ActiveSAM is a training-free OVSS pipeline on a frozen SAM 3. It has three parts. Contextual Prompt Expansion canonicalises class names and appends vocabulary-neighbour and WordNet-hypernym tokens. Evidence-Proportional Grounding runs a 336 px presence-only preview over all class prompts and keeps the classes whose presence score clears one global threshold. It then encodes the image once at 1008 px, re-checks presence after the first decoder layer, and fully decodes only the survivors. Exclusive Concept Decoding labels each pixel by comparing its class-score vector with class signatures estimated from unlabeled COCO images. It reports 66.0 mean mIoU on eight benchmarks with one shared hyperparameter set, and runs 7.5× faster than SegEarth-OV3 on average.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (reviewer C1) Which classes get the higher-resolution look, at what granularity (image or location), and under what rule?
The preview runs SAM 3 at 336 px over all C prompts and skips the segmentation head. It admits A(I) = {c : sigmoid(ρ_c) ≥ τ}, with one global τ = 0.1 across datasets (Eq 2) [full text, Sec 3.2, Sec 4]. The full-resolution image encoder then runs once at 1008 px. After one decoder layer each admitted prompt is re-checked against the same τ, and only prompts that pass get the full decoder and segmentation head (Eq 3) [Sec 3.2, App A]. The selection is therefore a two-stage elimination over the class axis with resolution as the precision knob. It is made per image: there are no crops, no per-location zoom, and the second look covers the whole image. The preview keeps 7.3 of 81 prompts on COCO-Object, 30.8 of 150 on ADE20K and 18.0 of 23 on Cityscapes. Its purpose is speed: it changes mIoU by at most 0.34 on any benchmark and gives 1.8–4.1× speed-ups, but only 1.25× on Cityscapes, because most classes there are admitted [Sec 4.2, App H].

### Goal: (reviewer C1) What mIoU does it reach on the eight benchmarks, and how does that compare with CLIP-based training-free methods, especially on Cityscapes, ADE20K and COCO-Stuff?
Table 1 [full text, Sec 4.1]:
- **ActiveSAM:** V21 83.4, P60 56.6, C-O 72.6, V20 97.1, P59 60.4, C-S 44.9, City 73.0, ADE 40.0, average 66.0.
- **SegEarth-OV3 (SAM 3):** average 63.9 (City 69.7, ADE 37.6, C-S 42.8).
- **Vanilla SAM 3:** average 57.5.
- **CLIP-based training-free rows in the same table:** ProxyCLIP City 33.9, ADE 19.7 (average 41.1); Trident City 42.9, ADE 21.9 (45.8); CorrCLIP City 51.1, ADE 30.7 (53.6); ReME City 59.0, ADE 28.2 (55.2); RF-CLIP City 43.0, ADE 21.0 (45.5).

The SAM 3 line is therefore 14–39 mIoU ahead of CLIP-based training-free methods on Cityscapes. All runs use one RTX 5090 and no per-benchmark tuning [Sec 4].

### Goal: (reviewer C1) What does it report about small objects, or about classes the low-resolution preview misses?
After both elimination stages, 93.8% of ground-truth image–class pairs are still available to predict. The rate is 93.9% for connected components smaller than 1,024 px and 90.6% for the least frequent third of each vocabulary [App H, Table 11]. Retention is lowest on COCO-Stuff (79.8%), yet decoding every prompt there gains only 0.34 mIoU [App H]. The authors state that "prompt filtering can also omit small or occluded objects" [Ethics statement]. Preview resolution from 336 to 672 px moves mean mIoU by at most 0.04 [App, Table 12]. Small structures are not otherwise analysed, and no stage re-encodes a selected region at a higher zoom.
