# Efficient Semantic Understanding from Digital Foveation

## What this paper does
An ECCV 2026 workshop paper that builds an active-vision semantic segmentation pipeline: a low-resolution preview yields a bottom-up (Itti-style) saliency map and a global context embedding; fixations are chosen by winner-take-all with inhibition of return; at each fixation a high-resolution foveal crop is segmented by a lightweight network (Lite R-ASPP) trained on sparse object views; logits are accumulated in a Gaussian-weighted "semantic canvas". Evaluated on CLEVR and an object-only ADE20K subset (98 classes) for object-level accuracy and recall versus FLOPs.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: (innovator C1, novelty check) What decides where the pipeline takes a high-resolution look, is it training-free and open-vocabulary, and what is it optimised for?
Fixations come from a class-agnostic bottom-up saliency map on the low-resolution image plus inhibition of return; no class evidence drives fixation selection, and the authors note that the semantic canvas "only weakly influences fixation selection and stopping decisions" [full text, Sec 3, Sec 6]. They also note that "saliency naturally favors large and visually distinctive regions", which is why training fixations are sampled from object masks instead [Sec 3]. The segmentation network is trained with dense supervision on foveal crops, over a closed set of ADE20K-Object classes (layout/background classes excluded) [Sec 3, Sec 4]. The goal is compute reduction: one fixation reaches 95.9% of baseline Top-1 at 4.7% of the compute; 16 fixations recover 90.6% of baseline object recall at 58.6% compute, while dense reconstruction "remains more challenging" [Sec 6-7]. So it neither re-queries a vision-language model nor targets classes that lose the coarse competition.
