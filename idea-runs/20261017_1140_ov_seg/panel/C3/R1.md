# Review R1 (area expert: training-free OVSS / frozen SAM 3 / test-time zoom), submission C3, CVPR 2027

## Summary
C3 is a training-free plug-in for frozen SAM 3 OVSS pipelines (ActiveSAM, SegEarth-OV3). It masks each class's already-labelled segments as attention keys after the shared backbone pass, re-reads SAM 3's presence token on aspect-preserving root tiles, and zooms in a best-first, depth-2 quadtree only where this "residual presence" still fires. Zoomed views may only add small, certified segments of flagged classes, and the coarsest valid view keeps authority. The claimed insight is that presence on an unedited crop cannot tell "all instances found" from "an instance still missed", while presence after removal can (a group-testing analogy). The predicted evidence is qualitative: residual presence has a higher AUROC than unmasked presence for "this view holds a missed component" on Cityscapes; recovery-versus-passes curves lie above uniform tiling and ZoomEye-style search; stuff IoU stays within noise; and mIoU does not fall on any base.

## Strengths
- The question is sharp and timely. Frozen SAM 3 is now the training-free state of the art (ActiveSAM 66.0 mean mIoU, CoCo-SAM3 64.3 [2606.16996][2604.19648]), and every one of these pipelines runs one squeezed 1008x1008 pass [2511.16719][2604.19648][2512.08730]. Recovering instances that pass never saw is a real gap that refiners cannot close by construction [2503.19777].
- The core mechanism is new as far as I can find. I found no training-free work that asks a frozen open-vocabulary recogniser "is c still here?" after hiding c's found segments and uses the answer to gate zoom. ZoomEye ranks unedited crops and visits every node for all-instance cues [2411.16044]. AdaCount amplifies found instances rather than removing them [2607.02139]. ActiveSAM and SegEarth-OV3 use presence only to drop classes [2606.16996][2512.08730].
- The cost is low by design. SAM 3's backbone does not depend on the prompt, and the fusion encoder and decoder run per prompt [2511.16719][2603.11441], so a per-class key mask yields a residual test for every tested class from one backbone pass.
- The baselines are well chosen. (iii) gives the same search without removal and isolates the gate. (ii) is the worst case at the depth cap. (i) separates out the aspect-ratio fix. The matched-budget adaptations of MagNet, ViCrop-Det and ROI-SAHI are the right controls.
- The study is honest. A go/no-go pilot comes first, the kill conditions are explicit, the limitations say up front that the mIoU effect may be small, the search is anytime so one run gives the whole budget curve, and every hyperparameter is fixed in advance.
- Authority fusion is a sensible safeguard, drawn from SNIP-style valid ranges and a containment condition [1711.08189][2610.09313]. A no-regression check on stuff and large objects is built into the evaluation.

## Weaknesses
- **[major] The paper that results is thin on the metric this community uses.** Every prediction is directional ("higher AUROC", "recovers more", "within noise", "without lowering mIoU"). At threshold, the paper therefore shows a gain on a recovery-rate protocol the authors built themselves, unchanged mIoU, and a cost of up to 43–64 view passes per image. That is 10–60x a single ActiveSAM pass, against ActiveSAM's ~0.25 s per Cityscapes image [2606.16996]. Five of the eight benchmarks (VOC, Context, COCO) are ~500 px images. On those, a depth-2 view (0.36 of the short side, ~135 px) is upsampled about 7.5x into the 1008 input, so the zoom only adds interpolated pixels and the eight-benchmark table will mostly show zeros. The paper's own estimate of where the gate matters most (pole, sign, light) covers 3 of Cityscapes' 19 classes. A CVPR OVSS reviewer who sees "+0.x mIoU at 20x cost" will reject unless the small-class IoU and recovery gains over baseline (i) are large, and the proposal commits to no magnitude.
- **[major] Much of the Cityscapes gain probably comes from baseline (i), and that part is standard practice, not a contribution.** Aspect-preserving overlapping windows are the default sliding-window protocol of training-free CLIP OVSS. Without windows, ProxyCLIP falls from 36.0 to 13.6 mIoU on Cityscapes, the largest drop of any benchmark, because that dataset's aspect ratio is furthest from square [2503.19777, Table 3]. For SAM 3, resolution is the main lever on small objects: 1008 to 644 px cuts COCO APS from 40.3 to 12.4 [2603.11441]. Listing "aspect-preserving root tiles" as a novel architectural element will read as overclaiming. More importantly, the gate's real contribution is only its increment over (i) plus uniform deeper tiling, and the predictions never say how large that increment should be.
- **[major] The strongest cheap competitor triggers are missing.** The base pass already provides class-specific "present but unlabelled" evidence at zero extra cost. First, SAM 3 decodes 200 queries with boxes and scores, and ActiveSAM throws away those below 0.3 [2606.16996][2511.16719]. Sub-threshold query boxes of c that fall outside F_c point straight at candidate missed instances. Second, the presence-gated semantic score S_c outside the dilated F_c is a dense residual map. The claim "no zoom criterion separates present from present but unlabelled" is only true for the triggers the authors chose to list. Baseline (iv) uses the top-1 minus top-2 margin of the argmax, which is not class-specific residual evidence. If a residual dense-score trigger matches the residual pooled test, the pooled-presence and group-testing story adds nothing. The pilot's AUROC comparison should therefore have this third arm, not just residual versus unmasked presence.
- **[major] Removing found segments after the backbone may leak, and the fallback breaks the compute argument.** The backbone mixes tokens through 336 px windowed attention in every layer and global attention in 4 of 32 layers [2511.16719]. By the time keys are masked, evidence of a found pole is spread well beyond a 1-token dilation. The presence head was also trained on whole images, so a masked-key input is out of distribution. The evidence on frozen models after erasing is mixed. Mask-free OVIS finds that once the object is erased, a frozen VLM's activation moves to background regions [2303.16891], and Adversarial Erasing reports context answering [1703.08448]. On the other side, CAFE finds SAM 3's presence fairly robust to context conflict (IL_MCC 0.857) [2605.09591]. If leakage is high, the stated fix is token dropping inside the backbone per class. That costs one backbone pass per tested class, about 18 on Cityscapes [2606.16996], and the gate then costs more than the tiling it replaces. A lenient τ = 0.1 also makes the second half of the kill condition (residual ≥ τ on most fully explained views) a live risk.
- **[major] In dense scenes the gate can save little, and "view passes" undercounts cost.** One view retires only when every tested class is negative. Cityscapes admits 18 of 23 prompts [2606.16996], and poles, signs and people appear across nearly every street view, so most views stay positive and C3 tends toward (ii). The fusion encoder and decoder cost grows linearly with the prompt count (34.7 ms for 8 prompts against 53 ms for the backbone at 1008 px [2603.11441]), so a view with 18 tested prompts costs well over one backbone pass. The "fraction of (ii)'s passes" prediction should be stated and tested in seconds or FLOPs, not in passes.
- **[minor] The certification rule conflicts with the motivating thin targets.** With 14 px tokens, r_min = 2 view tokens means 28 view px. On a 2048x1024 frame that is about 28 native px at a root, 17 at depth 1 and 10 at depth 2. A distant pole narrower than about 10 px can therefore never be written, at any depth. The motivating example (three distant poles) may be structurally unrecoverable. Recoveries will fall in a narrow 10–28 px width band, and the analysis should say so.
- **[minor] The novelty statements overreach in places.** "Erase-and-re-ask exists only with a retrained second look" is wrong. Mask-free OVIS iteratively mean-fills the most activated region and re-queries a frozen, unmodified ALBEF [2303.16891]. Its use is offline pseudo-labelling, so it does not touch the zoom-gate contribution, but the claim must be softened. The group-testing frame is mostly narrative: the proposal itself concedes that the k·log2(n/k) rate does not carry over, and the "shared backbone, per-prompt test" structure is already ActiveSAM's and DART's [2606.16996][2603.11441].
- **[minor] There are many hand-set constants** (τ, φ, m, ω, r_min, a_max, margin, child side and stride, D). "Fixed in advance" helps, but they were clearly chosen with Cityscapes geometry in mind, and reviewers will want sensitivity curves on the main metric, not only in an appendix.
- **[minor] The write-only fusion cannot fix base errors.** It only adds pixels. It cannot correct confusions (pole labelled as building is fixed only if the missed part falls outside F_building and the guard allows it) or remove base false positives. The precision of additions is measured but has no target.
- **[minor] Related work omits CoCo-SAM3** [2604.19648], a frozen-SAM 3 single-pass base at 64.3 mean mIoU that ActiveSAM does not compare against either, and DART [2603.11441] for SAM 3's cost structure.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2411.16044] ZoomEye: a training-free quadtree zoom ranked by the model's own crop-level presence confidence. It is the search skeleton C3 reuses. It does not kill novelty, because it tests unedited crops, visits all nodes for all-instance cues, and produces no dense output. C3 is essentially "ZoomEye plus removal of found segments plus per-pixel authority", and the paper must show that the removal is what matters (baseline iii does this).
- [2606.16996] ActiveSAM: presence re-checked after the first decoder layer with one global τ, per image, to eliminate classes. C3 runs the same operation per crop with key masks. It does not kill novelty: ActiveSAM serves as C3's base and has no crop-level use of presence.
- [2303.16891] Mask-free OVIS: a frozen VLM is re-queried after the most activated region is erased with a mean fill. The erase-and-re-ask principle with a frozen model overlaps. It does not kill novelty (it produces a localisation map for offline pseudo-labels, with no presence test and no zoom gate), but it contradicts the "only retrained" claim.
- [1703.08448] Adversarial Erasing and [2003.07080] PS-RCNN: erase and look again with retrained networks. The principle overlaps; neither kills novelty.
- [2607.02139] AdaCount: a second SAM 3 pass conditioned on the instances found so far (warping toward look-alikes). It is the closest "condition on found" design, but it amplifies rather than removes. Not a kill.
- [2512.08730] SegEarth-OV3: per-window presence filtering inside uniform sliding windows on very large images. It overlaps with presence evaluated per crop, but is used only to suppress classes. Not a kill.
- [2503.19777] LPOSS: documents aspect-preserving sliding windows as the standard OVSS protocol and its large effect on Cityscapes. This kills the novelty of the root-tiling element only, not of the gate.
- [2604.26806] ViCrop-Det and [2608.23923] ROI-Gated SAHI: training-free crop selection (attention entropy; where something was already detected). The overlap is in selective zoom. Not a kill, since they are class-agnostic and are included as baselines.
- [2610.09313] (concurrent): theory of when crop decompositions are safe and when adaptive zoom can beat tiling. C3 borrows its containment and graded-evidence conditions. Complementary, not a kill.
- [2604.19648] CoCo-SAM3 and [2603.11441] DART: frozen-SAM 3 OVSS and multi-class SAM 3 with a shared backbone. No overlap with the mechanism. They are relevant as a missing base and as the cost reference.
- [2605.09591] CAFE: indirect evidence on whether SAM 3's presence depends on context. It does not overlap with the method.

## Missing baselines or experiments
1. **A residual dense-score trigger.** Zoom where max over p outside D_m(F_c) of S_c(p) is high, using only the base pass, at matched budget, and as a third arm in the pilot AUROC. This is the decisive control for "pooled presence carries evidence that dense maps do not".
2. **A sub-threshold query-box trigger.** Crop around SAM 3 instance queries of c with score in roughly [0.05, 0.3) whose boxes lie outside F_c, SAHI-style, with the same certification and fusion.
3. **Standard SAM 3 sliding-window and multi-scale test-time inference** at native resolution (e.g., 1008 windows with stride 2/3 at scales 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0, merged with the same authority rule). This is the semantic-segmentation community's default recipe for small classes. Baseline (ii) only partly covers it.
4. **All gains reported over baseline (i)**, not only over ActiveSAM: per-class IoU for the Cityscapes small classes, mIoU, and recovery at matched seconds.
5. **One more high-resolution benchmark with many small classes** (e.g., Mapillary Vistas val or ACDC), because Cityscapes is the only one of the eight where zooming adds real pixels.
6. **A leakage diagnostic with ground truth.** Compare residual presence after key masking against presence after the found instance is actually inpainted out, on the same views, to measure how much post-backbone masking leaks.
7. **CoCo-SAM3 as a third base**, and compute in seconds and FLOPs as the primary x-axis of the matched-compute curves.
8. **A breakdown of missed components by width at each depth**, showing how many can be certified at all under r_min = 2 tokens.

## Questions for the authors
1. On Cityscapes, what fraction of base-missed pole, sign and light components are at least 10 native px wide, i.e. writable at depth 2 under r_min = 2 tokens? If most are not, is the recovery headline about poles still achievable?
2. Did you measure how much the found instance's evidence survives the backbone's 4 global-attention layers? What residual AUROC would make you switch to per-class backbone dropping, and what does that switch do to seconds per image on Cityscapes?
3. What mIoU and small-class IoU gain over baseline (i) do you expect on Cityscapes, and how many passes does C3 actually spend per image there when it stops on its own (no budget cap)?
4. Why use the pooled presence token rather than sub-threshold queries or residual semantic scores, which come free from the base pass? Have you checked which signal carries more graded evidence about sub-token targets?
5. How do C3's writes interact with ActiveSAM's Exclusive Concept Decoding? Are added pixels re-decoded against class signatures, or written outside ECD? How are the background classes of VOC21 and PC60 handled?
6. On the ~500 px benchmarks, are zoomed views capped so that no view is upsampled beyond some factor? If not, what keeps 7x upsampled crops from producing false positives that pass certification?
