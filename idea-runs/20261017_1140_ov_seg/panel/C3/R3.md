# Review R3 (experimentalist) — C3 "Remove the Found, Ask Again: Residual Presence Tests Tell a Frozen Segmenter Where to Zoom" — CVPR 2027 main track

## Summary
The submission wraps a frozen SAM 3 OVSS pipeline (ActiveSAM, with SegEarth-OV3 and ProxyCLIP as secondary bases). It tiles the image into aspect-preserving roots and, for each minority class, masks that class's already-labelled tokens as attention keys in the fusion encoder and decoder. It then reads SAM 3's presence after decoder layer 1 and spends a quadtree budget (depth 2) only on views and classes where this "residual presence" stays above τ = 0.1. Zoomed views may only add certified segments of flagged classes under a coarsest-valid-view authority rule. The claimed insight is the group-testing one: removing found items turns "is c present?" into "is an unlabelled c present?". The predicted evidence is a higher pilot AUROC than unmasked presence, a recovery-versus-budget curve above presence-ranked search (iii) and uniform tiling (ii) at some budget, stuff IoU within noise of the base, and mIoU that does not fall.

## Strengths
- The matched baselines are well built. (ii) and (iii)–(vi) share C3's child geometry, certification, write restrictions and fusion, so baseline (iii) isolates exactly the removal step. Most zoom papers do not do this.
- Compute is reported in the right units: view passes, decoded (view, class) pairs and seconds per image. The anytime search gives the whole recovery-versus-budget curve from one logged run, which is cheap and honest.
- The ablation list targets the mechanism directly: where removal happens, per-class versus union masks, removal margin, re-detection guard, each validity condition, roots, search order and base pipeline. Several fusion ablations can be recomputed offline from the logged writes.
- A go/no-go pilot with a written negative-result branch, and a context-answering rate as a reported metric. The proposal names its own most likely failure (context answering [1703.08448]; leakage through a windowed backbone [2511.16719]) instead of hiding it.
- The no-regression metrics (precision of additions; stuff and large-object IoU change) are the right guard against a method that "recovers" small things by spraying pixels.
- The one-configuration protocol follows ActiveSAM's [2606.16996], and the main runs fit the stated hardware. My own estimate of the depth-2 worst case for one method on all eight benchmarks is about 2.3 RTX 5090-days, using ActiveSAM's per-benchmark throughput ([2606.16996], App H Table 10). At the stated 2× slowdown that is about 4.7 RTX 4090-days, consistent with the proposal's "about 5".

## Weaknesses
1. **[major] The load-bearing comparison is against a strawman, and the obvious same-cost alternative gate is missing.** Both the go/no-go (prediction 1) and the gate claim (prediction 2, via baseline iii) compare residual presence only with *unmasked* presence. By the proposal's own argument, unmasked presence fires on every view that holds any instance of c, labelled or not. Beating it is close to guaranteed and says little. A skeptical reviewer will ask about the gate that needs no masking at all, "decode-and-diff": in the same view pass, decode c unmasked (C3 decodes flagged classes anyway) and open children for c iff the view yields candidate c-evidence outside D_m(F_c). That could be a candidate segment mostly outside F_c, or max S_c above a low threshold outside F_c. It costs the same pass, needs no attention surgery, and answers the same question ("is there unlabelled c here?") directly. Only if residual presence beats decode-and-diff at matched passes has the paper shown that *removing* found evidence adds information, rather than just looking at a zoomed view. Baseline (iv) is a top-1 minus top-2 margin on the base map, and is not this test.
2. **[major] At its stated thresholds the resulting paper has no committed effect on any standard metric.** No prediction names a magnitude. Prediction 2 is satisfied if C3's curve beats (iii) and (ii) at a single budget with non-overlapping CIs. Prediction 7 only requires that mIoU does not fall. There is no mIoU threshold for the main ActiveSAM base, and the limitations section already says the mIoU effect "may be small" and the paper "has to lead with recovery rate". Taken exactly at threshold, the paper has these results:
   - a self-defined recovery metric that improves over the authors' own adaptations;
   - flat mIoU on eight benchmarks, three of them near saturation (VOC20 97.1);
   - unquantified pole, sign and light IoU;
   - a cost of up to 43–64 view passes per image, against ActiveSAM's single pass (0.25 s per Cityscapes image on a 5090 [2606.16996]).

   CVPR OVSS reviewers read mIoU tables first. Without a pre-stated gain on Cityscapes small-class IoU, a reviewer can fairly call this an expensive analysis paper.
3. **[major] The default write rules make thin, tall structures, the headline class, largely unwritable at D = 2.** The full SAM 3 detector uses stride-14 tokens, 72×72 at 1008 px ([2511.16719], the encoder ablation describes the stride-28 variant as using "4× fewer tokens" than the default). On a Cityscapes root (1024 px side) a view token is about 14 native px; at depth 1 (614 px) about 8.5 px; at depth 2 (369 px) about 5.1 px. With r_min = 2 tokens across the narrow side, a pole must be at least about 28, 17 or 10 px wide to be written at depth 0, 1 or 2. The containment rule (box plus a margin of half its size inside the view) then caps its long side at roughly 185–245 px in a depth-2 view. A distant pole 6–9 px wide is therefore unwritable at any depth. A 12 px pole is writable only at depth 2 and only if it is shorter than about 15–20 times its width. The motivating example ("three distant poles") is mostly outside the reachable set. Expected result: recovery for poles near zero at D = 2, while signs and lights, which are compact, carry the result. The paper needs to report the *reachable set* (missed components that satisfy the validity rule at some depth ≤ D) and an oracle-gate upper bound. It also needs a rule for elongated segments, such as stitching partial segments across overlapping children or containing only the cross-section, or else it should drop poles from the headline.
4. **[major] The pilot selects the design on labelled evaluation data.** The pilot runs on Cityscapes val and 500 ADE20K val images with GT-derived labels. It decides go/no-go and picks the removal variant ("the cheapest variant whose residual AUROC is within 0.02 of the best"), and sensitivity is reported on the same val sets. This contradicts the claim that "no hyperparameter is set on evaluation labels". It also breaks the protocol of the base pipeline, which states that "no evaluation image is used before inference" and rejects even unlabelled val images for calibration ([2606.16996], Sec 4 and App C). The fix is cheap: run the pilot on Cityscapes train and ADE20K train, which a training-free method never uses otherwise.
5. **[major] The budget is tighter than stated, and the cost of the SegEarth-OV3 base is unspecified.** The 25-of-40 GPU-day figure counts "the three matched baselines", but six are listed, (i)–(vi). The 4090-days below assume that a 4090 is half a 5090, and nothing has been rerun:
   - (ii) and (iii) both run near the worst case, about 4.7 GPU-days each, because unmasked presence is positive almost everywhere.
   - (iv)–(vi) each run at C3's budget, about 2–3 GPU-days each.
   - C3 runs on two bases.
   - Per-class token dropping inside the backbone costs one backbone pass per tested class, roughly 10–18 on Cityscapes.
   - The D = 3 ablation costs 4× the depth-2 worst case on Cityscapes and ADE20K, since 3·(1+4+16+64) = 255 views against 63.
   - SegEarth-OV3 decodes the full vocabulary, and ActiveSAM reports itself 7.3–12.2× faster than SegEarth-OV3 on large-vocabulary sets [2606.16996]. If C3's views on the SegEarth-OV3 base have no admitted set A(I), that base alone exceeds the budget on ADE20K and COCO-Stuff.

   I count about 33 GPU-days on 4090s, and about 50 if the shared cluster hands out 3090s. Also, the work in week 1 includes per-prompt key masking inside fused attention, backbone token dropping under RoPE and windowed attention, ActiveSAM integration, four adapted baselines and a CLIP instantiation. That is a lot of engineering for three weeks.
6. **[major] The benchmarks do not match the story.** Five of the six image sets (VOC, Context, COCO-Object/Stuff, ADE20K) are natively at or below 1008 px on their long side. Every zoom beyond the root is therefore pure interpolation: an ADE20K depth-2 child is about 185 px upsampled to 1008. Even on Cityscapes the root is already at native resolution (1024 → 1008), so children are 1.6–2.7× upsampled. Resolution sensitivity on these sets is modest: EOVSAM gains about 2 mIoU on ADE20K going from 512 to 1152 px ([2608.02284], Table 3). The paper's own thesis, a sampling bottleneck on recurring thin street structures, calls for at least one benchmark where zoom adds *pixels*: a high-resolution driving set with many thin classes, such as Mapillary Vistas val (named, not read), or the high-resolution remote-sensing sets where SegEarth-OV3 is native [2512.08730]. Its absence will be read as avoiding the regime where the method should shine, or as an admission that it does not.
7. **[major] The main risk to the mechanism is evidenced, not hypothetical.** Masking keys after the backbone leaves the found instance's evidence spread across neighbouring tokens. These carry it through 336 px windowed attention and four global layers [2511.16719], and a 1-token dilation does not catch this spread. SAM 3's presence is designed to read scene-level context [2511.16719]. An independent study finds presence behaves like a scene prior and hallucinates on structurally similar content [2607.09583]. ViTs whose tokens are dropped "maintain their original predictions even when large parts of the image are removed" [2204.08945]. Classifiers asked again after erasing answer from context [1703.08448]. A street scene with its found poles masked still looks like a street with poles, and per class this cuts against residual presence being informative. The pilot is the right response, but given (1) its bar is too low.
8. **[minor] Statistical care is thin for a curve-based claim.** The kill condition ("CIs overlapping at every budget") allows a win at any one of about 10 budgets, on either of two datasets, against either of two comparators. That is a multiplicity problem, and overlap of marginal CIs is the wrong test for a deterministic pipeline evaluated on the same images. Pre-register one summary, for example recovery at 50% of (ii)'s passes or the area under the recovery-versus-passes curve, and test it with a paired bootstrap over images. "Within noise" for stuff IoU (prediction 3) and "without lowering mIoU" (prediction 7) need non-inferiority margins, and prediction 5 (touching instances) needs a stated count of such instances, because signs-on-poles may be a few hundred cases with little power.
9. **[minor] Writes bypass ActiveSAM's Exclusive Concept Decoding.** ECD is worth 3.3 mIoU on Cityscapes because rider overlaps person [2606.16996], and C3 writes from views by thresholds alone. It is unclear how a zoomed "rider" or "person" write is checked against the other. SegEarth-OV3 also uses per-dataset thresholds [2512.08730], which sits awkwardly with the claim of one shared configuration for both bases.
10. **[minor] The pilot's AUROC can be ill-conditioned.** At root level on Cityscapes nearly every view may hold some missed pole, so the base rate is close to 1. Report positives per (depth, class) and the AUROC with its CI.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2606.16996] ActiveSAM — presence-based class elimination at 336 px, then 1008 px, once per image, with no crops. It is the base, not a competitor for the zoom idea, and does not kill novelty. Its protocol (no evaluation images before inference) is what the pilot violates.
- [2604.19648] CoCo-SAM3 — a training-free SAM 3 OVSS method on the same eight benchmarks: 64.3 average, Cityscapes 70.7, a single 1008×1008 pass, about 0.93 s per image on a 4090. It is not cited. It does not overlap the mechanism and does not kill novelty, but it is a missing comparison row and a natural third SAM 3 base. It is also stronger than SegEarth-OV3's 63.9, which the proposal uses as its second base.
- [2512.08730] SegEarth-OV3 — per-window presence used only to suppress categories in uniform sliding windows on very large images. It does not kill novelty, but it is the closest training-free precedent for per-crop presence on SAM 3.
- [2411.16044] ZoomEye — a presence-ranked quadtree on unedited crops that exhaustively visits nodes to depth 2 for all-instance cues. It is the closest search scheme, already baseline (iii). The removal step and per-pixel authority differ, so novelty survives.
- [2610.09313] Concurrent theory on when crop decompositions cannot hurt, and when adaptive zoom can beat tiling (it needs graded sub-token evidence). It overlaps the validity rule and supplies the theoretical frame. It does not kill novelty, but it does raise the bar: the pilot is the paper's only test of the "graded evidence" premise.
- [2608.02284] EOVSAM — trained on COCO Panoptic, so not a training-free baseline. It is relevant as evidence that the resolution gains on low-resolution benchmarks are modest.
- [2607.09583] SAM 3 presence head analysed as a scene-level prior with hallucinations on look-alikes. It does not overlap the method, but it supports the context-answering risk.
- [1703.08448] and [2204.08945] — erase-and-re-ask with a retrained second look, and token dropping in global-attention ViT classifiers. Both are acknowledged. They do not kill novelty, and both point to the same failure direction (models keep answering after removal).

## Missing baselines or experiments
- **Decode-and-diff gate** at matched passes, as baseline (vii) and as the pilot comparator (Weakness 1). This is the experiment that isolates the claimed mechanism.
- **Standard multi-scale and sliding-window inference with score averaging** (the usual segmentation TTA) on ActiveSAM at matched seconds per image. Baselines (i) and (ii) use C3's own write-only fusion, but the baseline a segmentation reviewer expects is ordinary score averaging.
- **CoCo-SAM3 [2604.19648]** as a comparison row, and ideally as a base.
- **A high-resolution thin-structure benchmark** (Mapillary Vistas val, about 2,000 images, roughly 0.5 GPU-day at the depth-2 worst case) where zoom adds real pixels.
- **Reachable-set and oracle analyses**: the fraction of missed components writable at some depth ≤ D under the validity rule, binned by narrow side and aspect ratio. Add an oracle gate (zoom exactly where GT says a component is missed) to show the ceiling for any gate, and where C3 sits between (ii) and the oracle.
- **Pareto plot** of mIoU and of mean IoU over Cityscapes pole, light, sign and person against seconds per image on one 4090, including single-pass ActiveSAM, SegEarth-OV3, CoCo-SAM3 and vanilla SAM 3, not only curves among zoom strategies.
- **Hallucinated additions per image** (written segments with no GT component of that class) per class, alongside the precision of additions.
- **Pilot on train splits**, with base rates per depth and class reported.
- **A paired-bootstrap test on one pre-registered summary statistic**, with non-inferiority margins for stuff IoU and mIoU.

## Questions for the authors
1. Which single prediction carries the paper, and what minimum gain in Cityscapes pole, sign and light IoU (or mIoU) do you expect from ActiveSAM + C3 over ActiveSAM, and over baseline (i)?
2. With r_min = 2 tokens on the narrow side and the box-plus-margin containment rule, what fraction of the poles ActiveSAM misses on Cityscapes val can be written at any depth ≤ 2? How are tall thin segments that span several children assembled?
3. Why does the pilot (and the variant selection) run on val rather than on train? Will you rerun it on train?
4. For the SegEarth-OV3 base, which classes do the views test (it has no preview admitted set), and what does a view pass cost?
5. How is uniform tiling (ii) ordered at budgets below its full set, so that its curve is comparable with C3's best-first curve?
6. Does the kill condition for prediction 2 require a win on both Cityscapes and ADE20K, or on either? Which statistical test decides it?
7. How are rider/person and bicycle/motorcycle conflicts resolved for writes, given that ECD is bypassed?
8. On ADE20K and COCO, where a depth-2 child is a 3–5× upsampled crop, what fraction of recovered components comes from depth 2, and with what precision?

- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.4  (load-bearing: prediction 2, that the residual gate recovers more missed components than presence-ranked search without removal (iii) at matched view passes. Leakage through the backbone's windowed and global attention, SAM 3's context-driven presence [2607.09583][2511.16719], and the persistence of ViT predictions after token removal [2204.08945] all push residual presence toward unmasked presence. The lenient kill condition (any one budget) partly offsets this.)

Run the pilot on Cityscapes and ADE20K train, and pre-register a minimum gain over that gate in Cityscapes small-class IoU at a stated fraction of (ii)'s passes.
