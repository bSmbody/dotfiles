# C3 — Review by R2 (generalist), CVPR 2027 main track

## Summary
The submission wraps a frozen SAM 3 open-vocabulary segmenter (ActiveSAM as the base) in a training-free zoom search. For each class it hides the segments the current map already labels, re-reads SAM 3's presence score on a crop, and splits and re-encodes only the crops that still answer "yes". Zoomed views may only add small, contained segments of the flagged class, and coarser views keep authority over everything else. The claimed insight is that a "residual" presence test, unlike presence on an unedited crop, tells "present but unlabelled" apart from "present and already found". The predicted evidence: a higher pilot AUROC than unmasked presence on Cityscapes, better recovery of missed components than presence-ranked and uniform tiling at matched view passes, stuff IoU kept within noise, recovery concentrated in sub-token components, and gains carried over to SegEarth-OV3 and ProxyCLIP "without lowering mIoU".

## Strengths
- The core idea is crisp and easy to remember. "Remove the found, ask again" fits in a title, and the failure it fixes is real: presence-ranked zoom on unedited crops cannot stop once some instance is visible. ZoomEye's all-instance mode visits every node to depth 2 for exactly this reason [2411.16044].
- The motivation is honest and specific. SAM 3 and its OVSS pipelines squeeze every input into a 1008 px square [2511.16719][2606.16996], and every refiner in the related work redistributes the first pass's evidence rather than re-acquiring it.
- The paper is clear about what it does not do. It does not move boundaries between labelled classes, it claims accuracy at matched compute rather than group-testing rates, and it names the aspect-ratio fix (baseline i) as a possible explanation of much of the gain.
- The go/no-go pilot is pre-registered and the hyperparameters are fixed in advance, so the experimental logic is easy for an outside reader to follow. The negative-result path is stated.
- It is a plug-in. It wraps an unchanged base, and the anytime search gives a whole recovery-versus-budget curve from one logged run.

## Weaknesses
- **[major] At the stated thresholds, the paper has no headline gain in a standard metric.** None of the seven predictions commits to an mIoU or per-class IoU number. The accuracy predictions are "more recovery than (ii)/(iii)" with non-overlapping CIs at some budget, "stuff IoU within noise" and "without lowering mIoU". The limitations already say the mIoU effect "may be small and the paper has to lead with recovery rate", a metric the paper defines itself. A CVPR reader who has seen this year's SAM 3 OVSS tables (ActiveSAM 66.0 mean mIoU [2606.16996], EOVSAM, TAG [2610.14651]) will look first at the mIoU row and find ±0. A self-defined recovery rate as the headline, plus "no regression" on seven of eight benchmarks, is the profile of a paper that gets "interesting analysis, limited impact".
- **[major] The regime where the gate is selective and the regime where zoom helps barely overlap.** The group-testing logic pays off only when missed instances are sparse, so that most views come back negative and retire. On Cityscapes, the one benchmark where zoom clearly adds pixels (2048x1024 frames squeezed to 1008), misses are dense. Using the same criterion as C3 (less than half a component correctly labelled, components of at least 20 px), frozen SAM 3 fails 19,225 of 46,490 Cityscapes val components, about 38 per image [2610.14651, App D.5.1]. ActiveSAM is stronger than vanilla SAM 3, but a dense miss pattern of that kind means nearly every root and most first-level children legitimately hold a missed component of some tested class. The ideal residual test is then positive almost everywhere, and C3 becomes uniform tiling, which the authors concede for cost ("cost approaches uniform tiling"). Where misses are sparse (VOC, COCO-Object, Pascal Context, mostly ≤ 640 px images), zooming only upsamples pixels SAM 3 already had. EOVSAM's resolution sweep on such benchmarks moves mIoU by only about 1–2 points across 512–1152 px [2608.02284, Table 3]. So "tells a frozen segmenter where to zoom" may be true only where there is little to gain from zooming. The paper needs a benchmark where small objects are both numerous in the image and sparse per view, such as high-resolution street or aerial imagery, or a direct count of how many views the gate can actually retire on Cityscapes.
- **[major] The load-bearing signal is SAM 3's least reliable, most context-driven output.** SAM 3's presence token exists to pool "contextual cues from the entire image" [2511.16719]. TAG's oracle study finds that concept recognition and presence, not mask quality, are SAM 3's bottleneck, including under-activation of clearly visible objects [2610.14651, Sec 2.2]. Hiding found poles leaves a street scene in which "pole" is highly probable from context alone, the context-answering failure Adversarial Erasing reported after erasing [1703.08448]. The default removal masks keys only after a backbone with four global-attention layers, so information about the found instance has already spread to surviving tokens [2511.16719]. The lenient τ = 0.1 was calibrated by ActiveSAM for unmasked whole images, not masked crops [2606.16996]. Every one of these pushes residual presence toward "always yes", and the fallback (dropping tokens inside the backbone per class) costs one backbone pass per class, which undoes the compute story. The pilot is the right control, but my prior that it passes both of its kill clauses is below even.
- **[major] Too many moving parts for one memorable claim.** The method stacks aspect-preserving roots, per-class key masking, a minority-class filter (φ), a re-detection guard (ω), three validity conditions (r_min, a_max, margin), a removal margin m, best-first quadtree expansion and authority fusion. On top come a group-testing analogy and borrowed theorems from a VLM paper. Thirteen ablations try to untangle this. The reader leaves unsure whether the gain is the residual gate, the aspect fix, or simply tiling with careful write rules. The authors themselves expect baseline (i) to explain much of the Cityscapes gain, and at threshold the gate's own margin over (iii) is just "CIs not overlapping at some budget". For an 8-page CVPR paper, the gate needs one clean figure showing it is responsible for most of the gain, or the story should be reframed honestly around matched-compute tiling.
- **[minor] Prediction 2 is not internally coherent.** C3 visits a subset of the views of (ii) and uses the same decode, certification and fusion. At the depth-cap budget it therefore cannot recover more than (ii) unless masked decoding itself finds extra instances, which is a different and unclaimed mechanism. "Matches (ii) with a fraction of its passes" gives no fraction. Pick one claim (equal recovery at ≤ X% of the passes of (ii)) and state X.
- **[minor] The group-testing framing is decoration rather than analysis.** The proposal concedes that the k·log2(n/k) rate does not carry over, because one view tests every class and dense scenes keep views positive. What is left is the intuition "remove a found item before re-testing". The theoretical section's appeal to Thm 1/4 of [2610.09313] rests on monotonicity assumptions ("more tokens per object does not hurt") that the proposal itself says crops can violate (context loss, ProxyCLIP's drop at higher input size [2411.09219]). A generalist reviewer will read this section as borrowed rigour.
- **[minor] The novelty sentence about erase-and-re-ask is too strong.** The paper says erase-and-re-ask "exists only with a retrained second look". Seeing Through Clutter (3DV 2026) runs a training-free loop: a frozen VLM proposes an object, Grounded-SAM segments it, inpainting removes it, and the same frozen VLM is asked again until it finds nothing; it reports ADE20K things IoU 0.45 → 0.51 [2602.04053, Sec 3.1, Table 2]. That loop is class-agnostic, works in pixel space and does not gate zoom, so C3's specific contribution survives, but the sentence must be corrected and the paper cited.
- **[minor] The whole case rests on very recent preprints.** The base (ActiveSAM, Sept 2026) and several key comparisons are recent preprints [2606.16996]. If reviewers do not accept ActiveSAM as the reference state of the art, the "plug-in on the SOTA" framing weakens. The vanilla SAM 3 and SegEarth-OV3 rows help; the base-pipeline ablation is important to keep.
- **[minor] The schedule is tight.** Custom per-prompt attention masks inside SAM 3's buckets, three bases plus a CLIP instantiation, eight benchmarks and thirteen ablations must fit in three weeks. The compute fits; the engineering risk is real. I leave the details to the experimentalist.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2411.16044] ZoomEye: a training-free quadtree zoom ranked by the model's own presence confidence on unedited crops; its all-instance mode searches the whole tree to depth 2. C3 is essentially ZoomEye's search with found segments removed, plus per-pixel authority fusion. This does not kill novelty, but it puts all of the novelty on the residual gate; the search itself is not new.
- [2606.16996] ActiveSAM: presence elimination on frozen SAM 3 (336 px preview, then a 1008 px re-check after decoder layer 1), once per image, used for speed. C3 reuses this machinery per crop with removal. It is the base, not a competitor; no novelty conflict.
- [2607.02139] AdaCount: frozen SAM 3, a second pass steered by the instances found in the first pass, which are amplified by warping toward look-alikes rather than removed. It is single-concept counting, and it fails when nothing was found. Does not kill novelty, and it is a good contrast for the paper.
- [1703.08448] Adversarial Erasing: erase what was mined, then ask a retrained classifier for more; it reports context answering once most evidence is erased. It is not training-free, so it does not kill novelty, but it predicts C3's main failure mode.
- [2602.04053] Seeing Through Clutter: a training-free remove-and-re-ask loop with frozen VLM and Grounded-SAM, with segmentation gains on ADE20K. It contradicts the "only with retraining" claim. It does not kill C3, which differs by gating zoom with a class-specific test, masking tokens and producing dense output, but the paper must cite it.
- [2610.14651] TAG (concurrent, Oct 2026, under review): diagnoses frozen SAM 3's downstream failures as recognition and presence errors (41% of Cityscapes components failed, with failures covering whole objects) and fixes them with a 3.1M-parameter adapter trained on image-level labels. There is no mechanism overlap, and it is not training-free. It does compete for the same "missed objects" story and challenges C3's "sampling bottleneck" framing. Should be discussed.
- [2608.02284] EOVSAM: a trained, single-pass SAM 3 OVSS model. Its resolution sweep (A-150 mIoU 37.0 → 39.0 from 512 to 1152 px) bounds how much resolution alone buys on low-resolution benchmarks. No mechanism overlap.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- **A ceiling oracle: GT-guided zoom.** Zoom exactly on the base map's missed components, using the same decode, certification and fusion, and report recovery, small-class IoU and mIoU on Cityscapes and ADE20K. Without it a reader cannot tell whether a perfect gate would move any standard number, which decides whether the paper matters.
- **Decomposition of the base's misses.** Following TAG's object-level analysis [2610.14651], split ActiveSAM's misses by size and by cause (absorbed into background or another class at the right location versus confusion), and report the fraction a zoomed re-look could address at all.
- **Sparsity statistics for the gate.** On Cityscapes and ADE20K, report the fraction of root and depth-1 views with no missed component of any tested class, which is the most the gate can retire. If this is small, the "where to zoom" claim needs a different testbed.
- **A high-resolution, small-object benchmark** where zoom matters and misses may be sparse per view: Mapillary Vistas val, or a remote-sensing set from SegEarth-OV3's own domain (e.g. iSAID, UAVid, LoveDA) [2512.08730]. As written, the paper's real testbed is 500 Cityscapes val images.
- **A predicted per-class IoU for pole, traffic sign and traffic light** over baseline (i) at a stated fraction of the passes of (ii), as a headline number.
- **A τ calibration control.** Re-calibrate the residual threshold on held-out non-evaluation images, such as the unlabeled COCO train images ActiveSAM already uses for signatures, versus reusing τ = 0.1. This separates "the gate is uninformative" from "the gate is uncalibrated".
- **Context row:** TAG's weakly supervised numbers on Cityscapes, so readers can see how much of the "missed object" gap a cheap presence calibration closes.

## Questions for the authors
1. Under ActiveSAM, what fraction of Cityscapes root views and first-level children contain no missed pole, traffic sign or traffic light component? If it is small, what does the gate retire, and where does the matched-compute advantage over (ii) come from?
2. What gain in Cityscapes mIoU and in small-class IoU do you expect, and what is the smallest gain that would still make the paper worth publishing?
3. With masking applied only after the backbone, how do you rule out the found instance leaking through the four global-attention layers? Will the pilot report residual presence on views where every component of c is labelled and removed, for each removal variant?
4. Why reuse τ = 0.1, calibrated for unmasked whole images, instead of calibrating on held-out unlabeled images, which your protocol would allow?
5. At the depth-cap budget, by what mechanism could C3 recover more components than uniform tiling (ii)?
6. How does C3 differ from Seeing Through Clutter's training-free remove-and-re-ask loop [2602.04053], beyond zoom gating?
7. Thin stuff-like classes (fence, wall, vegetation strips) often exceed φ in a view and are never tested. Is that intended, and how much of the Cityscapes thin-structure error do they account for?

## Rating
- rating: 3
- confidence: 3
- p_core: 0.4  (The pilot needs residual AUROC above unmasked AUROC, which is a low bar and fairly likely, AND residual presence below τ = 0.1 on most fully explained views. SAM 3's context-pooling presence token, post-backbone leakage, a borrowed τ, and dense misses on Cityscapes all push against the second clause.)
- one_point_fix: Re-centre the evaluation on a standard-metric headline. Add a GT-guided zoom oracle as a ceiling, and one high-resolution small-object benchmark (Mapillary Vistas or a SegEarth-OV3 remote-sensing set) next to Cityscapes. Then commit to a predicted small-class IoU and mIoU gain over aspect-preserving tiling (i) at a stated fraction (≤ 50%) of the view passes of uniform tiling (ii). The residual gate would then be judged in a regime where it can retire views, with a number a CVPR reader cares about.
