# EAGLE-3: Scaling up Inference Acceleration of Large Language Models via Training-Time Test

## What this paper does
EAGLE-3 is a trained one-layer draft head for speculative sampling. Unlike EAGLE/EAGLE-2 it drops the feature-regression loss and drafts tokens directly. Its input fuses low, middle and high-layer hidden states of the target model through an FC layer, concatenated with the sampled token embedding. A "training-time test" simulates multi-step drafting during training, so that the draft head learns to take its own outputs as input. With this change acceptance keeps growing as training data grows. Drafting uses the EAGLE-2 dynamic tree (depth 8). Code: https://github.com/SafeAILab/EAGLE (stated in abstract).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the mechanism and which models and generation settings are evaluated?
Mechanism: [full text, Section 3.1-3.2]. Models: Vicuna 13B, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, LLaMA-3.3-70B-Instruct and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B (DSL). The 405B/671B models were not tested because of GPU limits [Section 4]. Tasks: MT-bench, HumanEval, GSM8K, Alpaca, CNN/DM. These are short-generation chat and benchmark prompts. The paper states no long-CoT (16K+) generation length. The DSL draft was additionally trained on OpenThoughts-114k-math [Section 4, Implementation]. The paper does not discuss the draft head's own KV cache or how acceptance behaves at long positions.

### Goal: What are the headline measured speedups?
Batch size 1, HF backend: 3.0x-6.5x over vanilla autoregressive decoding, 20-40% better than EAGLE-2, peaking at 6.5x and tau=7.5 on HumanEval [Section 4.1, Table 1]. DSL-8B at T=0 gets a mean 4.16x (GSM8K 5.01x, tau 6.93), against 3.26x for EAGLE-2 [Table 1]. Ablation on LLaMA-3.1-8B MT-bench: EAGLE-2 3.16x, then 3.82x after removing the feature constraint, then 4.40x with fused features [Table 2].

### Goal: How does the gain change with batch size?
The gain shrinks as batch grows [Section 4.3-4.4]. SGLang on H100 with LLaMA-3.1-8B (chain length 3, no tree): 1.81x at bs=2, 1.48x at bs=16, 1.38x at bs=64. EAGLE (v1) falls below 1.0 from bs=24 [Table 3]. vLLM with chain length 2: 1.75x at bs=2, 1.21x at bs=48, 1.01x at bs=56 [Table 5]. The authors say this follows because SD "leverag[es] redundant computational power. As batch sizes increase, this redundancy decreases" [Section 4.3]. The large-batch numbers come from short MT-bench outputs, not long generations.

### Goal: What limitations do the authors state?
The paper has no limitations section. Implicit limitations: 405B+ models untested [Section 4], speedup decays with batch [Section 4.3-4.4], and the draft must be trained per target model with target-generated data [Section 4, Implementation].
