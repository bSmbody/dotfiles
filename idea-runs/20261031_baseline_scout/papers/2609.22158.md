# StepKV: Step-Aware KV Cache Compression for LLM Agents

## What this paper does
StepKV compresses the KV cache of multi-step LLM agents. It treats reasoning steps (Thought / Action / Observation) as retention units. Each step gets a utility from its immediate contribution and from later reuse of its evidence. That utility is combined linearly with a token-level saliency score, and tokens are globally ranked under a budget. It targets "Reasoning Continuity Disruption" on multi-hop QA and web agents.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How are units defined and how is unit value estimated?
Units are agent steps given by the trajectory format [full text, Section 3, 5]. The step score is r_k = valid_k + gain_k − lambda red_k. Here gain is 1 minus the maximum overlap-based textual similarity to earlier steps, red is the similarity of the action to earlier actions, and valid marks a usable observation [Section 5.1.1, Eq 2]. A reuse accumulator c_k grows when later observations overlap step k's observation [Section 5.1.2, Eq 3]. S_k = clip(w_r clip(r_k) + w_c log(1 + c_k)) [Eq 4].

### Goal: How are units turned into a retention decision — set-level or additive?
Additive. Each token inherits its step's utility, P_i = alpha T_i + beta S_k, and "the final selector globally ranks tokens by P_i and retains the top B_t" [Section 5.2, Eq 5]. No step is protected. Reuse raises the value of the reused step instead of marking its later copies as substitutes, and nothing models how many heads or how much mass a unit keeps.

### Goal: What limitations does it state?
The lightweight signals may be unreliable when grounding is incomplete, tool responses are noisy, or important information is "implicitly expressed and difficult to extract". Evaluation covers a limited range of model scales and agent benchmarks [Appendix F]. It relies on the agent's explicit step format, not on free-form chain-of-thought.
