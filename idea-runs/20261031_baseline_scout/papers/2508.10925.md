# gpt-oss-120b & gpt-oss-20b Model Card

## What this paper does
Model card for OpenAI's open-weight MoE reasoning models gpt-oss-120b (36 layers, 116.8B total / 5.1B active) and gpt-oss-20b (24 layers, 20.9B / 3.6B). Covers architecture, MXFP4 quantization of MoE weights, harmony chat format, variable reasoning effort (low/medium/high), tool use, capability and safety evaluations. Attention alternates banded sliding-window (128 tokens) and dense layers, with GQA and a learned per-head softmax-denominator bias (attention sink).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What attention structure does gpt-oss use and what does its KV cache grow with?
"Attention blocks alternate between banded window and fully dense patterns, where the bandwidth is 128 tokens. Each layer has 64 query heads of dimension 64, and uses GQA with 8 key-value heads"; dense layers extended to 131,072 tokens with YaRN; "Each attention head has a learned bias in the denominator of the softmax ... which enables the attention mechanism to pay no attention to any tokens" [full text, Section 2.2]. So only the dense half of the layers holds length-proportional KV. Derived from these numbers (not stated in the card): per dense layer per token K+V = 2 x 8 x 64 = 1024 values, i.e. 2 KiB in BF16; for 120b (18 dense layers) about 36 KiB/token, about 4.5 GiB at 128K per sequence; window layers hold a fixed 128-token cache.

### Goal: What other inference-relevant design and stated tradeoffs are reported?
MoE weights (90+% of parameters) are post-trained into MXFP4 (4.25 bits/param), so 120b fits on one 80GB GPU and 20b on 16GB [Section 2.1]. Attention parameters are only 0.96B (120b) and 0.64B (20b) [Table 1]. Reasoning levels set in the system prompt; higher levels increase average CoT length with smooth accuracy gains on AIME/GPQA [Section 2.5.2, Figure 3], so at high effort the long-generation KV of the dense layers is the growing cost. The card gives no attention-efficiency ablation and no KV/latency measurements.
