# EpiKV: Epiphany-Aware KV Cache Eviction Without the Attention Matrix

## What this paper does
EpiKV is a training-free decode-time evictor for reasoning models (R1-Distill Llama-8B and Qwen-7B; MATH-500, AIME, GPQA). It scores each token at write time by a drift-corrected hidden-state shift: a rolling z-score of the residual-stream change at an early "Band A" layer minus the same at a later "Band B" layer. The bands are located against counterfactual occlusion labels. At each compaction, 128-token segments get caps by key-variance tier, and inside each segment tokens are ranked by how much the last few queries point to them. Prompt and the 128 most recent tokens are pinned. No attention matrix is needed, so it runs on FlashAttention in vLLM. Main finding: the accuracy that eviction costs goes to generations that loop to the length cap, not to finished wrong answers.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to value cached reasoning tokens, and is it set-level or dependency-aware?
A per-token write-time scalar s(t) = z_10(t) - z_21(t), the rolling z-scored L2 change of the hidden state at two layers [full text, Section 2.2, Eq 1-3]. The authors say large shifts mark "a committed step or an intermediate result, whereas fluent filler barely moves it". The bands were found by correlating g_l with labels from sliding 32-token occlusion: a window is important if overwriting it changes the regenerated answer. The authors call this label "recoverability under occlusion" [Section 2.2, "Measuring importance"; Appendix B.1]. s(t) is "computed when token t is generated and never revisited" [Section 2.3]. Within segments, ranking uses recent-query relevance [Section 2.3, "The query term"]. Nothing models dependencies between units, restatements, or how a value's worth changes when its operands are evicted.

### Goal: When eviction removes tokens the model later needs, does the model re-derive them, answer wrongly, or fail some other way?
It mostly fails to stop. On MATH-500 at K=1024, "the generations that finish are wrong about as often as without eviction"; the extra errors are "generations that loop until the length limit and never answer, under every policy and on all models we test" [Section 1; Section 3.2, Table 3]. In one example a policy lets the model reach the right answer, "restate it hundreds of times and write the digit 2 until the limit" [Section 1, Figure 4]. Filling the cache with more tokens by a fixed write-time score "makes the looping worse"; keeping the tokens the recent queries point to lets the generation stop [Section 1]. The paper does not report clean re-derivation of lost values as a recovery mode.

### Goal: What limitations does it state?
Both models are R1-distilled, all results are greedy decoding, and on AIME/GPQA the uncapped model itself often fails to stop, which bounds what any policy can recover [Limitations]. Kernel and throughput costs are measured on one GPU.
