# Correcting the LogQ Correction: Revisiting Sampled Softmax for Large-Scale Retrieval

## What this paper does
Revisits sampled softmax with logQ correction for two-tower retrieval over huge catalogs. The full-softmax gradient is an expectation over the catalog; sampled negatives from a proposal Q estimate it by (weighted) importance sampling. The paper points out that the positive item is present deterministically, not sampled from Q, and derives a corrected loss that removes the positive from the sampled denominator and weights the example by the stop-gradient probability of misclassifying the positive, itself estimated by importance sampling. Consistent Recall@k gains on public and industrial data.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does sampled softmax estimate the full-softmax normalizer and gradient from a few samples, and what bias does the deterministic (non-sampled) part introduce?
The full-softmax gradient is -grad f(u,p) + E_{d~P_theta}[grad f(u,d)]; sampling negatives from Q shifts that expectation to Q, so weighted importance sampling reweights each sample by exp(f - log Q(d)) in both expectation and normalizer (standard logQ) [full text, Section 3.2]. Treating the deterministically present positive as if drawn from Q is inconsistent and biased [Section 3.3]. Splitting P(d|u) = (1-P(p|u)) P(d|u, d != p) gives a loss with weight w_up = sg(1-P(p|u)), no positive in the denominator, and P(p|u) estimated as exp(f_p) / (exp(f_p) + (1/n) sum_i exp(f_di - log Q'(d_i))), i.e. exact term for the deterministic item plus an importance-sampled estimate of the rest; the same samples are reused for loss and weight [Section 3.3]. The general recipe: compute deterministically included terms exactly, estimate the remainder from samples of a known proposal with 1/Q weights.
