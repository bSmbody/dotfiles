# DFlash: Block Diffusion for Flash Speculative Decoding

## What this paper does
DFlash (ICML 2026) replaces EAGLE-3's autoregressive drafter with a small (5-8 layer) block-diffusion drafter. The drafter denoises a block of 10-16 masked tokens in a single forward pass. It is conditioned on hidden features fused from 5 target layers, and those features are injected into the K/V projections of every draft layer ("KV injection") rather than only into the input. Verification is standard and lossless. The drafter is trained on target-generated responses with random anchor positions and a loss decay over block positions. Code and Hugging Face models are linked (https://dflash.z-lab.ai) [abstract; comment on arXiv says code at github.com/z-lab/dflash].

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What speedups are measured on reasoning models and at what generation length?
- Thinking disabled, Transformers backend: max 2048 generated tokens. DFlash averages 4.9x over AR at T=0, 2.4x better than EAGLE-3 (tree 16). At T=1 it gets 4.1x [Section 5.1, Table 1 caption].
- Thinking enabled (Qwen3-4B/8B): about 4.2x-4.6x at T=0 (tau 5.2-5.8) and 3.6x-4.0x at T=1 on GPQA, MATH-500 and AIME25 [Section 5.2, Table 2]. The thinking-mode generation cap is not stated in the sections read.
- Training uses sequences of at most 3072 tokens (4096 for Coder) [App A.1].

### Goal: How does the gain change with batch/concurrency?
- SGLang on one B200 (FA4), Qwen3-8B Math500: 5.1x at concurrency 1, 4.5x at 4 and 8, 3.9x at 16, 2.8x at 32. Qwen3-4B HumanEval goes from 4.0x to 2.2x [Section 5.3, Table 3].
- vLLM, Qwen3.5-9B Math500: 4.0x at c=1, 3.2x at c=8, 2.5x at c=16, 1.9x at c=32. MT-Bench drops from 3.0x to 1.3x [App A.4, Table 12].
- So the gain roughly halves between concurrency 1 and 32.

### Goal: How does acceptance behave at long context?
The base Qwen3.5-27B drafter, trained at 4K, degrades past 4K context. gov_report tau: 4.53 at 1K, 3.32 at 8K, 2.67 at 16K, 2.09 at 32K. Fine-tuning on 1.6K LongAlign samples restores it to 3.81 at 16K and 3.56 at 32K [Section 5.4, Table 4]. This is long input on LongBench, not long generated CoT. The draft holds its own KV of injected target features, which grows with context [Section 4.1, A.3]. The paper does not measure draft-side memory or latency at 32K+.

### Goal: Stated limitations?
There is no limitations section. Implicit ones: a per-target trained drafter is required. Comparison is only against EAGLE-3, since other dLLM drafters have no open code [Section 5, Baselines]. Long-context acceptance needs extra adaptation [Section 5.4].
