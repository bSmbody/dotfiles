# Speculative Decoding: Performance or Illusion?

## What this paper does
A systematic benchmark of speculative decoding (SD) in production vLLM (v0.10.1.1, H100, CUDA graphs, continuous batching). It covers n-gram, EAGLE, EAGLE-3, draft-model and MTP drafting. Models: Llama3.1-8B, Llama3-70B, Qwen3-8B with thinking on and off, and GLM-4.5-Air-106B. Workloads include long-CoT reasoning on AIME22-24 and GPQA-Main with up to 32,768 output tokens. The paper breaks down time and memory, studies acceptance by position, request and dataset, and builds an oracle simulator that bounds the achievable speedup. Profiling and simulator code: https://github.com/orgs/SpecDecode-Bench/repositories [abstract].

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does SD speedup change with batch size and with tree vs chain?
Speedup falls with batch size, and the drop is larger for bigger models. EAGLE on Llama3.1-8B GSM8K goes from 1.73x at bs=1 to 1.21x at bs=128 [Section 3.2]. Trees help only at bs=1: Qwen3-8B GSM8K is 1.65x with a chain, 1.68x with k=6 and 1.85x with k=21. At bs=64 the k=21 tree falls below 1x on all workloads, while the chain stays above 1x [Section 3.2, Fig 2]. Verification takes 42-95% of runtime and its share grows with batch and model size. EAGLE drafting takes 12-20% at bs=1 and 3-7% at bs=512 [Section 5.1].

### Goal: What happens on long-CoT reasoning workloads, and with MTP?
Qwen3-8B-Thinking: EAGLE-3 gets 1.64x-1.80x on GPQA-Main and AIME22-24, n-gram 1.50x-1.58x [Section 3.2, Fig 3]. Reasoning runs were limited to medium batch sizes, "Reasoning tasks typically involve long generation sequences, which can trigger request preemption once the KV cache becomes full" [Section 3.2]. In other words, KV capacity, not compute, caps the batch for long CoT. GLM-4.5-Air MTP gets 1.3x-1.8x on GPQA. Only the first MTP module is released and it is reused autoregressively, so per-position acceptance drops 0.92 → 0.68 → 0.38 [Section 3.2].

### Goal: How does acceptance vary with position in long generations?
Accepted length grows with position in long reasoning outputs [Section 6, Fig 6, Qwen3-8B-Thinking GPQA]. EAGLE-3 goes from ~2.1-2.5 tokens in short outputs (<4K) to ~2.5-5.7 in the longest (>13K). n-gram goes from 1.6-3.7 to 2.7-5 and drops near the end, when the model writes the final answer.

### Goal: What headroom and limitations are identified?
Oracle proposal length: 2.75x vs 2.1x for the best fixed k (n-gram, InstructCoder, bs=1), and the gap widens with batch [Section 8.1]. An oracle that picks EAGLE or n-gram per position adds up to 2.2x more [Section 8.2]. This ignores the cost of keeping EAGLE's KV cache when requests are paused and resumed [Section 8.2]. Outputs are not bitwise identical across batch sizes because of numerical nondeterminism, so throughput, not latency per request, is the right metric [Section 3.1, Table 1].
