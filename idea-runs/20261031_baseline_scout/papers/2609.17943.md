# ASPIRE: Asynchronous Batched Self-Speculative Decoding for Long-Context LLM Inference

## What this paper does
ASPIRE (COLM 2026) is batched self-speculative decoding with a sparse-attention draft and a full-attention verifier, run without synchronization across the batch. A unified mixed forward lets some requests draft (sparse KV, 7% of pages) while others verify (full KV) in the same batched pass. A per-request online scheduler, driven by an acceptance-rate estimate and a batch-aware draft/verify cost ratio, picks each request's draft length. A "refresh layer" (the penultimate layer) runs full attention during drafting and updates the sparse KV selection at every draft step, which avoids the staleness of Vegas's fixed selection.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What settings and headline speedups?
Models: Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-8B, DS-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B on 1x H100 NVL. Benchmarks: AIME25 and CodeElo (short prompt, long CoT), plus LongBench 16-18K and LongBench-v2 30-40K / 80-100K inputs. Sampling at T=0.7 [Section 5.1]. Speedup over AR decoding throughput is 1.70x-4.58x. Short-context reasoning: up to 2.12x / 1.86x / 1.80x for the three models. Long context: up to 4.58x / 2.75x / 2.66x [Section 5.2]. Average gain over the strongest prior self-spec baseline (usually Vegas) is ~27% [abstract, Section 7]. Batch is the largest that fits memory, e.g. B=42 for Qwen3-1.7B and B=30 for Qwen3-8B on AIME25, with average generation ~13.6-14.7K tokens [App C.1, Table 6].

### Goal: How does the gain scale with context/generation length?
The gain grows with context: Qwen3-8B goes from 1.81x at LB 16-18K to 2.30x at 30-40K and 2.75x at 80-100K. Synchronized fixed-length drafting (ASPIRE-Fixed) falls behind as context grows: 1.73x → 1.98x → 2.22x [Section 5.3]. A simple FSM controller is close behind: average 3.26x / 1.94x / 1.93x vs ASPIRE's 3.34x / 2.08x / 2.14x [Section 5.3].

### Goal: Limitations / open issues?
There is no explicit limitations section in the parts read. Observations: baselines are only self-spec methods (MagicDec-StreamingLLM, Vegas) plus AR. MagicDec-SnapKV was dropped because its prefill-fixed selection "is poorly suited to our long-generation reasoning workloads, where the drafting cache grows with the generated sequence" [Section 5.1]. The batch size is bound by the full KV cache in memory, because self-spec keeps the full cache, so memory capacity is not reduced. Trained drafters (EAGLE-3) are not compared.
