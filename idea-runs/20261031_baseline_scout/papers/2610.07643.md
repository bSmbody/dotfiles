# Monte Carlo Estimation for KV Cache Eviction

## What this paper does
LORE-KV treats fixed-budget prompt KV eviction as estimating which memory will matter while answering. It samples M short continuations from the frozen model, uses their query states plus the prompt-window queries, scores each token by its projected leave-one-out attention-output deletion cost, and aggregates across futures with entropy/disagreement reliability weights. Training-free; evaluated on LongBench, NIAH and RULER up to 128K over six backbones; a single continuation already recovers ~89% of the gain at B=128.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
Per-token score S_i = mean deletion cost over prompt-window queries + lambda * reliability-weighted sum over lookahead trajectories [full text, Section 3.5, Eq 7]; deletion cost e = ||W_O * Delta o||^2 with closed-form leave-one-out Delta o ~ a/(1-a) * (v_i - o) [Section 3.4, Eq 5-6]. Top-scoring tokens per head kept after prefill; lookahead continuations discarded [Section 3.5].

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
Gains grow with prompt-to-future query mismatch (Spearman 0.28-0.57) and with context length [Section 4.5]. Stated limitations: one-time lookahead overhead, "makes irreversible eviction decisions, and does not explicitly model cross-head or cross-layer interactions" [Section 5].

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Per-token leave-one-out deletion cost: each token's cost is computed with all other tokens present, so the score is additive over tokens and ignores substitution between tokens that carry the same information; uniform per-head budget, per-head top-K [Sections 3.4-3.5]. Applies to prompt compression, not decoding-time eviction.
