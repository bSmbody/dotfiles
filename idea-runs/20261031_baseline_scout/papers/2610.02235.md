# One Distribution, Two Channels: Coverage-Calibrated KV Retention and Compensation

## What this paper does
CORE frames KV retention as set-level coverage. Offline it computes a soft teacher allocation over cached positions that trades query utility against a log-determinant coverage term in a random projection of the concatenated, normalized keys of all KV heads, and distills it into a lightweight feature-based indexer. At inference the indexer's Top-B ordering chooses the retained positions, and the excluded allocation mass parameterizes writes into a latent memory that compensates for evicted entries. It reports gains of up to 3.78 points on RULER at 90% compression and also runs decode-time recompression on AIME25 / Math500 with Qwen3-14B.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What set-level coverage objective does CORE optimize, and at what granularity (per head, per position, cross-head)?
Per layer, candidates are positions i; the coverage representation concatenates the L2-normalized keys of all KV heads at that position, projects them with a fixed random matrix, and centers them [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 3]. The teacher maximizes Σ w_i u_i + β log det(I + α Σ w_i z_i z_iᵀ) minus an entropy term (Eq 4), solved by fixed-point updates whose coverage marginal α zᵀΣ⁻¹z "discounts redundancy and favors under-represented directions" (Eq 5). Utility u_i is a max over queries of the top-mean of query-head scores within each KV group (Eq 2). So coverage is position-level within a layer, with heads fused into one vector; there is no explicit model of which heads keep which copy.

### Goal: Can its coverage model express superadditive (complementary) value across copies, or only diminishing returns?
Only diminishing returns. Appendix B.4 shows the cross derivative of the coverage marginal is −α²(x_iᵀΣ⁻¹x_j)² ≤ 0, "coordinate-wise diminishing returns", and the finite-set gap G_S ≥ 0 [Appendix B.4, Eq 47-48]: joint gain never exceeds the sum of singles. A threshold in which one copy is worthless but two or three together are readable cannot be represented. The multi-head remark only sums per-head error bounds (Remark 7).

### Goal: Does it address decode-time repeated eviction on long reasoning, and what limitations does it state?
Yes in part: Qwen3-14B on AIME25 and Math500 with recompression every 64-512 tokens; CORE leads MOMENTKV and IndexMem at every interval (e.g. 33.33 / 37.60 at interval 64) [Section 5.3]. It does not compare against a uniform-random null baseline. Limitations: stability analysis covers a fixed query, "extending it to full autoregressive trajectories remains future work"; largest backbone 14B [Appendix F].
