# OpenAttention: One Percent Is Enough with Tail Proxies

## What this paper does
Training-free decode-time sparse attention. It argues that the unread tail of a top-k selector should be represented by proxies rather than discarded or randomly sampled: one count-aware mean proxy already beats a random tail sample at low read densities. The method clusters keys under query geometry (QGC), allocates exact reads by an estimated per-cluster weight error (ErrorGate), selects top-k within clusters (QGS), and folds the rest into proxies, with a Triton FlashAttention-2 decode kernel. On RULER-32K HARD with Llama-3.1-8B it keeps 99% of dense accuracy at a 1% read.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: When does random sampling of the post-top-k tail work, and what happens when the selector leaves heavy hitters in the tail?
Sampling (as in vAttention and MagicPIG) "presume[s] that the softmax output of the tail can be estimated from a small sample", but its error "grows sharply as the density decreases": a random sample improves on top-k at high density yet is worse than top-k alone at low density, behind both HashAttention and oracle top-k [full text, Section 1, Section 2.1 / Figure 3]. The normalizer error of a uniform sample is tau*CV_T/sqrt(m), growing as the residual mass concentrates on fewer effective tokens (N_eff) [Appendix A.2]. At a 1% read behind HashAttention, "the reported inclusion probability of the heaviest unread token is 0.5%" over 137,216 rows [Appendix A.2, App. B.2]. Related work states that at low reads samples "can miss the heavy hitters a practical selector leaves in the tail" [Section 5, "Sampling the tail"]. Inference only; nothing is trained.
