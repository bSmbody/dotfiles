# LightTransfer: Your Long-Context LLM is Secretly a Hybrid Model with Effortless Adaptation

## What this paper does
LightTransfer converts a pretrained Transformer into a layer-wise hybrid by finding "lazy" layers (whose last queries put most attention on initial plus recent tokens) and replacing their full attention with streaming attention (sink + window KV only). LightTransfer-Test picks lazy layers per prompt at prefill with a priority queue using FlashAttention's log-sum-exp; LightTransfer-Train pre-selects lazy layers by frequency over (question, answer) training samples and then SFTs the hybrid. Evaluated on LongBench, NIAH, and o1-like long reasoning (Qwen2.5-32B distilled on QwQ-STILL data, 50% of layers replaced, AIME24/MATH-OAI/GSM8K at 32K).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it choose which layers lose full attention, and is the signal computed on the converted model's own generations?
By a lazy ratio r_i = mean attention mass of the last w_last queries on {initial, recent} tokens [full text, Section 5.1, Eq 1]. For long reasoning, prompts are too short for r_i to be informative, so it feeds question plus reference answer from the training set and counts how often each layer is lazy, then keeps the most frequently lazy layers as streaming [Section 5.2]. The signal is computed on the original teacher's attention over fixed text, not on rollouts of the converted model, and there is no iteration or re-selection after training. A bound ties the logit error to the sum of discarded attention mass over replaced layers [Section 5.3, Theorem 5.1].

### Goal: What does it show on long chain-of-thought reasoning, and against which layouts?
Replacing 50% of layers in a 32B reasoning model and SFT on about 5K examples keeps MATH-OAI (+0.5%) and AIME24 (+6.6%) relative to QwQ-STILL, while LongGen's predefined layout (ends replaced, middle kept) loses 30.0% on AIME24 and 12.4% on MATH-OAI; greedy decoding, 32K max tokens [Section 6.2, Table 3]. On LongBench, data-driven selection beats Pyramid and Random replacement layouts [Section 6.3, Figure 7] and Shapley-value and iterative least-influential (BERTology) replacement [Appendix C.5, Table 11]. Retained-layer ratio is swept 0.25-0.75 on LongBench only [Section 6.3, Figure 6].

### Goal: Does it compare layer removal against, or stack it with, intra-layer KV compression at matched memory?
Partly. LightTransfer-Test beats SqueezeAttention (layer-wise KV budget) at the same compression ratio across retention ratios on LongBench and gives higher throughput because it cuts peak prefill memory [Section 6.3, Table 5]. Stacking with SnapKV on the non-lazy layers further reduces KV with preserved performance on Qwen2.5-3B [Appendix C.2, Figure 8], i.e. a composition result, but on long-context understanding (prompt KV), not decode-growing KV in long CoT, and not as a matched-bytes frontier against quantization.

# LightTransfer: Your Long-Context LLM is Secretly a Hybrid Model with Effortless Adaptation

## What this paper does
Converts a pretrained Transformer into a layer-level hybrid by identifying "lazy" layers (those whose queries put most attention mass on initial-plus-recent tokens) and replacing their full attention with streaming (sink + window) attention, keeping full KV only in the remaining layers. LightTransfer-Test does this training-free per prompt during prefill; LightTransfer-Train, used for o1-like long reasoning, pre-selects lazy layers by frequency over question+answer training samples and then SFTs the hybrid. Reports QwQ-STILL (Qwen2.5-32B) with 50% streaming layers matching or exceeding the dense model on AIME24 (53.3%), and shows stacking with SnapKV.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it choose which layers keep full attention, and is the signal computed on the model's own generations?
Lazy ratio r_i = mean attention mass of the last w_last queries on {initial, recent} tokens (Eq 1), computed from FlashAttention's log-sum-exp [full text, Section 5.1, Table 1]. For long reasoning the prompt is too short, so selection feeds the training-set question and reference answer (teacher/off-policy text) and picks the layers that are most often lazy; the selection is then fixed and the hybrid is SFT-ed [Section 5.2]. Signal is attention-pattern based and off-policy; no on-policy rollouts, no failure localization.
### Goal: Does it evaluate layer-level KV removal on long chain-of-thought reasoning, and what does it find?
Yes: QwQ-STILL distilled from Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, 50% of layers streaming, ~5K SFT examples, greedy decoding up to 32K tokens; Math-OAI +0.5, AIME24 +6.6, GSM8K -0.1 relative to the dense model, while LongGen (hand-set end layers) loses 30.0 on AIME24 [Section 6.2, Table 3]. The authors read this as layer-level KV redundancy existing in o1-like reasoning models. Random and Pyramid replacement strategies are worse than lazy-ratio selection on LongBench [Section 6.3, Figure 7].
### Goal: Does it show that removing layer-level KV stacks with intra-layer KV compression?
Yes, qualitatively: LightTransfer-Test combined with SnapKV on the non-lazy layers (Qwen2.5-3B, LongBench) further reduces KV size while preserving performance, presented as orthogonality [Appendix C.2, Figure 8]. It also compares against SqueezeAttention (layer-wise budget) and MiniCache and reports higher throughput [Section 6.3, Table 5]. No matched-bytes frontier against quantization and no long-CoT stacking experiment.
