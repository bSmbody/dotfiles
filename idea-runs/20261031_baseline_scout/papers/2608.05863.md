# Runtime Observability for Heterogeneous Attention Memory

## What this paper does
Companion to WitCert [2607.28699]. Defines a runtime observability contract with three operators (write/update, select, read) covering dense KV, latent caches, learned sparse selectors and recurrent state, gives local per-stage bounds, and composes them into a typed request-level risk ledger in which each claim is certified, partially certified or empirical. Instantiated on six configurations across five families, including MLA latent caches on GLM-5.2 and a served DeepSeek-V4 stack with a packed compressed-KV prototype.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does any prior runtime certificate already cover MLA latent caches, and how tight is it at low bits?
Yes. A latent cache is the instance that "appends a compressed representation and reconstructs on read" [full text, Section 2.1]. The latent meter is validated over 4,032 cells with 0 Tier-A violations and 98.9% dithered Tier-B coverage at tau=0.2 on a small proxy; on GLM-5.2, fp8 storage residual is 0.93% of the latent norm [full text, Section 5.3]. Replacing the worst-case norm witness by the exact rounding distribution with a centred bound TV <= tanh(osc/4) makes 43/43 layers non-vacuous, but "no family reaches tau=0.2 retention at two-bit coarsening" [full text, Section 5.6]. Score error is bounded through a witness-to-score bridge |Delta(scale q k)| <= scale ||q|| ||Delta k|| and a separate score-to-TV bridge [full text, Appendix I]; no joint score-value optimization over a quantization cell is described in the parts read.
