# Tailoring the Quantization Space for 1-Bit KV Cache Compression

## What this paper does
TaSQ: a 1-bit-regime KV vector quantizer. For keys it applies query-guided channel weighting, cross-head shared-scale normalization and covariance-aware channel grouping, then Fisher-weighted VQ (8-channel groups, 1024 centroids); transforms are RoPE-compatible and merged into weights and codebooks. Values use plain contiguous VQ. SGLang + Triton implementation; general, long-CoT and NIAH benchmarks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What bit widths do K and V get, and how is V handled?
About 1.266 bits for K and 1.250 for V (indices plus per-token metadata; static codebooks, the full-precision prefix of 64 and recent window of 256 excluded) [full text, Sections 5.1, Appendix B]. Values get plain contiguous VQ because they show flatter channel sensitivity (CV 0.176 vs 0.841) and weaker inter-channel correlation (0.067 vs 0.136) than keys; key-side tricks did not help values [Section 4.4 "Value path", Appendix C].
### Goal: How does ~1.25-bit KV fare on long-CoT reasoning and retrieval?
Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507: AIME24 68.89 vs BF16 74.44, AIME25 56.67 vs 74.44, LCB-v6 43.00 vs 45.85; Phi4-14B-Reasoning-Plus AIME25 60.00 vs 66.67 [Table 2]. NIAH stays ≥92% to 64K on Llama-3.1-8B; Qwen3-4B-Thinking 81.5 vs 95.3 at 64K [Table 3]. Prior 1-bit VQ (CQ, NovaKV) collapses on long CoT (CQ AIME25 14.44) [Table 2]. Output lengths and cap-hit rates stay close to BF16 [Section 6.1].
### Goal: What serving gain does it report?
On one RTX 6000 Ada, KV pool 235,257 → 2,935,184 tokens, max batch 6 → 84, peak throughput 220.8 → 412.6 tok/s (1.87x) for 2048-in/32768-out; VQ encode adds 10-14% TTFT [Section 6.1, Figure 4].
