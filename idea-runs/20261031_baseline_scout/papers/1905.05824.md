# Counterfactual Off-Policy Evaluation with Gumbel-Max Structural Causal Models

## What this paper does
Builds structural causal models for discrete-state POMDPs so that, for an observed trajectory, one can generate the counterfactual trajectory under a different policy that shares the same exogenous noise. It introduces counterfactual stability as the categorical analogue of monotonicity, shows the Gumbel-max SCM satisfies it, gives posterior sampling of the noise, and uses the counterfactual-minus-observed reward difference per episode to pick out the specific episodes where a learned sepsis policy would have changed the outcome.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism, and what property makes a shared-noise counterfactual meaningful?
Each categorical transition is written as Y = argmax_j {log P(Y=j | parents) + g_j} with fixed Gumbel noise g [full text, Section 3.3, Definition 6]. The expected reward difference between two policies decomposes into per-episode differences between the observed trajectory and its counterfactual under shared noise [Section 3, Lemma 1, Corollary 1]. The Gumbel-max SCM satisfies counterfactual stability: if the intervention does not raise the relative likelihood of any alternative outcome over the observed one, the counterfactual outcome equals the observed one [Section 3.2, Definition 5; Section 3.3, Theorem 2]. Intuition: "the Gumbel variables are fixed. Thus, in order for the argmax ... to change in the counterfactual, the log-likelihood of an alternative outcome must have increased relative to our observed outcome."

### Goal: What does the counterfactual trajectory look like relative to the factual one, and what assumption does it rely on?
Counterfactual trajectories "are identical to the observed trajectory up to a divergence in actions", unlike a freshly simulated trajectory from the same start [Section 5.4, Figure 5], which is what lets the method localize where outcomes part. When the noise is not observed it must be inferred by posterior sampling (top-down truncated Gumbels) [Section 3.4]; counterfactuals are non-identifiable from data, so the SCM choice is an assumption [Section 3.1]. A wrong dynamics model gives misleading counterfactuals (the glucose example) [Section 5.4].
