# RelFlexformer: Efficient Attention 3D-Transformers for Integrable Relative Positional Encodings

## What this paper does
RelFlexformer adds relative-position modulation f(r_i - r_j) to Performer-style linear attention for point clouds. The masked attention (A tilde ⊙ M) V is computed via the Choromanski et al. 2022 algorithm, which needs only fast matvecs with M; RelFlexformer supplies such a matvec (FastMult) in O(L log L) by writing M u as a convolution, approximating it by a quadrature over S frequencies, and evaluating both the forward Fourier sums and the output sums with NU-FFT. Evaluated on 3D classification and segmentation.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Where in the attention computation is the NU-FFT applied, and is softmax kept?
On the mask matvec w = M u only: F_P(xi_s) = sum_l u_l exp(-2 pi i xi_s^T r_l) by NU-FFT, then w(z) = sum_s b_s exp(2 pi i xi_s^T z) at the L token positions by a second NU-FFT [full text, Section 3.2, Eq 8-12, Algorithm 1]. This is plugged into Performer linear attention phi(Q)(phi(K)^T V) (Eq 13), so the softmax is replaced by a feature-map kernel; there is no exact softmax.
### Goal: What does Lemma 3.3 say about RoPE?
For 1D positions with f(z) = cos(theta_d z), F_f collapses to Dirac deltas at ±theta_d/2pi, so fixing the quadrature frequencies to those values "bypasses approximation and analytically evaluates the exact RoPE embedding" [full text, Section 3.2, Lemma 3.3 and proof]. In other words, for RoPE the frequency set is just the d/2 RoPE frequencies, and the sum is evaluated at those discrete frequencies; the lemma gives no speed analysis for this case.
