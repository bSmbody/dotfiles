# HiSparse: Scaling Sparse-Attention Decoding with Hierarchical KV Cache Management

## What this paper does
A serving system (merged into upstream SGLang) that keeps each request's full KV history in host memory and only a small fixed-size GPU cache of KV records, resolving each layer's top-k selection (hit detection, LRU replacement, host-to-device fetch) inside the decode CUDA graph with a fused kernel, plus exact layer-wise prefetch for models that share selections across layers. Exact (outputs unchanged) and indexer-agnostic; evaluated on DSA (GLM-5.1), DeepSeek-V4-Flash, NSA and Quest on H200, B200, GH200.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does top-k sparse attention reduce KV memory at serving time, and how large is the remaining KV bill?
No: "attention became orders of magnitude cheaper, while its memory bill did not drop by a byte", because selections drift and every position must stay addressable [full text, Section 1]. A 128K GLM-5.1 request holds 13.09 GB of BF16 KV; a 1M request would need >100 GB of an H200's 141 GB [Section 1]. On 8xH200 at 32K in / 8K out each request holds up to 4 GB; the full-KV baseline saturates at ~60 concurrent requests (~240 GB KV), ~15 at 128K [Section 2.2]. Indexer keys and page tables stay GPU-resident: "at most a few hundred bytes per token, versus ~100 KB of KV records", and "the indexer's own computation is untouched" [Section 3.2].

### Goal: What gains and overheads does hierarchical KV give, and what limits are stated?
Staging only the current top-k misses 30% of the next step's selections; LRU at 2x top-k gives an 87% hit rate on a LongBenchV2 trace [Section 1]. DeepSeek-V4-Flash on 2xB200 at 32K/8K: throughput 600 -> 1257 tok/s at concurrency 64 (2.1x), 1511 -> 4308 decode-only (2.9x); "HiSparse does not make an individual decode step faster"; TTFT 829 s -> 171 s at concurrency 64 [Section 4.2]. Up to 4.7x peak throughput overall [Abstract]. Limits: exposed IO costs 7-8 ms/token TPOT at low concurrency when misses are synchronous, ~3 ms with exact prefetch; no benefit when not capacity-bound; host DRAM on Grace GB200/GB300 (~480 GB) is comparable to or smaller than HBM, shrinking the multiplier [Section 5]. Note: it labels DeepSeek-V4-Flash's attention as "NSA" in Figure 4, whereas the V4 report calls it CSA (DSA over 4-token compressed entries).
