# Same Luck, Different Choice: Twin-World Credit Assignment for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

## What this paper does
(Note: withdrawn by arXiv administrators for incorrect author affiliation listing; treated here as concurrent prior of the generic idea, not as validated evidence.) TWICE gives turn-level credit to RL-trained LLM search agents by rolling out a counterfactual "twin" of an episode: an alternative turn is sampled with fresh noise, and the rest of the episode reuses the factual episode's Gumbel noise keyed by (episode, turn, line, position). The paired outcome difference is an unbiased turn advantage added to GRPO's episode advantage. A square-root rule spends the twin budget where outcomes are predicted to disagree.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the mechanism, what does it assume, and what does it claim about variance?
Keyed Gumbel-max sampling makes an episode a deterministic function of decisions and noise when tools and grader are deterministic [full text, Section 4.1]. Twin credit D_t = U - U'_t is unbiased for any coupling quality (Prop 1); variance is the independent-branch variance times (1 - rho) (Prop 2); if next-token distributions differ by at most eps in TV over L positions, continuations are identical with probability >= ((1-eps)/(1+eps))^L (Lemma 1, "coupling is local"); sign detection needs Theta(1/Delta) instead of Theta(1/Delta^2) pairs (Prop 3) [Section 5]. Self-twins (alternative equals factual) give D = 0 at no cost [Section 4.2]. Keying by line lets a divergence re-synchronize at the next line [Section 4.1].

### Goal: Where is it applied, what limits does it state, and does it touch memory or KV decisions?
Applied only to search-agent QA and ALFWorld/WebShop, at most eight search calls and models up to 14B [Sections 6, Limitations]. Twins differ in a visible text turn; nothing on KV cache or memory decisions (no mention in the text). Stated limits: coupling weakens "at high temperature and for long continuations, where divergence compounds"; twins credit a whole turn [Limitations]. Reported: twins tie in 71% of pairs and reverse sign in 7% vs 48%/22% for independent branching; twin correlation is 0.72 for near-zero-advantage decisions [Section 6.5].
