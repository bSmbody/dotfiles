# OSDN: Improving Delta Rule with Provable Online Preconditioning in Linear Attention

## What this paper does
Replaces DeltaNet's scalar write step beta_t with beta_t D_t, a diagonal preconditioner learned online by hypergradient feedback (OSGM), which is algebraically a per-feature scaling of the write-side key, so the WY chunkwise kernel carries over. Adds Adaptive Preconditioner Forgetting (APF) for non-stationary contexts and proves residual-contraction bounds against a right-Newton comparator. Evaluated at 340M/10B and 1.3B/100B tokens on JRT-style recall and LongBench.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What memory update rule does it use and what determines the write/erase strength per token?
Eq. 2, 5 [full text, Section 4.1, 4.3]: S_t = S_{t-1}(I - beta_t k_t k~_t^T) + beta_t v_t k~_t^T with k~_t = d_t ⊙ k_t; d_t follows an O(K)-state affine recurrence driven by hypergradients. Motivation [Section 1]: a scalar gate must compromise across keys with "different empirical curvature"; the fix is only useful "if it does not require a dense second-order state ... and does not break the chunkwise parallel DeltaNet kernel".

### Goal: How close does the cheap preconditioner get to the second-order (interference-aware) solution?
Limitations [Section 7]: (i) the main theorem is a regret bound "against the dense right-Newton comparator D* = Sigma_k^dagger; the D* regret is not proved for the implemented diagonal update"; (iii) bounds concern the inner regression objective, not next-token loss; (v) downstream WikiText/LAMBADA/commonsense/LongBench averages remain at parity ("do not claim a universal benchmark lift"). Future work: "lifting the diagonal preconditioner to a low-rank or per-head block-diagonal form to close the gap to the full right-Newton comparator". Reported gains: +32% JRT recall over DeltaNet at 340M; 39% lower recall residual ratio at 1.3B (abstract).
