# Native Sparse Attention: Hardware-Aligned and Natively Trainable Sparse Attention

## What this paper does
NSA replaces full attention with three gated branches over GQA/MQA KV: compressed coarse tokens (learned block compression), selected fine-grained blocks (top-n blocks scored by the compressed branch's attention probabilities), and a sliding window. A Triton kernel loads all query heads of a GQA group at one position and iterates over the group's shared selected KV blocks, giving FlashAttention-level training/prefill speed. Pretrained natively at 27B scale.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does NSA produce selection scores, and how does the selection receive a training signal?
Selection-block importance is not learned by its own objective: it reuses the compression branch's softmax scores p_cmp = Softmax(q^T K_cmp), aggregated to selection blocks and summed over the heads of a GQA group, then top-n blocks are kept [full text, Section 3.3.2 / Eq 8-11]. Top-n is discrete; gradient reaches the scoring only indirectly via the compression branch's own output. The authors report that alternatives fail: learned importance scores need an auxiliary loss (KL to mean-pooled attention, SeerAttention-like) that "increases operator overhead and often degrades model performance", and Quest-style parameter-free scores have low recall; both had worse 3B loss curves [Section 6.1].

### Goal: What hardware constraints shape NSA's design?
Blockwise (contiguous) selection is required for coalesced loads and tensor cores; selection must be shared across the heads of a GQA group so decoding memory access is not the union of per-head selections [Section 2.1, Section 3.3.2 / Eq 10]. The kernel's inner loop is over the shared selected blocks for a group of query heads at one position [Section 3.4].

### Goal: Is there evidence that attention relevance is spatially clustered over adjacent keys?
Yes, qualitatively. Visualizing the attention map of the pretrained 27B full-attention model shows "attention scores tend to exhibit blockwise clustering characteristics, with nearby keys often showing similar attention scores", and this motivated selecting contiguous key blocks [full text, Section 6.2 / Figure 8]. No quantitative measure is given.


### Goal: What is NSA's decode cost profile, and is the reported decode speedup measured or analytic?
Per decode step NSA loads at most floor((s-l)/d) compression tokens, n*l' selected tokens and w window tokens, s = cached length [full text, Section 5.2]. The compressed-token term still grows linearly with s (by 1/d), and the full KV cache stays stored for selection. The "up to 11.6x speedup at 64k" comes from Table 4, which reports "Memory access volume (in equivalent number of tokens) per attention operation during decoding", with expected speedup "approximately linear with the volume of memory access" [Table 4 caption, Section 5.2], i.e. an analytic memory-volume estimate, not a measured end-to-end latency. Training-kernel speedups (measured) are up to 9.0x forward and 6.0x backward at 64K [Section 5.1]. The authors frame two failure modes of earlier sparse methods: phase-restricted sparsity (prefill-only or decode-only) and incompatibility with GQA/MQA, where per-head selection makes KV access the union over heads [Section 2.1].
