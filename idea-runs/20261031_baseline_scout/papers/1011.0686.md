# A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning

## What this paper does
Introduces DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) for imitation learning and structured prediction. A learner trained only on the expert's state distribution can incur cost growing quadratically in horizon T because its own mistakes lead it to states never seen in training. DAgger iteratively executes the current learner, queries the expert on the states the learner itself visits, aggregates these into one dataset and retrains, which via a no-regret reduction gives cost linear in T. Demonstrated on Super Tux Kart, Super Mario Bros. and OCR.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Why does a policy trained (or evaluated) on the expert's state distribution compound errors, and what is the bound?
If the learner's surrogate loss is epsilon under the expert distribution d_{pi*}, then J(pi) <= J(pi*) + T^2 epsilon, and the bound is tight: "as soon as the learner makes a mistake, it may encounter completely different observations than those under expert demonstration, leading to a compounding of errors" [full text, Section 1; Section 2.1 / Theorem 2.1, Kaariainen example].

### Goal: What decomposition ties total cost to per-step behaviour on the learner's own distribution?
Theorem 2.2: if the loss is epsilon under the learner's own distribution d_pi, then J(pi) <= J(pi*) + u T epsilon, where u bounds the increase in expert cost-to-go from one deviating action. The proof telescopes J(pi) - J(pi*) into a sum over t of E_{s~d_pi^t}[Q^{pi*}(s,pi) - Q^{pi*}(s,pi*)], i.e. the cost of deviating at step t on the learner's own states, followed by the expert. u is O(1) when the expert can recover from the learner's mistakes within a few steps (e.g. rapidly mixing dynamics), and can be O(T) in the worst case [Section 2.2 / Theorem 2.2, proof and footnote].

### Goal: What is DAgger's iterative mechanism and why must it iterate?
At iteration i, run pi_i = beta_i pi* + (1-beta_i) pi_hat_i, collect visited states labeled by the expert, aggregate D <- D U D_i, train pi_hat_{i+1} on D [Section 3, Algorithm 3.1]. Iteration is needed because the state distribution depends on the policy itself (non-i.i.d.), so each new policy visits new states; aggregation builds up "the set of inputs that the learned policy is likely to encounter during its execution" [Section 3]. Guarantee: with N = O~(uT) iterations, J <= J(pi*) + u T epsilon_N + O(1) [Theorem 3.2].
