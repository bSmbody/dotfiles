# Back to the Definition: Estimating Step-Level Advantages via Trajectory Graphs for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

## What this paper does
GRAFT merges the N rollouts of an agentic task into a trajectory graph, by exact hashing or embedding similarity, with terminal sink nodes. It estimates node values by Gauss-Seidel Bellman iteration over empirical count-based policy and transition frequencies. Step advantage is gamma V(s') - V(s), extended to a "Graph GAE" over k-hop reachable edges and normalized within each node's outgoing-edge group. Proposition 1 shows that, at the population level, graph TD credit has lower error than GiGPO-style and GRPO credit.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does GRAFT aggregate shared continuation evidence, and are the weights optimized for variance?
V(u) = sum_a pi^(a|u) sum_v P^(v|u,a) gamma V(v), with pi^ and P^ given by edge counts [full text, Section 3.2 Eq 5-6]. The weights are fixed empirical frequencies, not chosen for estimator variance. Graph GAE averages TD residuals over the edges reachable in exactly k hops with uniform weights [Section 3.3 Eq 9-10]. Proposition 1 compares population-level credit signals and does not model the finite-sample covariance among edges that share terminals [Section 3.3; Appendix C].

### Goal: How does it relate to GraphGPO?
It says GraphGPO [2605.26684] uses a shortest-path-distance heuristic reward r = r_succ w^{d(s)} with node-group normalization, while GRAFT uses Bellman values [full text, Appendix A.2].
