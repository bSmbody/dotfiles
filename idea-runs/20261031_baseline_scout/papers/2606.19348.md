# DeepSeek-V4: Towards Highly Efficient Million-Token Context Intelligence

## What this paper does
Preview report of DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T total / 49B active) and DeepSeek-V4-Flash (284B / 13B), both with 1M context. Attention layers interleave Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA: compress KV of every m=4 tokens into one entry, then DSA-style lightning-indexer top-k over the compressed entries) and Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA: compress every m'=128 tokens, dense attention), each with a 128-token sliding-window branch, attention sink, shared-KV MQA with head dim 512, grouped output projection, partial RoPE. Also mHC residuals, Muon, FP4 QAT for experts and the indexer QK path, and a heterogeneous KV cache manager with on-disk prefix storage.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What are the CSA/HCA structures and configs, and what grows with context length per decoded token?
CSA: learned softmax-weighted pooling of 2m overlapping KV entries into one entry per m tokens [full text, Section 2.3.1 / Eq 9-12]; indexer keys compressed the same way; index score I_{t,s} = sum_h w_{t,h} ReLU(q^I_{t,h} . K^IComp_s) over n_h^I heads [Eq 13-16]; top-k compressed entries used as both K and V in MQA [Eq 17-19]. HCA: non-overlapping pooling of m' tokens, no sparsity, dense MQA over all n/m' entries [Section 2.3.2 / Eq 20-26]. Configs [Section 4.2.1]: Flash 43 layers, first 2 pure SWA, then CSA/HCA interleaved; m=4, n_h^I=64 indexer heads, c^I=128, top-k=512, m'=128, n_h=64, c=512, n_win=128. Pro: 61 layers, first 2 HCA, top-k=1024, n_h=128. Thus per decoded token, the growing terms are (a) the CSA indexer scan over n/4 compressed keys with 64 heads x 128 dims, and (b) HCA dense attention over n/128 entries; core CSA attention is fixed at top-k entries.

### Goal: What inference cost numbers do they report (KV bytes, FLOPs) versus baselines?
At 1M context, V4-Pro needs 27% of the single-token FLOPs and 10% of the KV cache of DeepSeek-V3.2; V4-Flash 10% FLOPs and 7% KV [Section 1, Figure 1 right]. Versus a BF16 GQA8 head-dim-128 baseline, V4 KV cache is "approximately 2%" at 1M [Section 2.3.4]. Contributing choices: RoPE dims in BF16 and others in FP8 (about half the KV bytes), indexer attention in FP4, smaller top-k than V3.2 [Section 2.3.4]. FP4 QAT on the indexer QK path ("QK activations are cached, loaded, and multiplied entirely in FP4") plus BF16 index scores gives "2x speedup for the top-k selector, while preserving a 99.7% recall rate of KV entries" [Section 5.2.1]. The authors single out the indexer as the term they quantize "under extremely long contexts", i.e. they treat it as a long-context hot spot; no per-component decode-time breakdown is given.

### Goal: What serving / KV management problems does the hybrid structure create, by their own account?
"The hybrid attention mechanism violates fundamental assumptions behind PagedAttention": different KV sizes per layer, the indexer's extra KV dimensions, SWA's distinct hit/eviction policy, and uncompressed tail tokens that must be buffered until m tokens accumulate [Section 3.5.1]. They use a fixed-size per-request state cache for SWA + tail tokens and a block layout covering lcm(m,m') tokens. For on-disk prefix reuse, SWA KV is "approximately 8 times larger than the compressed CSA and HCA KV entries"; they offer full SWA caching (write-heavy, poorly matched to SSD), periodic checkpointing every p tokens, or zero SWA caching with recomputation of the last n_win*L tokens [Section 3.5.2].

### Goal: What limitations do the authors state?
Architecture is "relatively complex" because many validated tricks were kept; they plan to distill it to essentials; training-stability tricks (Anticipatory Routing, SwiGLU Clamping) are not understood; they will "continuously investigate low-latency architectures and system techniques to make long-context deployment and interaction more responsive" [Section 6]. No ablation isolating CSA vs HCA quality is in the sections read.
