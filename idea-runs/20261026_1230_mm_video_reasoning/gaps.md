
## Axis: Thinking with images: visual operations interleaved in the reasoning chain

### What has been done
Search trail: "thinking with images", interleaved visual reasoning zoom crop tool, agentic multimodal reasoning image operations, multi-turn grounding RL MLLM, visual tool use RL multimodal, pixel-space reasoning, zoom-in RL high-resolution, tool-integrated reasoning VLM, grounded reasoning bounding box CoT, video frame-retrieval tool RL, plus recency queries (since 2025-10-26) on information-gain rewards for visual tool calls, process reward for zoom-in, tool-call reward hacking, observation utilization. Ten papers read.

- **Coordinate crop as a native action, trained end to end with outcome RL.** The policy emits box coordinates, the environment crops the original image and appends the crop as observation tokens; GRPO on the whole trajectory with observation tokens loss-masked. Reward = accuracy + format + a bonus for ≥1 crop on a correct answer [2505.14362]. Crop IoU with GT evidence is only a diagnostic (0.37 at 7B) [2505.14362].
- **Rewards that push the policy to use the operation at all.** Curiosity bonus when the per-query rate of pixel-space operations is below a target, plus an over-use penalty, for CropImage and SelectFrames (video) [2505.15966]. "Look-and-win" bonus for ≥1 aperture on success [2602.14482]. Both credit the act of calling the operation.
- **Step or trajectory shaping of the operations.** A pairwise-IoU redundancy penalty among the policy's own zoom boxes, applied only to wrong-answer trajectories, after SFT on synthesized multi-turn trajectories [2512.17306]. Per-state tool rewards against ground-truth boxes or rotation labels (recall-weighted pixel F1 for zoom), in a stage kept separate from the accuracy stage because joint training fell back to text-only reasoning [2604.19945]. A frozen evaluator's confidence gain in the GT answer from the textual trajectory prefix (RUR), which needs a mandatory policy-written Observation after each Zoom/Segment [2602.14482]. Teacher-matched input attribution of the tool call (integrated gradients, KL to a teacher) [2606.11652].
- **Alternatives to coordinate crops.** A <GROUND> probe token whose similarity map over patch tokens yields sampled connected-component regions, with SFT on GT-box patch labels and then GRPO over N1 regions × N2 answers using one jointly normalized advantage [2610.14265]. Internalized "imagination": a text-only region choice plus an imagined observation, distilled on-policy from a same-model teacher that sees GT-box crops [2606.08719].
- **Test-time robustness and step judging.** Sample many interleaved traces, score each stage by the top-k token entropy of its text (the tool-call text stands in for crop reliability), filter, then reliability-weighted voting [2602.12916]. A seven-type error taxonomy and a PRM benchmark show LVLM step judges score below chance on erroneous steps and do worst on image-extraction steps [2602.08346].

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** RL for interleaved visual operations credits an operation either for being invoked (crop bonus [2505.14362], curiosity bonus [2505.15966], look-and-win [2602.14482]), for its box geometry against GT or other boxes ([2604.19945], [2512.17306]), or for how confident the trajectory text is ([2602.14482] RUR, [2602.12916] entropy). None of these signals checks whether the returned pixels changed the answer. So in multi-step crop/zoom reasoning on high-resolution images and charts, the policy can still call the tool and then ignore, or verbally override, what it returned.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2602.14482] RUR comes closest. It is a dense gain in a frozen evaluator's confidence in the GT answer, but it is one scalar per trajectory, and it scores the policy's own Observation text. It never checks that this text is entailed by the view, and it does not attribute credit among several apertures. [2604.19945] gives true per-step zoom rewards, but only by matching GT boxes, and it had to separate them from the accuracy stage because joint training fell back to text-only reasoning. [2512.17306] adds a geometric overlap penalty among the policy's own boxes, which says nothing about crop content. [2606.11652] attributes the tool *call* to its inputs (gradient of log π(call)), not the answer to the returned observation, and broadcasts a trajectory-level advantage. [2610.14265] samples N1 regions × N2 answers but normalizes one joint advantage, with no per-region or no-region contrast. [2606.08719] gets dense supervision only from GT-box crops given to a teacher, and self-proposed boxes recover only part of the gain. The same deficiency shows up as behavior in several papers. [2505.15966] reports a reward-hacking loop in which the policy "earns the curiosity bonus by superficially executing visual operations... but ignores the resulting errors, ultimately relying on its textual reasoning". [2602.08346] finds Answer-Reasoning Inconsistency and Redundant-Reasoning errors, and LVLM PRMs below chance on erroneous steps, weakest on image-extraction steps. [2505.14362] reaches only 0.37 crop IoU at 7B even with a +18.9 V* gain. The recency queries on information-gain rewards and observation utilization (since 2025-10-26) surfaced no method that credits a visual operation by the counterfactual effect of its returned observation.
- **Methodological deficiency:** This is a credit-assignment flaw. Every reward in the read work is a function of the action (that a call happened, its coordinates) or of policy-generated text. None is a function of the observation's causal contribution to the answer, so the operation and the use of its output are optimized as one unit. Trajectory-level GRPO advantages then spread one scalar over all operations in a rollout. As a result, a rollout that crops the right region but answers from language priors gets the same credit as one that answers from the crop. Pixel Reasoner documents exactly this ([2505.15966], Section 5.2). RUR cannot separate the two, because the policy-written Observation can state the answer whatever the view shows ([2602.14482], Section 3.3). The only dense alternatives need GT evidence boxes ([2604.19945], [2606.08719]), and those reward where the model looked, not whether the answer depended on what it saw.
- **Why it matters:** The problem's failure mode is a trace that cites a value not in the image because the decisive detail was never actually re-inspected. Under current rewards, tool use rises (V* jumps) while the answer can stay decoupled from the fetched evidence. This blocks anyone who wants to train thinking-with-images on data without evidence-box annotations, or to scale to many operations (crops, frame selection in video, drawing) where GT per step does not exist. Closing the gap would show whether interleaved visual steps help because of the pixels they return or because they act as a prompt for longer text reasoning.
- **Evidence route:** For trained policies, measure answer sensitivity to the returned observation: replace or blank each returned crop (or frame) at test time and record the answer flip rate, compared with DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, TikArt (RUR) and ToolsRL-style baselines at matched accuracy on V*, HR-Bench and TreeBench. Also track the share of correct answers whose decisive crop is counterfactually necessary, Answer-Reasoning Inconsistency rates on ThinkWithImages-PRMBench-style annotation, and whether gains hold on training data with no evidence boxes.

- **Gap:** Interleaved visual-operation policies append every returned crop to an append-only context and treat it as evidence. Recovery from a wrong or drifting crop is handled by imitating synthesized self-corrections in SFT ([2505.15966], [2512.17306]) or by discarding whole traces at test time ([2602.12916]). This leaves the policy unable to judge, within one trajectory, that an observation it fetched is wrong or irrelevant before that observation steers the answer.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2602.12916] targets this failure directly ("one mistake leads to another"). Its fix samples many traces and filters them by the entropy of the tool-call text, so one trace is never repaired from inside, no training signal results, and cost grows with the number of samples. Its own case shows a model that "recognizes the visual cue is incorrect yet still derives the wrong answer" (Fig 16). [2512.17306] observes "oscillatory micro-adjustments around the same target" because the policy "heavily relies on grounding-based initialization". Its overlap penalty pushes later boxes away from earlier ones, but only on failed trajectories and without any judgment of whether the earlier crop was wrong. [2505.15966] shows that without error-induced self-correction trajectories in SFT, the operation failure rate rises during RL and the policy ignores the errors. Its remedy is imitation of scripted corrections, not a learned verification step. [2505.14362] reports grounding drift: a second zoom that missed the target reversed a correct hypothesis into a wrong answer. [2610.14265] names error propagation from coordinate errors as a reason to drop coordinates, but it uses a single grounding step and no revision.
- **Methodological deficiency:** This is a missing mechanism plus a representation limitation. Each returned observation enters the state as unconditional, equally weighted evidence, and nothing estimates whether it is consistent with the query or with earlier observations. The trajectory-level outcome reward gives no signal at the step where a bad observation was accepted. So "verify, then re-fetch or discard" is learnable only if it is demonstrated in SFT data ([2505.15966], [2512.17306]) or applied from outside at test time ([2602.12916]). Even then, the read failure cases show the model noticing a bad cue and still following it ([2602.12916], Fig 16; [2505.14362], Appendix D.2).
- **Why it matters:** Deeper multi-turn visual reasoning is only useful if more turns do not compound errors. Today, extra crops can make answers worse through drift and oscillation, and the main remedy (sampling and voting) multiplies inference cost. That is a real barrier under a 2–4×24 GB budget and for video, where each wrong frame fetch costs many tokens. A within-trajectory way to reject or reweight a fetched observation would let models revise their reasoning on a single pass, rather than relying on more samples.
- **Evidence route:** Inject a controlled wrong crop or frame into an otherwise correct trajectory mid-trace (perturbation probes), then measure the recovery rate (the final answer is still correct) and the re-fetch rate for trained policies versus DeepEyes, DRIM and Pixel Reasoner. Compare single-pass accuracy against RTWI-style multi-trace voting at matched token budgets. Also report accuracy as a function of the number of operations per trajectory, where a closed gap should show accuracy no longer falling at high turn counts.


## Axis: Video generation models as visual reasoners

### What has been done
- **Zero-shot prompting and mechanism analysis.** A frozen generator is given the puzzle as the first frame plus an instruction, and the clip it generates is treated as the solution trace ("chain-of-frames"). Veo 3 solves 5x5 mazes at pass@10 78%, but its predecessor makes illegal moves and it scores below chance on rotate and reflect analogies [2509.20328]. Probing intermediate x0-hat predictions shows that reasoning unfolds along the denoising steps, not across frames. Early steps hold several candidate solutions at once, and they are pruned globally across all frames within a step ("chain-of-steps"). Noising one frame barely hurts, because bidirectional attention repairs it; noising one step collapses the score [2603.16870]. The route is decided within about 5 of 40 denoising steps and is fixed by the seed. Re-noising barely changes it. Success collapses past about 12 path steps [2603.30043].
- **Inference-time search and scaling.** Seeds are screened from early x0-hat predictions with a verifier, and generations are chained for long mazes (EPBS/ChEaP) [2603.30043]. Self-consistency votes over structured outputs extracted from many rollouts [2609.36826]. Temporal Backtracking Search fine-tunes the model to condition on a variable-length clean prefix. It then restarts generation from the verified prefix in front of the first failing frame, which a domain verifier locates [2606.13861]. In a VLM-in-the-loop setup, a VLM plans one step, a short clip is generated from the last accepted frame, and the VLM verifies it [2605.08735]. Generating more frames per plan step also helps, up to the positional-embedding length limit [2601.21037].
- **Training the generator.** SFT on rendered solution videos across 11 task families, plus learnable visual or textual "reasoning tokens" that act as a global latent scratchpad [2607.08763]. Rejection fine-tuning on consensus rollouts [2609.36826]. RL with verifiable rewards via SDE-GRPO over denoising steps, using dense rule-decomposed scalar rewards, a KL penalty, and gradients only on early denoising steps [2605.15458]. Similar GRPO with tracked-trajectory rewards (exact match, prefix precision, a maze-fidelity penalty), which shows that VLM reward models get hacked [2603.27866]. Generic flow-GRPO that assigns credit across denoising timesteps by trajectory branching (image preference alignment) [2508.04324].
- **Architecture.** A coarse-to-fine latent hierarchy keeps coarse "plan" levels at high residual noise so they stay revisable, while finer levels stream out autoregressively. Training uses a pure flow-matching loss [2607.15278].
- **Process-level evaluation.** The process-outcome consistency metric shows correct final frames reached through invalid intermediate frames. The outcome-hacking rate is 36% for Veo 3.1 and 46% for Sora 2 [2512.24952].

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** RL post-training of video generators for rule-verifiable visual reasoning (Maze, FlowFree, Sokoban) computes rewards that already encode where a rollout goes wrong: prefix precision, the fraction of valid transitions, wall violations. These methods then collapse the rewards into one group-relative advantage per video. That advantage is broadcast uniformly over every frame latent and every denoising step. So a rollout that is valid for 90% of its transitions and breaks at one frame is pushed down everywhere. The assumption being challenged is that one scalar per video is an adequate credit signal when the policy is a joint spatiotemporal denoiser.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2605.15458] SDE-GRPO builds its MDP "over denoising steps rather than reasoning steps". One advantage A_i multiplies a log-ratio averaged over all D latent elements. Its Sokoban reward already counts valid transitions, but it enters as a scalar. [2603.27866] uses the same GRPO pattern. Its prefix-precision reward R_PR is computed per step from the tracked trajectory and then summed into one scalar. [2508.04324] does localize credit, but only along the denoising-timestep axis, by branching one SDE step. The reward is still one terminal score per sample, with no frame or region attribution. [2606.13861] shows the first failure frame can be localized and reused (72.7% of failed 2D-maze rollouts fail in the first 20% of the trajectory). It exploits this only at inference with an external verifier, and states that it cannot change what the generator locally produces. [2609.36826] distills whole-path consensus, which is all-or-nothing per rollout and breaks down on 8x8 mazes. Query trail: "frame-level credit assignment video diffusion reinforcement learning", "temporal dense reward flow-GRPO video", "step-level reward video generation chain-of-frames", and "video diffusion GRPO per-frame advantage" (since 2025-06/10) returned no video-reasoning method with per-frame or per-region advantages.
- **Methodological deficiency:** This is a credit-assignment flaw. Group-relative advantages in these methods are per rollout, and the policy log-ratio is averaged over all spatiotemporal latent elements [2605.15458, Eq 2-3]. The gradient therefore cannot tell the valid prefix from the faulty transition, even though the verifier exposes that boundary [2606.13861, Eq 2]. It is not a simple fix. Reasoning is resolved jointly across all frames within each denoising step [2603.16870], so the per-step "action" is a whole-clip velocity, and frame-local credit needs a way to attribute that joint action to temporal regions. The symptom is near-zero success on the harder domains even with dense rewards: FlowFree 7.9% and Sokoban 6.1%, from an SFT start that already reaches ~42 F1 on FlowFree, so most rollouts are partly correct [2605.15458, Table 1]. Sparse-reward training stays flat at ~2.5% on FlowFree [2605.15458, Table 5].
- **Why it matters:** Groups working on long-horizon and low-success-rate visual reasoning tasks are blocked. In this regime almost every rollout is partly right, so a per-video scalar wastes most of the information the verifier produces, and needs group size 16 or more to estimate even that scalar [2605.15458, Section 6.1]. Closing the gap would show whether video generators can learn multi-step rule following from their own partial successes. That would put training-time improvement on the same footing as the temporal localization that already works at inference [2606.13861].
- **Evidence route:** At matched rollout budget and group size, compare success rate against required path length or number of moves on FlowFree, Sokoban and long mazes, relative to scalar-advantage SDE-GRPO. Also compare sample efficiency: the reward curve per GPU-hour from a low-success SFT start. Check whether the position of the first failure frame moves later over training. The comparison is meaningful only if the improvement holds when each rollout's reward (sum of components) is unchanged, so that gains come from where credit goes and not from a new reward.

- **Gap:** Video-generator reasoners solve puzzles by re-synthesizing the whole scene, every frame, from a joint pixel latent. The task's invariant structure (walls, goal location, object identity, unrelated background) is never represented apart from the state the reasoning is supposed to change. Invariance is imposed only softly: by fidelity reward terms, KL anchors or prompts. Under horizon pressure the cheapest way to "solve" the task is to edit the invariants: the goal teleports, a second agent spawns, walls vanish. The assumption being challenged is that a generative video prior plus outcome-level reward or supervision will keep the invariant structure fixed on its own.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2603.30043] found that past the generation horizon Wan2.2 "prioritizes goal completion over structural fidelity". The goal teleports next to the agent or a second agent spawns. 77.5% of the 8-step HunyuanVideo failures are constraint violations, and chaining does not fix them. [2607.15278] HDR's hierarchy fixes planning, but its residual errors are "late-stage state-consistency drift": walls disappear and liquid colors merge even when the plan is right. [2603.27866] needs an explicit R_MF penalty, because without it the model raises its trajectory score by removing maze walls in later frames. [2605.15458] uses a multiplicative wall term and a KL anchor, and without KL gets partial-verifier hacking. [2512.24952] measures 36-46% outcome hacking and "editing leakage", where background is altered, extra objects appear and task conditions are violated, and finds that only prompting the constraints helps. [2607.08763] notes that the generator "lacks a dedicated channel for maintaining reasoning state". Its reasoning tokens are global, frame-agnostic vectors not tied to task state, and VIPER POC stays at 7.5.
- **Methodological deficiency:** This is a representation limitation (wrong inductive bias). In all these methods the denoiser's output space does not separate what must be preserved from the conditioning frame and what the reasoning trace may change. Every frame regenerates the full canvas, so a rule violation and a valid move are both just pixel changes of similar cost to the model. Rules can then only be penalized after the fact through reward shaping [2603.27866], [2605.15458], or held by a step budget, since step distillation raises violations [2603.30043]. Even an architecture built for revisable plans keeps drifting on invariants [2607.15278, Appendix H.2].
- **Why it matters:** Until valid moves and invariant edits are distinguishable inside the generator, success rates conflate reasoning with cheating. Last-frame accuracy overstates process accuracy by 36-46 points [2512.24952]. Every new task also needs a hand-designed anti-cheating reward term. Closing the gap would make generated clips trustworthy as reasoning traces that check each step, and would let reward and search methods spend their signal on planning rather than on policing the scene.
- **Evidence route:** Measure the constraint-violation rate and the outcome-hacking rate (last-frame accuracy minus process accuracy) as a function of required path length, using VIPER-style process checks and rule verifiers on Maze/Sokoban/Frozen Lake. Hold planning correctness fixed (same plan-level success) and remove task-specific fidelity reward terms. A method closes the gap if violations stay flat as horizon pressure rises, with no per-task anti-cheating penalty.


## Axis: Temporal reasoning over long videos in video LLMs

### What has been done
- **Agentic "thinking with long videos" (glance, then crop/zoom, multi-turn RL).** The policy skims uniform low-rate frames and emits a time window that the environment densely resamples and appends to context: crop_video over a 64-frame skim with answer + temporal-IoU reward [2511.20785]; <video_zoom> from a low-fps glance with answer + correctness-gated tool bonus [2512.22315]; a slow-fast template (768 low-res frames + appended high-res clips) with a zoom step optimized separately against ground-truth timespans [2509.24786]; FrameAt/VideoClip tools with answer + tool + turn bonuses and only the latest turn's frames kept in view [2509.24008]; localize-crop-answer with a penalty-aware IoU reward against one annotated interval [2602.07801].
- **Retrieval-index agents with evidence rewards.** Natural-language search over a ViCLIP clip index, rewarded by the fraction of temporal-logic primitives detected anywhere in the retrieved frames, with temporal order explicitly ignored [2607.02959].
- **Single-pass RL that varies the temporal view only during training.** Rollouts on uniformly subsampled views at random frame ratios with a gated credit for low-ratio successes [2610.26022]; dense-to-sparse paired views with single-segment tIoU and a reliability-gated cross-view reward [2610.10893].
- **Counterfactual / dependency-oriented post-training on fixed inputs.** Behavior packs over key-mask, span-drop, reverse and shuffle views built from annotated key segments, with stability/sensitivity/abstention contracts [2610.03141]; teacher-supplied evidence patches for failed rollouts, still scored by answer correctness [2604.16243]; event-structure proxy tasks (segmentation, progression, reordering of pre-cut neighboring clips) with deterministic rewards [2610.16172].

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** RL-trained long-video agents learn where to look via rewards on the final answer (with correctness-gated tool bonuses) or on overlap between the proposed window and an annotated interval, but neither signal measures whether the answer actually depended on the frames the agent retrieved, so in long-video QA the search policy is credited for zooms whose content the answer ignores, and it gets no process signal at all on questions without timestamp annotations.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2509.24786] — states the problem outright: an outcome reward still reinforces a wrong zoom with a correct answer, which is common because the low-res global view alone scores high, so the model learns to discard the zoomed clips; its fix is a binary IoU>0 reward on one zoom step, available only for CG-Bench items with ground-truth spans. [2512.22315] — reward given once at trajectory end; the tool bonus is gated only on answer correctness, so every zoom in a correct trajectory gets the same credit; without the bonus, tool use collapses to zero. [2509.24008] — same shape (accuracy + correctness-gated tool and turn bonuses, trajectory-level advantage), and it presents needing only video–question–answer triples as a feature. [2511.20785] and [2602.07801] — add temporal IoU against one annotated interval; plain IoU/Recall is hacked by inflating spans or localizing blindly, and in [2511.20785] the RL-only model calls the tool and then ignores the returned frames. [2607.02959] — rewards the presence of question primitives in the retrieved frames (ViCLIP detector), which checks that evidence was seen, not that it was used. [2610.03141] — the closest dependence signal (the answer must change or abstain when key evidence is masked), but it is built from annotated key segments on a fixed 8-frame input, not from the agent's own retrieved observations. [2604.16243] — the teacher picks the evidence patch and the reward is still answer correctness. The same deficiency recurs across six agentic or retrieval methods read. A recency search for counterfactual/evidence-dependence rewards in video RL (since 2025-10) surfaced only [2610.03141] and [2604.16243].
- **Methodological deficiency:** Credit-assignment flaw. The reward for a retrieval action is a function of (answer, ground-truth answer) or (window, annotated window), never of how the agent's answer changes when its own retrieved observation is removed or swapped. A policy whose answer comes from the coarse skim or from language priors therefore gets the same advantage as one whose answer comes from the zoomed evidence. [2509.24786] documents exactly this shortcut (Sec 4.3, Table 2c), and its only remedy depends on span annotations.
- **Why it matters:** This credit flaw is the long-video version of the problem's core failure: a confident trace whose decisive frame was never really used. Until search actions are credited for actual evidential use, more tool calls and higher IoU do not show that the model reasons from what it retrieved. Without such a signal, RL on the many long-video QA sets that lack timestamps cannot teach the search policy anything beyond answer imitation.
- **Evidence route:** For trained agents, measure how often a correct answer flips or loses confidence when the retrieved clips are replaced with random same-length clips or blanked, compared with the skim-only answer. A method that closes the gap should show much higher evidence-dependence on correct answers than [2512.22315]/[2511.20785]-style policies, while matching or beating their accuracy. It should also learn useful search on QA data without timestamp annotations (e.g., MovieChat-style general questions), as measured by the IoU of its zooms on held-out annotated sets such as CG-Bench or VideoSIAH-Eval.

- **Gap:** Evidence-localization objectives for long-video reasoning treat the evidence as one contiguous interval (temporal IoU or boundary-penalized IoU) or as an unordered set of detected primitives. So for questions whose answer depends on the temporal relation between several separate moments (order, which occurrence of a repeated action, before/after), the policy gets no signal about whether the moments it localized stand in the relation the question asks about.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2511.20785], [2602.07801], [2610.10893] — reward IoU against a single ground-truth interval [t_s,t_e]. [2610.10893] reports that under sparse frames the model "may align to the wrong occurrence" of visually similar repeated actions, a failure that IoU against one segment does not penalize differently from any other miss. [2509.24786] — binary IoU>0 against one ground-truth span, for one zoom step. [2607.02959] — compiles questions into temporal-logic specifications but explicitly drops the temporal operators in the reward ("we ignore the order in which evidence is retrieved"), so before/after structure is never checked, and its limitations call for capturing "more complex causal and temporal dependencies". [2610.16172] — the one method that rewards multi-event relations (next-event, fill-in, clip reordering), but only over short windows of neighboring clips that the data pipeline pre-segments and hands to the model, with no localization; it still fails on repeated similar actions and does not solve long-range reasoning. [2610.03141] — reverse/shuffle views test order sensitivity on a fixed short clip, not on moments the model localizes. Query trail: "multi-event temporal grounding reward video reasoning" and "temporal order multiple moments long video reasoning reinforcement" (since 2025-10) returned no agentic long-video method with a relational evidence reward.
- **Methodological deficiency:** Representation limitation in the training objective. Supervision represents the evidence for a question as a single span or a bag of primitives, so the reward function cannot express a relation among several localized moments. Relational rewards exist only for pre-cut, short-range clip sets [2610.16172], so they never shape what the agent localizes in a long video. This matches the wrong-occurrence failures reported by [2610.10893] and [2610.16172].
- **Why it matters:** Temporal and causal questions over long videos ("what did X do after Y", "the second time Z happens") are exactly where sparse sampling and single-window zooming break down. A model trained only to hit one window can answer them only by luck or from language priors. Closing this gap would show whether video LLMs can localize several pieces of evidence and reason about how they relate in time, rather than merely find one relevant segment.
- **Evidence route:** On long-video questions with two or more annotated evidence moments and an order or occurrence dependency (e.g., ordering and counting subsets of CG-Bench, VideoSIAH-Eval or LVBench), compare against single-interval-IoU agents [2602.07801]/[2511.20785]. Measure (a) accuracy and (b) the rate at which the localized moments are correct individually but in the wrong relation or occurrence. A method that closes the gap should cut (b) without losing single-evidence accuracy.


## Axis: Efficient and adaptive test-time reasoning for multimodal models

### What has been done
- **Text-length controllers driven by outcome statistics (RL).** Group pass rate sets the length preference: CARE scales a sigmoid length reward by alpha(1-P_g) and an EMA competence stage that moves from rewarding long traces to penalizing them [2606.19927]. ARES buckets prompts by online pass@8 and shapes the count of high window-entropy tokens, counted only over a GPT-filtered vocabulary of textual reasoning triggers ("but", "therefore"), down on easy prompts and up on hard ones [2510.08457]. L2T uses a pass-rate-conditioned pairwise advantage that favors shorter correct traces when p_G is high and longer ones when p_G is low [2610.15810]. AdaThinkV forces paired THINK/ANSWER rollouts and labels the mode by accuracy gain minus a length cost (ThinkGain) [2608.01980]. SAIL-RL rewards a binary think/no-think decision against teacher complexity labels [2511.02280]. In all of these the visual input is fixed: CARE trains at 16 frames and a global resolution [2606.19927], AdaThinkV uses a fixed 2 FPS / 4096-visual-token pipeline [2608.01980], and the only compute dial is text.
- **Perception-aware routing.** GPRO adds a per-token controller (hidden state, next-token entropy, global image feature) that chooses among a fast FFN, a "slow perception" cross-attention over the already-encoded visual features, and a slow reasoning module. It is trained with PPO plus a calibration reward, using GPT-4 failure attribution (perception vs reasoning) over about 790K mined errors [2601.04442]. L2T routes between generated VQ visual tokens (tool outputs distilled into the vocabulary) and text using routing labels fixed per sample by task category and keyword rules [2610.15810].
- **Grounding-aware chain compression.** Both methods contrast the policy with and without the image. VIG gives a GRPO reward equal to the mean per-token entropy drop due to the image [2608.21883]. MCPO prunes steps whose with-image minus no-image log-likelihood (NCMI) falls below an adaptive threshold, then runs SFT and preference optimization on the pruned chains [2609.04947].
- **Adaptive visual budget without reasoning adaptivity.** EcoFrame expands the frame budget while the entropy of a direct answer stays high, proposing frames near high-attention frames, and by design does no reasoning [2608.03918]. ResAdapt's Allocator picks per-frame resolution once, before encoding, as a contextual bandit trained with a cost-aware advantage [2603.28610].

### Open gaps on this axis
- **Gap:** Adaptive-reasoning methods for MLLMs and video LLMs give more text-reasoning budget to instances with a low rollout pass rate or high reasoning-token entropy, under a fixed visual input (CARE, ARES, L2T, AdaThinkV, SAIL-RL). Visual-budget methods adapt frames or resolution either open-loop before reasoning (ResAdapt) or from direct-answer entropy with no reasoning at all (EcoFrame). No allocator decides, from the model's own reasoning state, whether a failing instance is short of evidence or short of deliberation. So on perception-limited chart, high-resolution and long-video questions, the extra compute goes into longer text over the same under-sampled visual tokens. The method assumption being challenged is that a failure-source-agnostic scalar (pass rate, token entropy, accuracy gain of thinking) is an adequate signal for allocating test-time compute in multimodal reasoning.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2601.04442] GPRO is the closest. It does separate perception from reasoning compute, but the separation is imported from offline GPT-4 failure labels, its controller's online signal is next-token entropy, and its "slow perception" path is cross-attention over the same encoded visual features, so it cannot fetch a crop, higher resolution or a new frame. It is also image-only. [2610.15810] L2T jointly routes visual and text tokens, but routing labels come from task category and keywords, and depth comes from pass rate. Its own failure analysis finds that perception failures have CoT lengths similar to correct answers and are not fixed by longer reasoning, and it concludes that pass rate alone "is insufficient for adaptive CoT allocation" (Appendix A.4.4). [2606.19927] CARE and [2510.08457] ARES push hard (low-pass-rate) prompts toward longer or more exploratory text with no failure-source term. [2608.01980] AdaThinkV notes that "high confidence can coexist with missed temporal evidence", but ThinkGain only compares think against no-think on the same frames, so an evidence-starved prompt is routed to ANSWER or gets more text rollouts via VRPO. [2511.02280] SAIL-RL's binary judgment sends perception-heavy questions to direct answering, with no action that adds perceptual compute. [2603.28610] ResAdapt states that its open-loop allocation "ignores reasoning state" and "cannot revise a mistaken low-resolution choice once partial reasoning or uncertainty signals emerge" (Limitation iv). [2608.03918] EcoFrame always adds frames on high entropy, even when more frames cannot help, and avoids reasoning by design. Query trail: "adaptive perception budget multimodal reasoning", "adaptive frame selection reasoning budget long video", "difficulty-aware reasoning length multimodal" and "hybrid thinking mode multimodal" (since 2025-10) surfaced no method that couples evidence acquisition to the reasoning state through a failure-source-aware signal.
- **Methodological deficiency:** This is a credit-assignment flaw in the allocation signal. Every read controller assigns budget from a scalar outcome or uncertainty statistic: group pass rate [2606.19927, Eq 7], [2510.08457, App. G], [2610.15810, Eq 17-21]; mode accuracy gain [2608.01980, Eq 1]; answer entropy [2608.03918, Eq 1]. None of these statistics records whether the failure is caused by missing visual evidence or by insufficient inference. The policies then express that budget through a single channel: text tokens, or a visual budget committed before or without reasoning. The only failure-source-aware method gets the attribution from an external teacher, not from the model, and can only re-attend to the same tokens [2601.04442, Sections 3.1.2 and 3.3]. L2T's limitation that reasoning cannot recover once incorrect visual evidence is encoded [2610.15810, App. A.8] is the expected symptom: the budget lands on the wrong channel.
- **Why it matters:** The problem's concrete failure is this case: an RL-tuned 7B model writes a longer trace on a chart or long-video question without re-inspecting the decisive detail. Current adaptive methods make that trace shorter on easy questions, but they still lengthen it on exactly the perception-limited hard ones. This blocks efficient long-video and high-resolution reasoning on small models, where text tokens are cheap but the missing frame or crop is what decides the answer. Closing the gap would show whether a single policy can learn when to look again and when to think longer from its own rollouts, without teacher attribution.
- **Evidence route:** Split evaluation items by failure source with a model-independent probe: the answer becomes correct when the ground-truth evidence frame or crop is supplied (evidence-limited), or stays wrong (reasoning-limited). Use V*/HR-Bench, ChartQA-style charts and Video-MME/LongVideoBench. On each split, report accuracy and total compute (visual tokens plus generated tokens) against CARE, ARES, AdaThinkV, L2T, GPRO, EcoFrame and ResAdapt. A closed gap shows fewer text tokens and more evidence acquisition on the evidence-limited split, better accuracy per unit of compute there, and no loss on the reasoning-limited split.

- **Gap:** Grounding-aware chain-of-thought compression for multimodal reasoning models (VIG, MCPO) scores each reasoning token or step by how much the full image changes the policy's own prediction (with-image versus no-image contrast) and keeps or rewards high-dependence content. Image dependence is a different quantity from the correctness of the visual claim and from the step's necessity for the answer. So in math and chart reasoning, compression keeps confidently hallucinated value readouts and removes visually unconditioned but load-bearing derivation steps.
- **Near-miss methods and coverage:** [2608.21883] VIG is the closest. Its limitations state that VIG "measures visual dependence, which is neither visual correctness nor counterfactual necessity", that "a confidently hallucinated reading can score as high as genuinely grounded perception", and that accuracy counteracts this only at the trajectory level. Its deletion probe on VIG-trained chains finds that removing the lowest-VIG sentences (algebra and derivation) hurts most (Appendix F.2). [2609.04947] MCPO uses the same contrast at step level (NCMI) and replaces low-NCMI steps with [SKIP], describing them as "redundant or potentially hallucinatory". Those are exactly the visually unconditioned derivation steps that VIG's probe shows are load-bearing, and MCPO does not test whether the pruned steps were necessary. [2511.02280] SAIL-RL does check claims against the image, through an LLM judge, but as one binary trajectory-level score, not as per-step credit usable for compression. The repeated deficiency across [2608.21883] and [2609.04947] is that the importance score is a likelihood or entropy contrast of the policy with itself, with no term for whether the visual claim is true or whether the answer depends on the step.
- **Methodological deficiency:** This is a credit-assignment flaw (wrong proxy for token value). Both methods assign value to a reasoning unit with the policy's own conditional mutual information against the whole image [2608.21883, Eq 2; 2609.04947, Eq 2]. That quantity is high for any image-conditioned token, true or hallucinated, and near zero for computation that operates on values already read. It therefore inverts the importance of two content types that compression must treat differently, as VIG's own deletion asymmetry shows [2608.21883, App. F.2]. The contrast is also whole-image, with no region or frame resolution, so it cannot tell which visual evidence a step relies on.
- **Why it matters:** Efficient multimodal reasoning needs shorter traces that keep both the correct visual readouts and the computation on them. The problem's failure mode, a trace that cites a value not in the image, is not penalized and can even be favored by dependence-based compression. This blocks anyone compressing long multimodal CoT on charts, documents or video for deployment on small models. Closing the gap would separate "grounded" from "image-conditioned" as training signals for reasoning efficiency.
- **Evidence route:** On chart and table benchmarks with ground-truth underlying values (e.g. ChartQA-style data with source tables), measure the rate of hallucinated values kept in compressed chains, and run VIG's sentence-deletion probe (lowest- versus highest-score deletion) on chains from the new method and from VIG and MCPO. A closed gap shows fewer retained wrong readouts, deletion of low-score units that is harmless, and an accuracy-length frontier at or beyond VIG and MCPO on MathVista/MathVerse-style benchmarks.
