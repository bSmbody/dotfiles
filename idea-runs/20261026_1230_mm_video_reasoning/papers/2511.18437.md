# Perceptual-Evidence Anchored Reinforced Learning for Multimodal Reasoning

## What this paper does
PEARL derives, for each training question, a checklist of short perception sub-questions with verifiable answers. Each step it runs separate perception rollouts on the checklist (answers checked by rule), and uses their mean accuracy both as a dual-objective perception reward and as a fidelity gate: if the mean is zero the reasoning rollouts for that question are skipped; otherwise the reasoning advantage is scaled by min(mean, 0.5). It shows that GRPO lowers reasoning errors on WeMath while perception errors stay almost unchanged.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it reward perception, and at what granularity does the perception signal enter the reasoning update?
Perception rollouts answer the serialized probes without reasoning; per-probe rule-based rewards are averaged per rollout, then over G rollouts to R_bar_p [full text, Section 3.3]. If R_bar_p = 0 the reasoning update is skipped; else A_hat_r <- A_hat_r * min(R_bar_p, 0.5) (Section 3.4 / Eq 3); total objective J_GRPO(A_r) + lambda J_GRPO(A_p). The perception score is per question instance and comes from different rollouts than the reasoning trace, so it scales all reasoning trajectories for that question equally and does not inspect the visual facts each reasoning trajectory actually states.

### Goal: What failure of outcome-only RLVR does it document?
"the frequency of perception-related errors remains almost unchanged ... the model learns to generate logically plausible steps based on fundamentally flawed visual understanding, simply because the final outcome is correct" [full text, Section 1, Fig 2].

### Goal: What limitations does it report?
Only a limited number of distinct probes can be extracted from one source question, and probes cannot guarantee complete understanding of the visual context [full text, Section 7].
