# MCPO: Modality-Contrastive Preference Optimization for Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Compression

## What this paper does
MCPO compresses multimodal long chains-of-thought in two stages with under 900 training samples. Stage one segments a chain into steps and scores each step with Normalized Cross-Modal Mutual Information (with-image minus no-image length-normalized log-likelihood, where no-image zeroes both input visual embeddings and DeepStack features); steps below an adaptive CV-based threshold are replaced with [SKIP]. Stage two runs SFT on pruned chains, then a preference loss contrasting pruned (chosen) and original (rejected) chains with an extra no-image contrastive term.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What does the compression criterion treat as a redundant step, and is that a measure of correct grounding or necessity?
NCMI(S_i) = log P(S_i | I, T, S<i) - log P(S_i | I_empty, T, S<i), length-normalized [full text, Section 3.2 / Eq 2-4]. A step with NCMI about 0 is characterized as "redundant or potentially hallucinatory ... reliant primarily on language priors that should be pruned"; steps below tau = mu + beta*sigma are replaced by [SKIP] [Section 3.2 / Eq 5]. The criterion is image dependence only; a text-only derivation step (algebra on values already read) has NCMI near 0 by construction and is a pruning target, and a step that confidently states a wrong visual value would have high NCMI and be kept. The paper does not test whether pruned steps were load-bearing.

### Goal: Does it adapt per instance, and does it act on visual evidence?
Adaptivity is only in the per-chain pruning threshold via the coefficient of variation of step NCMI [Section 3.2]. The visual input is fixed; the method is offline compression of teacher chains followed by preference alignment [Section 3.3].
