# Wan-R1: Verifiable-Reinforcement Learning for Video Reasoning

## What this paper does
Wan-R1 adapts Flow-GRPO to Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (LoRA, after SFT) for maze solving (2D/3D/trap mazes) and robotic mapless path planning (Target-Bench). It studies reward design: VLM reward models (Qwen2.5-VL) are hacked; a verifiable multi-component reward built from a tracked agent trajectory (exact match, prefix precision, maze-fidelity) gives stable gains, e.g. +29.1% exact match on complex 3D mazes over SFT.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to make a video generator reason, and how is credit assigned?
GRPO over an SDE-converted flow-matching sampler, with denoising reduction (30 train / 50 eval steps) [full text, Section 3.3]. The reward is computed on the full video: the agent is tracked across frames, then R = 0.3 R_EM + 0.5 R_PR + 0.2 R_MF, where R_PR = mean over steps of the indicator that all steps up to j are correct (a prefix-correctness signal) and R_MF penalizes background (wall) pixels changing relative to frame 0 [full text, Section 3.4, Eq 8-11]. The combined scalar is used as a group-relative advantage for the whole rollout [full text, Section 3.3].
### Goal: What limitation does it report on multi-step / long-horizon reasoning?
RL from the base model without SFT gives near-zero exact match (cold start) [full text, Section 6.2]. Sparse rewards (SR-only or EM-only) give limited improvement; SR-only learns suboptimal routes [full text, Section 6.4]. Without R_MF the model "achieves high trajectory scores by altering the maze structure in later frames (e.g., removing walls)", a visual cheating mode [full text, Section 6.4]. VLM reward models reward "plausible-looking but incorrect paths" [full text, Section 6.4]. Limitations: needs strictly definable success criteria; fixed reward weights; suggests adaptive weighting [full text, Section 8].
