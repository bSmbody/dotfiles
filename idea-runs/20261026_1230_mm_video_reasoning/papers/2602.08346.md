# What, Whether and How? Unveiling Process Reward Models for Thinking with Images Reasoning

## What this paper does
The paper manually analyzes 7,558 trajectories from four thinking-with-images models on four benchmarks, defines seven error types, shows that prompting LVLMs as process reward models (PRMs) for guided search gives 1–8% gains, and releases ThinkWithImages-PRMBench (1,206 step-annotated trajectories) on which existing LVLMs are evaluated as step judges.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: Which error types occur at the visual-operation steps of thinking-with-images trajectories?
Seven types [full text, "What Issues Persist" section]: Object Detection Failure, Attribute Recognition Error (region found, attribute wrong), Relationship Understanding Bias, Logical Understanding Error, Answer-Reasoning Inconsistency (final answer not supported by preceding reasoning, "hasty conclusion-drawing"), Tool Invocation Error (inadequate visual processing), and Redundant Reasoning (answer fixed at an early step, later steps superfluous). Analysis is limited to crop/zoom models [Limitations].

### Goal: Can current models judge whether an intermediate image-extraction step is correct, i.e. supply step-level credit?
Poorly. Most LVLMs score below chance (50%) on incorrect samples, with strong positive bias (Gemini-2.5-Flash 78.89% on correct vs 38.81% on incorrect) (Table 3) [Detailed Analysis]. Accuracy fluctuates sharply across steps, and "most models achiev[e] higher accuracy during text reasoning while experiencing degradation during image extraction" steps [same section]. PixelReasoner-7B gains little from PRM-guided search [Performance Analysis]. So a generic LVLM judge is not a reliable source of per-visual-step reward.
