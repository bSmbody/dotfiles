# Perception-R1: Advancing Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities of MLLMs via Visual Perception Reward

## What this paper does
Perception-R1 shows that accuracy-only RLVR leaves multimodal perception essentially unchanged (most failures of RLVR-trained Qwen2/2.5-VL-7B on MathVista/MathVerse are perception errors; McNemar tests find no significant perception change versus the base model). It adds a visual perception reward: atomic visual annotations are extracted from a proprietary MLLM's CoT on the training set, and a text-only judging LLM checks which annotations the policy's response reflects; the fraction is added to the GRPO reward with accuracy, format and repetition terms. Trained on Geometry3K.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it reward perception, and at what granularity does the perception signal enter the policy update?
r_v = (number of reference annotations judged present in the response) / (number of annotations) [full text, Section 4.2.2 / Eq 4]; total reward alpha r_f + beta r_a + gamma r_v + r_p (Eq 5), then standard GRPO. The perception signal is a trajectory-level scalar; it is recall over a fixed reference list, so a response is not penalized for additional visual claims that are false but absent from the list. Annotations come from a SOTA proprietary MLLM's CoT, extracted by a text LLM (Section 4.2.1); about 4% of samples had minor errors (Appendix B.2).

### Goal: What does it report about the reliability of the perception reward?
With weaker judging LLMs the perception reward rises quickly and saturates early, and the 7B judge yields a model below the base on MathVerse and MathVision, which the authors attribute to reward hacking [full text, Section 5.4, Fig 3(b,c)]. Using Qwen2.5-VL-32B as an image-seeing reward model also causes rapid saturation and lower accuracy reward, i.e. reward hacking [Appendix B.6, Fig 5]. Adding r_v alone also increased repetition, requiring an n-gram penalty (Section 4.2.2).

### Goal: What does it say about why accuracy-only RLVR fails to improve perception?
It attributes this to reward sparsity: "answer correctness does not guarantee accurate multimodal perception" [full text, Section 4.2], with 72-78% of analysed failures caused by perception errors (Section 4.1).
