# Visually-Guided Policy Optimization for Multimodal Reasoning

## What this paper does
VGPO (ACL 2026) observes text-dominated attention, sparse early "glances" at the image and "temporal visual forgetting" (visual attention decays along the reasoning chain; correct samples keep a higher late/early visual ratio, 0.680 vs 0.532). It computes a per-token Visual Focus Score as cosine similarity between the token hidden state and a pooled visual prototype, amplifies late-step scores (Visual Attention Compensation), and multiplies the outcome advantage by intra- and inter-trajectory visual-focus factors.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to make the reasoning trace visually grounded/faithful?
rho_{i,t} = (cos(h_{i,t}, mu_v)+1)/2 [full text, Sec 3.1 / Eq 2-4]; compensated w = rho*(1 + G*beta*t/T) with a tail gate [Eq 5-6]; advantage A^V = A*(1+psi_{i,t})*(1+phi_i) [Eq 12]. The score correlates r~0.67 with true attention mass to image tokens [App C].
### Goal: Does its signal test whether the final answer causally depends on the trace's visual claims, or only claim truth / image sensitivity / attention?
Only visual engagement (an attention proxy). It does not check claim correctness or answer dependence. Its own failure analysis says VGPO "can actually reinforce" a misperception (blurry 6 read as 8) by making the model attend strongly to a flawed embedding, and that some incorrect samples have high focus scores: looking closely is "necessary but not sufficient" [App E].
### Goal: What counterfactual/intervention operator does it use, and what limitation does it report?
No intervention. Limitations: the linear late-step amplification is a heuristic that may be wrong when late steps are pure calculation, and the upper bound is set by the visual encoder [Limitations].
