# More Thinking, Less Seeing? Assessing Amplified Hallucination in Multimodal Reasoning Models

## What this paper does
A diagnostic study (not a training method) of Qwen2.5-VL-based multimodal reasoning models. It finds that reasoning models hallucinate more than their non-reasoning bases on perception tasks, that they allocate less attention to visual tokens (more to instruction tokens), and that lengthening the chain further lowers visual attention. Reasoning length has a non-monotonic, task-dependent effect. It proposes the RH-AUC metric and RH-Bench.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it identify for hallucination growing with longer reasoning in RL-trained MLLMs?
Reasoning models assign consistently lower attention to visual tokens across layers, falling further in deeper layers, with attention shifted to instruction tokens [full text, Section 3.1, Fig 5]; under "overthinking" induced by latent-state steering, attention to visual tokens drops further [Section 3.2, Fig 6]. Perception benchmarks peak at shorter lengths than math benchmarks [Section 4.2].

### Goal: Does it propose a training-time fix?
No. It controls length at inference (budget forcing, "Wait" extension, latent steering) and evaluates; conclusions about training data are observational, without controlled retraining [full text, Sections 4.1, 7 Limitation].
