# Beyond the Last Frame: Process-aware Evaluation for Generative Video Reasoning

## What this paper does
VIPER is a 16-task, 6-domain benchmark (309 items) for generative video reasoning with a Process-outcome Consistency metric POC@r: a VLM judge checks that some sampled frame meets the goal (OC) and every sampled frame meets process constraints (PC). It documents "outcome-hacking": correct final frames reached through invalid intermediate frames.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How often do video generators reach correct outcomes via invalid processes, and what failure modes appear?
On a 50-item diagnostic set, Veo 3.1 Acc_last 66% vs Acc_process 30% (hacking 36%); Sora 2 70% vs 24% (46%) [full text, Section 2.2, Table 1]. State-of-the-art models reach ~20% POC@1.0 [full text, Section 1]. Denser frame sampling lowers PC (Veo 3.1 PC 33.7 -> 16.5 from r=0.5 to 2.0), i.e. violations are spread over intermediate frames [full text, Section 5.3, Table 4]. Failure patterns: constraint violation (e.g. breaking maze structure), stop failure (keeps moving after completion), editing leakage (background altered, extra objects, task conditions violated), garbled text [full text, Section 5.4]. Explicitly prompting the constraints improves Veo 3.1 [full text, Section 5.4, Table 5]. Pass@k helps (20.3 -> 43.2 at k=8) but saturates [full text, Section 5.3].
