# FrameGRPO: Incentivizing Video Reasoning via Multi-Frame Rollouts and Temporal Credit

## What this paper does
FrameGRPO targets advantage collapse in video GRPO: rollouts in a group share the same frame sampling ratio, so outcomes correlate and groups become all-correct or all-wrong. It decodes the video once at f* frames, reserves some rollout slots for the full view, and gives the others uniformly subsampled views at random ratios ρ. Gated Temporal Efficiency Credit adds a bonus α·log(1/ρ) to correct low-ratio rollouts only when all full-ratio rollouts fail. The policy update is always conditioned on the full-ratio input.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to locate or re-inspect temporal evidence in long videos?
None at inference: a single pass over uniformly sampled frames. Temporal variation is only a training-time exploration variable: uniform subsampling of the same interval at different ratios, preserving order [full text, Sec 3.2].
### Goal: How does its RL reward assign credit to the evidence the model uses (outcome-only, IoU vs annotation, other)?
Verifier reward on the answer; credit is redistributed at the level of whole views (which sampling ratio succeeded) via the gated TEC bonus b_i = α·g·c_i·log(1/ρ_i) [full text, Sec 3.3, Eq 3–4]. Credit attaches to frame density, not to which frames or events contain the evidence.
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report on long-video temporal reasoning?
Benefit depends on how strongly a question responds to frame density; appearance-centric questions gain little; "aggressive subsampling can omit brief but decisive events when precise temporal localization is required" [full text, Sec 5].
