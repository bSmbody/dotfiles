# Hierarchical Denoising For Multi-Step Visual Reasoning

## What this paper does
HDR targets the trade-off between bidirectional video diffusion (global revision, ~38 s per streaming step) and streaming autoregressive diffusion (fast but irreversible). It organizes video latents into a tree of hierarchy levels; coarse levels are denoised only partially (kept at high residual noise as revisable plan hypotheses), finer levels refine them, generated coarse-to-fine and left-to-right with a sparse hierarchical attention pattern. On a six-task benchmark (maze, Hanoi, one-line drawing, sliding puzzle, Sokoban, water pouring) success rises from 34.22 (CausalForcing) to 60.29.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it let the generator revise multi-step plans, and what is the reasoning state?
AR diffusion "creates an irreversible rollout: once z_i is generated ... later tokens must condition on this committed history and cannot revise it" [full text, Section 3.1, Eq 2]. HDR's revisable state is the coarse-level noisy latent; level-dependent sampling budgets K_1<...<K_L keep coarse levels stochastic [full text, Section 3.2-3.3, Eq 4]. Training is pure layer-wise flow-matching (no reward, no verifier) [full text, Section 3.3].
### Goal: What failure modes remain?
Residual errors are "late-stage state-consistency drift": a maze wall disappears near the end, a Sokoban wall vanishes, one liquid color collapses into another, even when the plan is correct; authors suggest "constraint-aware refinement or lightweight verification during the final generation stage" [full text, Appendix H.2].
