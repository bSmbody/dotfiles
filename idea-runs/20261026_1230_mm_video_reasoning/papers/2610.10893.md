# Less from More: Reinforcing Sparse Video Reasoning from Dense References

## What this paper does
SAVER is a dense-to-sparse RL post-training method: each training example gives the same policy a dense and a sparse frame view; both predict a temporal interval scored by tIoU minus a boundary penalty, and a reliability-gated reference reward pulls the sparse prediction toward the dense one when the dense one grounds better. Trained on only 1,250 Time-R1 temporal grounding samples, it is evaluated at low fps on grounding and six VideoQA benchmarks, matching dense Qwen3.5 baselines with fewer frames.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to locate or re-inspect temporal evidence in long videos?
None at inference: fixed sparse low-fps frames, "no dense video pre-scanning, adaptive frame selection, or test-time optimization" [full text, Method]. Temporal grounding is used as a training proxy for evidence localization.
### Goal: How does its RL reward assign credit to the evidence the model uses (outcome-only, IoU vs annotation, other)?
R_grd = tIoU(τ̂, τ*) − λ_b(|t̂_s − t*_s|/T + |t̂_e − t*_e|/T) against a single ground-truth segment τ*; R_ref = w_d·tIoU(τ̂_s, τ̂_d) gated by w_d = 1[R_grd(dense) > R_grd(sparse)] [full text, Reward Design, Eq 4–6]. Evidence is represented as one contiguous interval.
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report on long-video temporal reasoning?
Two failure modes: when sparse frames miss the key event the model predicts a broad or shifted interval; "when visually similar actions occur multiple times in a video, the model may align to the wrong occurrence" [full text, Limitations and failure cases]. Suggests adaptive online sampling as future work.
