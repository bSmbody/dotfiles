# CORA: Analyzing and bridging thinking-answer gap in Multimodal RLVR via Consistency-Oriented Reasoning Alignment

## What this paper does
Analyzes ~719K GRPO rollouts of four Qwen LVLMs (SAT/CVBench, Math-40K/MathVision/MathVista, PuzzleVQA) labeled by GPT-5 and finds thinking-answer inconsistency is widespread, grows during training, and among inconsistent samples the final answer is more often correct than the thinking-implied answer. CORA trains a ModernBERT NLI consistency reward model (premise = question + thinking, hypothesis = answer) on 90K labels and adds its score as a reward with split (separately normalized) advantages.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to make the reasoning trace visually grounded/faithful?
r_cons = lambda * p_phi(cons | q, t, a) from a frozen text-only ModernBERT CRM [full text, Sec 3.1 / Eq 2-3]; hybrid reward advantage splitting [Sec 3.2].
### Goal: Does its signal test whether the final answer causally depends on the trace's visual claims, or only claim truth / image sensitivity / attention?
Neither image grounding nor causal dependence: the CRM never sees the image and scores textual entailment between thinking and answer. A trace written to entail the answer satisfies it regardless of whether the answer was computed from it or whether its visual statements are true [Sec 3.1]. The analysis finding that answers are more often right than the thinking-implied answers [Sec 2.3] indicates answers are produced partly outside the trace.
### Goal: What counterfactual/intervention operator does it use, and what limitation does it report?
None. Limitations: models <=7B only, image-text only (no video) [Limitations].
