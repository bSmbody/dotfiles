# Thinking in Frames: How Visual Context and Test-Time Scaling Empower Video Reasoning

## What this paper does
Fine-tunes a video generator for visual planning in MazeNavigation (discrete, low visual change) and TangramPuzzle (continuous, high visual change), frames as intermediate reasoning steps. Reports zero-shot OOD generalization, that visual context (icon in first frame) controls better than text, and a "visual test-time scaling" effect from generating more frames.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it scale reasoning compute in a video generator, and what limits it?
Raising frame count 61 -> 121 improves spatial and temporal OOD maze navigation; more frames per maze step (kappa 7-9 vs 5) helps; but 141 frames / kappa=11 (~200 frames) degrades, attributed to positional embeddings failing to extrapolate beyond training length [full text, Section 5.2]. Planning policy is P(V | s_start, g) with constraints c implicit: "the video model outputs continuous high-dimensional dense transitions, requiring the implicit learning of latent rules and causal dynamics" [full text, Section 3.1].
