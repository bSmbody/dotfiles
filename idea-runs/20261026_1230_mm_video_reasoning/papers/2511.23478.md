# Video-R2: Reinforcing Consistent and Grounded Reasoning in Multimodal Language Models

## What this paper does
Video-R2 diagnoses that video reasoning models (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, VideoRFT) produce traces that contradict their final answers (Think-Answer Consistency) and rely weakly on visual content (an LLM-judged Video Attention Score). It trains with SFT on timestamp-aware reasoning, then GRPO with a Temporal Alignment Reward: timestamped claims are extracted from the predicted and a reference reasoning, matched one-to-one under a temporal tolerance and a sentence-embedding similarity threshold, and scored by precision, gated by think-answer consistency.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it reward use of temporal evidence, and what does the reward compare against?
Claims (timestamp, sentence) are extracted by an LLM from predicted and reference reasoning [full text, Section 3 / Eq 3]; T_ij = 1 if |t_pred - t_ref| <= Delta (Eq 4), S_ij = 1 if embedding cosine >= tau (Eq 5); greedy one-to-one matching (Eq 6-7); TAR_prec = matched / n (Eq 8); TAR = g * TAR_prec with g the consistency gate. The comparison target is a reference reasoning that the authors note "may not be fully exhaustive", so a valid predicted claim at a moment the reference does not mention lowers precision. The video itself is never consulted by the reward; the reward is a trajectory-level scalar.

### Goal: Why precision rather than recall, and what does that imply?
"with a sparse reference, recall is an unreliable signal that fails to penalize overgeneration" [full text, Section 3], so they reward only predictions supported by the reference. The reward is defined only on samples with timestamp-annotated reference reasoning (their curated dataset, Section 4.1).
