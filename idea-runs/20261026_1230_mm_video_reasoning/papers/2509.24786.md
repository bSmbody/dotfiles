# LOVE-R1: Advancing Long Video Understanding with an Adaptive Zoom-in Mechanism via Multi-Step Reasoning

## What this paper does
LOVE-R1 gives a Qwen2.5-VL-7B a slow-fast template: a "fast" view of up to 768 low-resolution frames (32 tokens/frame) covering the whole video, plus "slow" high-resolution clips appended after it when the model decides to zoom into [t1,t2]. Three stages: slow-fast template SFT (153K, including temporal grounding data), 38K CoT cold start with decoupled "answer" vs "zoom-in" prefixes, and decoupled RL that optimizes the zoom step separately with ground-truth timespans. It reports +6.2 on LVBench over the base model.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to locate or re-inspect temporal evidence in long videos?
The policy reads the dense low-res fast video, decides whether information is sufficient, and if not selects a span to zoom; slow clips are encoded at high resolution and appended in temporal order after the fast video [full text, Sec 3, Fig 2(c)]. Each step is prefixed "I need to zoom in on the video." or "I get the answer." [Sec 4.2].
### Goal: How does its RL reward assign credit to the evidence the model uses (outcome-only, IoU vs annotation, other)?
The paper states the credit problem directly: with outcome rewards, "when the model zooms in on an error video clip but gets a correct answer, the outcome rewards will still encourage this undesired behavior", which is not rare because the fast video alone already yields high accuracy, so models "may learn to answer the question based solely on the fast video while discarding the slow ones" [full text, Sec 4.3; Table 2c]. Fix: for CG-Bench questions with annotated timespans, a single zoom step is optimized alone with binary reward 1[IoU(pred, gt) > 0]; for general data (MovieChat) the whole multi-step CoT gets a shared answer-accuracy reward [Sec 4.3, Eq 1–2]. Process credit thus exists only where ground-truth spans exist, and only for one step.
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report on long-video temporal reasoning?
No explicit limitations section; the documented failure is the outcome-reward shortcut above (answering from the fast view, ignoring slow clips) [Sec 4.3].
