# Learning via Self-Consistency for Diffusion-Based Video Reasoning

## What this paper does
Treats a video generator as a stochastic solver: sample G rollouts, extract a structured prediction (path, point, mask), aggregate by task-space consensus (modal path group, spatial vote, pixel vote), optionally read out early from x0-hat to halve denoising cost, then distill accepted consensus into the model by rejection fine-tuning (LoRA, flow-matching loss). Visual search 48.4% -> 99.0% with consensus; 4x4 maze single-rollout strict success 72.0% -> 84.0% after RFT.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What learning signal does it use, and at what granularity?
Whole-trajectory agreement: maze paths count as one group only if their full cell sequences are identical; the accepted group's generated latents become flow-matching targets [full text, Sections 3.2, 3.4, Eq 5]. No ground truth or verifier is used [full text, Abstract]. The signal is all-or-nothing per rollout; there is no step- or frame-level credit.
### Goal: What limitation does it report?
"It remains challenging for complex video reasoning, such as 8x8 maze solving, when individual predictions are more diverse" (exact full-path agreement becomes rare); it suggests comparing a consensus-based reward with GRPO [full text, Section 4.5 Limitations].
