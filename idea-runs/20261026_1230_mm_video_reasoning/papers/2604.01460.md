# From Plausible to Grounded: Reinforcing Consistency in Video MLLMs with Structured Rewards

## What this paper does
For video captioning, the paper shows via a top-down audit that Qwen3-VL-8B often gets a root relational claim right while the supporting attribute (about 60%) or object existence (about 80%) is wrong. It trains with REINFORCE using structured rewards: a revision-based scene-graph match between sampled and reference captions, a temporal branch and a hierarchical factual VQA branch in which yes/no questions built from the reference (plus slot-matched negatives from the sampled caption) are answered by the policy itself on the video.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it verify factual/temporal claims, and what does the reward compare against?
Both captions are parsed into anchored objects, attributes, relations and events [full text, Section 3.1]; q_sg is a matched-mass F-score between sampled and reference units; temporal and VQA questions are instantiated from the reference caption (positives) and from anchor-compatible conflicts in the sampled caption (negatives), answered by the detached policy with label a*(q) from the reference [Section 3.2]. Rewards are centered and summed into one sequence-level reward; temporal questions exist "only when the reference caption explicitly expresses temporal structure". Negatives are restricted to matched slots to "avoid penalizing valid but unmentioned video content" (Section 3.2), i.e. claims outside the reference get neither credit nor penalty.
