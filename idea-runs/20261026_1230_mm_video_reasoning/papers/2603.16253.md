# Grounding the Score: Explicit Visual Premise Verification for Reliable VLM Process Reward Models

## What this paper does
EVPV is a test-time calibration layer for vision-language process reward models. The policy is prompted to attach to each step a visual premise (or null); a trained constraint extractor predicts structured visual facts (numeric readings, relations, composition) once per instance; each premise is matched to these constraints, the per-claim support scores are aggregated into one candidate-level reliability by a smoothed geometric mean, and step rewards of visually dependent steps are gated by a sigmoid of that reliability. Used for Best-of-N reranking on VisualProcessBench and six benchmarks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it verify individual visual claims in a reasoning trace against the image, and how is the result used?
Yes at claim level: p_j = m(v_j, C) in [0,1] per checklist claim against extracted constraints C = E_phi(I, q) [full text, Section 3.2 / Eq 3-4]. But p_j is aggregated to one candidate-level r (Eq 5) and the gate alpha(r) multiplies every visually dependent step's base reward (Eq 9-10). It is used for test-time reranking, not as an RL training signal (Sections 3.4-3.5, 6: "a test-time framework").

### Goal: What does it report about how VL-PRMs fail on visual premises?
Black-box VL-PRMs reward hallucinated premises (false positives, e.g. a nonexistent "cylindrical hole") and penalize correct grounded statements (false negatives); visual misinterpretation is a major share of step errors on VisualProcessBench [full text, Section 1, Fig 1A-B].

### Goal: What limitations does it report?
Depends on constraint coverage/accuracy and checklist completeness; "We currently use a candidate-level (global) reliability signal, which may propagate a local visual misread to the whole trajectory; step-local reliability" is left to future work; constraints come from teacher-generated pseudo-gold [full text, Limitations and future work].
