# Spotlight on Token Perception for Multimodal Reinforcement Learning

## What this paper does
VPPO (ICLR 2026) measures each generated token's visual dependency as the KL between the policy's next-token distribution under the true image and under a non-informative perturbed image (random 14x14 patch blackening, p=0.5). Finding that dependency is sparse across tokens and heterogeneous across trajectories, it (i) restricts policy-gradient updates to the top-k% most image-dependent tokens and (ii) rescales each trajectory's GRPO/DAPO advantage by its normalized mean dependency. Evaluated on visual math benchmarks (MathVerse, DynaMath, Geo3k, MathVision, MMK12).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to make the reasoning trace visually grounded/faithful?
Token visual dependency S(s_t, I) = KL(pi(.|s_t, I) || pi(.|s_t, I')) [full text, Def 3.1 / Eq 3]; token-level gradient filtering mask on top-k% S [Eq 4]; trajectory-level advantage shaping alpha(tau) from min-max-normalized mean S [Eq 5]; combined objective [Eq 6].
### Goal: Does its signal test whether the final answer causally depends on the trace's visual claims, or only claim truth / image sensitivity / attention?
Only image sensitivity of the trace tokens. The KL is unsigned and blind to whether the token's claim is true of the image, and the outcome reward still decides the sign of the update; nothing measures whether the answer depends on those tokens [Sec 3.2]. (2609.36572 later shows that under VPPO unsupported claims become more persistent.)
### Goal: What counterfactual/intervention operator does it use, and what limitation does it report?
Random Patch Blackening, chosen among several destructive perturbations in an ablation [App F, Sec on masking strategy, line ~1730]. It notes the dependency calculation is contingent on the perturbation choice and the optimal masking may be task- or domain-dependent [Limitations].
