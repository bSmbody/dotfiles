# Rethinking Latent Visual Reasoning: Grounding Latent Reasoning in Visual Evidence

## What this paper does
Analyzes latent visual reasoning (LVR) MLLMs that reason in continuous latent tokens and finds a "latent evidence-credit gap": under answer-changing image edits (color, presence, shape, left-right) the correct answer changes in 81-86% of pairs but LVR's prediction changes in only 5.7-13.1%. ReaLVR supervises the model's own free-running latent trajectory with a visual contrast (cosine margin to the relevant-ROI prototype vs mismatched-image prototypes) weighted by answer contrast (attention readout under the correct answer minus under the model's own wrong answers). Scales to 235B.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to make the reasoning trace visually grounded/faithful?
g_t = sim(z_t, p+) - max sim(z_t, p-) [full text, Sec 4.2]; selective credit gamma_t = [r_t^+ - r_t^-]_+ from answer readout attention [Sec 4.3]; readout-weighted visual loss backpropagated through latent generation [Sec 4.4].
### Goal: Does its signal test whether the final answer causally depends on the trace's visual claims, or only claim truth / image sensitivity / attention?
No. It separates Readout, Grounding and Utility ("does intervening on the token change the answer") and states that "intervention utility remains a separate evaluation criterion" and is "not part of the training objective" [App B]. Training uses attention readout and prototype similarity.
### Goal: What counterfactual/intervention operator does it use, and what limitation does it report?
Answer-changing edits that preserve the scene while altering a decisive cue, but only for analysis/evaluation [Sec 5.3, Table G.5]; training negatives are prototypes from mismatched images [Sec 4.2].
