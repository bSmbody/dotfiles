# VIG: Visual Information Gain as a Reward Signal for Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Compression

## What this paper does
VIG (EMNLP 2026 Findings) adds a GRPO reward equal to the clipped mean, over the think block, of per-token visual information gain: the entropy of the next-token distribution without the image minus the entropy with the image, computed by two forward passes of the same policy. It never rewards brevity directly; shortening emerges as visually disengaged filler is suppressed. Experiments on Qwen3-VL-Thinking 2B/4B/8B across six math-style multimodal benchmarks show better accuracy-length trade-offs.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What does the compression reward measure as a token's value, and is that a measure of correct grounding?
VIG(t) = H(t | q, no image, t<) - H(t | q, I, t<), identified with per-token conditional mutual information [full text, Section 3.1 / Definition 1, Eq 2]; reward = clipped mean over the think block, combined as w_f R_format + w_a R_acc + w_v R_VIG with w_v=1 [Section 3.2.3 / Eq 4]. The limitations state: "VIG measures visual dependence, which is neither visual correctness nor counterfactual necessity ... a confidently hallucinated reading can score as high as genuinely grounded perception; the accuracy reward counteracts this only at the trajectory level" [Limitations].

### Goal: Does high VIG identify load-bearing reasoning content?
No. In the deletion probe on VIG-trained chains, deleting the lowest-VIG sentences hurts most, because low-VIG sentences are "enriched in algebra and derivation, which is visually ungrounded yet computation-bearing"; high-VIG sentences are value readouts recoverable from the image still in context [Appendix F.2]. On plain GRPO chains the ordering reverses [Appendix F.2]. The authors conclude VIG suits training-time shaping "but not ... post-hoc pruning" [Limitations].

### Goal: Scope and adaptivity?
Static images only, Qwen3-VL-Thinking models; the visual input is fixed and VIG contrasts full image versus no image, with no region- or frame-level contrast [Limitations; Section 3.2.2].
