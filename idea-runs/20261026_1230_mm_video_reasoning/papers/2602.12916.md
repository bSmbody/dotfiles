# Reliable Thinking with Images

## What this paper does
RTWI studies "Noisy Thinking" in thinking-with-images (TWI) models: errors in cue mining (crop of the wrong or too-coarse region) and in answer reasoning propagate along the interleaved trace. It is a training-free test-time-scaling method: sample many TWI traces, score each stage's reliability from the top-k token entropies of the textual CoT (the tool-invocation text is used as a proxy for visual-cue reliability), filter out unreliable traces with adaptive thresholds, and vote using the "reliability leap" from mining to reasoning. Evaluated with Qwen3-VL Thinking/Instruct and other TWI models on V*, HR-Bench, TreeBench, VisualProbe and others.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How does RTWI decide whether a visual cue (crop) obtained in the trace is reliable, and does it look at the crop content?
It does not inspect the visual cue. The authors argue visual uncertainty is hard to estimate because "visual signals are continuous and unsegmented" and image-level uncertainty "is not necessarily aligned with cue uncertainty", so "the image-manipulation textual CoT itself could implicitly serve as a natural proxy for visual cue reliability" [full text, Section 1]. Stage reliability is the negative mean of the top-k token entropies in that stage's text (Definition 3.1, Eq 3) [Section 3.2]; Table 5 shows the high-entropy tokens concentrate in tool-invocation text [Section 4.3]. Reliability is thus a property of how confidently the model wrote the box, not of what the returned crop shows or whether the answer used it.

### Goal: What does it report about how errors in an earlier visual step propagate, and how is it corrected?
Errors compound ("One mistake leads to another"): wrong or task-irrelevant crops in the mining stage lead to wrong answers even when later reasoning is sound [Section 1, Fig 1]. Case studies include a model that locates a coarse region but misses the fine detail, repeated failed searches, and a false-positive crop (stone pillar read as trash can) where the model "recognizes the visual cue is incorrect yet still derives the wrong answer" [Appendix F, Figs 14–16]. Correction is only across traces: discard whole traces and vote over survivors [Sections 3.3–3.4]; nothing repairs a trace from within, and no training signal is produced. Cost scales with the number of sampled traces (Fig 4) [Section 4.3].
