# From Visual Affinity to Learning Credit in Multimodal Reinforcement Learning

## What this paper does
OVC (Outcome-Conditioned Visual Credit) argues that visual relevance is a measurement, not a quantity to maximize. It computes a context-relative visual coordinate (cosine affinity of a token's hidden state to the mean image representation minus affinity to the prompt representation), concatenates it with an uncertainty-scaled hidden state, and calibrates it against positive- versus negative-advantage reference means in the mini-batch (a diagonal Mahalanobis distance contrast). The resulting evidence becomes positive, normalized token coefficients that rescale the GSPO-token sequence advantage while preserving its sign.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal does it use to assign token-level credit for visual reasoning, and how is it combined with the outcome reward?
rho_it = cos(S h_it, S g_img) - cos(S h_it, S g_ctx) [full text, Section 3.2 / Eq 7]; z_it = [(1-p_it) h_it ; rho_it] (Eq 8); evidence e(z) = ||z - mu_-||^2 - ||z - mu_+||^2 under D^-1, where mu_+/- are advantage-weighted means over tokens of positive/negative-advantage responses (Section 3.1 / Eq 2-5). Normalized Credit Allocation turns signed evidence into positive coefficients that "modulate the original sequence advantage while preserving its sign" [Section 1; Section 3.3].

### Goal: Does it recognise or fix the problem that a correct visual observation inside a failed response gets a negative update?
It names the problem explicitly: "a valid visual observation may occur in a negative-advantage response when later reasoning fails. Upweighting it would strengthen its negative update even if the error occurs elsewhere" [full text, Section 1], and "The sign c_i indicates relative reward, not token correctness" (Section 3, Eq 1). Its fix is to stop giving visual affinity an unconditional bonus and to calibrate magnitude by how well a token's features separate positive from negative responses across the batch (Eq 5). The coefficients stay positive, so a correct observation in a failed response can be down-weighted but is still pushed down; and the calibration uses batch-level outcome statistics, not a check of that observation against the image.
