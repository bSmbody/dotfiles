# When and Where to Look: Adaptive Visual Evidence Scheduling for Efficient Long Video Understanding

## What this paper does
EcoFrame is a training-free frame scheduler for long-video VLMs. Starting from a small frame budget, the VLM answers directly; if the mean output-token entropy of the answer is above a round-dependent threshold, the budget is multiplied by alpha and new candidate frames are proposed around frames that received concentrated attention from the last query token, then the input set is rebuilt by relevance and coverage. It reports better accuracy-efficiency trade-offs on Video-MME, LongVideoBench and MLVU across backbones.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal decides how much visual evidence an instance gets, and does it involve reasoning?
The stop/expand decision uses e_out, the mean token entropy of the generated answer [full text, Section 2 / Eq 1; Section 4.2], compared against a relaxing threshold tau_t = tau_1 + (t-1)Delta_tau. Observation 1 shows correct answers have lower entropy and entropy drops when the budget reaches the minimum needed, but "absolute entropy levels vary across query groups ... Output entropy therefore indicates evidence sufficiency but is not a universal correctness certificate" [Section 3, Observation 1]. The loop is designed for "avoiding additional reasoning or verification" [Section 4.1]: the VLM answers directly each round; no chain of thought is produced or used.

### Goal: Can it tell when more frames will not help (reasoning-limited) versus when they will?
No such mechanism: high entropy always triggers more frames until B_max [Section 4.2]. Thresholds and B_max are global [Appendix C, Scenario-Dependent Compute Configuration]; brief needle evidence can be missed [Appendix C].
