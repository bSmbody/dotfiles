# Behavior Pack Optimization for Video MLLM Post-Training

## What this paper does
BPO argues that video MLLMs barely change predictions when frames are shuffled or the supporting segment is masked, and attributes this to rewards computed on a single response to the original clip. It replaces the GRPO group with a "behavior pack": responses to the original view plus question-typed counterfactual views (key-mask, non-key-mask, span-drop for event/count questions; reverse, shuffle, key-mask for order questions), scored jointly with contracts for stability, sensitivity and abstention, and an anchor-relative advantage. Gains on TempCompass, MVBench, NExT-QA with Qwen2.5-VL-7B; transfers to Video-MME and LongVideoBench. NeurIPS 2026.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to locate or re-inspect temporal evidence in long videos?
None: the policy reads a fixed input (8 uniformly sampled frames per clip in training) [full text, App. training details line ~1600]; there is no retrieval or re-inspection action.
### Goal: How does its RL reward assign credit to the evidence the model uses (outcome-only, IoU vs annotation, other)?
Per-view reward R_view = w1·R_ans + w2·R_ev + w3·R_topo + w4·R_fmt + λ_v·R_contract − β·Pen [full text, Sec 3.5, Eq 13]; R_contract asks the answer to change/abstain when key evidence is masked and stay stable when non-key content is masked. This is a counterfactual-dependence signal, but the views are "built from training-side annotations" of key-evidence segments [Sec 4.1; Sec 3.1], and the counterfactual is applied to a fixed input clip, not to observations the model chose to retrieve.
### Goal: What limitation or failure mode does it report on long-video temporal reasoning?
"BPO depends on useful interventions and reliable verifier signals ... open-ended generation, dense videos, and larger backbones may require new view families and stronger audits" [full text, Sec 5 Limitations].
