# Entropy Is Not Enough: Unlocking Effective Reinforcement Learning for Visual Reasoning via Vision-Anchored Token Selection

## What this paper does
VEPO shows that the text-RL recipe of training only on high-entropy tokens fails on visual reasoning (top-k entropy selection falls below full-token training), because many visually grounded tokens are low-entropy. It scores tokens by combining JSD and absolute entropy gap between the policy conditioned on the clean image and on a noise-perturbed image (soft noisy-OR), modulated by entropy, and restricts the policy-gradient update to the top-k tokens by this score.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What signal does it use to assign token-level credit for visual reasoning, and how is it combined with the outcome reward?
Two forward passes with clean image I and noisy image I'; JSD_t and |Delta H_t| between the two next-token distributions [full text, Section 3.1 / Eq 1-6]; score c_t = (1 - (1-j_t)^alpha (1-|dH_t|)^(1-alpha)) * h_t after per-response min-max normalization (Def 3.1 / Eq 7); only the top-ceil(kT) tokens receive gradient (Eq 8). The selected tokens are updated with the ordinary outcome advantage, so selection decides which tokens learn, not in which direction.

### Goal: Does the credit signal check whether a token's visual content is correct, or only how much it depends on the image?
Only sensitivity to image corruption. Section 2.2 motivates the score by showing visually sensitive tokens are often low-entropy and missed by entropy selection; nothing compares the emitted content with the image [full text, Section 2.2-3.2].

### Goal: What limitations does it report?
Extra forward pass overhead; limited exploration of perturbation types; no rigorous theory for why visual-sensitive token selection helps [full text, Limitations].
