# Thinking Without Images: Internalizing Visual Manipulation with On-Policy Self-Distillation (Imagine-OPD)

## What this paper does
Imagine-OPD trains a model to reason with textual "imagination" steps (choose a region, describe what zooming would reveal) instead of real crop calls. A frozen teacher copy of the same base model is given privileged zoomed views cropped from annotated evidence boxes plus the answer, and supervises the student's own on-policy imagination trajectories via token-level reverse KL. Reports best average on V*, HR-Bench 4K/8K and other vision-centric benchmarks at 1.5–2.7× lower latency than tool-based methods (Qwen3-VL 4B/8B).

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What does it say about outcome-reward training of visual operations, and what supervision does its dense signal need?
"When such behaviors are learned mainly from outcome-level reward, the generated intermediate crops or visual cues may be noisy or fail to faithfully capture task-relevant visual evidence" [full text, Abstract, Section 1]. Its dense signal needs annotated evidence boxes B_1:M to build privileged views [Section 3.2]. Ablations: removing evidence views drops V* 88.0→82.2; replacing GT boxes with self-proposed boxes "recovers part of the gain but remains weaker"; GRPO alone is below OPD; using the current student as teacher collapses to 0.0 accuracy [Section 5.3]. Limitation: "our current experiments require region annotations available during training" [Limitations].

### Goal: Does internalizing the operation remove the need for the returned visual evidence?
At inference yes — the imagined observation o_k is generated text, not a re-encoded image [Section 3.1]. Evidence-region attention coverage rises only modestly (8B: 26.0→29.6 on TreeBench) [Section 5.2], so imagined observations are not guaranteed to be read off the region.
