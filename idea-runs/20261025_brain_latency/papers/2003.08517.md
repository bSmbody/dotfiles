# Provably Constant-time Planning and Replanning for Real-time Grasping Objects off a Conveyor Belt

## What this paper does
Motion planning for picking known objects off a conveyor moving at known velocity: offline preprocessing builds a set of reusable root paths and data structures so that online (re)planning to any perceived goal pose completes within a user-specified bound T_bound, letting the robot start moving on noisy early pose estimates and replan as perception improves. PR2 real-robot and simulation.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism maps a delayed high-level decision/observation onto the current state?
Goals are specified as an object pose on a belt of known velocity, so planning "to a pose g" means planning to intercept the object "at some future time" [full text, Section III]; perception keeps updating g during execution and the planner replans within T_bound until a replan cutoff t_rc [Section III, assumptions A1-A3]. Latency is handled by making the planner provably fast, plus kinematic forward projection along the known belt motion.

### Goal: What does the method assume about the slow decision-maker (trainable, white-box features, output type, delay scale)?
Known belt velocity, known object geometric models, bounded perception error ε_P, environment static up to t_rc [Section III, A1-A3]; the decision is geometric, not semantic.

### Goal: What does it report as a limitation relevant to this axis?
One-shot planning (on the early noisy pose or on a delayed accurate pose) "frequently fails", which is why constant-time replanning is required [Section I]. The approach presumes the decision can be recomputed within the time budget, which is false for a multi-second VLM call.
