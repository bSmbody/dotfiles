# RESCUE: Is This Alarm Worth Acting On? Counterfactual Evaluation of VLA Runtime Monitors

## What this paper does
RESCUE evaluates runtime failure monitors for VLAs (OpenVLA-OFT, π0.5 on LIBERO-Spatial/LIBERO-Plus) as intervention rules: it snapshots simulator+policy state at action-chunk boundaries, branches counterfactual continuations under an intervention repertoire {Continue, scripted Recover}, estimates a recoverability horizon (latest checkpoint where some intervention still succeeds with probability ≥ τ), and computes decision value against stop-and-reset with a closed-form break-even false-alarm rate. 1,562 trunk episodes and 128,340 branches.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the brain pre-specify monitors and local recoveries that fire without re-calling the brain, and how are they parameterized?
No brain is involved. The recovery is "a fixed open-loop manoeuvre": freeze 8 steps; if gripper was closed, lower 6 and release 4; retract 8, settle 4 [full text, Sec. IV-A]. Monitors are existing families (steps-since-progress, SAFE-style, etc.) evaluated as decision rules.

### Goal: Does it measure wall-clock latency/time saved by avoiding brain re-calls, and does the world keep evolving (no pause) during brain calls?
It measures alarm timing relative to the recoverability horizon: a sizable fraction of alarms arrive after the horizon (positive alarm-minus-horizon lag) [Sec. IV-B, Fig. 3]. Routing alarms to scripted Recover rather than stop-and-reset reduces excess cost by 3.1–13.4× [Sec. IV-D]; at τ=0.5, 14/66 OpenVLA-OFT/LIBERO-Plus failures are recoverable under Continue vs 34/66 with Continue or scripted Recover [Sec. I]. Delay of an intervention is not modeled as a decision latency (no planner call); the scripted recovery begins at the alarm checkpoint. The paper lists that hardware "may introduce ... latency ... absent from this benchmark" [Sec. V].

### Goal: What limitation does it report on recovery/monitoring?
"We evaluate only Continue and one fixed scripted Recover, and make no claim about richer or learned recovery repertoires" [Sec. V]; all 262 failures are timeouts; costs are declared, not calibrated.
