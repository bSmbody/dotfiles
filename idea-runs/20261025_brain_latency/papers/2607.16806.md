# Token-Wise Latent Streaming from Slow Reasoners to Fast Planners for Dynamic Vision Language Navigation

## What this paper does
SPARK-VLN streams intermediate hidden states of a slow VLM (VILA-8B) during autoregressive generation, compresses them into 8 latent slots, and conditions a fast flow-matching trajectory planner on them throughout inference, rather than waiting for the finished answer. Introduces a dynamic social VLN benchmark with paused ("Idealized") and non-paused ("Realistic") modes where pedestrians keep moving during inference.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the system measure, ignore or compensate the brain's latency, and does the environment pause while the model thinks?
It measures staleness and evaluates non-paused. The paper states mainstream VLN benchmarks are synchronous — "the simulator freezes while the model thinks" — so "observation staleness is neither penalized nor even measured" [full text, Sec I]. "All methods degrade once the simulator no longer pauses during inference"; streaming beats wait-then-act by 10.0 SR points and cuts per-update latency from 0.788 s to 0.185 s [Sec I].

### Goal: What mechanism predicts the future state or reconciles a stale high-level decision with the current state, and what does it assume?
It shortens effective staleness by delivering partial reasoning early; the fast planner fuses the streamed latent with the current RGB-D observation [Sec III-C, Eq 5]. There is no prediction of the future state for the reasoner: the VLM still reasons on x_{t'} (the inference-start frame) [Sec III-B, Eq 3]. Assumes white-box access to intermediate-layer hidden states of a local VLM and a planner trained on those latents; not applicable to an API brain.

### Goal: What limitation does it report on dynamic tasks?
Navigation only; the reasoner's semantic signal still carries lag t − t' [Sec III-B].
