# Shared Autonomy via Hindsight Optimization

## What this paper does
Javdani, Srinivasa & Bagnell (RSS 2015). Shared-autonomy teleoperation is posed as a POMDP whose only hidden variable is the user's goal. Instead of predict-then-blend (guess one goal, assist toward it), the robot assists toward the whole goal distribution. The belief comes from MaxEnt IOC over user inputs. Actions are chosen by hindsight optimization (QMDP): an expectation over goals of per-goal action values, assuming the goal becomes known at the next step. Goals can have several acceptable targets (grasp poses), with closed-form values for deterministic dynamics. A 10-user study on a robot arm found faster task completion and less input than predict-then-blend, but users trended towards preferring blend.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does the robot act under a distribution over a slower agent's eventual goal before that goal is known, and does it move to keep several goals cheap?
It picks a to minimize Q(b,a,u) = Σ_g b(g) Q_g(x,a,u), the belief-weighted per-goal cost-to-go, following its gradient [full text, Sec V]. "When the system is able to make progress for all goals, it does so automatically. When a good assistance strategy is ambiguous (e.g. the robot is in between two goals), the output can be interpreted as a blending" [Sec I]. It is explicitly contrasted with predict-then-blend (assist only toward the most likely goal) [Sec I]. This is the expected-value hedge over an authoritative agent's possible choices, with a belief from a predictor.

### Goal: What does it assume, and what is shown?
Goals are static objects with known per-target cost functions. Transitions are deterministic. The QMDP assumption is that full observability arrives at the next step, so the robot never gathers information [Sec V, VI-A]. The variant used assumes "the robot takes over" after the goal is known [Sec V-2]. There is no explicit reveal time, no moving goals and no closing feasibility windows. Results: completion time significantly lower (F(1,9)=12.98, p=0.006) and less control input (p=0.021) than predict-then-blend; preference trended towards blend (not significant) [Sec VII-C].
