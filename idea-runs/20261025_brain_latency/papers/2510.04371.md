# Speculative Actions: A Lossless Framework for Faster Agentic Systems

## What this paper does
Transfers speculative execution (microprocessors) and speculative decoding (LLMs) to agent loops. While a slow, authoritative Actor (a frontier LLM or an external API) computes the next action, a fast Speculator guesses the top-k likely actions. The system pre-launches the calls those guesses imply and commits them only if the Actor's real answer matches. Evaluated on chess, e-commerce, HotpotQA (lossless) and OS tuning (lossy). It reports up to 55% next-action accuracy and up to 20% latency reduction.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the speculate-then-verify mechanism and what does its losslessness rest on?
Algorithm 1: the Actor's request returns a future; in parallel, the Speculator returns k guesses, and for each guess the system pre-launches the next call. When the Actor resolves, a matching branch is committed and the others are discarded [full text, Sec 2, Alg 1]. Losslessness rests on Assumption 2, "pre-launched calls that do not correspond to the realized trajectory have no externally visible side effects (or can be rolled back)" [Sec 2]. Proposition 1 bounds the speedup at 50% for one-step speculation [Sec 2]. Using top-k matters: k=3 gives 54.7% hit rate and 19.5% time saved in chess [Sec 3.1.2], and HotpotQA accuracy improves "significantly from top-1 to top-3" [Sec 3.3]. The best chess speculator is the same model with low reasoning effort [Sec 3.1].

### Goal: What does it say about irreversible or physical side effects?
"Many systems involve irreversible or externally visible effects ... where naive speculation is harmful. Thus, speculation must be limited to cases where mispredictions are reversible, via forking, snapshot restoration, or roll-forward repair" [Sec 2, "Side effects and safety"]. No embodied or physical-control domain is evaluated. The lossy OS-tuning extension relies on settings that can simply be overwritten [Sec 4].
