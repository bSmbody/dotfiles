# LookOut: Diverse Multi-Future Prediction and Planning for Self-Driving

## What this paper does
LookOut is an end-to-end self-driving stack that (1) predicts a small, diverse set of scene-consistent futures with a learned diverse sampler, (2) scores each future's probability, and (3) plans with a contingency planner: one short-term action that is safe under all predicted futures, followed by a separate long-term trajectory per future. The diverse sampler is trained with a "planning diversity" reward so that the sampled futures are ones that require different ego plans, not just different trajectories. Evaluated in closed-loop simulation and open-loop metrics.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is the contingency plan structured and why is it better than optimizing one plan in expectation?
Standard planners pick the single trajectory minimizing expected cost over futures (Eq 12), which "does not exploit the fact that only one of the predicted scenarios will happen", while a max over futures is over-conservative [full text, Sec 3.4]. LookOut instead finds an immediate action τ_{0:t} that is safe w.r.t. all futures (max cost over Y) plus a probability-weighted cost-to-go, where each future Y_i gets its own optimal continuation g(x_t, Y_i) (Eq 13). "Such decision-postponing avoids over-conservative behaviors while staying safe until more information is obtained," and it works only if the future set is diverse and covers the realizations [Sec 3.4]. Ablation M4: the contingency planner vs a single-plan (PLT) planner reduces collisions by 38% [Sec 4.3].

### Goal: How does it decide which futures are worth representing?
The planning diversity energy rewards sets of futures whose induced ego plans differ: R(Y) sums ℓ2 distances between the contingency planner's trajectories τ(Y_i) for different samples (Eq 7–8), so samples are spent on "distinct future scenarios that require different SDV plans" rather than on "irrelevant actors or actions that do not impact the SDV's behavior" [Sec 1, Sec 3.2]. Removing it raises collision rate (28% lower with it) [Sec 4.3, M2]. A scenario-scoring module supplies probabilities so unlikely futures do not dominate [Sec 3.3, M3].
