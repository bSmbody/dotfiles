# EagleVLA: Towards Onboard Real-Time Robot Control via Foresight-Aligned Asynchronous Inference

## What this paper does
Onboard (Jetson) asynchronous π0/π0.5 deployment. A 40M-parameter future correction module predicts the compressed VLM final-layer state at t+Δ from the state at t and the committed actions, so the action expert predicts actions starting at t+Δ; a confidence head drives scheduling between VLM and action-expert calls; a llama.cpp engine speeds inference. Beats VLASH by 14.8% on LIBERO.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What latency does it address, and does it involve a high-level (frontier/VLM planner) brain?
Low-level VLA inference latency Δ measured in action steps (up to Δ=9 in the LIBERO sweeps) on edge hardware [full text, Sec 3, 5.1]. No separate high-level planner; the "slow" part is the VLA's own VLM backbone.

### Goal: What mechanism predicts the future state or reconciles a stale decision with the current state, and what does it assume?
"We take the compressed final-layer output of the VLM at t and the committed actions, then pass them through future correction module that predicts the compressed VLM final-layer output at t+Δ" [Sec 4.1]; trained in two stages with ground-truth future hidden states and random Δ. This is a cerebellum-like action-conditioned forward model, but in the VLA's private latent space: it requires white-box hidden states, joint training with the action expert, and sub-second Δ within one action chunk (H=20). It feeds the executor, not a reasoning brain; nothing reasons over the predicted state.
