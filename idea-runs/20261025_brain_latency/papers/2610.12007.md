# REACT: Rolling Denoising and Dual Decoupling for Reactive Robot Control with VLA Models

## What this paper does
Rolling denoising for π0.5 (staggered flow timesteps; each executed block refined across several recent observations) plus dual decoupling, an inference-time scheduler that separates sensing, a pool of VLM workers, DiT denoising and execution. Improves success and reaction latency on RoboTwin 2.0 and real dynamic tasks (pour rice, reaction game).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What latency does it address, and does it involve a high-level (frontier/VLM planner) brain?
VLA-internal latency: logged VLM embedding age has mean 233 ms, p95 383 ms, max 416 ms [full text, Sec 3.2]. No high-level brain.

### Goal: What mechanism predicts the future state or reconciles a stale decision with the current state, and what does it assume?
No forward prediction; staleness is bounded by provisioning enough VLM workers and always consuming the freshest embedding ("a late worker never regresses the conditioning") [Sec 3.2, Eq 4]. Trained per fixed rolling schedule (K,S); validated only on π0.5 [Sec 6].
