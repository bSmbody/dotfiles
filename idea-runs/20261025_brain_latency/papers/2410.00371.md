# AHA: A Vision-Language-Model for Detecting and Reasoning Over Failures in Robotic Manipulation

## What this paper does
AHA instruction-tunes an open VLM (13B) on synthetic failure trajectories (generated by perturbing RLBench demonstrations into a taxonomy of failure modes) to answer, per sub-task, whether it succeeded and, if not, a free-form explanation. The input is a concatenated grid of keyframes across views. It is plugged into Eureka reward reflection, PRoC3S TAMP replanning and Manipulate-Anything sub-task verification.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the brain pre-specify monitors and local recoveries that fire without re-calling the brain?
No. AHA is a post-sub-task VQA judge: "predicting a binary success condition ... of the given sub-task ... If the answer is 'No', the VLM is expected to generate a concise ... explanation" [full text, Sec. 4.1]. In downstream use, its explanation is fed back to an LLM that re-plans or re-prompts (PRoC3S: "replanning triggered if not") [Sec. 5.3]; recovery always goes through another model call.

### Goal: Does it measure latency, and what limitation does it report?
No latency or execution-time measurement. Limitation: outputs are "closely aligned with the failure scenarios in the fine-tuning data" [Sec. 6].
