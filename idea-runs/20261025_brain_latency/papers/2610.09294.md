# RT-Safe: Benchmarking Agent Safety in Real-Time Embodied Environment

## What this paper does
A simulated urban navigation benchmark in which the world keeps evolving during VLM inference and action execution; compares static (paused-during-inference) and real-time modes across eight VLM agents, and trains an offline-RL policy to reduce collisions.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the evaluation pause while the model thinks, and what happens when it does not?
In static mode "the world is paused during inference"; in real-time mode "the simulator continues to advance before that action executes" [full text, Sec 2.1]. Task completion is similar (91.3% static vs 94.1% real-time) but safe success falls from 19.8% to 0.7% and collisions rise 12.3x (3.31→40.68 per episode); passive contacts during inference account for ~84% of real-time collisions [Sec 3.3]. Mean response latency explains 78.4% of variance in collisions [Sec 3.2].

### Goal: Do the agents compensate latency by any mechanism?
No mechanism: the agent idles during inference (passive collisions) and the only adaptation is prompt-level — telling the model the world keeps moving shifted choices toward shorter moves (4 m moves 32.7%→15.2%) [Sec 3.3]. Higher reasoning effort does not consistently improve safety [Sec 3.4].

### Goal: Relevance to manipulation?
Navigation only; establishes that paused-world evaluation hides latency-induced failures of frontier VLMs.
