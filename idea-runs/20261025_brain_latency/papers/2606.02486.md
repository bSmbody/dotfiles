# Intercepting the Future: Latent-Space Predictive World Model for Dynamic VLA Manipulation

## What this paper does
AHEAD: a 4.9M-parameter predict-then-act wrapper around a frozen OpenVLA. RAFT optical flow gives per-patch velocity/acceleration; a language-and-motion saliency mask selects 30-60 of 196 patch tokens; a conditional flow-matching latent world model rolls these tokens forward with an analytic constant-acceleration update V_k = V_0 + A·kΔt, halting when sample variance exceeds a threshold; predicted tokens are spliced back and fed to the unchanged action decoder. Conveyor, rolling-ball, paddle, projectile tasks in sim and on an xArm 7. CoRL 2026.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism maps a delayed high-level decision/observation onto the current state?
It replaces the stale input of the decision-maker with a forecast of its input: the frozen decoder acts on \hat v_{t+K} instead of v_t [full text, Section 4, Section 4.2 Eqs. 2-3]. This is a predictive display for the policy, in the policy's own feature space.

### Goal: What does the method assume about the slow decision-maker (trainable, white-box features, output type, delay scale)?
The decision-maker is frozen but white-box: the world model predicts in the VLA's patch-token space ("to align with the action decoder's input distribution") and a feature alignment layer is trained [Section 4.2; Appendix B.7]. Horizon K_max=10 steps; full loop ≈158 ms, designed for a ≈200 ms reactive budget, the lookahead spans "3 to 5" steps of delay [Appendix B.3]. Object motion is assumed constant-acceleration over the horizon [Section 3, Scope].

### Goal: What does it report as a limitation relevant to this axis?
Constant-acceleration update breaks under chaotic dynamics (Plinko 48.6% at the longest reaction window); RAFT is image-plane only; sample variance shares one flow estimate and "can be confidently wrong" [Section 6.1]. Nothing in the method targets multi-second horizons or a decision-maker that cannot accept injected tokens.

# Intercepting the Future: Latent-Space Predictive World Model for Dynamic VLA Manipulation

## What this paper does
AHEAD: a 4.9M-parameter predict-then-act wrapper around a frozen OpenVLA-7B. A flow-matching latent world model forecasts future patch tokens in the VLA's feature space, conditioned on per-token velocity/acceleration from optical flow and a language-and-motion saliency mask; rollout halts adaptively when sample variance exceeds a threshold; the frozen decoder receives predicted tokens instead of current ones. 79–97% success on 20 dynamic simulation scenarios vs 31–58% for the best baseline; strong real conveyor/rolling-ball/catching results.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What latency does it address, and does it involve a high-level (frontier/VLM planner) brain?
The executor's own observation-to-action latency in dynamic scenes (real robot 171 ms per action step; K_max=10 prediction steps) [full text, Sec 4.3, 5.1]. No high-level brain.

### Goal: What mechanism predicts the future state or reconciles a stale decision with the current state, and what does it assume?
Prediction of the future scene in VLA token space with a constant-acceleration kinematic prior, horizon set by uncertainty, frozen VLA kept intact [Sec 3, 4.2–4.3]. Assumes object motion ≈ constant acceleration over the horizon, image-plane flow, and access to the VLA's patch-token interface; post-collision chaos breaks it [Sec 6.1]. Feeds a frozen executor; the forecast is never offered to a reasoning model or used to re-target a semantic command.
