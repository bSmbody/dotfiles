# KnowDR-REC: A Benchmark for Referring Expression Comprehension with Real-World Knowledge

## What this paper does
A referring-expression benchmark (1,042 image/expression/box triplets plus edited negatives) whose expressions need external world knowledge and multi-hop reasoning to resolve (person entities from ComplexWebQuestions, HotpotQA, KQA Pro, MetaQA, rewritten by GPT-4o; images from Wikipedia with at least four people). It evaluates 16 MLLMs zero-shot, separating text-level referent resolution (Acc_Text) from box accuracy, and tests rejection of negative expressions.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How large is the gap between a ~7B open VLM and frontier models at resolving which instance a knowledge-laden expression refers to, separately from localization?
Text-level referent resolution, Acc_Text: Gemini 2.5 Pro 89.1, Gemini 2.5 Flash 84.7, Grok-2 Vision 71.0, versus Qwen2.5-VL-7B 40.8, Qwen2.5-VL-3B 34.6, Qwen2.5-VL-32B 43.2, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 61.6 [full text, Table 1]. Localization runs the other way: the small Qwen models have better Acc@0.5 (59–61) than Gemini 2.5 Pro (21.9) [Table 1, "KnowDR-REC is Challenging"]. Grounding is much better when the text reasoning is right (e.g. Qwen-VL-Max 80.4 vs 59.0) [Sec "Textual Reasoning and Visual Grounding are Tightly Coupled"]. Only the Gemini models and Grok-2 reliably reject expressions with no valid referent [Results].

### Goal: Does the domain transfer to household-object referents?
Only weakly. All expressions are about person entities with multi-hop encyclopedic knowledge [Sec "Referring Expression Collection"], not commonsense about fruits, drinks or containers. The result shows that the referent-reasoning gap between 7B open and frontier models can be ~48 points when resolution needs knowledge and composition; it does not measure the gap on simpler household-commonsense referents, which may be much smaller.
