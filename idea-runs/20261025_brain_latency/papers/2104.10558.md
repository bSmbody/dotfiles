# Contingencies from Observations: Tractable Contingency Planning with Learned Behavior Models

## What this paper does
CfO (ICRA 2021) plans a closed-loop robot policy instead of a single trajectory: the plan is a set of conditional actions that depend on how other agents' stochastic behavior turns out. It parameterizes the robot's part of a learned autoregressive normalizing-flow behavior model (z^r), so a fixed choice of z^r reacts differently to different realizations of the other agents. It optimizes these parameters under a MAP-style criterion (behavior prior + goal + constraints, in expectation over the other agents' noise). On CARLA left-turn, overtaking and right-turn scenarios with a car that may or may not yield, it compares against noncontingent "underconfident" and "overconfident" planners.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism of contingency planning and why does it beat a single committed plan when the future is multimodal?
The planner outputs a policy whose later decisions are conditioned on future events not yet known at planning time [full text, Sec I, III]. The appendix argument is the Jensen gap: a noncontingent planner computes max over a later decision of an expectation over the uncertain intermediate outcome, i.e. "the single best compromise decision common to all possible values", while the contingent planner puts the max after the expectation and so realizes "the best of all worlds, by planning optimal contingencies for each future outcome"; hence V^NL ≤ V^HL and V^RL ≤ V^CL [Appendix A-G, Eqs 11–17]. Noncontingent planning is either underconfident (frozen robot that waits) or overconfident (assumes the other agent complies) [Sec III-C, Fig 2]. Results: near-expert success RG* is 0/30 for the underconfident noncontingent planner and 9–12/30 for the overconfident one, vs 27–28/30 for CfO in all three scenarios [Sec IV-C, Table II].

### Goal: What does contingency planning assume must hold for it to pay off?
(i) The uncertain event must be revealed by observation before the contingent decision has to be executed ("the other driver's intention to yield must be revealed before we commit") [Sec I]; (ii) agent positions are fully observable, only intentions hidden [Sec III-A]; (iii) a behavior model that covers the relevant modes (trained on goal-directed data with diverse outcomes) [Sec III, III-B]. The discrete analysis shows the parameterization represents sequences of n-th best responses, but not every arbitrary contingency [Appendix A-E].
