# Critic in the Loop: A Tri-System VLA Framework for Robust Long-Horizon Manipulation

## What this paper does
Tri-System VLA shares a PaliGemma backbone between a "Brain" (subtask generator) and a flow-matching "Cerebellum" action expert, and adds a separately trained Florence-2 Critic that asynchronously outputs a discretized progress value or an <aci> anomaly token. Event-driven scheduling keeps the Cerebellum running at ~20 Hz and wakes the Brain only on subtask completion, anomaly or stagnation; recovery combines rules and data-backed strategies. Real-robot long-horizon tasks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the brain pre-specify monitors and local recoveries that fire without re-calling the brain, and how are they parameterized?
No. The monitor is a learned critic, not specified by the brain: anomaly is a trained token over "the final 20 frames of anomalous trajectories" [full text, Sec. 3.3]. On interrupt, the framework preempts the policy and awakens the Brain with memory "accident happened" to generate the next subtask [Sec. 3.2, 3.4] — recovery requires a brain call. The Brain is a small co-trained VLM, not a black-box frontier model.

### Goal: Does it measure latency / does the world keep running during brain calls?
It runs asynchronously on a real robot (Critic in parallel with execution at ~20 Hz; Brain "dormant during nominal execution and ... awakened exclusively by Critic-driven interrupts") [Sec. 3.4], so latency is real, but the paper reports success-rate gains and efficiency qualitatively; brain latency is not an independent variable and time saved by avoiding brain re-calls on failure is not isolated.
