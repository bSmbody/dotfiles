# Model-Based Reinforcement Learning under Random Observation Delays

## What this paper does
Treats random, possibly out-of-sequence observation delays in POMDPs; proposes delay-aware filtering in a Dreamer-style RSSM world model where each latent step uses the posterior if its observation has arrived and the prior dynamics otherwise, with retroactive recomputation when late observations arrive, and trains the policy on these beliefs. Gym MuJoCo and Meta-World.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism maps a delayed high-level decision/observation onto the current state?
Belief filtering to the present: ψ_{τ,t} uses q_θ(x_τ|x_{τ-1},a_{τ-1},o_τ) if o_τ has arrived by t, else p_θ(x_τ|x_{τ-1},a_{τ-1}); φ_t is computed recursively (particles optional) and the policy acts on it [full text, Section 4.1, Eqs. 3-6].

### Goal: What does the method assume about the slow decision-maker (trainable, white-box features, output type, delay scale)?
The policy is trained on the world model's latent belief state, so it must consume that latent; delays are U{0,10}/U{0,20} control steps [Section 4.2; Appendix B.2 Table 4]. Applying belief inference to a policy trained without delays ("inference only") underperforms delayed training in half the environments [Appendix B.2].

### Goal: What does it report as a limitation relevant to this axis?
Recursive filtering accumulates one-step prediction errors and costs scale linearly with the maximum delay [Section 7].
