# Slow Brain, Fast Planner: Latency-Resilient VLM-Augmented Urban Navigation

## What this paper does
A VLM (1–3 s per query) selects one trajectory index from a learned sidewalk planner's candidate set; a training-free fusion layer turns that stale selection into real-time scoring of freshly generated candidates by geometric similarity weighted with exponential staleness decay (Score Fusion / Probability Fusion). ~2,000 real scenarios show 30% ADE reduction; in simulation fusion holds >80% success up to 5 s delay.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the system measure, ignore or compensate the brain's latency?
It compensates it and sweeps it: a delayed-oracle "VLM" returns its choice Δt∈{0,…,5} s later; Score Fusion stays >80% to 5 s while executing the stale VLM trajectory ("VLM Hold") collapses past 2 s and is near zero by 4 s [full text, Sec 4.2].

### Goal: What mechanism reconciles a stale high-level decision with the current state, and what does it assume?
The stale VLM trajectory is "motion-compensate[d] ... into the current robot frame using odometry", its remaining segment is progress-aligned, and similarity to each fresh candidate is added to the planner score with weight λ·exp(−Δt/τ_decay), τ_decay≈3–5 s [Sec 3.3, Eq 1–3]. This is training-free reconciliation of a black-box VLM output, but it compensates only ego-motion; there is no model of how other objects/agents moved, and stale intent is down-weighted rather than re-targeted. The brain still reasons over the old frame (no prediction for the brain).

### Goal: What limitation does it report?
Inherits the planner's candidate set; closed-loop simulator models the VLM as a delayed oracle that "cannot exhibit real-world scene drift" [Limitations]. Navigation only.
