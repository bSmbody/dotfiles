# Acting in Delayed Environments with Non-Stationary Markov Policies

## What this paper does
Analyzes MDPs with execution delay m: proves that state augmentation is exponentially expensive (policy-iteration lower bound), introduces the Execution-Delay MDP formulation without augmentation, proves non-stationary deterministic Markov policies suffice for optimality while stationary ones can be suboptimal, and proposes Delayed-Q, which forecasts the state m steps ahead with a forward model applied over the pending-action queue and acts greedily on the forecast. Atari/Maze/CartPole up to m=25; ICLR 2021.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism maps a delayed high-level decision/observation onto the current state?
Forward prediction (Smith-predictor/MBS style): \hat s_{t+m} = f(...f(s_t, a_{t-m})..., a_{t-1}), then a_t = π(\hat s_{t+m}) [full text, Section 6]. Related work: Walsh et al. (2009) MBS uses a most-likely present-state estimate assuming near-deterministic transitions [Section 1].

### Goal: What does the method assume about the slow decision-maker (trainable, white-box features, output type, delay scale)?
The decision-maker is a Q-network trained on the forecast state; the forward model takes the agent's own pending actions; the delay m is in decision steps and the decision recurs every step [Section 6]. Authors note a model-free variant failed because "the Q-function is unable to implicitly learn the m-step transition" [Section 6].

### Goal: What does it report as a limitation relevant to this axis?
Point estimates of the future state "can be devastating" when the future is multimodal (bimodal random-walk example) [Section 6, Point-Estimate Approaches]; probabilistic estimates and image-based learned forward models are left for future work [Section 8].
