# Delay-Aware Diffusion Policy: Bridging the Observation-Execution Gap in Dynamic Tasks

## What this paper does
DA-DP retrains a diffusion policy for a measured inference delay δ: zero-delay demonstrations are time-compressed (skip states so the trajectory still reaches s_n by T_target given per-chunk delay δ) and the policy is conditioned on δ. Tested on ping-pong, rolling-ball pickup, etc., with δ in tens to hundreds of ms.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What mechanism maps a delayed high-level decision/observation onto the current state?
Training-data correction: n' = (nΔt + δ)/(Δt + δ/H_act) states are kept so that the delay-compensated trajectory arrives on schedule, and the policy gets delay conditioning [full text, Section IV-A/B, Eqs. 2-5]. The policy implicitly learns to act for s_{t+δ}.

### Goal: What does the method assume about the slow decision-maker (trainable, white-box features, output type, delay scale)?
The delayed model is the trainable low-level policy itself with continuous actions; δ is "tens to hundreds of milliseconds" [Abstract; Section I], measured and systematic.

### Goal: What does it report as a limitation relevant to this axis?
Future work: combine with asynchronous execution because streamed trajectories still lag; applies to "predictable, systematic delays" [Section VI]. Requires retraining the delayed model, which is excluded for a frozen frontier brain.
