# PreAct-Nav: Agentic Reasoning Before Action for Urban Navigation

## What this paper does
PreAct-Nav wraps a frozen navigation policy with a VLM reasoner (e.g. Qwen3.8-27B) that keeps a subgoal memory and can call a "predictive sandbox": an action-conditioned world model that generates a short video of what would happen if a proposed movement were executed. The reasoner reviews the proposed action (optionally with the predicted rollout), accepts or revises it within a small budget, executes the first supported action, and then compares the real outcome with its expected cues to update memory. Urban navigation (UrbanNav, CityWalker).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the VLM reason over predicted future observations, and of what?
Yes, but the predictions are action-consequence previews: the world model generates rollouts conditioned on the reasoner's own candidate movement, \hat O_t^j = W(o_t, C(a_t^j), ℓ, ε) [full text, Sec 3.3, Eq 5], requested only when the observation-based assessment is unresolved [Sec 3.4, Eqs 6–7]. The reasoner outputs one accepted action per decision (first supported action, else the policy action) [Sec 3.4, Eq 10]. Predictions are not forecasts of where independently moving objects will be when the reasoner's answer arrives, and the decision is not contingent.

### Goal: Does it treat the reasoner's own latency as something to compensate?
No. It profiles per-request latency of the reasoner and world model on an H200 [Appendix, Table 13], but the read sections contain no mechanism that accounts for the world changing during the reasoner's call.
