# Learning to Act While Waiting: RL Finetuning of Generalist Robot Policies Under Inference Latency

## What this paper does
ARLI enables RL finetuning (DSRL noise steering) of flow/diffusion VLAs under asynchronous inference. The RL noise policy is conditioned on the inference-start state, the committed intermediate actions played during inference, and a mid-inference state taken just before the action expert runs (exploiting the VLM-backbone / action-expert split). Evaluated on Kinetix dynamic tasks (t_delay=4 steps), ALOHA transfer-cube with pi0 (t_delay=20 steps), and three real bimanual UR5e tasks.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it decide what the robot does during a seconds-long high-level decision about which object to act on?
No. The latency is sub-second VLA chunk latency; during inference the robot executes the previous chunk's committed actions [full text, Sec 3 "Asynchronous inference", Sec 4.1]. There is no discrete semantic choice among targets and no hedging over a slow model's possible outputs; the contribution is state augmentation that restores near-Markovian structure for RL [Sec 4.1, Sec 6].

### Goal: Does it overlap the "act while waiting" framing of a hedge policy?
Only in framing (title). Its "acting while waiting" means continuing a committed chunk and conditioning the later RL correction on fresher intermediate information [Sec 4.1, Fig 2-3]. It is learned (RL), chunk-level and requires a VLM/action-expert split [Sec 6]. Not a novelty threat to belief-weighted in-call hedging over a frontier brain's discrete answer, but it is relevant related work on the executor side of latency.
