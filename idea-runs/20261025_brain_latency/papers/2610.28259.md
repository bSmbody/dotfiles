# Large Multimodal Models for Direct Robot Control: GPT-6 as a Beginning

## What this paper does
Evaluates GPT-6 Astra as a direct closed-loop manipulation controller through GPT-GEAR, a tool-mediated agent (Observe / Reference / Act / Status / Finish). Astra emits bounded end-effector pose increments that a controller executes synchronously; it is tested on five simulated benchmarks (26.92% RoboDojo, 76.0% RoboTwin 2.0), a new LIBERO-GeoMotion benchmark (11.1% zero-shot) and five real tasks (40% zero-shot, 80% with one in-context demo). Identifies deficits in consequence-aware planning, precision, and latency.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the system measure, ignore or compensate the brain's latency, and does the environment pause while the model thinks?
It measures latency only as aggregate episode wall-clock time and does not compensate it. Act "converts a model decision into a bounded, synchronous control command" [full text, Sec 3.6]; Observe "does not recapture images, advance the simulator, or consume environment control steps" [Sec 3.4], so the world is effectively paused between decisions. Sec 5.3.4 reports 7.82 min per LIBERO-Plus episode (8.6–9.1x starVLA) and 28.43 min per RoboDojo episode (>10x), and ~20 min real-world episodes; latency is named as an obstacle to deployment but no mechanism addresses it.

### Goal: How are spatial commands grounded in time — relative to which robot state?
Commands are relative increments: p_goal = p_start + Δp, where p_start is "the measured end-effector pose ... at the start of the call" [Sec 3.6, Eq 7]. Because the arm is stationary while Astra reasons, the start-of-call pose equals the pose Astra saw; the formulation would mis-place targets if the arm moved during the call, but this case never arises in their protocol.

### Goal: What limitation does it report on dynamic or moving-object tasks?
No moving-object tasks are evaluated. Limitations listed are consequence-aware motion planning, precision under tight geometry, high-order coordination, and latency [Sec 5.3, 7.4–7.5].
