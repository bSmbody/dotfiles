# CarryOn: A Dual-Loop Harness for Personalized and Reliable Robot Assistance

## What this paper does
CarryOn wraps a frozen VLA (π0.5, StarVLA) with a planning loop (persistent user/scene memory for personalized subtask decomposition) and an execution loop: a geometric shield filters each VLA action chunk against predicted contact risk using a damped end-effector response model, and a Judge-triggered reactive recovery suspends the VLA and runs a control-based recovery skill (re-localize, re-grasp, holding checks) before the VLA resumes. Offline reflection refines recovery skill parameters/strategy across rounds. Evaluated in RoboLab/LIBERO sim and on a real robot.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the brain pre-specify monitors and local recoveries that fire without re-calling the brain, and how are they parameterized?
Partly. The recovery is a shared library skill ("one shared base skill for all targets") invoked "when the Judge detects a subtask failure", with gripper-width holding checks after lift and after transport [full text, Sec. 3.3.2]. It is not armed per stage by the planner with stage-specific expected events or tolerances; skill parameters/strategy are refined offline between rounds by reflectors and an admission check [Sec. 3.3.3]. The shield uses VLM-inferred target/destination descriptions to build obstacle sets [Sec. 3.3.1].

### Goal: Does it measure wall-clock latency/time saved by avoiding brain re-calls?
No measurement of time saved by avoiding brain re-calls during execution; planning latency is reported only as median time to dispatch for RoboLab planning without physical execution [Table 2]. Recovery success improves across reflection rounds (29.0% to 80.0% cumulative completion over R0–R2) [Sec. 4.3].
