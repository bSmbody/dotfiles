# Augmented Reality Predictive Displays to Help Mitigate the Effects of Delayed Telesurgery

## What this paper does
SARPD overlays, on the delayed stereo endoscope video, a rendering of where the surgical robot's tools will be once the operator's already-sent commands take effect. The predicted tool pose comes from the operator's commanded pose via inverse kinematics; an EKF corrects the hand-eye transform online so the virtual tools register with the image, and an adaptive transparency hides the overlay when prediction and image agree. A 10-person da Vinci user study at 1 s round-trip delay shows 19% faster peg transfer with no change in errors.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What exactly is predicted and overlaid, and from what information; what is left unpredicted?
Only the robot's own tools are predicted: the target slave pose s_m[n] is computed from the operator's master input (Eq 1–2), the predicted joint angles follow by inverse kinematics, and CAD models of the tools are rendered at that pose over the delayed image [full text, Sec II, II-B]. So the ego part (effect of commands already issued) is known exactly from the command stream and shown immediately. The environment (tissue, rings) is not predicted; it is shown as delayed video. Registration error between prediction and image is corrected with an EKF on the hand-eye transform, smoothed with an IIR/slerp filter to avoid jumpy overlays [Sec II-A, Eq 4–6]; opacity scales with the distance between the predicted pose and the pose visible in the delayed image so small mismatches do not confuse the user [Sec II-B, Eq 7–8].

### Goal: What did the study show and what limitations are stated?
Weighted error 4.9 (no SARPD, 1 s) vs 3.7 (SARPD, 1 s) vs 2.8 (no delay), not significant; completion time 60.6 s vs 49.2 s vs 29.6 s, significant (p=0.02 vs delayed) [Sec IV, Table II]. Earlier predictive displays (ETS-7 satellite, up to 7 s round trip) are cited as the lineage [Sec I]. Stated future work: tasks with more environmental interaction, endoscope motion, occlusion of tools by the environment [Sec V] — i.e. the display does not model independently moving parts of the scene.
