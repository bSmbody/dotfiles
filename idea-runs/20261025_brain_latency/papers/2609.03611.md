# FailBench: How Reliable are VLMs at Judging Robot Task Success?

## What this paper does
FailBench pools 2,197 manipulation attempts from 14 public sources (12 real, 2 sim; 75% natural failures) and evaluates 13 VLM-based success/failure detectors given the instruction and recording. It diagnoses which evidence types make detection hard and tests an evidence-localization (cropping) pipeline.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How reliable are VLM judges as the event/failure detector a brain-pre-specified monitor would otherwise rely on, and which event types are hardest?
The best detector reaches 0.77 mean balanced accuracy ("roughly one incorrect judgment in four") [full text, Sec. 4.2]. "Detectors are reliable when failure can be inferred from which object moved, but approach chance when success depends on whether physical contact was established; no model exceeds 0.60 balanced accuracy on contact-rich assembly" [Sec. 1]. Robot-failure-specialized fine-tunes score below their base models [Sec. 1]. Evidence localization (cropping to the outcome region) adds +2.3 points [Sec. 1/4.4]. Non-visual streams (force-torque, audio) are not used even where recorded [Sec. 5].

### Goal: Does it address monitoring during execution or latency?
No. It scores one binary outcome per completed attempt and "says nothing about whether a detector can ... anticipate a failure before it happens ... or estimate progress during execution" [Sec. 5]. No latency measurements.
