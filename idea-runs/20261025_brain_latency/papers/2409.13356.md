# Automatic Behavior Tree Expansion with LLMs for Robotic Manipulation (BETR-XP-LLM)

## What this paper does
BETR-XP-LLM uses GPT-4 to interpret a natural-language goal into formal BT goal conditions, which a reactive PDDL-style planner expands into a behavior tree over a hand-designed skill database. When an action fails (in planning simulation or on the real ABB YuMi), the error message is sent to the LLM, which proposes missing preconditions; these are inserted permanently into the tree and expanded by the planner, so the same failure is handled reactively thereafter without the LLM.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the brain pre-specify monitors and local recoveries that fire without re-calling the brain, and how are they parameterized?
Recovery structure is retained, not pre-specified: the LLM is called after a failure to produce new precondition(s) for the failing action, which are inserted "as the first preconditions of the failing action and expanded further by the planner" [full text, Sec. III, item 13]. The BT then reacts to world-state conditions (symbolic predicates from object positions) without further LLM calls. Recoveries come from the planner over skills with known preconditions/effects; there are no continuous tolerances or sensory-event expectations.

### Goal: Does it measure wall-clock latency/time saved by avoiding brain re-calls, and does the world keep evolving during brain calls?
It argues qualitatively that "Given the cost and time delay associated with each call to an LLM, a reactive policy is highly preferable" [Sec. III] and counts LLM calls, but ticking time is "negligible and thus not a concern" and planner time "almost instantaneous compared to the LLM" [Sec. III]. No wall-clock measurement under a running world.

### Goal: What limitation does it report?
Requires a skill database with known preconditions and effects and a symbolic world state; errors detectable in planning depend on simulation completeness [Sec. III].
