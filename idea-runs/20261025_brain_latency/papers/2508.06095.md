# Incremental Language Understanding for Online Motion Planning of Robot Manipulators

## What this paper does
Abrams et al. (IROS 2025) couple a reasoning-based incremental speech parser with an online MPC motion planner (BoundPlanner + BoundMPC) on a 7-DoF arm. The robot starts moving as soon as a meaningful partial parse exists ("Grab the mug"), and later fragments ("from the top", "but don't spill it", "but move faster") change the goal set, constraints or cost online without stopping. Object and grasp poses are assumed known; three real-robot demonstrations against an offline planner (VP-STO).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the robot act before the authoritative goal is fully known, and does it hedge over candidate goals or commit to one?
It acts early but commits to one interpretation. "The ambiguity in of the grasping pose is resolved randomly to a side grasp", and the robot replans when the correcting fragment arrives at t1 = 3.1 s [full text, Fig. 1, Sec V-A]. The parser keeps multiple candidate parses, but the motion planner receives only the current best parse; the instruction "is constant in eq. 2 because it is not predicted over the time horizon" [Sec IV-A]. So this is predict-then-commit-then-replan, not a belief-weighted hedge. The gain reported is task time vs an offline planner that stops to replan (VP-STO plans 5.6 s before moving) [Sec V-A, Table III].

### Goal: What does it assume about the scene and the source of the goal?
Static scenes, known object and grasp poses ("assumed to be known to focus on the language processing") [Sec V]; the goal source is a human speaker whose words arrive incrementally, not a slow model with a latency distribution; no moving targets or closing reachability windows. The authors motivate early action by irreversibility ("the actions taken by the robot might not be reversible anymore") [Sec I].
