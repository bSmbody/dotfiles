# Fast Plans, Faithful Actions: Closing the Planning-Execution Gap in Hierarchical Vision-Language-Action Models

## What this paper does
Studies a π0.5-derived waypoint hierarchy (VLM planner emits waypoints, action expert executes). Finds token-AR planning needs 57 VLM passes and that erasing waypoint endpoints barely affects success (executor ignores the plan). Proposes waypoint-aligned block-autoregressive decoding (57→8 passes, 8.7x lower planning latency on a real dual-arm robot) and normalized goal modulation with anti-shortcut training (LIBERO-Long 91.0%→96.2%).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does the system measure, ignore or compensate the planner's latency, and does the robot idle while the planner thinks?
It measures and reduces latency but does not compensate it. On the real robot token-AR takes 1094 ms per plan and 1266 ms per request, "longer than the roughly 0.5 s action segment it commands, so the arms are in motion only 28.7% of the time"; Block-AR cuts planning to 125 ms and raises the motion duty cycle to 65.6% [full text, Sec 6.2]. The planner is still blocking: the arm idles while the plan is decoded.

### Goal: What mechanism (if any) reconciles a plan with the state at execution time?
The executor generates actions from the current state s_i and the waypoint target q_i [Sec 3, Eq 2]; waypoints are absolute configurations, so they do not drift with arm motion, but there is no model of state change between planning observation and execution and no prediction. The paper's concern is that the executor ignores the plan (endpoint sensitivity 0.6%→42.6% with NGM) [Sec 7].

### Goal: What limitation does it report?
Validated on one π0.5-derived hierarchy; Block-AR assumes fixed-format plan tokens and NGM relies on π0.5's AdaRMS conditioning [Sec 7]. Static tasks only.
