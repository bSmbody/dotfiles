# ReKep: Spatio-Temporal Reasoning of Relational Keypoint Constraints for Robotic Manipulation

## What this paper does
ReKep proposes semantic keypoints (DINOv2 features clustered inside SAM masks), overlays them as numbered marks, and has GPT-4o write per-stage sub-goal and path constraints as Python functions over keypoint positions. A hierarchical optimizer re-solves for end-effector sub-goals and paths at ~10 Hz using keypoint positions from a 20 Hz tracker, giving a closed-loop, training-free policy that reacts to disturbances; backtracking handles violated path constraints.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How are the VLM's outputs tied to tracked entities, and is the binding late?
The constraint functions "do not directly manipulate the numerical values of the keypoint positions"; they are "only instantiated when invoked with actual keypoint positions tracked by a specialized 3D tracker" [full text, Sec 3.4]. Sub-problems are re-solved at ~10 Hz on fresh tracked keypoints [Sec 3.3]. So a VLM answer expressed over mark IDs is late-bound to the tracked entity, which already re-anchors "where" for a stale answer.

### Goal: When is the VLM queried, what does it see, and what is assumed about scene motion?
Keypoints are proposed and marked on one RGB image, and GPT-4o is queried once per task to produce all stages [Sec 3.4, A.6]. The marks show where keypoints are at proposal time; nothing predicts where they will be. The keypoint forward model assumes grasped keypoints move rigidly with the gripper and "other keypoints stay static", justified as local over the 0.1 s solve [Sec 3.3]. Changing stage skeletons "requires running keypoint proposal and VLM at a high-frequency, which poses considerable computational challenges" [Sec 5]. VLM latency is not measured or compensated.

### Goal: What are the main failure sources and limitations?
The point tracker causes the largest share of failures (occlusion), then keypoint proposal and the VLM referring to the wrong keypoints [Sec 4.3]. Limitations: rigidity forward model, dependence on tracking, fixed stage skeleton [Sec 5, A.11].
