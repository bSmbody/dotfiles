# R1 review: Belief-Weighted Answer-Branching for Moving Targets under Seconds-Scale VLM Latency

## Summary
A frozen frontier VLM (GPT-6 Astra) picks which of several moving DOM objects the instruction refers to, and its answer arrives seconds later. During that call the arm runs a greedy 10 Hz waypoint choice. The choice maximises the belief-weighted probability that the brain's eventual answer will still be catchable at reveal time, averaged over the measured latency distribution. The belief is a temperature-scaled Qwen2.5-VL-7B prediction of the brain's answer. The claimed insight is that the AESOP/DDTO answer-branching hedge must be weighted once moving targets make joint feasibility impossible, and that the weights should model the slow oracle rather than the truth. Concretely, value the hedge at reveal time instead of at the next step as Hindsight Optimization does. At threshold the predicted evidence, on a newly built DOM-Sem condition, is: ≥50% of the paused-minus-wait gap recovered at 4 s; ≥8 points over the uniform hedge on contested trials (≥3 overall); ≥5 points over next-step HO on contested slow-band trials at 8 s; and ties on DOM SR/MP/CR negative controls.

## Strengths
- The proposal is honest about what is new. The limitations section admits the mechanism is a composition of AESOP, HO/LookOut and DDTO, and every ingredient has a kill condition. The pilot gate (G1/G2) and the negative controls (SR, MP, CR, privileged-label script) are better experimental hygiene than most papers in this area.
- The problem is real and under-served. Frontier-brain manipulation papers from Sept–Oct 2026 (GPT-GEAR, CALL, PyRUA-Lean) all pause the world or idle the executor during the call. RT-Safe shows idling during inference is where real-time failures come from. I found no paper that moves the arm during a seconds-long semantic call to keep several moving referents reachable. My recency searches turned up only VLA-level async inference, digital idle-time speculation [2605.22154] and confidence-gated cascades [2610.27677].
- The comparison is clean. One cached brain answer per trial is replayed across latencies, and the DOM demonstrator is unchanged after reveal, so any difference between arms comes only from the in-call phase.
- The ablation set targets each claimed ingredient directly: belief source (brain-calibrated vs GT-calibrated vs uniform vs top-1) and value horizon (next-step vs p90 vs posterior vs oracle D).
- The cost fits the constraints: about 15 GPU-days and about $410 of API spend, with no training.

## Weaknesses
- [fatal] At threshold, the effect that belongs to this paper is very small. P3 (≥50% of P−W) is measured against brain-wait, an idle straw man. Any "don't idle" policy (centroid, uniform hedge) gets much of that. The novelty-bearing delta is P4: +3 points overall and +8 on contested trials over the uniform hedge, which is itself the AESOP/DDTO transfer. P5 adds +5 on contested trials over next-step HO, only in a hand-slowed speed band at 8 s. With 200 trials per condition and a contested fraction near the 0.15 gate, the contested subset holds about 30–60 trials. An 8-point difference there sits within one standard error, even with paired McNemar testing. The +3 overall is equally marginal. A CVPR panel that has rejected "thin deltas over 2026 work" would read the result as: don't idle, plus a small, statistically fragile reweighting.
- [major] The setting looks built to create the problem it solves, and an obvious alternative may remove it. The brain is needed only because the track table is class-agnostic and DOM-Sem was written to need commonsense; the privileged-label script solves the task. The latency is 4–8 s only because Astra runs at default reasoning effort on a one-token referent question about 16 grocery categories. A specialist's first question: does Astra at minimal effort, or a fast frontier tier, answer in about 1 s with nearly the same accuracy? If yes, the contested regime vanishes. Speculative Actions [2510.04371] found the best speculator is the same model at low effort. The proposal never measures this latency–accuracy frontier.
- [major] The geometry may leave few contested trials. DOM reach is 0.75 m and objects move at 0.15–0.75 m/s, with `prob_static` 0.5. A Franka-class end-effector crosses the reach disk in about 1 s. So whether an object is catchable at reveal is decided mostly by whether it is still inside the reach disk, which no arm motion changes. Arm position matters only for objects about to leave reach within roughly one arm-travel time after reveal. The authors already expect too few contested trials at default speeds and fall back to a 1/3-speed band and a 10 s + D horizon. Both are reasonable patches, but they make the headline long-latency claim rest on a non-standard condition. The proposal states no v_max for the demonstrator, and that number decides whether G2 passes.
- [major] "Modeling the oracle, not the truth" is oversold. One temperature per family cannot change the speculator's ranking; it only sharpens or flattens the belief. And G1 requires the brain to be ≥15 points more accurate than the speculator, so brain answers ≈ ground truth on gated families. Calibrating to the brain and calibrating to ground truth will then give nearly the same temperature, and the ablation will tie. So the "beyond AESOP" ingredient is in practice "softmax of a 7B VLM's logits", which is a standard belief source.
- [major] The CVPR fit is weak. There is no visual learning, perception runs on simulator ground-truth tracks, and the executor is a scripted state machine. The only vision component is off-the-shelf SoM referent resolution. The authors say themselves that this better suits RA-L, ICRA or CoRL. Embodied-track reviewers at CVPR will ask what the vision contribution is.
- [major] The latency injection breaks a real correlation. Jitter is resampled independently of the trial. Reasoning-model latency plausibly grows with instruction difficulty, which is exactly when the speculator is unsure and trials are contested. Long calls on hard trials would hurt every arm differently from what i.i.d. jitter predicts. The 200 live calls should be used to measure latency conditioned on the speculator's entropy, at least.
- [minor] Whether a trial is contested is defined through the speculator's own belief (p ≥ 0.05). Reporting gains on contested trials therefore conditions on the method's model. It is pre-registered and applies to all arms, but a belief-free definition, such as all scene objects or the brain's answer distribution under resampling, should be reported alongside.
- [minor] Engineering risk is real for a team new to robotics. DOM's multi-object test specifications are not in the released repo and must be regenerated. The DOM-Sem generator, attribute sheet and certifier are all due in days 1–4. A slip removes the slow-band and ablation runs first, which are the runs that carry P5.
- [minor] The greedy waypoint argmax over about 30 grid points is never compared against a non-greedy tree or MPC hedge (Think Fast, DDTO). A gain over HO that disappears against a proper planner would not be attributable to the reveal-time value.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2407.08735] AESOP: branches over a slow LLM's possible answers, with K set from measured latency. This is the closest prior work. It does not kill novelty, but it reduces the contribution to weighting plus moving targets, as the authors say.
- [2610.17003] DDTO with moving candidates (Oct 2026, RA-L submission): shared-prefix branches to several moving candidates, separating as late as feasible, with fallback toward a retained candidate. Applied with uniform weighting, it is essentially the proposal's uniform-hedge baseline. It does not kill novelty, because identity there comes from sensor queries rather than a slow VLM. But it means the full moving-candidate hedge is concurrent prior art, so only the weighting delta remains.
- [1503.07619] Hindsight Optimization: the belief-weighted QMDP hedge. The proposal is HO with a VLM belief and a reveal-time value. It does not kill novelty, but P5 is the only thing separating the two.
- [2512.01108] Think Fast: a QMDP interception value over an action tree. It beats most-likely-goal 74% vs 63%, which shows that hedging interception under a belief is known. The uncertainty there is over one target's state rather than identity. It does not kill novelty, but it sets the expectation for a planner-quality baseline.
- [2607.05482] TypeGo: a fast model's first physical action during slow LLM planning, preempted otherwise (about 60% match). It is a baseline here and does not kill novelty.
- [2510.04371] Speculative Actions: a top-k speculator for a slow actor, lossless only for reversible effects. The proposal's physical, feasibility-preserving variant is a genuine extension, and the paper also implies a missing baseline (same model at low effort). It does not kill novelty.
- [2605.22154] IdleSpec (May 2026): uses idle time to draft contingency plans conditioned on the pending observation, with Thompson-sampled strategy weights. It is digital-only and nothing is executed, so it does not kill novelty. It shows the "use waiting time for weighted contingencies" framing is already in the agent literature.
- [2610.27677] CovNav (Oct 2026): temperature-scaled small-model logits plus conformal prediction, escalating to the slow reasoner only when unsure. It is a different decision (whether to call the slow model, not how to move during the call) and does not kill novelty. It is the template for a cascade baseline the proposal lacks.
- [2609.31112] DualManip: semantic VLM path plus fast geometric adaptation for dynamic scenes, with measured 6.4 s GPT replanning. It is reactive after the decision and does not kill novelty.
- [2606.20458], [2409.01652]: repair where a stale decision lands, after the fact. They do not kill novelty.

## Missing baselines or experiments
1. **Latency–accuracy frontier of the brain**: Astra at minimal or low reasoning effort, plus one fast frontier tier, with accuracy and latency on DOM-Sem. The hedge's gain should be reported over the best point on this frontier, not over default-effort Astra with brain-wait.
2. **Confidence-gated cascade** (CovNav-style [2610.27677]): commit to the speculator's choice when its conformal set is a singleton, and call the brain or hedge only otherwise. It is cheaper and matches the constraint of needing the frontier model less. It is the natural competitor to "always call the brain and hedge".
3. **Oracle-answer-at-t0 arm**: the brain's answer is known at t0 but the real-time world still runs. This is the perfect-speculator bound, and it separates "hedging quality" from the unavoidable loss of objects that leave reach.
4. **Non-greedy hedge planner**: DDTO-style shared-prefix optimisation or a Think-Fast-style tree with the same belief, to show the reveal-time value survives a better planner.
5. **Latency conditioned on difficulty**: use the live-call subset to measure latency against speculator entropy, and rerun the main cell with conditional rather than i.i.d. jitter.
6. **Power analysis**: report paired tests with confidence intervals on the contested subset, and either raise trials on gated cells or pre-register the minimum detectable effect.
7. **Arm-speed sensitivity**: sweep the demonstrator's v_max to show where contested trials exist. This is the key confounder of G2.

## Questions for the authors
- What end-effector speed does DOM's Warp pick state machine actually achieve? Given it, what fraction of default-speed trials at 4 s have any object whose catchability depends on the arm's in-call position?
- What are Astra's latency and referent accuracy at minimal reasoning effort on a single-token mark-ID answer? If it answers in 1 s at the same accuracy, what is left of the paper?
- On gated families, how different are the brain-calibrated and GT-calibrated temperatures? If the brain is ≥15 points better than the speculator, why expect calibrating to the brain to matter?
- How many contested trials does the 4 s cell hold under the plan, and what minimum detectable difference does that give with paired tests?
- Why fix the brain query at motion onset? A query on a pre-motion frame, or a second query at t0 + 1 s, would change the problem. Is the single onset query a benchmark constraint or a design choice?
- What does the in-call policy do when every candidate is already uncatchable? Does it ever stay home?

## Rating
- rating: 2
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.3
- one_point_fix: Add the brain's latency–accuracy frontier (Astra at minimal effort plus a confidence-gated speculator cascade) as baselines, and commit the belief-weighting effect over the best of them rather than over default-effort brain-wait and the uniform hedge.
