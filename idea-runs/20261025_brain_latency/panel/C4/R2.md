# R2 review (generalist): "Hedging on What the Slow Brain Will Say"

## Summary
The paper studies a frozen frontier VLM (GPT-6 Astra) that takes seconds to say which of several rolling objects the robot should pick. During that call it moves the arm to the waypoint that maximises the belief-weighted chance that whichever object the brain names is still catchable when the answer arrives. The belief comes from Qwen2.5-VL-7B, temperature-calibrated to predict Astra's answers, and catchability is averaged over Astra's measured latency distribution. Assuming every prediction lands exactly at threshold, the evidence would be: a pilot gate showing that Astra beats Qwen-7B by at least 15 points on at least one commonsense-referent family of a new "DOM-Sem" condition; at 4 s latency, recovery of 50% of the paused-minus-wait gap; +8 points over a uniform hedge on contested trials and +3 points overall; +5 points over next-step Hindsight Optimization on contested trials in the slow speed band at 8 s; and ties on the negative controls.

## Strengths
- Real-time (non-paused) evaluation of frontier-brain manipulation is a real blind spot. The proposal is right that recent frontier-as-controller systems pause the world or let the executor idle (GPT-GEAR, CALL, RT-Safe). Saying so clearly is useful to the community.
- The authors are unusually honest. The limitations section says the method is a combination of known parts (AESOP, HO, LookOut, DDTO), and that the result fits RA-L or CoRL better. The pilot gate can kill the paper before the main runs.
- The experiments are well controlled. The executor is DOM's released demonstrator, unchanged, so differences come only from the in-call phase. Answers are cached and replayed across latencies, which is valid because the brain sees only the t0 frame. SR, MP and CR serve as negative controls. The ablations isolate each ingredient (uniform belief vs next-step value vs full latency posterior).
- One idea is crisp and transferable: calibrate the small model to the big model's answers, mistakes included, rather than to ground truth. That is the right target when the executor may not override the brain, and the calibrated-to-ground-truth ablation tests it directly.
- Cheap and feasible on paper: no training, about $410 of API spend and about 15 GPU-days, all inside the constraints.

## Weaknesses
- [fatal] **The insight is "don't idle; keep options open, weighted by a guess". At threshold, the evidence for the novel part does not reach a CVPR bar.** Most of the recoverable gap (P − W) will be captured by any reasonable hedge, including the uniform hedge, which is itself an AESOP/DDTO transfer. The parts specific to this paper are (a) the speculator weighting and (b) the reveal-time horizon. Their committed margins at threshold are +3 points overall and +8 on contested trials over the uniform hedge, and +5 over next-step HO on contested trials at 8 s in the slow band only. Contested trials are gated at a fraction ≥ 0.15, so a 200-trial condition can hold as few as about 30 contested trials. At that size, an 8-point gap has a standard error of roughly 8–12 points, and a 3-point overall gap on 200 trials is within one standard error even with pairing. A reviewer who has seen many latency papers this year will read the result as "a hedge helps, the weighting is in the noise". The authors' own kill condition then applies: "the result reduces to 'do not idle', an AESOP/DDTO transfer."
- [fatal] **Poor fit for CVPR's audience.** No vision, perception or learning component is new. The brain, speculator and executor are frozen, the motion predictor is constant velocity, the certifier is a scripted reachability test and the executor is a scripted state machine. Vision enters only through off-the-shelf SoM referent resolution. The benchmark condition (DOM-Sem) was built so that a frontier brain is needed. Sim-only, with simulator-state tracks. A CVPR embodied-track AC will see a planning-under-uncertainty paper with a narrow delta, which is how the authors describe it too.
- [major] **The problem setup is contrived, and it is load-bearing.** Latency hurts only because the instruction arrives at motion onset and the brain is called on the t0 frame. The referent here is categorical ("the one you need a bottle opener for") and does not change as the objects roll, so in most realistic deployments it could be resolved before motion, or the moment the objects are seen. The privileged-label script solves the task, so the brain is needed only because the track table is deliberately class-agnostic. A fast pipeline would plausibly shrink D from 4 s to well under 1 s, where the paper predicts ties: an open-vocabulary classifier or per-crop Qwen labelling of the 16 categories, followed by text-only commonsense reasoning (by Qwen, or by Astra at low effort on a few tokens). The motivating example would need conveyor-style continuous arrivals, where per-item decisions really overlap motion, to be convincing.
- [major] **The central gate is a coin flip and could close the paper on day 7.** G1 needs Astra ≥ 15 points above Qwen-7B on household commonsense over 16 rendered categories, with Qwen's top-1 agreement ≤ 0.8. The cited evidence for a large frontier gap ([2508.14080]) is multi-hop person-entity knowledge, not "which object needs a bottle opener". On simple household commonsense, a 7B VLM is likely close to the frontier, and the cases it gets wrong are more likely recognition errors in small 480x360 texture-augmented renders, which hurt both models.
- [major] **Contested trials may be rare where the claims are made.** At DOM's default speeds (0.15–0.75 m/s), objects travel 0.6–3 m during a 4 s call against a 0.75 m reach. Many trials will be either lost for every arm or trivially hedgeable, which leaves few contested trials. The 8 s claim already depends on DOM's commented slow band and an extended 10 s + D horizon, which are evaluation choices made for the method.
- [major] **Engineering risk under the deadline.** The team is new to robotics, and everything must be built and debugged in 7 days before the gate: a real-time Isaac/Warp evaluator integration, a DOM-Sem generator with four-object regeneration, an asynchronous brain client, a certifier and a waypoint controller layered on DOM's state machine. Then roughly 75k episodes must run in about 6 days. This is feasible only if nothing breaks. Wall-clock pacing on a shared cluster also adds timing noise that could swamp 3-point effects.
- [minor] The theory paragraph claims the QMDP assumption "costs nothing" in-call. That holds only for a single reveal independent of the arm. The greedy 10 Hz waypoint argmax over about 30 points is not shown to be near-optimal, so a smarter uniform hedge with trajectory optimisation might close the gap.
- [minor] "Speculative" and "answer-branching" oversell what is a weighted pre-positioning heuristic. No branches are executed and none are committed.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2407.08735] AESOP: branches over a slow LLM's possible answers and keeps all of them feasible for the 95th-percentile measured latency. This is the same core idea. The deltas are moving targets, weighting and the latency posterior. Does not kill novelty outright, but it makes the uniform-hedge arm the true baseline and leaves only the weighting delta.
- [1503.07619] Shared Autonomy via Hindsight Optimization: belief-weighted per-goal hedge. The deltas are where the belief comes from and the reveal-time horizon (evaluated as an ablation). Partial overlap.
- [2610.17003] DDTO with moving candidates: one branch per moving candidate with a shared applied control and a fallback toward a retained candidate. Same physical hedge over moving candidates; the identity source differs (sensor queries, not a slow VLM). Strong overlap on the motion side, concurrent (Oct 2026).
- [2512.01108] Think Fast: QMDP expected-interception value under uncertainty about where one projectile will be. The interception-hedge structure overlaps; the uncertainty is over target identity here. Partial.
- [2607.05482] TypeGo: a fast model acts while a slow planner deliberates, and is preempted. This is the top-1-then-switch baseline. Does not kill novelty.
- [2510.04371] Speculative Actions: top-k speculation of a slow actor, digital only. Conceptual precedent for "model the slow model". Does not kill novelty.
- [2608.23831] ARLI, "Learning to Act While Waiting" (read this run): uses committed actions and mid-inference observations to act during sub-second VLA inference, RL-trained. The framing overlaps; the mechanism and timescale do not (no discrete semantic choice, no hedging). Does not kill novelty but should be cited.
- [2610.28019] PACT-VLA (Oct 2026, abstract only): "inference delay can be used as decision time", choosing among candidate chunks with a pre-commit observation. Adjacent framing at chunk level. Not a threat, worth citing.
- [2609.31112] DualManip: slow VLM semantic path plus fast geometric path for dynamic objects. Reactive reconciliation, not in-call hedging. Not a threat.

Net: no single paper kills novelty, but the residual is a narrow combination (weighting from a calibrated small model plus a latency-posterior horizon), as the authors themselves say.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- **A fast-pipeline baseline that removes most of the latency:** an open-vocabulary classifier or per-object Qwen labels, then text-only commonsense reasoning (by Qwen-7B, or by Astra at minimal effort over category names). Also Astra at low reasoning effort, or a smaller frontier tier. If any of these brings D under 1 s at similar accuracy, the problem goes away.
- **An oracle-belief hedge** (the brain's answer known at t0, the arm still waits for nothing). This bounds how much any weighting could add over uniform, and shows whether +3 points is near the ceiling.
- **Pre-motion resolution:** query the brain when the scene appears or the instruction is given, before motion onset. This is the deployment a practitioner would actually use.
- **A stronger uniform hedge** (trajectory optimisation, or DDTO-style shared-prefix MPC rather than a greedy waypoint grid), so the weighting is not credited with fixing a weak hedge.
- **A power analysis and paired-bootstrap CIs** for contested-trial comparisons, with the actual number of contested trials per cell stated up front. Possibly more trials per gated cell instead of the 9-arm × 4-latency grid.
- **A perception-noise variant** (an estimated rather than simulator-state track table, or detector noise), so the vision audience sees something visual at stake.
- **A conveyor or continuous-arrival setting**, where per-item decisions really overlap motion.

## Questions for the authors
1. In what realistic deployment does the instruction arrive at the same moment the candidates start moving, with a categorical referent that could not be resolved earlier?
2. What is Astra's latency and accuracy at minimal reasoning effort, or with text-only input of detector labels? If it is under 1 s, what is left of the problem?
3. How many contested trials per gated (family, latency) cell do you expect, and what minimum detectable effect does that give for the +8 and +3 point predictions?
4. Beyond "household commonsense needs a frontier model", what is your prior that Qwen-7B is ≥ 15 points worse than Astra on DOM-Sem? What will you publish if G1 fails?
5. Does the greedy waypoint policy ever do worse than brain-wait (for example, by moving toward a wrong cluster and away from the true target)? How often does that happen on uncontested trials?
6. Why CVPR rather than CoRL or RA-L, given that no component is visual?

## Rating
- rating: 2
- confidence: 4
- p_core: 0.3
- one_point_fix: Recast the setting so the latency is unavoidable and the gain is not thin. For example, use continuous arrivals or a conveyor with repeated decisions per item. Include fast-pipeline and pre-motion-resolution baselines that the method still beats. Commit a weighting effect that is statistically resolvable: enough contested trials per gated cell for +8 points to clear a paired 95% CI, and an overall gain over the uniform hedge well above 3 points.
