# R3 (experimentalist) — C4: Belief-Weighted Answer-Branching for Moving Targets under Seconds-Scale VLM Latency

## Summary
A frozen frontier VLM (GPT-6 Astra) picks which of several moving objects on DOM to grasp, which takes seconds. While it thinks, a scripted executor greedily moves the arm to the waypoint that maximises a belief-weighted chance that the brain's eventual pick is still catchable at a reveal time drawn from the measured latency posterior. The belief comes from Qwen2.5-VL-7B, temperature-calibrated against the brain's own answers. The claimed insight is that the AESOP/DDTO-style "keep every answer feasible" hedge fails for moving targets, so the branches have to be weighted by a model of the brain and valued at reveal time rather than at the next step (as Hindsight Optimization does). The predicted evidence, all at threshold, applies only to pilot-gated cells of a self-built DOM-Sem condition: the method recovers >= 50% of the paused-vs-wait gap at 4 s; it beats the uniform hedge by >= 8 pts on contested trials and >= 3 pts overall; it beats next-step HO by >= 5 pts on contested trials at 8 s in a slowed speed band; and it ties the baselines on DOM's SR/MP/CR negative controls.

## Strengths
- The experimental logic is unusually honest and well structured. A pre-registered pilot gate (G1: is the brain needed and is the speculator unsure; G2: do contested trials exist) sets the conditions under which effect sizes are committed, and kill conditions are stated for every pillar.
- Each claimed ingredient has a matched ablation, so the mechanism is isolated rather than asserted:
  - belief source: brain-calibrated vs GT-calibrated vs uncalibrated vs uniform vs top-1;
  - value horizon: next-step vs fixed p90 (AESOP-K) vs full posterior vs oracle D;
  - top-k truncation;
  - motion predictor: fitted vs privileged.
- The negative controls are well chosen. On DOM SR/MP the referents can be computed from the table, and CR has a single object. The method is predicted to tie the baselines there, which guards against the gain coming from a better approach trajectory instead of hedging.
- The executor is held fixed. DOM's released Warp pick state machine is used unchanged after the reveal, so differences between arms come only from the in-call phase. The brain's answer is cached at t0 and replayed across latencies, a legitimate variance-reduction design for comparing executors.
- The budget arithmetic is plausible: about 350 GPU-h (about 15 GPU-days) against a 40 GPU-day cap, and about 2,900 API calls at a measured per-call cost (budgeted at about $410). A third party (RAVEL [2609.34170]) has already run DOM non-paused on an RTX 4090.
- Calibrating against the brain's answers rather than ground truth is the right target for an executor that is not allowed to override the brain, and it is tested directly against GT calibration.

## Weaknesses
- [major] **The load-bearing effect is too small, at threshold, to be statistically convincing, and the proposal specifies no statistics.**
  - Main runs use 200 trials per family. With a contested fraction near G2's 0.15 gate, the +8-pt "contested" claim rests on roughly 30 contested trials per gated family. Even a paired (McNemar) test on 30 trials cannot separate 8 points from noise unless almost every discordant pair goes one way.
  - The ±3-pt "ties" (uncontested trials and the negative controls) are equivalence claims. A ±3-pt margin needs far more than 200 trials, so with n = 200 a "tie" just means the test lacked power.
  - No confidence intervals, test, number of seeds or multiplicity correction is named. Across 3 families × 4 latencies × 2 speed bands × 9+ arms there are many comparisons, and only the cells that pass the gate are reported.
- [major] **The predictions are internally inconsistent at threshold.** If the method gains 8 pts on contested trials and ties on uncontested trials, the overall gain is 8 × (contested fraction). At G2's 0.15 gate that is about 1.2 pts, not the predicted >= 3. Getting 3 pts overall needs a contested fraction of at least 0.375, which nothing in the proposal predicts. Under the instruction that every prediction lands at its threshold, the realistic headline for the novel ingredient is a 1–3 pt overall gain over the uniform hedge, which is within noise.
- [major] **No positive result is on a standard benchmark.**
  - Every committed effect is on DOM-Sem, a condition the authors build: rewritten commonsense instructions, environments regenerated at four objects, a custom latency injection, a 10 s + D horizon, and for the 8 s claim DOM's commented-out slow speed band.
  - DOM's standard sub-dimensions appear only as negative controls, where the method is predicted to tie.
  - The trial specifications for DOM's multi-object test environments are not in the released repo [2601.22153 digest], so even the "standard" SR/MP controls may be regenerated rather than DOM's official splits.
  - A skeptical reviewer will read this as a benchmark tuned until the effect exists. The gating makes it worse: effects are committed only on (family, latency, speed band) cells chosen because they show the phenomenon.
- [major] **Most of the 50%-recovery headline belongs to "do not idle", which is prior work.** The uniform hedge (an AESOP [2407.08735] / DDTO [2502.06623][2610.17003] transfer) and centroid pre-positioning will recover much of P − W on their own. The novel parts are the weighting (+3 overall, about 1.2 at a consistent threshold) and the reveal-time value (+5 contested, in the slow band at 8 s only). Both are small deltas on narrow slices. The proposal itself concedes the contribution is "a narrow systems delta better matched to RA-L, ICRA or CoRL" (Limitations).
- [major] **Little vision content for a CVPR main-track paper.**
  - Tracks are simulator ground truth (a "class-agnostic track table").
  - The executor is a privileged-state scripted demonstrator, and the motion predictor is constant velocity with friction.
  - The only visual step is a VLM picking a mark ID from one 480×360 frame.
  - No perception noise, no learned policy (DynamicVLA is explicitly not used) and no real-robot test. CVPR reviewers will ask what the computer-vision contribution is.
- [major] **Gate G1 is a coin flip, and a failure ends the paper.** The commonsense gap between Astra and Qwen2.5-VL-7B over 16 household categories may well be under 15 pts. The authors' own evidence is that the gap is large on multi-hop person knowledge [2508.14080] but small on appearance referents [2502.13923]. If the gate fails, the paper becomes a protocol-only report. If it passes narrowly, everything rests on one family.
- [major] **The baselines leave out the obvious latency-reduction competitors.**
  - A faster brain: Astra at low reasoning effort, or a fast frontier tier. Speculative Actions found the best speculator is the same model at low effort [2510.04371].
  - A confidence-gated cascade: act on the speculator's answer when it is confident, otherwise wait for or hedge on the brain. This also matches the stated goal of needing the frontier model less.
  - Without these, a reviewer cannot tell whether hedging beats simply shortening D.
- [minor] **The uniform-hedge baseline is weaker than it needs to be.** It is uniform over all scene objects, including implausible ones such as a static lemon. Part of the "weighting" gain could simply be dropping obviously wrong objects. A uniform hedge over the speculator's plausible set (p >= 0.05) would isolate whether the actual probabilities matter beyond set membership. The top-k ablation covers this only partly, because it is not run against the contested-trial metric.
- [minor] **The headroom of the weighting is never bounded.** There is no arm with a one-hot belief on the brain's actual answer ("oracle speculator"), so the reported gain cannot be read as a fraction of the achievable gain.
- [minor] **The "contested" subset is defined by the method's own speculator belief** (p >= 0.05 at p90). Both arms are evaluated on the same subset, but the subset changes with calibration choices, which complicates the belief-source ablation. Contested status should be fixed once, from a reference belief.
- [minor] **The schedule is tight for a team new to robotics.** Days 1–7 cover the certifier, the DOM-Sem generator, multi-object regeneration, both VLM clients and the pilot. Main runs take days 8–13, which leaves no slack for Isaac Sim / Warp debugging. The schedule is feasible only if nothing breaks.
- [minor] **Greedy receding-horizon waypoint argmax at 10 Hz can chatter** between basins when the belief is near uniform. There is no comparison to a short-horizon trajectory optimiser (such as a DDTO-style solve), which a robotics reviewer would expect as the "full" version of the method.

## Overlapping prior and concurrent work
- [2407.08735] AESOP: branches over a slow LLM's possible answers, all kept feasible for the p95 of measured latency. This is the same answer-branching structure. The delta is the weighting plus moving targets. It does not kill novelty, but it makes the "do not idle" portion non-novel.
- [2502.06623] DDTO: keeps several candidate targets reachable as long as possible, with priorities that are an order, not probabilities, and static targets. It covers the trunk/branch hedge itself. No kill.
- [2606.22277] AS-DDTO (L-CSS 2026): adds a belief-weighted term (sum_j pi(j) phi(d)) to DDTO's shared trunk, biasing it "toward likely candidates before branching", and prunes candidates with low belief. This is belief-weighted hedging over candidate targets, but the weighting serves information gain, the candidates are static, and identity is revealed gradually by the robot's own sensing. It weakens the claim that belief weighting of reachability branches is new. No kill, but it should be cited and contrasted.
- [2610.17003] Multi-agent DDTO for moving candidates (Oct 2026): one branch per moving candidate with a shared applied control, and a fallback toward a retained candidate. It covers the moving-candidate part, with identity coming from sensor queries rather than a slow VLM. No kill. Together with AESOP it leaves only the "brain-calibrated weights + reveal-time posterior" combination.
- [1503.07619] Hindsight Optimization: a belief-weighted per-goal hedge with a next-step reveal and static goals. The proposed value is HO with the evaluation time moved to a latency posterior. It is tested as an ablation, and that delta is only predicted to matter in the slow band at 8 s.
- [2512.01108] Think Fast: a QMDP interception hedge over one moving target's state that beats move-to-most-likely-goal (74% vs 63%). The mechanism is close but the uncertainty is over a different variable. No kill.
- [2607.05482] TypeGo and [2510.04371] Speculative Actions: speculate a slow model's answer with a fast one and act on it. Both are used as baselines or framing. No kill.
- [2601.22153] DynamicVLA / DOM: the benchmark and the executor. Its multi-object test specifications are not in the repo, which affects how "standard" the setting is.

## Missing baselines or experiments
- A faster-brain arm: Astra at low/minimal reasoning effort, or a faster frontier tier, as the brain at its measured lower latency. This answers whether to hedge or simply be faster.
- A confidence-gated cascade: act immediately on the speculator when its calibrated confidence is above a threshold, otherwise run the brain plus hedge. Report success against the number of API calls, which gives the cost/performance curve the constraints ask for.
- A uniform hedge restricted to the speculator's plausible set (p >= 0.05), and an oracle one-hot belief on the brain's answer (headroom bound).
- A DDTO / AS-DDTO-style short-horizon trajectory optimisation with belief weights, in place of greedy waypoint selection, to show that the gain does not depend on a weak greedy planner.
- Statistics:
  - paired McNemar or paired bootstrap 95% CIs on every committed comparison;
  - TOST equivalence tests for the ±3-pt ties;
  - a pre-stated multiplicity correction across gated cells.
  - Power: oversample contested trial specifications, which the offline certifier makes free to find, to at least 300 contested trials per gated family and cell.
- Robustness to state noise: add Gaussian noise or dropout to the track table, or run a learned tracker from pixels, so the result is not purely privileged-state.
- At least one DOM sub-dimension in its official form with a positive prediction. For example, MP or SR with the brain replaced by a speculator-weighted hedge when the referent is ambiguous to the script, so that one committed effect is on an unmodified benchmark split.

## Questions for the authors
1. Given the +8 contested / tie uncontested / >= 3 overall predictions, what contested fraction do you actually expect in gated cells? If it is near 0.15, why is the overall gain >= 3 and not about 1.2?
2. What statistical test, number of trials on contested subsets, and equivalence margin back each "beats" and "ties" claim? Are trials paired across arms?
3. Are DOM's official multi-object test specifications available to you? If not, how will SR/MP "standard" controls match the benchmark?
4. How much of P − W does the uniform hedge alone recover at 4 s? If it is already about 40%, what is left for the weighting?
5. What is the measured latency of Astra at low reasoning effort, and what is its referent accuracy on DOM-Sem? Would simply using it close more of the gap than hedging does?
6. Why is the uniform hedge uniform over all scene objects rather than over the plausible set?
7. Does the greedy 10 Hz argmax oscillate when the belief is near-flat? What fraction of in-call time is spent switching targets?
