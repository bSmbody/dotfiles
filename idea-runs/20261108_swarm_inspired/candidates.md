
### C3 — Replay KV: evict hard, then periodically regenerate the whole cache exactly from token ids and re-choose

- **Intuition**: Treat the KV cache as a disposable view of the token ids, not as the state: decode with a tiny resident cache, and every few thousand tokens rebuild the full, exact KV of the whole trace by a cheap layer-by-layer re-prefill, use that pass's free by-products (what the newest queries actually read over the full history, and the full-context probability of every token written since the last rebuild) to pick the next resident set and to roll back a step that went wrong, then throw the rest away again. Nothing is ever permanently deleted, and the error that eviction injects stops compounding.

- **Gap it attacks**: Gap L3-1. Current evictors rank tokens with a noisy score and delete the losers forever: once every method protects the prompt, the score barely matters (random matches SnapKV/R-KV) and a fact stated once and needed 57 compression rounds later is retrieved 0% by random eviction [2609.03430]; accuracy tracks the permanently deleted fraction, collapsing from 22% to 0% between 70% and 50% budget [2605.09490]. The only reversible fixes (DDR tiers [2605.09490], WakeKV's CPU reservoir [2610.02713]) keep the full KV somewhere, so total memory is not reduced, and they cannot repair a second, hidden error: every token generated while the cache was sparse got its own deeper-layer K/V computed from a corrupted context, and later steps read that corrupted K/V. Closing the gap would let a long-CoT request run at a small fraction of its KV footprint (several times larger batch at memory saturation) without the cliff.

- **Source inspiration**: Two out-of-domain mechanisms. (1) Residual replacement in pipelined Conjugate Gradient [1601.07068] (numerical linear algebra): pipelined CG swaps an expensive explicit quantity (the true residual b - Ax) for cheap incremental recurrences; rounding errors make the recursive residual drift from the true one, and the fix is to recompute it explicitly from ground truth at a few chosen iterations, triggered when a cheap running estimate of the gap crosses a tolerance. Replacing too often hurts, so it fires only on a crossing; 39 replacements in 1474 iterations keep most of the parallel speedup (3.4x vs 3.8x) while restoring attainable accuracy [1601.07068, Sections 2.4, 3, 4.3]. (2) Dynamic Tensor Rematerialization [2006.09616] (ML training systems): a tensor evicted under a memory budget is regenerated exactly by replaying the pure, deterministic operator that produced it; DTR's key lesson is that the real price of regenerating one tensor is its whole chain of already-evicted ancestors, and that only "banished" data with no producer cannot come back [2006.09616, Section 2]. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts.

- **How it maps to this problem**: Token ids play the role of the ground truth (b, A, x) and of DTR's constants: they cost 4 bytes per token against roughly 147 KB of BF16 KV per token for an 8B GQA model, so they are never evicted. The resident sparse cache plays the role of the cheap recursive residual: fast to use, but drifting, both because entries are missing and because K/V written during sparse decoding were computed from a wrong context. A full re-prefill from token ids is the "explicit recomputation" that resets the drift to zero. DTR's chain-cost lesson says why this must be a bulk event, not a per-entry fetch: the evicted neighbourhood of any single K/V entry is the whole prefix at all shallower layers, so regenerating one entry costs as much as regenerating all of them; therefore pay once, get everything, and re-choose. What runs on the GPU: the normal decode batch runs with a small per-request, per-head paged budget. A background "replay lane" on the same GPU, scheduled into the tensor-core time that memory-bound decode leaves idle (as chunked prefill is today), takes one request at a time and pushes its whole trace through the model layer by layer: compute layer l's K/V for every position, run the usual fused attention pass, keep only the selected (head, position) entries in that request's pages, drop the rest of layer l, move to layer l+1. Peak extra memory is one layer's full K/V plus the trace's hidden states (hundreds of MB at 32K, against several GB for the full cache). Three by-products fall out of that pass for free: (a) the attention mass that the last few hundred generated queries put on every position of the uncorrupted history, read inside the same attention kernel, which is a hindsight selection signal over the full past rather than over survivors; (b) since every refresh draws over the whole history, a once-stated fact gets a fresh chance every round instead of a survival probability that decays geometrically round after round, which is what killed random eviction's passcode retrieval; (c) the full-context next-token probabilities of all tokens written since the last refresh, which, compared with the probabilities logged during sparse decoding, give the exact "residual gap" for this request: where they disagree sharply (for example, the model copied a variable value it could no longer see), truncate the trace back to that position and resume, which costs nothing extra because the refresh already produced exact KV for every prefix; how large the gap was sets when this request is refreshed next, per the CG trigger rule.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**: The mechanism needs three conditions, each supported by the literature. (i) Exact replay: K/V is a pure deterministic function of the token ids, the same purity DTR relies on [2006.09616, Section 2], and problem.md notes 16K tokens re-prefill in under 1 s on H200 for an 8B model, while decoding those tokens took minutes; decode at long context stays memory-bound even at large batch [2408.11049], so compute for replay is the slack resource. (ii) Refreshes can be rare: residual replacement works with a few dozen explicit recomputations per thousand-plus iterations as long as each fires before the gap grows large [1601.07068, Sections 3, 4.3]; on reasoning traces the text and the heads are redundant within a window, so a sparse random or hindsight subset carries the local work, and what fails is the once-stated fact needed much later [2609.03430, Sections 5.2-5.3], exactly what a periodic draw over the full history restores; the harm is permanent deletion, not low residency [2605.09490]. (iii) The by-product signals are informative: heads change what they read over time, so a fresh read of where current queries attend beats a one-time or cumulative score [2610.02713]. Key assumption that must hold: between two refreshes a few thousand tokens apart, sparse decoding rarely commits an error that rollback cannot catch, so the refresh interval can stay long enough that prefill demand fits inside the compute decode leaves idle.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: Closest is MagicDec [2408.11049], which drafts with the same model over a compressed KV and verifies against a full KV kept resident (96-token outputs); offload hierarchies [2605.09490, 2610.02713] keep full KV in CPU memory. Here the full KV exists nowhere: the backing store is token ids plus spare compute, replay also repairs the K/V of tokens written during sparse decoding, and verification is a coarse per-refresh rollback over thousands of tokens, not per-step acceptance. (not novelty-checked)

- **Main risk**: Replay cost grows with trace length (quadratic attention at 40K-60K tokens means several seconds per refresh), so if accuracy needs refreshes every few hundred tokens rather than every few thousand, prefill demand exceeds the idle compute and eats the batch-size gain; rollback can also misfire on legitimately low-probability tokens sampled at temperature 0.6.


### C5 — Verlet-skin decode attention: certified page lists that expire, not re-selected every step

- **Intuition**: During long-CoT decoding the query of a head drifts slowly, and the exact logits of a full attention pass are already computed and then thrown away. Keep them: after one full pass, give every KV page a certified "skin" (how far the query may drift before that page could matter) and, like a molecular-dynamics neighbor list, attend only to the listed pages until the measured query drift uses up some page's skin.

- **Gap it attacks**: Gap L5-1. Today's decode-time sparse attention re-decides the read set from scratch at every one of 10K-60K steps. It either scans per-page metadata for the whole context each step (Quest's page bounds [2406.10774]; DSA-style indexers, whose per-token full-context pass becomes the decode bottleneck [2603.12201]) or trains a gate (SeerAttention-R [2506.08889]). The choice is heuristic, and its errors compound. Training-free selectors stretch reasoning traces (Quest at a 2K budget pushes Qwen3-8B AIME24 from 15.1K to 30.0K tokens and from 74.5% to 13.3% [2506.08889]; "less is less" [2601.03043]). Cheap signals for when a head starts reading new positions reach only 0.08-0.20 precision [2610.02713]. Closing the gap would give a per-step read set that is (a) cheaper than any per-step scan, because most steps touch no information about unread pages, and (b) safe: each step carries a deterministic bound on the attention mass it skipped. Cost per correct answer then drops without the length inflation that wipes out sparse-attention gains.

- **Source inspiration**: Three mechanisms, all from outside ML for LLMs (novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts).
  - (1) Verlet neighbor lists in molecular dynamics [physics/0311055]. Each atom keeps a list of neighbors within cutoff plus a "skin". The list stays correct until some atom has moved far enough to cross the skin, so force evaluation reads only listed pairs. The list is rebuilt either on displacement or, with per-cell dirty flags, only where atoms moved, so the skin can stay thin without frequent global rebuilds [physics/0311055, Sections 2.1, 2.3.2].
  - (2) Sequential safe screening for the Lasso [1211.3966]. Once you have the exact solution at one parameter, the solution at a nearby parameter lies in a ball around it. A feature's largest possible score over that ball is its old score plus the ball radius times the feature's norm, so features whose bound stays below threshold are certified inactive with one comparison each, using only by-products of the previous solve [1211.3966, Section 2.2, Thm 3, Cor 5].
  - (3) Look-ahead screening [2105.05648]. It turns the same test around: it computes, per feature, the whole range of future parameters over which the feature is guaranteed inactive, so it is screened for many steps at once at "diminutive" cost because all quantities are by-products [2105.05648, Section 2].
  - These work there because the expensive object (atom positions, the design matrix) is fixed or moves slowly and the moving part's displacement is cheap to measure. Both conditions hold for keys and queries during decoding.

- **How it maps to this problem**:
  - Roles: the KV cache's keys are the fixed "features/particles". The current RoPE-rotated query of a KV head (taking the worst case over its GQA query heads) is the moving point. A KV page is one item. The "cutoff" is a logit margin below the current maximum, below which a page's softmax weight is negligible.
  - **Rebuild pass** (occasional, per head). It is the ordinary dense flash-decoding pass, plus one extra write: each page's exact max logit against this reference query, a by-product the kernel already computes and discards. Together with per-page max key norms stored once at write time (split into a few RoPE frequency bands), this gives every page an expiry radius: the amount of query drift after which it could rise to within the margin. Pages already within the margin, plus sinks and the recent window, form the neighbor list. All other pages are kept in a short array sorted by expiry radius.
  - **Ordinary step**. The GPU (i) computes the head's query drift from the reference, per band, including the RoPE rotation accrued since the reference, which is a few hundred FLOPs. (ii) It binary-searches the sorted radii to find the few pages whose certificate has just expired; these get read and re-anchored, the dirty-flag partial update. (iii) It runs a paged sparse decode over listed pages, newly expired pages and newly written tokens. The online-softmax running max and normalizer, again free by-products, turn the unexpired pages' bounds into a certificate of skipped attention mass. A full rebuild for that head fires only when this certificate exceeds a tolerance.
  - **Extreme scale**. Rebuilds are per (layer, KV head) and naturally staggered. With 256 concurrent long-CoT requests at memory saturation, the dense-read cost is spread over hundreds of steps per head instead of being paid every step.
  - **Free by-products**. The sorted expiry radii double as a time-to-need schedule for prefetching offloaded pages, so unlisted pages can sit in host memory. A head's rebuild rate is a free "the reasoning just changed topic" signal that other mechanisms, such as precision control (L1), could consume.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**:
  - **Soundness.** The bound is safe by construction. Keys never change after being written, and the query drift is measured exactly, not predicted. So the DPP "center value plus radius times norm" test [1211.3966, Thm 3] applies directly, without even needing a nonexpansiveness argument.
  - **Tightness** is the open question, and two read facts support it.
    - RoPE rotation, the part of query drift that happens every step regardless of content, mostly hits the high-frequency chunks. Trained models push key norm in those chunks toward zero except in a few positional heads, mostly in the first and last layers, and put most key/query norm in slowly rotating low frequencies [2410.06205, Sections 4-5]. Bounding drift per frequency band therefore makes position-induced drift nearly free for most heads, and positional heads are served by the always-listed recent window.
    - The Verlet and look-ahead experience shows the regime where this pays off: when most items are far from the cutoff, a thin skin with targeted partial updates screens out more than 99% of items for many steps [physics/0311055, Section 2.3.2; 2105.05648, Section 2].
  - **Engineering.** The kernel work is modest: a dense pass that also writes per-page maxima, a per-head sorted array, and an existing paged sparse decode kernel. It fits a vLLM/SGLang attention backend.
  - **Key assumption.** Within one reasoning step the low-frequency part of each head's query stays close enough to the reference that its drift, multiplied by page key norms, stays well below the logit gap between listed and unlisted pages for tens to hundreds of steps.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work** (not novelty-checked):
  - Quest [2406.10774] also uses a page upper bound, but recomputes it every step from per-page key min/max against the new query, scanning all page metadata. C5's bound is anchored on a past exact logit plus measured query drift, so between expiries no metadata for unread pages is touched, and it yields a skipped-mass certificate.
  - vAttention [2510.05688] gives probabilistic per-step guarantees by sampling.
  - IndexCache [2603.12201] reuses top-k across layers without any certificate.
  - WakeKV [2610.02713] reacts to misses rather than certifying no-miss.

- **Main risk**: The Cauchy-Schwarz-style bound may be too loose. Real query drift is spread across many dimensions while the bound assumes worst-case alignment with each page's largest key. If so, certificates expire almost every step, and the mechanism degenerates into a per-step scan like Quest with extra bookkeeping. The fix (per-band or key-principal-subspace norms) might not tighten it enough on reasoning heads that genuinely shift focus often [2610.02713].


### C2 — Coalescent Swarm: resampled long-CoT branches whose path degeneracy becomes free KV sharing, with precision set by page refcount
- **Intuition**: Run hundreds of reasoning branches for one problem as a particle filter: at each fork point, branches that are looping or unconfident are killed and their slots are refilled by copy-on-write forks of surviving branches. In statistics this resampling makes lineages merge into one shared "trunk" plus a short "crown" of recent branches, a known defect called path degeneracy. Here it is the mechanism: the swarm's KV becomes one high-precision trunk that every branch reads at once, plus short private 2-bit tips. Memory then grows like trunk length plus a small crown, not N x trace length.
- **Gap it attacks**: Gap L2-1. Today, N parallel branches cost N full KV caches. A branch that fails cannot take over another branch's partial progress. A branch that runs away keeps its whole budget, and at 2 bits runaways rise from 11% to 42% of requests on Qwen3-8B AIME25 [2609.38121]. By my back-of-envelope count, one 32K-token BF16 Qwen3-8B trace needs about 4.7 GB of KV, so an H200 holds only about 25 independent branches. If memory followed trunk plus crown, the same GPU could hold hundreds of branches per problem, and each extra branch would cost little attention bandwidth. That raises the main test-time-scaling lever, branches per GPU-second, by an order of magnitude.
- **Source inspiration**: Computational statistics and population genetics, combined with an inference-systems primitive. (recombination-of-distant-concepts)
  - Jacob, Murray and Rubenthaler [1307.3180] show why the trunk forms. Resampling makes particle paths coalesce, so the ancestry tree of N particles over T steps is one trunk plus a crown whose size does not depend on T. The expected stored size is bounded by T + C·N·log N, not T x N. Their storage scheme inserts each new generation into freed slots and prunes nodes with no living descendant using offspring counts, which is reference-counted copy-on-write.
  - Koskela et al. [1804.01811] show what controls the merging. The pair-merger rate per generation equals the inverse effective sample size of the weights, so how unequal the weights are sets how fast lineages merge. The genealogy follows Kingman's coalescent and can be predicted before the run.
  - Hydragen [2402.05099] is the changed cost profile. With tree-shaped prefix sharing, the queries of all descendants attend to a shared node's KV in one matrix-matrix pass, so shared KV is read once instead of N times. The gain is largest exactly when shared prefixes are long and private suffixes are short. Paged attention alone removes redundant storage, but not redundant reads.
- **How it maps to this problem**:
  - Roles in the filter:
    - A particle is one reasoning branch: its token ids plus its paged KV blocks.
    - A generation is a chunk of decoding that ends at a high-entropy fork token such as "wait" or "however", where reasoning directions actually split [2506.01939], or after a fixed token count.
    - A particle's weight comes from by-products the decoder already produces. A loop or n-gram-repetition detector gives zero weight, so runaways are culled; loop rate tracks 2-bit accuracy loss with r = -0.85 [2606.02011]. The chunk's token confidence sets the remaining weight, and a PRM can be added optionally [2608.15797].
  - Resampling is pure block-table surgery. Offspring point at the parent's KV pages, which raises their reference counts, and dead branches' private pages are freed. Paged-KV reference counts play exactly the role of the offspring counts in the storage algorithm of [1307.3180], so no KV is copied.
  - Diversity protection: so that selection does not pour every slot into one mode, survivors are drawn with SwarmEvolve's free diversity filter, farthest-point selection on each branch's mean hidden state over the chunk [2611.08483].
  - The memory dial is selection pressure. Because inverse ESS is the merger rate [1804.01811], the scheduler sets the sharpness of the weights (a resampling temperature) to hit a target KV footprint for the crown.
  - Precision follows genealogy, using a second free by-product, the page reference count.
    - A freshly written private page has count 1 and is likely to die, so it is stored at 2 bits.
    - When a chunk becomes ancestral to many live branches, it is re-prefilled from its token ids at FP8. The recompute is exact, attends only to its already-FP8 ancestors, and is a short compute-bound batched prefill whose cost is shared by all descendants. Cheap exact recompute from token ids is the same primitive ArborKV uses for rehydration [2605.22106].
    - The trunk is read once per step for all branches by tree-shaped Hydragen attention.
    - This replaces the seed's "precision by role" with "precision by how many live branches depend on this page".
  - What runs on the GPU:
    - One decode batch of N = 128-512 branch queries per problem.
    - The attention kernel makes one matrix-matrix pass of all N queries over the FP8 trunk pages, then per-node passes over the small crown tree, then each branch's own 2-bit tip.
    - At each chunk boundary, a tiny step computes weights from logits and hidden states already in memory, picks offspring and rewrites block tables. It also queues a short FP8 re-prefill for chunks whose reference count just crossed the promotion threshold.
  - Final answer: the vote is weighted by genealogy. The ancestry tree is a free record of which answers are correlated, so answers from branches that share a recent ancestor count as fewer independent votes.
- **Why it could work / feasibility**:
  - Coalescence is guaranteed when weights vary [1307.3180, 1804.01811], and the crown size can be predicted from the ESS [1804.01811]. If weights are uniform, nothing merges and the method falls back to ordinary independent sampling, so the downside is bounded.
  - Shared-prefix tree attention gives exactly the trunk-and-crown shape the most benefit, and tree sharing is already supported [2402.05099].
  - Runaway and degenerate traces are detectable online with cheap entropy, gzip and n-gram signals [2606.02011, 2608.15797], so a 2-bit tip that starts looping is culled before it eats its budget.
  - 2-bit damage comes from a long low-precision context. Here each branch's context is mostly the FP8 trunk with only a short 2-bit tip; OSCAR already keeps sinks and a recent window in BF16 for similar reasons [2609.38121].
  - Implementation reuses existing vLLM and SGLang parts: paged blocks with reference counts, forked parallel sampling and prefix caching. Tree-shared attention exists as a kernel family [2402.05099].
  - Key assumption: chunk-level self-signals rank partial long-CoT traces well enough that selecting on them does not cost more accuracy, through lost diversity and correlated votes, than the extra branches and culled runaways gain.
- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: (not novelty-checked)
  - SwarmEvolve [2611.08483] shares only a parent program's KV among short 40-step latent agents, which stay independent. It has no resampling of long decoded chains, no trunk-plus-crown memory law, and no precision set by sharing.
  - ArborKV [2605.22106] manages a tree that a search controller sets, by evicting and rehydrating. It does not use selection-driven coalescence or per-page precision.
  - KV-Rescue [2608.15797] does PRM-guided stepwise best-of-N with pruning of degenerate candidates, but makes no KV-sharing or precision argument.
  - Multiverse [2506.09991] merges branches without reducing total KV.
  - Particle-filter or SMC test-time scaling for LLMs exists outside papers/ and has not been checked.
- **Main risk**: Coalescence can collapse diversity. If confidence-style weights are miscalibrated on long CoT, selection funnels every branch into one confident but wrong trunk, and the genealogy-weighted vote ends up worse than independent sampling despite having more branches.


### C4 — State patches: summarise each branch segment of a hybrid model as a fixed-size "patch" on its recurrent memory, and merge branches like a version-control three-way merge

**Intuition**
In a Gated-DeltaNet layer, everything a stretch of tokens does to the recurrent memory is one fixed-size object, a "patch": how it transforms the old memory (a key-by-key matrix per head) plus what it writes (a matrix the size of the state). This holds whether the stretch has 40 tokens or 4,000. If branches exchange patches, a hybrid model can fork, merge and rebase reasoning branches much like git does with code. Two patches can be applied in either order when they commute, and when they do not, the size of their commutator is a free signal that the two branches disagree.

**Gap it attacks**
Gap L4-1. Transformer tricks that fork, concatenate or share KV (SwarmEvolve's latent agents and KV-concatenation crossover [2611.08483], branch merging, prefix sharing with edits) do not carry over to GDN/Mamba hybrids. A recurrent state is a fixed-size summary of everything before it, so there is no per-branch piece to concatenate. Today a hybrid has two options: give each parallel branch its own copy of the state and never let branches exchange information before the final vote, or re-prefill another branch's text through the whole model. Closing this gap would let hybrids (Qwen3.5/Qwen3-Next), whose states are small and constant in length, run multi-branch reasoning that exchanges information partway through, without re-prefilling.

**Source inspiration**
Two sources outside ML (novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts).
(1) Patch theory of version control [1311.3903]. A version-control system never merges file *contents* directly. It merges the *patches* two users applied to a common ancestor, by building the smallest result that contains both changes, so the ancestor counts once and each change applies once. This is well defined, and does not depend on order, exactly when the patches touch disjoint places. Patches that edit the same line conflict. Instead of failing, the system represents the conflicting state explicitly and resolves it later.
(2) Trotter error theory from quantum simulation [1912.08854]. Applying several operator evolutions one after another instead of jointly costs an error that scales with the nested commutators of the pieces, not with their size. When the pieces nearly commute, sequential application is almost exact whatever the order, and the commutator expression is cheap to evaluate.
Together they give a merge rule, an order-independence condition, and a cheap conflict detector for any algebra of operators.

**How it maps to this problem**
- The file is a branch's GDN state per layer and head. A patch is one segment of a branch's tokens: for each GDN head, (i) the segment's transition, a d_k x d_k matrix that is the product of its per-token decays and rank-1 erasures, and (ii) its write term, a d_v x d_k matrix. The GDN update is affine in the previous state, its coefficients depend only on the segment's keys, gates and betas [2406.06484], and the transition acts only on the key index [2608.30310]. So the patch reproduces that layer's replay of the segment exactly, from any starting state. It is folded together by the same chunked WY kernel that prefill already runs, applied once at segment end to the token-level factors that DeltaLog/Bole-style decoders already keep for recent tokens [2608.15533, 2608.01651]. Its size does not depend on segment length: about two states per segment, which is smaller than token-level factors beyond about d_k = 128 tokens.
- Fork is free: copy the state, and share the attention layers' prefix KV pages by reference.
- Rebase: apply a branch's patch to a different base state, for example to move a branch onto an updated trunk without re-prefilling its text.
- Three-way merge (the "pushout"): starting from the common ancestor state, apply every merged branch's erasures and writes once, and apply the shared per-head decay once rather than N times. Naively chaining branches one after another would erase and decay the ancestor once per branch.
- Conflict signal: per head, the commutator of two branches' transitions (equivalently, how much their write footprints overlap in key space) is one small GEMM. A near-zero commutator means the branches wrote to different memory directions, so the merge is order-free and lossless. A large one means both branches overwrote the same memory slots with different content. That is a merge conflict, and also a free disagreement/diversity measure across branches, the hybrid analogue of SwarmEvolve's hidden-state novelty filter. Conflicts are resolved per head by a policy: the branch the verifier or vote prefers wins, or the conflict is left unresolved by keeping both branches alive.
- The few full-attention layers do what SwarmEvolve does: merged branches' KV pages are appended by reference, with RoPE re-rotation.
- What runs on the GPU: N branches decode as an ordinary batch. Every segment boundary, for example every 512-2K tokens, one chunked kernel turns each branch's recent factors into a patch. A batched GEMM computes all pairwise commutator norms (N x N per head, tiny). The scheduler then merges chosen groups into a trunk with a tree of GEMMs, which works because patch composition is associative, or prunes redundant branches and re-forks from the merged trunk.
- Uses: (a) fork, explore, merge long-CoT on Qwen3.5/Qwen3-Next, where branches pool verified partial progress mid-flight instead of only at the final vote; (b) SwarmEvolve on hybrids, where hundreds of latent agents' 40-step thoughts become patches and a summariser absorbs all of them. Its GDN memory stays constant however many agents it reads, whereas transformer KV grows with agents x steps [2611.08483].

**Why it could work / feasibility**
- The patch is exact per layer, given each token's keys, values and gates as they were computed in the branch's own context. That is the same approximation SwarmEvolve makes when it concatenates agent KVs computed in their own contexts, and there latent passing matched or beat text passing (0.804 vs 0.796/0.767) [2611.08483].
- Hybrids tolerate approximate states. Rebuilding GDN states from a short replay keeps 92.8-98.9% of LongBench/RULER quality [2608.30310], and retrieval mostly lives in the attention KV, which this mechanism shares exactly [2608.30386].
- Composing and comparing patches are dense GEMMs with high arithmetic intensity, unlike decode-time state updates, which run at about 0.5-1 FLOP/byte against about 208 FLOPs/byte machine balance on H200 [2608.15533]. Merging is therefore nearly free compared with decoding.
- SGLang-integrated GDN kernels already exist for factor-based state reconstruction on Qwen3.5 [2608.01651]. This is plausibly a 3-month build.
- Order-independence comes from small commutators [1912.08854], and the merge-with-explicit-conflicts semantics from [1311.3903].
- Key assumption: branches that differ in substance mostly write to nearly orthogonal key directions in the long-horizon heads. If that holds, merges are mostly conflict-free, and conflicts are localised to the heads where branches truly disagree.

**Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**
(not novelty-checked) Two works come closest. Bole [2608.01651] stores GDN token-level factors for speculative-tree verification, but only reconstructs the single accepted branch; it never merges several branches, uses fixed-size segment operators, or reads a conflict signal. SwarmEvolve [2611.08483] merges by KV concatenation and states that hybrids cannot be merged. Tail-Replay [2608.30310] and DeltaLog [2608.15533] replay or defer updates for a single sequence only.

**Main risk**
A merged state may be out-of-distribution for the layers that follow, since the model was never trained on a memory holding two divergent lines of thought. Short-horizon heads are also dominated by recent tokens. Together these could make decoding after a merge no better than simply continuing the best branch.


### C1 — Adaptive precision refinement: an FP8 working set that follows attention, filled by re-prefilling only the read spans against the INT2 cache

- **Intuition**: An INT2 token can be brought back to full precision without any stored copy and without replaying the whole trace. Its K/V was originally computed from the INT2 cache that is still resident, so re-prefilling just that span against that cache, at a cost proportional to the span length, approximately reproduces the values that were rounded away. Use this as the "refine" step of adaptive mesh refinement: keep the whole trace on a coarse INT2 grid, keep a small set of FP8 patches over the spans current queries actually read, and regrid every few steps as attention moves.

- **Gap it attacks**: Gap L1-1. Every current scheme fixes each token's precision when the token leaves the recent window: by token, head or layer type (ThinKV [2510.01290], KVarN [2606.03458], OSCAR [2605.17757]), or by a static per-channel salience. A long CoT keeps coming back to spans that looked unremarkable when they were written: the intermediate result being checked, the case being re-verified. Heads also change what they read as generation goes on [2610.02713]. At 2 bits those spans are read through 2-bit noise, and a minority of requests run away (Qwen3-8B AIME25 cap hits rise from 11% to 42% under OSCAR INT2 [2609.38121]). The only remedies today are a stored FP16 copy in CPU RAM (Runtime-Certified [2605.20868]), keeping all 8 bits resident (ReadKV [2610.11245]), upgrading the whole request, or restarting from scratch in high precision [2606.02011]. Closing the gap would keep INT2's batch size while giving the read path near-FP8 fidelity, which matters because runaways dominate cost per correct answer at memory saturation.

- **Source inspiration**: Block-structured adaptive mesh refinement, as packaged in AMReX [2009.12009] (computational physics), with selective reliability [1206.1390] (HPC fault tolerance) as the correctness discipline. Novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts.
  - In AMR a coarse level covers the whole domain. Finer levels are small rectangular patches, for example 12.5% and 6.25% of the domain in AMReX's Rayleigh-Taylor runs.
  - At user-set intervals (every 2 steps in that benchmark), an error estimate tags cells, and tagged cells are clustered into patches.
  - A new fine patch gets its boundary (ghost cells) from the coarse level and is then advanced locally. Coarse data are fetched only where fine data do not exist, and restriction later writes fine results back onto the coarse level [2009.12009, Sections 5.2, 6.1, 6.4].
  - It works because resolution is needed only where the solution is currently changing, and a patch can be computed locally from coarse boundary data instead of re-solving the whole domain.
  - Selective reliability adds the operating rule: keep most data and compute in the cheap, unreliable mode, keep a small reliable core, and at the end of every inner phase "refresh the correct values ... even if no faults were detected there" [1206.1390, Sections 5.2, 7.3.4, 7.4].

- **How it maps to this problem**:
  - **Coarse level.** The paged INT2 cache (OSCAR-style rotation, BF16 sinks and recent window) covers the whole trace. The always-reliable core is the token ids (4 bytes per token) plus the weights.
  - **Fine patches.** Each request holds a small pool of FP8 page runs, with spans as the unit (page-aligned groups of tokens).
  - **Error estimate / tagging.** The split-KV decode kernel already computes per-split softmax partial sums for its final merge. Exposing them gives, nearly for free, how much attention mass the current queries put on each page, aggregated over the GQA group, as Runtime-Certified does for its high-mass blocks [2605.20868]. Pages that keep receiving mass over a short horizon are tagged, and tagged pages are clustered into contiguous spans.
  - **Refine.** A tagged span is filled by local re-solve with coarse boundary data. It is pushed through the model as an ordinary chunked prefill whose prefix is the request's current mixed-precision cache, the same "prefill against a cached prefix" path that prefix caching already uses in SGLang. Its new K/V overwrite the span's pages in FP8 instead of being appended. Cost scales with span length times context length read at INT2, not with the trace squared, and tokens after the span are untouched.
  - **Coarsen.** When a span stops receiving mass it is requantized to INT2, which needs only the FP8 values in hand. Because it is requantized from freshly recomputed values, the drifted write-time values are also refreshed, in the selective-reliability sense.
  - **What runs on the GPU.** The normal INT2 decode batch serves hundreds of requests. In the tensor-core slack of every step, a refinement lane runs one fused prefill chunk drawn from a global queue of tagged spans across all requests. Precision becomes a fluid working set regridded every few steps, not a label fixed at write time.
  - **Free by-products.**
    - (a) Each refinement yields the span's measured INT2 error, the difference between recomputed and stored values, per head. Multiplied by attention mass, it becomes an exact "error x read mass" priority that sharpens the next regrid, the way AMR's estimate improves on refined patches.
    - (b) On an n-gram loop detection [2606.02011], the loop's induction-style attention already points at the span being copied. Truncating to loop onset only frees pages. The copied-from spans are force-tagged and refined, and decoding resumes from the onset instead of restarting.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**:
  - **The read path may be what matters.** Pure quantization inflates reasoning length up to 5.1x, while eviction, which keeps whatever is read at full precision and drops the rest, does not inflate length [2510.01290, Section 6.3]. Making the read set high-precision while the rest stays INT2 resembles eviction from the read path's point of view, but nothing is deleted. Runtime-Certified's high-mass FP16 promotion, from a stored copy, recovers near-exact attention with a small promoted set [2605.20868, Sections 3.3-3.4].
  - **Refinement is cheap and fits idle compute.** Long-context decode is memory-bound regardless of batch [2502.10424, Section 3.1.2]. The incremental cost of linear-layer work rises little with tokens added to a decode step, and prefill and decode linear ops can be fused into one matmul [2308.16369, Sections 3.1, 4.3.1]. A refinement chunk reads the prefix at INT2, so its attention traffic is about one decode step of that request.
  - **Implementability.** OSCAR already runs INT2 inside SGLang's paged cache with prefix caching and Triton kernels [2605.17757]. The new parts are an FP8 page type, the mass read-out, and an overwrite-instead-of-append prefill.
  - **Key assumption.** Most of the damage on the read path comes from the 2-bit rounding of the spans being read, not from error already folded into their values through their INT2 context. Re-prefilling against the INT2 prefix removes the former but not the latter.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work** (not novelty-checked):
  - Runtime-Certified [2605.20868] promotes high-mass blocks from a full FP16 copy held in CPU RAM. ReadKV [2610.11245] varies read depth per query but keeps all 8 bits resident.
  - ArborKV [2605.22106] re-prefills evicted thought blocks only when tree search backtracks to them.
  - C3 replays the whole trace periodically.
  - Here no high-precision copy exists anywhere. Promotion is served by span-local recomputation against the low-bit cache itself, and is regridded continuously from free attention mass.

- **Main risk**: Error propagated through the INT2 context may dominate. Spans re-prefilled against a corrupted prefix would then stay too far from BF16 values to stop runaways, and refinement chunks would compete with decode for compute faster than the attention working set settles.


### C6 — Sigma-Delta KV: every token's rounding error is carried into the next similar token, so the errors attention adds up cancel out

- **Intuition**: Attention never reads one cached token by itself. It reads weighted sums over many tokens. Today's low-bit KV quantizers round each token on its own and throw the rounding error away, so tokens that look alike (above all the repeated content of a long chain of thought) get the same error, and those errors add up in every attention sum. Instead, handle the KV cache as a sigma-delta converter handles an oversampled signal: carry each token's leftover rounding error ("debt") into the next token whose key looks similar, before that token is rounded. Then the errors of any group of similar tokens cancel to at most one quantization step in total, however many tokens the group holds. The cache uses the same 2-bit (or fewer) codes, but what attention adds up is nearly exact.

- **Gap it attacks**: Gap L1-1 (low-bit KV for long CoT, specifically how each token's cache entry is represented). Every current method decides one thing: how precisely each token is stored. OSCAR/WUSH-KV choose the rotation and clipping [2605.17757, 2609.38121], KVarN normalises scales [2606.03458], TaSQ shapes a VQ space [2610.03027], ThinKV picks per-thought precision [2510.01290], and ReadKV picks a read depth [2610.11245]. All of them round each token independently, a memoryless quantizer in signal-processing terms. That is the regime where errors on near-duplicate tokens are identical and do not average out. The concrete failure: at 2 bits a minority of long-CoT requests run away. On Qwen3-8B AIME25, budget exhaustion goes from 11% (BF16) to 42% (OSCAR INT2), and a Hadamard-only quantizer hits the cap 100% of the time [2609.38121]. ThinKV notes that pure quantization can inflate generation length up to 5.1x, while eviction, which removes information outright, does not [2510.01290]. That pattern points to systematic error, not missing information. KVarN likewise attributes the damage to errors that accumulate over long decoding [2606.03458]. If the attention-visible error stopped growing with trace length, 2-bit (and possibly ~1-bit) caches could stop paying the runaway tax. That would recover the ~3x batch gain at memory saturation that runaways now cancel.

- **Source inspiration**: Two sources from outside ML systems (novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts).
  (1) Noise-shaping / sigma-delta quantization from signal processing and frame theory [1502.05807]. When a signal is oversampled and later reconstructed by a smoothing (summing) operator, per-sample accuracy is the wrong goal. What matters is that the error vector lies where the reconstruction does not look. Sigma-delta achieves this causally. Each sample is rounded after its predecessor's leftover error has been added in, so the error sequence becomes differences of a small bounded state, and every partial sum of errors telescopes to at most about one step. The reconstruction error is then bounded by the "variation" of the reconstruction coefficients along the order in which samples are visited. Ordering the samples so these coefficients change smoothly (frame paths) is the design lever. The scheme stays stable even with 1-bit or 3-level alphabets, provided the quantizer's range covers the input plus the carried error. Plain rounding cannot exploit redundancy at all: its error falls at best linearly in the number of samples [1502.05807, Sections 2, 3.1-3.3].
  (2) Error feedback in compressed distributed optimisation [1901.09847]. A biased compressor (sign) makes SGD diverge because its bias accumulates. Storing the compression residual and adding it to the next update makes any compressor behave like the uncompressed method. The running SUM of compressed updates tracks the true sum with a bounded residual, and that sum is the only thing downstream computation consumes [1901.09847, Section 4, Lemma 3, Theorem II].
  Both say the same thing. When the consumer of quantized data is a sum, keep the rounding residual and pass it forward, and the consumer sees almost no error even though each item is coarse.

- **How it maps to this problem**:
  - The oversampled signal is the stream of K and V vectors that a head writes during a long CoT: tens of thousands of highly redundant samples per head. The reconstruction operator is attention itself: values combined by softmax weights, and keys combined into the softmax normaliser and each group's attention mass. The reconstruction coefficients are the attention weights.
  - The debt register: per layer, per KV head, per K/V, a small vector holding the rounding error that is still unpaid. When a token leaves the full-precision recent window and is quantized (OSCAR/KVarN already quantize at exactly this point, in token order), its debt is added before rounding and the new leftover becomes the debt. The stored code format, page layout and attention kernel do not change. Decoding pays nothing extra; writing pays one add and one subtract.
  - The visiting order (which "next token" inherits the debt) is the frame-path choice. The simplest choice is time order, which helps heads whose attention is spread smoothly over contiguous spans. The stronger choice follows key similarity. Softmax gives tokens with nearly equal keys nearly equal weights for *every* query, so chaining similar keys keeps the attention weights smooth along the chain whatever the query. The chain label can come for free: in a rotated 2-bit cache, the top codes of a token's leading key channels already act as a locality-sensitive hash. That gives a handful of debt registers per head, one per bucket, far smaller than the cache.
  - The free by-product reused as a mechanism, in SwarmEvolve's sense [2611.08483]: every quantizer already computes the rounding residual and throws it away. Here it becomes the state that makes the cache exact in aggregate. The cost profile flips too. For recurrent-state caches, noise shaping was dismissed because the residual must be stored for every entry, which doubles the cache [2609.39185]. In a KV cache the chain runs *across* tokens, so one register serves the whole sequence, and its cost is O(head dim), not O(sequence length).
  - Pushed to the extreme: the redundancy that sigma-delta converts into accuracy grows with trace length. Long CoT therefore stops being the worst case for low-bit KV and becomes the best case. That makes the ~1-bit regime worth trying for long reasoning, where memoryless 1-bit VQ collapses today (CQ: 14.44 on AIME25) [2610.03027].
  - It is orthogonal to existing codecs. Debt is carried in whatever coordinate system the codec quantizes in (after OSCAR/WUSH rotations or KVarN scaling), and it works with scalar or VQ codes, since error feedback needs only that the compressor is approximately contractive [1901.09847]. Forked branches inherit a copy of the tiny registers along with the shared prefix pages.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**:
  - The guarantee needs only three things, and each plausibly holds. (a) Redundancy: a long CoT has many tokens per effective degree of freedom, and repetition is the norm, not the exception, in runaways. (b) Reconstruction coefficients that vary smoothly along the chain: softmax weights are smooth in the key, so a key-similarity chain makes this hold for every query by construction [1502.05807, Section 3.2]. (c) A non-overloaded quantizer: per-token scales are set after the debt is added, and first-order shaping needs the range to cover only the input plus one step, which 4 levels comfortably allow [1502.05807, Proposition 3.1].
  - Error feedback provably removes the accumulated bias of biased compressors in a setting where only sums are consumed [1901.09847]. Attention is such a consumer.
  - In-domain, the closest evidence points the same way. For recurrent state caches, rounding errors across steps are correlated, not independent, and accumulated error equals the discrepancy of the rounding sequence. Choosing a lower-discrepancy rounding sequence measurably lowers the damage, and plain rounding gets worse the longer one decodes [2609.39185]. Rounding bias in a cache also visibly lengthens generations (Nemotron's FP16 state casting: up to +37% length, cited in [2609.39185]).
  - Implementation: only the cache-write kernel changes, in SGLang/vLLM paths that already exist for OSCAR [2605.17757] and KVarN [2606.03458]. Quantization at window exit is sequential within a head, a tiny scan parallel over channels and heads. It is a clear 3-month build, and the first test needs no kernel work at all: simulate it in HF on Qwen3-8B AIME25 and measure cap-hit rate versus OSCAR at equal bits.
  - Key assumption: most of the harm from 2-bit KV in long CoT comes from errors that add coherently across groups of similar tokens (repeated phrases, recurring sub-results), not from the per-token error on a few isolated, uniquely keyed tokens.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: (not novelty-checked) The closest work is Low-Discrepancy Dither [2609.39185], which only changes the rounding threshold for recurrent-state caches. It explicitly sets noise shaping aside because the residual would double the state cache, and it notes that KV-cache quantizers do not feed errors back. Next closest are DiscQuant [2501.06417], which does dependent, discrepancy-style rounding of weights offline with gradients, and dithered Hadamard quantization [2605.13810], which makes errors unbiased and independent, not anti-correlated. The difference here: error feedback across tokens of a KV cache, chained by key similarity, so that attention itself acts as the noise-shaping decoder, at zero read cost and with no change to the code format.

- **Main risk**: Shaping roughly doubles the worst-case error on a single token. Sharp retrieval heads that put nearly all their weight on one distinctive token (a number, a variable name) with no similar-key neighbours could therefore get worse. If runaways are driven by those heads rather than by coherent errors over similar tokens, the net effect could be neutral or negative.


### C8 — Pseudo-token folding: carry old reasoning as a few synthetic KV entries that reproduce its attention field, the way N-body codes carry a far star cluster as pseudo-particles

- **Intuition**: When a stretch of reasoning leaves the exact recent window, replace its hundreds of KV entries in each head by a handful of synthetic "pseudo-tokens" (key, value, mass) built so that, for any query the model will ever send, they pull roughly the same total attention and return roughly the same value as the original stretch. Nothing is selected or deleted: every old token keeps its share of attention through the pseudo-tokens, and the unmodified attention kernel reads them as ordinary cache entries.

- **Gap it attacks**: Gap L3-1. Every compression of a long CoT today is a subset choice: keep some tokens, delete the rest. The choice signal barely matters (uniform random keep-sets match SnapKV/R-KV once the prompt is protected [2609.03430]); what hurts is the permanently deleted fraction, with a cliff from 22% to 0% accuracy between 70% and 50% budget [2605.09490]. Deletion also has a hidden second error: each deleted token's attention mass is handed to the survivors, so every later read is biased toward whatever happened to survive. Offload tiers avoid deletion only by keeping the full KV somewhere [2605.09490]. A representation that keeps the whole history's attention mass in a constant number of entries per old stretch would turn "how much may we delete" into "how coarsely may we describe the past", and memory per request would grow far slower than trace length.

- **Source inspiration**: Recombination-of-distant-concepts, from computational astrophysics plus kernel methods.
  (1) The pseudo-particle multipole method [astro-ph/9806213]. Tree codes and FMM summarise a distant star cluster by its multipole moments, but GRAPE-type N-body hardware can only compute point-to-point interactions, so it could not evaluate those summaries. Makino's fix is to turn the summary back into particles: a few pseudo-particles at fixed positions around the cluster centre, whose masses are a fixed linear transform of the real particles' masses, reproduce the cluster's field exactly up to a chosen order. The hardware then evaluates the far field as ordinary particle-particle work. Because positions are fixed, no nonlinear solve is needed. Coarser cells are built by treating the children's pseudo-particles as sources (the M2M step). The spread of the pseudo-particles trades spurious high-order terms (too wide) against exploding masses (too tight) [astro-ph/9806213, Sections 3.1, 4, 5.2]. Accuracy holds only for evaluation points outside the cluster radius, the well-separated condition [Section 3.1].
  (2) Kernel herding [1203.3472]. A large sample set can be replaced by a few deterministic "super-samples" chosen to match its kernel mean embedding. Since every expectation of an RKHS function is an inner product with that embedding, matching it controls the error for all such functions at once, at O(1/T) instead of the O(1/sqrt(T)) of i.i.d. subsampling. The gain comes from negative correlation: the summary covers the distribution instead of hitting it at random [1203.3472, Sections 1, 2.1].

- **How it maps to this problem**:
  - A star cluster becomes one finished block of reasoning tokens in one KV head. The cluster's far field becomes the block's attention response to a query: how much un-normalised attention mass the block attracts, and which mass-weighted value it returns. Both quantities are evaluated through the exponential kernel between the query and the block's keys. So the block's response to every possible future query is the kernel mean embedding of its keys, weighted by their values [1203.3472, Section 2.1].
  - GRAPE becomes the paged FlashAttention/FlashInfer kernel, which only knows token-to-token interactions. Pseudo-tokens are ordinary key/value entries plus one additive log-mass on their logit. This is the same per-entry logit bias that Hájek-corrected retention already applies to kept tokens [2607.21475]. Paging, prefix sharing and KV quantization therefore apply to the folded past unchanged.
  - Makino's fixed ring of pseudo-particles becomes a fixed layout around the block's mean key: the centroid plus a symmetric pair along each of the block's few dominant key directions. Masses are set so that the block's total attention mass and its key spread are reproduced. Pseudo-values are then solved linearly so that the block's mean value, and how values co-vary with keys, are reproduced. This gives exact low-order moments for any query, with no search and no nonlinear fit. Makino's ring-radius trade-off carries over directly as the choice of spread.
  - The M2M step becomes re-folding. When two adjacent folded blocks age further, their pseudo-tokens are treated as sources and folded again into one coarser cell. The far past is then described more and more coarsely with age, like an FMM tree or LSM levels. A request's cache becomes an exact recent window plus a short ladder of increasingly coarse cells, rather than a flat token list.
  - What runs on the GPU: the fold happens once per block, per KV head, at the moment the block leaves the window. It reads K/V that are already resident, and is a few small dense matrix products on one block, negligible next to decoding. It is query-agnostic, so one fold serves every query head in a GQA group. The fold is applied unconditionally to every aged block. There is no score, trigger or per-token decision.
  - Free by-product for parallel reasoning: folded cells are ordinary KV entries. Branches that share a prefix share its folded cells by reference. SwarmEvolve-style KV concatenation of several branches' histories [2611.08483] would then cost pseudo-tokens rather than full traces.

- **Why it could work / feasibility**:
  - Mass preservation is supported in-domain. Correcting the kept entries' logits for the mass of what was dropped is what makes subset estimates unbiased in [2607.21475]. A fold keeps each block's mass by construction rather than estimating it.
  - Uniform coverage beats random subsets in principle. Random keep-sets, which are i.i.d. subsampling of the trace, already match the best scoring rules [2609.03430]. Herding shows that a deterministic summary matching the mean embedding beats i.i.d. subsampling uniformly over test functions [1203.3472]. Here the test functions are exactly the future queries.
  - Reasoning traces are redundant in text and across heads [2609.03430]. So most of an old block's influence is diffuse, which a low-order description captures.
  - The analogue of well-separatedness is that a block's keys are tight relative to the query's sharpness. This plausibly holds for the many heads that behave like streaming heads [2410.10819]. The minority of retrieval heads can keep exact KV as a fixed per-head layout chosen offline, not as a runtime rule [2410.10819].
  - The pseudo-particle construction is linear and exact to its order, and runs on hardware that only does pairwise interactions [astro-ph/9806213, Sections 3.1, 5.2]. The engineering is a fold kernel plus a logit-bias hook in an existing attention backend: a plausible 3-month, training-free build on Qwen3.
  - Key assumption: in long CoT, what later steps need from old reasoning is mostly carried by low-order statistics of each block's keys and values (where the reasoning was heading, which quantities it was manipulating) rather than by sharp single-token lookups.

- **Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**: Three works fold dropped KV into a compact memory, and each differs. LESS [2402.09398] folds evicted pairs into a low-rank recurrent state read through per-layer kernels that must be trained for a given eviction policy, and still relies on that policy's subset. Transformer-VQ [2309.16354] keeps only counts and value sums per global code (a zeroth-order summary) and is trained from scratch. LoLA [2505.23666] needs a linearised model and routes pairs by a recall-error rule. Here the summary is training-free on a pretrained softmax model. It consists of synthetic softmax tokens with matched first- and second-order moments, read by the unchanged kernel and re-folded hierarchically, with no routing or selection rule. (not novelty-checked)

- **Main risk**: A single sharp fact inside an old block, such as a variable's value referenced much later [2605.09490], cannot be reproduced from low-order moments. Also, RoPE rotation spreads one block's keys in the high-frequency dimensions, which widens its "radius". If long CoT leans on sharp long-range lookups in many heads, folding helps little beyond random eviction.


### C9 — Private minds, shared tape: hybrid branches keep their recurrent states private and exchange thought only through one physically shared attention KV

**Intuition**
A hybrid model already has two kinds of memory: recurrent states that are overwritten at every token, and attention KV entries that never change once written. Parallel SAT solvers share learned clauses but keep each solver's own search state private. In the same way, N reasoning branches of a hybrid can write their attention KV into one shared, append-only pool that every branch's attention layers read, while each branch's GDN/Mamba states stay private and never see foreign tokens. Branches then exchange intermediate results continuously, at no extra KV memory, and no recurrent state is ever merged.

**Gap it attacks**
Gap L4-1. Hybrids cannot use the KV tricks that let transformer branches communicate, because a recurrent state cannot absorb another branch's tokens without a state merge. In SwarmEvolve this is a stated limitation [2611.08483]. Hogwild!-style concurrent attention [2504.06261] makes every layer of every worker read every worker's tokens, so it cannot run on GDN layers at all. Today a hybrid's parallel branches therefore either reason in isolation until the final vote or re-prefill each other's text. Closing the gap lets Qwen3.5/Qwen3-Next run collaborating swarms of branches whose only per-branch cost beyond its own tokens is a ~10 MB state [2610.36950]. It also gives SwarmEvolve a way to run on hybrids that does not depend on state algebra (contrast C4).

**Source inspiration**
Two sources (novelty type: recombination-of-distant-concepts, plus reinforcing a weak connection to [2504.06261]).
(1) Gimsatul, a parallel portfolio SAT solver [2207.13577]. Threads share learned clauses physically: one copy in memory, passed by pointer with atomic reference counts. Each thread keeps its own assignment trail, watch lists and heuristics. The split works because a learned clause is an immutable fact that is valid for every thread, while the trail is mutable and specific to one search. A thread "imports" a clause by fitting it into its own private structures (choosing watches, fixing its trail), never by synchronising trails. Physical sharing removes the per-thread memory cost of copying and scales linearly with the number of threads.
(2) Mallob [2205.06590] scales clause sharing to thousands of diversified solvers. It shows that sharing volume matters: starving the exchange is "highly detrimental". It also shows that an import mechanism which interrupts and restarts the receiving solver's private search was "disruptive" and hurt performance. Sharing helps when imports leave each searcher's private state intact.

**How it maps to this problem**
- Learned clause = an attention-layer KV entry. It is written once and never modified, so it can be shared by reference. Private search state = each branch's GDN/Mamba states (and conv states), which change at every token and so must stay per-branch.
- N branches start from a shared prompt. Each branch appends its tokens' attention KV to the common pool and keeps its own recurrent states. At every attention layer, each branch's query reads the whole pool: prompt, its own tokens, and the other branches' tokens up to the previous step. Positions are handled with Hogwild!'s per-block query rotation and its chat-like layout, which keeps others' latest steps close by [2504.06261]. Only the few attention layers need positions.
- The GDN layers process only the branch's own token stream, in order, so the branch's line of thought is never interleaved with foreign text. Foreign information enters only as attention outputs in the residual stream. The branch's own later GDN layers then write what the branch made of it into its private state. This is the analogue of a solver importing a clause through its own watch lists: the model itself does the "merge", in its own words, and no state algebra is needed.
- Memory: the pool holds each token once, so total attention KV equals that of N independent branches with a shared prefix. The only extra per branch is one state. Bandwidth: every branch reads the same blocks, so each page can be read once per step for all N queries as one matrix-matrix product [2402.05099]. This is a better fit for decode than independent branches, and the overhead sits in only ~1/4 of the layers.
- Uses: (a) collaborating long-CoT swarms on hybrids for AIME/HMMT/LiveCodeBench, which can split sub-tasks, cross-check and reuse each other's partial results, compared at equal total tokens and memory with self-consistency; (b) SwarmEvolve on hybrids. Latent agents write only attention KV to the pool plus their private states. The summariser reads all agents through its attention layers, and its own GDN state stays a clean, constant-size record of its own decoding.
- Free by-product: the attention mass each branch puts on each other branch's blocks is an exact, free record of who used whose results. It can be reported alongside votes to show which answers are not independent.

**Why it could work / feasibility**
- Reasoning models collaborate zero-shot over a shared KV with no fine-tuning, and Hogwild!'s query-rotation kernel keeps one copy of each block [2504.06261]. The hybrid version only removes foreign blocks from the GDN layers.
- In hybrids, retrieval lives in the attention layers: zeroing all linear-layer states changes NIAH by at most 1 point [2608.30386]. So foreign facts can be looked up through the attention layers alone. Long CoT is locally Markov [2606.11052], which is the part a private recurrent state carries well.
- Hogwild!'s stated failures come from all layers seeing the union of workers' tokens. It loses robustness at longer contexts, and 6 workers saturate because the union outruns the trained length [2504.06261]. In the hybrid split, 3/4 of the layers see only one branch's length, which is the "non-disruptive import" condition that Mallob found necessary [2205.06590].
- Engineering: paged KV with shared blocks plus per-block query rotation (an existing kernel pattern), and ordinary per-request GDN states in SGLang/vLLM. This is plausibly a 3-month build.
- Key assumption: one branch's attention layers can pick up another branch's intermediate result and act on it, even though the branch's recurrent context never contained that text.

**Novelty vs. closest in-domain work**
(not novelty-checked) Hogwild! [2504.06261] shares one KV cache among workers of dense Transformers, where every layer reads all workers' tokens. It does not cover recurrent or hybrid layers and has no private per-worker memory. SwarmEvolve [2611.08483] communicates by KV concatenation and states that hybrids are unsupported. C4 instead merges recurrent states algebraically, whereas here states are never merged and exchange runs only through the immutable attention memory.

**Main risk**
With only ~1/4 of the layers seeing foreign tokens, and the branch's recurrent context unaware of them, imported results may be too weak a signal to change what a branch does. Branches would then ignore one another, like the "too focused" workers in [2504.06261], and the swarm would behave like self-consistency with extra attention reads.
