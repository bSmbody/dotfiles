# Generalized Forgetting Recursive Least Squares: Stability and Robustness Guarantees

## What this paper does
Adaptive-control paper (Lai and Bernstein, IEEE TAC) that writes RLS with forgetting as one family, GF-RLS. Forgetting is a separate symmetric "forgetting matrix" F_k subtracted from the information matrix, and the data step then adds the full weighted regressor. Every forgetting scheme is a choice of F_k. The paper gives Lyapunov-stability conditions on F_k for fixed-parameter estimation, ultimate-boundedness guarantees under parameter drift and noise, and a survey that recasts ten published RLS extensions as special cases (exponential, variable-rate, resetting, several directional-forgetting schemes, multiple forgetting).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does GF-RLS separate the forgetting step from the data step, and does the data step always use the full regressor?
Theorem 1 [full text, Section II / Eq 3, 9-10]: P_{k+1}^{-1} = P_k^{-1} - F_k + phi_k^T Gamma_k^{-1} phi_k and theta_{k+1} = theta_k + P_{k+1} phi_k^T Gamma_k^{-1} (y_k - phi_k theta_k). It is the exact minimiser of a cost with a loss term, a subtracted "forgetting" term ||theta - theta_i||_{F_i}^2, and a prior term [Eq 4-7]. F_k is any symmetric matrix that keeps the information positive definite [Eq 3]. The data step always adds the full regressor phi_k^T phi_k. The forgetting matrix is a free design choice, made independently of what is written. Exponential forgetting is F_k = (1-lambda) P_k^{-1} [Section V-B / Eq 40].

### Goal: What conditions on the forgetting matrix give stability and covariance bounds?
Section III, conditions A1-A4 [full text, Section III / Theorem 2]. A1: F_k >= 0 ("proper"). A2: the information after forgetting is bounded below, (P_k^{-1} - F_k)^{-1} <= b I, i.e. no windup. A3: P_k >= a I, i.e. information bounded above, so the filter keeps adapting. A4: persistent excitation. A1+A2 give Lyapunov stability, A1-A3 uniform Lyapunov stability, and adding A4 gives global uniform exponential stability. The paper notes that an upper bound on P_k alone does not imply A2 [Section III-A2]. These are conditions on F_k and the data jointly, not tied to a particular F_k.

### Goal: Is there a directional-forgetting precedent in which a threshold gates only the forgetting, not the write?
Yes. Section V-G / Eq 58-63 recasts the information-matrix-decomposition directional forgetting of its ref [15] (scalar measurements). The information is discounted along the current regressor only if ||phi_k|| > epsilon, otherwise not at all [Eq 61-62]. The data step R_{k+1} = R_bar_k + phi_k^T phi_k and the parameter update use the full phi_k in both cases [Eq 58-60]. So the threshold controls only where forgetting happens, and nothing is dropped from the write. The scheme is shown proper (F_k >= 0, R_bar_k > 0). Section V-H (variable-direction forgetting via an SVD of the information matrix) and V-J (multiple forgetting with per-direction rates) are further members.

### Goal: Does GF-RLS admit a forgetting matrix built from the previous step's regressor (lagged forgetting)?
Yes, in one surveyed case. Section V-I / Eq 67-73 recasts a directional-forgetting tracker by shifting indices, so its forgetting matrix is F_k = (1 - beta_{k-1}) phi_{k-1}^T phi_{k-1}. That is built from the previous regressor and is still a proper GF-RLS member. The general theorems apply to any F_k that satisfies (3) and A1-A3, so a forgetting matrix computed from already-seen data is covered by the framework. Specific bounds for such a choice must be checked case by case.
