# A JoLT for the KV Cache: Near-Lossless KV Cache Compression via Joint Rank-bit Allocation

## What this paper does
Training-free prefill-cache compressor. Layers are clustered into groups with aligned token-mode bases; each group's K (pre-RoPE) and V form 4th-order tensors (layer × head × token × feature) compressed by partial Tucker (ST-HOSVD on token and feature modes only), plus a randomly rotated low-bit residual (b ∈ {0,2,4,8}). One Lagrangian dual allocates per-group Tucker ranks and residual bit-widths under a global byte budget. FlashJoLT uses randomized SVD and a fused Triton decode kernel that attends over the factors. Generated tokens stay in a small dense tail.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is compressed and how is the budget allocated?
Only the prefill cache; newly generated tokens are kept dense [full text, Sec 3, Sec 4.5]. Rank (r_T, r_d) and residual bits are chosen jointly per (group, K/V) cell by Lagrangian relaxation under a byte constraint; joint allocation matches K/V-decoupled at 2× and trails it at 4× (PPL 6.93 vs 6.72) [Sec 3 "Joint Lagrangian allocation", Sec 4.4].
### Goal: What quality results?
Five models (Mistral-7B, Llama-2-13B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B, OLMoE): 2–3× compression with <0.2% PPL change [Abstract]. RULER 64K Llama-3.1-8B: +0.23 pp at 2×, −0.10 at 3×, −0.90 at 4×, −2.40 at 5× [Sec 4.3, Fig 3]. GSM8K 8-shot: near-lossless at 2–3×, Mistral −8.49 pp at 4× [Sec 4.3, App C Table 7]. LongBench 16K Mistral within 0.52 pp through 4× [Sec 4.3]. No long-CoT reasoning models.
### Goal: Efficiency?
Peak decode KV memory 1.43×/2.40×/3.61×/4.73× below BF16 at 2/3/5/8× targets (Mistral, 8K); the fused kernel is slower than uncompressed attention at every measured point because RoPE forces per-tile key reconstruction [Sec 4.5]. So it only helps when memory capacity binds; it does not apply to decode-generated KV, which is the bulk of long-CoT KV.
