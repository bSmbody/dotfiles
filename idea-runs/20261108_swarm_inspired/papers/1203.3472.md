# Super-Samples from Kernel Herding

## What this paper does
Chen, Welling and Smola extend herding to continuous spaces with kernels. Kernel herding is a deterministic, infinite-memory process that produces pseudo-samples greedily minimising the RKHS distance between the target distribution's mean embedding and the empirical mean of the samples chosen so far. The error of expectations of any function in the RKHS then decays as O(1/T) instead of O(1/sqrt(T)) for i.i.d. samples. Applied to compress large MCMC sample sets into a few "super-samples" for Bayesian predictive averaging.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism and why does it beat i.i.d. subsampling?
Updates x_{t+1} = argmax_x <w_t, phi(x)>, w_{t+1} = w_t + mu_p - phi(x_{t+1}) [full text, Section 2.1, Eqs 1-2]; in the dual view herding greedily minimises E_T^2 = ||mu_p - (1/T) sum_t phi(x_t)||^2, the squared RKHS distance between the mean operator mu_p and the samples' empirical mean [Section 2.1, Eqs 3-5]. Because E_x~p[f(x)] = <f, mu_p> for every f in the RKHS, matching the mean embedding controls the error of all such expectations at once [Section 2.1]. The fast rate comes from negative autocorrelation: the process remembers all previous samples and steers away from already (over)sampled regions, like quasi-Monte Carlo and Bayesian quadrature [Section 1, Section 2.1]. Subsampling an MCMC chain i.i.d.-style is suboptimal for the same reason [Section 1].

### Goal: What assumptions does the guarantee rely on?
Proposition 1 needs the argmax to be found exactly each iteration and bounded feature norms (e.g. ||phi(x)||=R); the O(1/T) result holds for functions in the RKHS of the chosen kernel, and requires being able to compute mu_p (convolve p with the kernel), which is hard in general but easy when p is itself a large finite sample set to be reduced [Section 1, Section 2.1-2.2]. The authors note that the greedy set is not jointly optimal for a fixed T and that sqrt(T) super-samples matching T i.i.d. samples is "a little optimistic" in practice [Section 1, Section 2.1].
