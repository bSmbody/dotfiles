# Asymptotic genealogies of interacting particle systems with an application to sequential Monte Carlo

## What this paper does
Studies the genealogical tree embedded in weighted particle systems that resample offspring in proportion to weights (SMC, particle filters). Under conditions controlling family-size fluctuations and a time rescaling by the pair-merger rate, the genealogy of any finite sample of particles converges to Kingman's n-coalescent as N grows. This gives a priori, tractable predictions of the shape and size of SMC genealogies (e.g., mean and variance of the number of generations to the most recent common ancestor). Simulations show the predictions hold non-asymptotically and for multinomial, residual, stratified and systematic resampling.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What sets the speed at which particle lineages coalesce?
The per-generation pair-merger rate c_N(t), whose conditional expectation given the weights equals the sum of squared normalized weights, i.e. 1/ESS(t), approximately (1/N)(1 + Var of normalized weights) [full text, Section 2, Eq. near line 310]. Time is rescaled by accumulating c_N; in rescaled time the genealogy behaves like Kingman's coalescent. So the more unequal the weights (the lower the ESS), the faster lineages merge.
### Goal: How long until a set of lineages shares a common ancestor, and how does this compare to the all-particle bound?
For any finite number of leaves the expected number of generations to the MRCA scales linearly in N; this compares with the N log N upper bound of Jacob-Murray-Rubenthaler for all N leaves [Section 1, Corollary 2].
### Goal: What trade-off does resampling frequency control?
Resampling less often reduces path degeneracy but increases weight degeneracy (estimator variance grows exponentially in T without resampling); intermediate strategies balance them, and the limiting genealogy is presented as the only a priori estimate of path degeneracy for choosing that balance [Section 1, Section 4].
### Goal: Limitations?
Assumptions essentially need a compact state space and multinomial resampling; convergence is only in finite-dimensional distributions; simulations suggest the assumptions are not necessary [Section 4].
