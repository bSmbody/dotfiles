# SpotAttention: Plug-In Block-Sparse Routing for Pretrained Long-Context Transformers

## What this paper does
Attaches a small lightning-indexer-style block selector to each full-attention layer of a frozen pretrained LLM (Qwen3 4B-32B, hybrid Qwen3.5 4B/9B) and trains only the selector by forward KL to the layer's head-averaged attention. It compares DenseKL (over all keys) and SparseKL (renormalized over the selector's own top-K), and at inference uses static top-K or a dual top-p rule that reads a per-query, per-layer budget from the calibrated selector distribution. Trained on 100M tokens at 16K, it tracks dense accuracy up to 128K and gives 3.9x decode speedup over FlashAttention at 128K.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does the selector get its teacher signal on blocks it did not select, at what training length, and does it need dense attention?
DenseKL computes the teacher over all T keys, i.e. dense attention on the frozen backbone; SparseKL restricts both teacher and student to the selector's own top-K set and renormalizes there [full text, Section 2.2 / Eq 3-4]. Training uses L=16K with K_train=8K selected tokens, 100M tokens of FineWeb-Edu [Section 3, "Backbones and training"]. So off-support signal comes only from the dense teacher, but at a short training length; no sampling of unselected keys is used.

### Goal: Does a selector trained with a short-length teacher generalize to long context, and how does the KL scope change the learned selector?
Yes for its setting: trained at 16K, both static K=0.5L and dual top-p track the dense curve at every length bin up to 128K on RULER, InfiniteBench and LongBench-v2, with no decay relative to dense [Section 4.4, Figure 5]. KL scope changes the shape: DenseKL makes the selector "razor-sharp" (top-1 block carries 72% of residual mass on Qwen3-4B, 84% on Qwen3.5-4B) and breaks calibration for top-p, while SparseKL tracks the teacher spread (34% / 5%) [Section 5.2, Figure 7; Limitations]. Caveat: the evaluated budget is large (K=0.5L), so this says little about recall at small budgets.
