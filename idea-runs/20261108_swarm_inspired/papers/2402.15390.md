# Explorations of Self-Repair in Language Models

## What this paper does
Extends self-repair from narrow distributions and whole-layer ablations to individual attention heads on the full pretraining distribution (Pythia, GPT-2, Llama families). Self-repair is measured as Δlogit − ΔDE_head under resample ablation. It shows self-repair exists broadly but is imperfect and noisy, attributes up to ~30% of it to the final LayerNorm scale change and part of it to sparse, prompt-specific anti-erasure neurons, and discusses the Iterative Inference hypothesis.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is self-repair measured per head, and how large and reliable is it on the full distribution?
Self-repair = Δlogit − ΔDE_head (Eq 1), with resample ablation of one head's output; heads with −1 < Δlogit/DE < 0 are self-repaired [full text, Section 2.1]. Many heads, including last-layer heads, are partly self-repaired on 1M Pile tokens in Pythia-1B, across model families and sizes, measured on each head's top 2% tokens by direct effect [Section 2.2]. It is imperfect: ablation still gives a "small, but noticeable" logit change [Section 2.2]. It is noisy: it varies strongly token by token within one head, sometimes overcorrects, and some heads with large direct effect show no correlation between DE and Δlogit, i.e. near-full repair [Section 2.3, Figure 3].

### Goal: What mechanisms produce it, and what caution does it give about interventions?
Up to ~30% of self-repair comes from the change in the final LayerNorm scale factor; sparse, prompt-varying sets of MLP neurons perform anti-erasure [Sections 3, 4]. Ablations take the model off-distribution, so some self-repair may be a side effect; the authors suggest avoiding zero ablation, freezing LayerNorm, or using path patching to stay closer to the data distribution [Section 5.2]. Because importance is heavy-tailed, imperfect self-repair rarely flips which components are important, but borderline components can be misclassified [Section 5.1].
