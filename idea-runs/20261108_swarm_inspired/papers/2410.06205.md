# Round and Round We Go! What makes Rotary Positional Encodings useful?

## What this paper does
Empirical and theoretical study of how LLMs (Gemma 7B, with consistent Llama 3.1 8B results in the appendix) use RoPE frequencies. Argues against the "RoPE decays attention with distance" explanation; shows query/key norm is concentrated in the lowest frequencies, while the highest frequencies are used by a few positional heads (diagonal, previous-token), mostly in first and last layers; argues low frequencies act as semantic channels that are not robust over very long contexts; proposes p-RoPE, removing the lowest frequencies.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is query/key norm distributed across RoPE frequencies, and which heads use the high-frequency (fast-rotating) components?
By Cauchy-Schwarz the contribution of frequency chunk k to a logit is bounded by ||q^(k)|| ||k^(k)||, so the authors plot mean per-chunk 2-norms [full text, Section 4]. "Learning has assigned much higher norm on average to the lowest frequencies", true at each layer, with some high-frequency use especially in the first and last layers [Section 4, Fig 3]; medium-to-high frequency chunks have norm pushed toward 0, and this does not happen for values (unrotated) [Section 4]. Heads that use the highest frequencies are purely positional heads (diagonal, previous-token) [Section 5, Figs 4-6]. Highest frequency rotates 1 radian per token; the lowest about 1/10,000 rad per token [Section 4]. Low-frequency "bands" carry semantic matching but become misaligned over very long relative distances [Section 6, Theorem 6.1].
