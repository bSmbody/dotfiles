# The Communication-Hiding Conjugate Gradient Method with Deep Pipelines

## What this paper does
Cornelis, Cools and Vanroose (HPC numerical linear algebra). On large machines the Conjugate Gradient method is bound by the latency of the global reductions behind its dot products, not by the sparse matrix-vector product (spmv). Pipelined CG restructures the algorithm so a reduction launched in iteration i is consumed only l iterations later (p(l)-CG), and the l spmvs in between hide its latency. The algorithm is identical to CG in exact arithmetic; in finite precision, attainable accuracy falls as the pipeline gets deeper. Parallel speedups and the accuracy trade-off are measured on 2D Poisson problems.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism, i.e. how is an expensive global operation made latency-tolerant?
The algorithm is rewritten with an auxiliary basis Z (shifted polynomials of A applied to the Krylov basis V, Eq 2-4), so that the dot products computed in iteration i are needed to finish basis vector v_{i-l+1} only l iterations later [full text, Section 2.1, Eqs 1-4]. In the kernel schedule (Alg 3), iteration i runs the spmv (K1), then waits on the non-blocking reduction issued in iteration i-l (MPI_Wait(req(i-l))), does local scalar/axpy updates, computes new local dot-product contributions (K5) and launches their reduction (MPI_Iallreduce ... req(i)) without waiting [Section 3.1, Alg 3]. The global reduction phase therefore overlaps with the next l spmvs. The cost of being one or l steps "behind" is absorbed by extra local recurrences, so the iterates match CG in exact arithmetic.

### Goal: What does deepening the pipeline buy and cost (the performance-accuracy trade-off)?
Theoretical maximum speedup over CG is t(spmv + 2 glred) / max(t(spmv), t(glred)/l). In the ideal case t(glred) = l t(spmv) it is (2l+1)x [Section 5.1]. Measured: p(3)-CG on 20 nodes is about 5x faster than CG on the same nodes, p(1)-CG about 3x, and deeper pipelines keep scaling after p(1) stagnates [Section 5.1, Fig 4]. Costs: start-up overhead to fill the pipeline and extra axpys (for l >= 4 these slow the method on 48 nodes), and lower maximal attainable accuracy that "degrades significantly for longer pipelines", because local rounding errors propagate through the extra recurrences; p(3)-CG even hits a square-root breakdown and restarts [Sections 5.1, 5.3, Figs 6, 9-11]. Gains are largest when communication dominates computation. When one spmv already hides the reduction, l > 1 adds little [Section 5.2].

### Goal: What condition does the mechanism rely on?
That the expensive global quantity is not needed immediately: the algorithm can be reorganized so its result enters the computation l steps later, with enough independent local work to fill the gap. The price is numerical robustness, which depends on the pipeline length and on the auxiliary basis (shift choice) [Sections 2.1, 4.2, 5.3].
