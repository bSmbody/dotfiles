# HeadWiseKV: Budgeted Per-Head Cache Residency for Hybrid Long-Context Language Models

## What this paper does
Training-free static KV compression for the residual global-attention layers of hybrids (Qwen3.6-27B, Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-style models). Each physical KV head is assigned offline one contiguous suffix history length from a codebook {8K, 16K, 32K, full}; SeqCalib chooses lengths layer by layer in execution order conditioned on lower-layer choices, picking the cheapest window meeting a cosine threshold; a grouped-cache runtime realizes physical residency. Keeps near-Full-KV RULER/LoCoMo and extends max verified context from 114K to 161K.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What criterion decides how much KV each head keeps, and does it account for the recurrent/linear paths?
SeqCalib compares candidate pre-gate attention outputs of each head against a conditional full-history reference under the already-chosen lower-layer policy and takes the lowest-cost window meeting a mean-cosine threshold [full text, Section 1 and Section 3.2]. The framework "compresses the residual global KV caches ... while preserving their native local, recurrent, and linear paths" [Abstract] - the recurrent layers are untouched and do not enter the distortion criterion, which is per-attention-head output reconstruction. Retained histories are recency suffixes, not content-selected tokens [Section 3.1, Eq 1].

### Goal: What limitation does it report?
The window matrix is prompt-independent, "so it cannot recover evicted evidence or adapt to requests with atypical long-range dependencies"; selection is layerwise, not a global optimum; needs recalibration under shift [Appendix B.4]. Memory saving is modest (8.59% peak at 112K) [Abstract].
