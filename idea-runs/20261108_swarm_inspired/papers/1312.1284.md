# Multiple Time Step Integrators in Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics

## What this paper does
Luehr, Markland and Martinez (computational chemistry). Multiple-time-step (MTS, RESPA) integrators split forces into fast-varying and slow-varying parts and evaluate the expensive slow part (typically long-range electrostatics) less often than the cheap fast part. The paper shows that ab initio forces, which do not split naturally, can be split by fragment decomposition or by range-separating the Coulomb operator. A cheap short-range model force is used at every inner step and the full-minus-model difference is applied at outer steps. Water clusters and solvated hydroxide run with a 2.5 fs outer step and speedups up to 4.4x at the same energy conservation and accuracy as 0.5 fs Born-Oppenheimer MD.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the core mechanism and why does it work?
Trotter factorization of the Liouville operator gives a symplectic, time-reversible integrator. Splitting the force f = f^F + f^S gives an outer half-kick by the slow force over Delta T, N inner velocity-Verlet steps under the fast force only, then another slow half-kick (Eq 8) [full text, Theory, Eqs 1-8]. The outer step is set by the fastest *slow* force, so the slow part is evaluated 1/N as often, an N-fold saving when it dominates cost [Theory, after Eq 8]. For ab initio forces they write F = F^mod + (F^AI - F^mod): a cheap short-range model force every inner step and the exact difference at outer steps, so the dynamics stays on the exact surface. The speedup holds only if the difference contains no fast, short-range components [Eq 9 and following text].

### Goal: What limits how coarse the slow part can be in time?
Accuracy and energy conservation hold up to a 2.5 fs outer step and degrade significantly at 3 fs. RESPA-type integrators suffer nonlinear resonance, with a limit Delta T_max = tau/pi tied to the fastest oscillation period tau (about 2.7 fs here); special Langevin thermostats could push it toward 10-20 fs [Results text around the energy-drift figure; Conclusions]. A model force that leaves fast components in the difference force caps the outer step and removes the speedup [Theory, after Eq 9].

### Goal: How is the near/far split constructed?
(1) Fragments: independent ab initio gradients per water molecule, optionally plus TIP3P Lennard-Jones/Coulomb between fragments, whose cost is linear in system size, so the short-range force is "essentially free" for large systems [Theory, Eq 10]. (2) Range separation: the Coulomb operator 1/r = erfc(omega r)/r + erf(omega r)/r. The short-range attenuated part is evaluated every inner step and the full operator at outer steps; omega = 0.33 Bohr^-1 works [Theory; Table 1 caption]. Measured speedup is up to 4.4x (MTS-LJFRAG, 2.5 fs outer step) [Table 1].
