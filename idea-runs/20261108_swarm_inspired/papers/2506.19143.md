# Thought Anchors: Which LLM Reasoning Steps Matter?

## What this paper does
Analyzes reasoning traces (mainly R1-Distill-Qwen-14B on MATH, plus Qwen3-30B-A3B on MMLU) at the sentence level. It measures each sentence's counterfactual importance by resampling, finds "receiver heads" that focus attention from later sentences onto a few earlier ones, and builds a sentence-to-sentence causal graph by suppressing attention to one sentence and measuring the KL change of later sentences' logits. Planning and uncertainty-management sentences are the thought anchors; computation sentences matter less to the final answer and receive little receiver-head attention.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: How are sentence-to-sentence dependencies measured, and how well is the cheap measure validated?
Attention suppression: all attention (all heads) to a sentence is masked, and the effect on each later sentence is the mean token log-KL between logits with and without the mask, normalized by that later sentence's average effect from all prior sentences [full text, Section 5.1; Appendix M]. It is "mostly equivalent to omitting a sentence from a CoT". It correlates positively with the resampling-based sentence-to-sentence measure and costs about 100x less [Section 5.1; Appendix N]. In the case study the graph links a verification decision to the result it later produces, with "most of the intermediate text ... performing arithmetic" in between [Section 5.2].

### Goal: Which sentences receive downstream attention, and do computation sentences receive it?
Receiver heads are identified by the kurtosis of the attention each sentence receives from sentences at least four sentences later; the head ranking is reliable across problem halves (r = .84) and receiver heads mostly agree on which sentences they attend (r = .56) [Sections 4, 4.1]. "Plan generation and uncertainty management sentences consistently receive the most attention via receiver heads, whereas active computation sentences receive relatively minimal attention" [Section 4.2, Fig 5]. So downstream attention marks planning anchors, not computed values.

### Goal: What structure do the causal links of math CoTs show, and what limitations are stated?
Accurate, mathematical CoTs have stronger close-range links (distance 1-2 sentences) and weaker long-range links (16-64) [Section 6.2, Fig 7]. Limitations: downstream sentences may be overdetermined by "independent sufficient causes", receiver-head scores are confounded by sentence position, and attention suppression feeds the model out-of-distribution input [Section 8].
