# DiscQuant: A Quantization Method for Neural Networks Inspired by Discrepancy Theory

## What this paper does
Treats weight rounding (given any grid) as a discrepancy problem: if the gradients of the loss over the data are approximately low rank, one can round all but O(m) weights so that m linear constraints (first-order loss changes) are nearly preserved. The practical algorithm minimizes a KL-distillation loss plus a linear term over interpolation variables x in [0,1]^n by projected SGD, landing near a vertex of the constraint polytope, then rounds leftovers by RTN. Beats GPTQ and RTN on Phi-3-mini and Llama-3.1-8B weights.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Is dependent / discrepancy-style rounding (jointly choosing up/down directions to preserve linear functionals) already used in LLM quantization, and against what comparator?
Yes, for weights. The paper frames rounding as finding a vertex of K = [0,1]^n ∩ V, where V is the affine subspace preserving the gradient constraints; the Lovett-Meka algorithm gives provable guarantees and DiscQuant is the heuristic version [full text, Sections 3, 4, Appendix B]. Its comparators are RTN and GPTQ, the main LLM rounding method, which minimizes ||WX - ŴX||² with OBS-style error feedback [Section 2.2]. The constraints are data-derived (gradients on calibration samples), offline, and the method needs backpropagation, so it is not an online KV-write rule.
