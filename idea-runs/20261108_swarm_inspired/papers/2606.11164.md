# ReasonAlloc: Hierarchical Decoding-Time KV Cache Budget Allocation for Reasoning Models

## What this paper does
Training-free, decoding-time non-uniform KV budget allocation for long-CoT reasoning models. An offline probing pass measures each layer's token demand (smallest top-attention set reaching mass rho = 0.93) and preallocates layer budgets (power-smoothed, clipped to [0.25, 2] x mean); an online step reallocates per-head budgets every 128 tokens by real-time utility. Instantiated on R-KV's importance-minus-redundancy scorer. Beats uniform R-KV, SnapKV and Pyramid-RKV on MATH-500/AIME24 with R1-Distill-Llama-8B, R1-Distill-Qwen-14B and AceReason-14B, most at small budgets.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it allocate decode-time KV budgets non-uniformly across layers for long-CoT, and how is per-layer demand measured?
Yes. Raw demand per layer = number of top tokens reaching cumulative attention mass rho during generation; normalized across layers, task-invariant within a model ("Reasoning Wave": high shallow, low oscillating middle, spiking deep) and model-specific [full text, Sections 4.1-4.3, Findings 1-2]. Offline layer preallocation via a robustification operator R with gamma = 0.5 and clipping [0.25 B-bar, 2 B-bar] [Section 5.2, Eqs 1-3]; head budgets rerouted online every Delta = 128 tokens [Section 5.3]. Because of the 0.25x floor, no layer is ever dropped entirely.
### Goal: How large are the gains over uniform budgets at tight budgets on reasoning?
R1-Llama-8B: MATH-500 at budget 512, 82.50 vs 76.48 (uniform R-KV) vs 63.62 (SnapKV); AIME24 at budget 256, 20.00 vs 10.42 (R-KV); vs Pyramid-RKV 49.17 vs 39.17 on AIME24 at 1024 [Section 6.2]. avg over 8 samples, 32K max generation [Section 6.1]. Dense models only, no hybrids.
