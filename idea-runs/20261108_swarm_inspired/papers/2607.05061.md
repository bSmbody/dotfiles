# KVpop -- Key-Value Cache Compression with Predictive Online Pruning

## What this paper does
A post-training sparse-attention retrofit for dense reasoning Transformers (Qwen3-4B/8B). Each KV head keeps 4 sink tokens, a protected window of the w most recent tokens, and a learned long-range top-k cache. Lightweight per-head scorers are trained against a future-attention target at the eviction boundary (computed with a transposed-attention pass). A stateful mLSTM scorer can delay scoring until a token leaves the protected window, so the keep/drop decision uses near-future context. It retains 94-99% of dense AIME/HMMT performance at 75-88% KV compression.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it already postpone the irreversible keep/evict commitment to gather evidence after a token is written, and how long is the deferral?
Yes. The paper's framing is exactly this: methods with a protected window "delay eviction but not the eviction decision itself. Retention of a given token is decided at insertion time, forgoing the evidence that accumulates while the token remains in the protected window" [full text, Section 1]. Its stateful scorer updates its memory with tokens up to q and only then scores token q-w as it exits the window, "using near-future context relative to the token being scored" [Section 3.3, Eq 10; Appendix E.4, Eq 41]. The window is w=256 tokens, with top-k budgets 2016 and 4032 [Appendix F, Table 3]. The window is funded from the KV budget itself; there is no recurrent-state term (dense models only; hybrid dense-sparse layers named as future work) [Section 5, Limitations].
### Goal: How much does the delayed, evidence-informed commitment gain over committing at insertion?
Small. "After 2,000 steps, delayed mLSTM scoring achieves a 0.2-point increase in token accuracy over immediate scoring" [Section 4.2, Figure 5]. The paper gives no end-task (AIME) ablation of the delay and no sweep of w.
