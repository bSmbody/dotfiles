# Arnoldi Singular Vector perturbations for machine learning weather prediction

## What this paper does
Builds an adjoint-free singular-vector (SV) method, Arnoldi-SV (A-SV), that finds the initial-state perturbations which grow fastest under a forecast model. It applies the full nonlinear model repeatedly to perturbed states to build a Krylov subspace, then takes the leading right singular vectors. Applied to the 24h Pangu-Weather ML model (initialised from ICON analyses, Dec 2024 to Feb 2025), A-SV turns random noise into flow-dependent perturbations that grow from the first forecast step, whereas random perturbations are damped. The paper positions SVs as a basis for initialising ML ensemble prediction systems, and contrasts them with GenCast's per-step diffusion sampling.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: Why do ensemble prediction systems perturb along fastest-growing directions instead of adding random noise?
Error growth depends on local dynamics, so "random noise perturbations do not necessarily grow". They grow only in unstable regions, or after slowly self-organising into flow-dependent patterns over days [full text, Section 1]. SVs are "orthonormal flow-dependent perturbation vectors, which point in the directions of the fastest error growth". Prior work found that adding SVs to initial-condition uncertainty "decreases the predictability time by about 1 day", meaning the ensemble spreads realistically [Section 1]. Formally, growth over a window is governed by the eigenvectors of A^T A, the right SVs of the local propagator A [Section 3.1, Eq 1].

### Goal: What happens to random versus SV perturbations in a learned (ML) forecast model?
Pangu-Weather "heavily dampens the amplitudes of random perturbations already in the first forecast time step" (negative mean exponential growth rate). Random perturbations need about four days to recover their initial amplitude, while A-SV perturbations "grow right from the beginning of the forecast" [full text, Section 5.4, Figs 6-7]. The authors attribute the damping to the model's small latent space acting as an implicit filter that removes unpredictable modes. Growing perturbations "concentrate in a few sensitive areas related to dynamically active circulation structures; in most places errors do not significantly grow" [Section 5.4].

### Goal: How do re-sampled rollouts (GenCast) differ from one-shot perturbed ensembles, and what limits are stated?
GenCast draws a fresh stochastic increment at every 12h step, so "the longest untouched forecast rollout time is 12h". This keeps the forecast active and less prone to regression to the mean, but K members need K stochastic trajectories. An SV ensemble needs only one SV computation at initial time [full text, Section 4]. Limits: the Krylov dimension is small compared with the ~13M perturbed variables, so diversity among the SVs is limited [Section 5.4]. Building actual ensembles and checking reliability is left to future work [Sections 4, 6].
