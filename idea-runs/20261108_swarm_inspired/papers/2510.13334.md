# Taming the Fragility of KV Cache Eviction in LLM Inference (DefensiveKV)

## What this paper does
ICLR 2026 prompt-cache eviction method. Argues the "stability assumption" (a fixed subset of entries stays important) is fragile, so mean aggregation of importance over an observation window fails in rare but severe cases. Defensive aggregation: take the maximum observed importance per entry (worst-case risk; max over heads in a GQA group), then apply an adaptive head-level prior correction R_i = max(R̃_i, mean_i R̃_i). Layer-DefensiveKV adds layer-wise budgets. Used by QEvict as an eviction baseline.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the scoring/aggregation rule and why?
Importance I_{j,i} = A_{j,i}·||v_i W_O|| over 32 recent observation queries [full text, Sec 3.2]. With 50% eviction on Llama-3.1-8B GovReport, retained importance averages 0.92 but drops sharply at some steps; defensive aggregation raises worst-case retained importance from 0.33 to 0.61 and removes 65 outlier instances [Sec 3.2-3.3, Fig 3, Table 1]. O(n), same cost as mean aggregation [Sec 3.3].
### Goal: In what regime is it evaluated and against what?
Context compressed once before the question arrives (question-agnostic), Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-32B on LongBench (16 datasets) and RULER; baselines StreamingLLM, SnapKV, AdaKV, CAKE, CriticalKV, DuoAttention [Sec 4.1]. Reported: quality loss reduced 2.3× (DefensiveKV) and 4.3× (Layer-DefensiveKV) vs strongest baseline at 20% cache [Abstract]. No decode-time eviction and no reasoning benchmarks.
### Goal: Does it combine with quantization?
Only a simple stack: 40% retention then HF/Quanto INT4 (10% memory), LongBench avg 49.21→48.55 [App E, Table 5]. The worst-case-risk idea is relevant to precision allocation under attention drift (cf. F11: 63–85% of heads shift focus), but the paper does not test it there.
