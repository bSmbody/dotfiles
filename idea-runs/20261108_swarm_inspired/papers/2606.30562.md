# Morphing into Hybrid Attention Models

## What this paper does
FlashMorph: Transformer-to-hybrid conversion (Qwen3 series, GLA and GDN linear branches) that treats layer selection as a budget-constrained joint subset optimization. It pairs every frozen full-attention layer with a hidden-state-aligned linear branch, mixes them with one scalar gate per layer, and optimizes only the gates on synthetic passkey retrieval data with an L1-style linearization penalty; top-K gates keep full attention, followed by standard distillation and long-context finetuning. Claims better RULER than KL-LS, HALO, PostNAS-style and uniform baselines at ~20M selection tokens.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to decide which layers keep full attention, and on what data/objective is that decision scored?
Per-layer scalar gate alpha_l mixes H_full and H_lin (Eq 9); all weights frozen, only gates trained with L_align = hidden-state L2 to the teacher at the answer-token positions of synthetic passkey documents plus lambda * sum(alpha) (Eqs 10-12); discretize by top-K of alpha [full text, Section 3.3]. The input is a teacher-forced prefill of a fixed document; supervision is only at the passkey answer positions. A variant with generic LM supervision is weaker on RULER (57.2 vs 59.0 GLA; 61.6 vs 64.7 GDN) [Section 4.4, Fig 4(b)].

### Goal: What does it report as its limitation, and does it evaluate long generation or reasoning?
It argues layerwise (isolated) scoring ignores inter-layer dependence [Section 3.1]. Evaluation is long-context retrieval (RULER), commonsense and recall-intensive tasks; no long chain-of-thought reasoning benchmark is used [Section 1 contributions, Section 4.4]. The ratio is still a user-set budget (6:1, 3:1, 1:1) [Section 4.4]. No explicit limitations section was found in the body.
