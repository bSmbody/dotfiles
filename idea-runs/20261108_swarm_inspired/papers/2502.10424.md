# QuantSpec: Self-Speculative Decoding with Hierarchical Quantized KV Cache

## What this paper does
QuantSpec is a self-speculative decoding method in which the draft is the target model with INT4 weights and an INT4 KV cache. The INT8 KV cache that the target uses is stored as an upper and a lower INT4 half (hierarchical KV). The draft reads only the upper 4 bits; verification reads both halves as INT8. The draft therefore needs no separate KV copy. A double full-precision buffer of size 2G keeps the most recent tokens in FP16, so that rejected tokens can be rolled back without re-quantizing. The paper also contributes custom INT4/INT8 attention kernels. Evaluation uses LLaMA-2-7B-32K-Instruct and LWM-Text-Chat-128k on PG-19, InfiniteBench-Sum and Multi-LexSum, at 4K-128K prompt lengths.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What are the headline speedups and the evaluation setting?
1.61x to 2.08x on Multi-LexSum at 8K and 32K prompts, and up to 2.49x at 128K [Section 5.2, Table 3]. Setup: batch size 1, 8x RTX A6000, output capped at 90 tokens, gamma tuned per dataset-model pair [Section 5.1, Table 3 caption]. QuantSpec beats StreamingLLM and SnapKV self-speculation (KV budget = 1/4 of context) on acceptance, most clearly on summarization, "where the whole context is important, sparse KV cache methods are much more lossy" [Section 5.2, App H]. Kernel level: INT4 attention is 2.88x faster than FP16 FlashAttention at 64K [Table 4].

### Goal: How does the gain depend on context length and batch?
Ablation: at short contexts most of the gain comes from weight quantization; at long contexts KV quantization dominates [Section 5.3, Fig 4]. In the arithmetic-intensity analysis, long-context decoding is memory-bound "irrespective of the batch size", because attention does not batch [Section 3.1.2]. End-to-end numbers exist only for bs=1 [Table 3].

### Goal: Is losslessness exact, and what are the limitations?
The verifier runs on an INT8 KV cache, not FP16. The paper supports INT8 ≈ FP16 only with LLaMA-2-7B perplexity (6.4595 vs 6.4696 on WikiText2) [Section 4.2, Table 2]. Outputs are therefore lossless relative to the INT8-KV model, not the FP16 one. There is no limitations section. Untested: long generation (output capped at 90 tokens), batch > 1, and reasoning models. The authors leave the combination with sparse KV to future work [Section 2.3].
