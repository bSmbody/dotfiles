# Look-Ahead Screening Rules for the Lasso

## What this paper does
Short paper extending Gap Safe screening for the Lasso path: instead of screening predictors only for the next lambda on the regularization path, it solves the Gap Safe inequality for the range of future lambda values over which a predictor is guaranteed inactive, and screens it for that whole stretch of the path. On the leukemia data, the first step discards 99.6% of predictors for steps up to 5 and 99.3% up to 10. Outperforms the active warm-start version of Gap Safe rules in path-fitting time.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does look-ahead screening compute, per predictor, how long it stays certifiably inactive, and what does it cost?
The Gap Safe rule discards predictor j at lambda* if |X^T theta_lambda|_j + ||x_j|| sqrt(G(beta_lambda, theta_lambda; lambda*)/lambda*^2) < 1, a ball test whose radius comes from the duality gap [full text, Section 2, Eq 4]. Because this inequality is quadratic in lambda*, its boundary points come from the quadratic formula, giving for each currently-inactive predictor a range of upcoming lambdas for which it must stay inactive [Section 2]. "All of the quantities involved in the rule are available as a by-product of solving the problem at the previous step, which means that the costs of look-ahead screening are diminutive" [Section 2]. Effectiveness is greatest early in the path and shrinks later (at step 20 nothing is discarded) [Section 2, Fig 1]. The authors note the idea is general to any safe rule [Section 4].
