# Retrospective Sparse Attention for Efficient Long-Context Generation

## What this paper does
RetroAttention runs on top of Quest-style dynamic sparse attention. KV pages loaded for the current query are used to supplement the attention outputs of up to n earlier queries that did not see them. A small output cache holds those earlier outputs, and they are merged by the FlashAttention LSE identity. The revised outputs are re-embedded in the next layer, where they overwrite that layer's stale K/V entries. Evaluated on LongGenBench, AIME24/GPQA/LiveCodeBench (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) and PG-19.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How does it apply late-arriving attention contributions, and to which token?
The late contribution goes to the *original* query's token, not to a later one. The supplementary attention output for a past query is merged into its cached output, O_org → O_up [full text, Section 2.3, App C]. In layer l+1 the updated outputs are re-projected, and their K/V overwrite the outdated entries, so "all subsequent decoding steps automatically leverage these changes" [Section 2.4, Fig 4]. With n = 1 to 7 future queries, effective KV exposure grows 1.17x to 1.60x [Section 2.2]. The paper reports up to 21.9% higher accuracy than SOTA KV compression methods [Abstract].
### Goal: What does it say about error accumulation in long generation?
Long-output tasks accumulate attention errors because the model conditions on its own imperfect outputs. Short-output long-input tasks show "far less error accumulation" [App, around the LongBench vs LongGenBench discussion].
