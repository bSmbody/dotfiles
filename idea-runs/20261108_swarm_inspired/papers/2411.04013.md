# kNN Attention Demystified: A Theoretical Exploration for Scalable Transformers

## What this paper does
Theory paper on kNN attention. It rewrites each attention row as an expectation under the softmax distribution and approximates it by sampling with lazy Gumbel noise, where the top sqrt(n) inner products are found by k-MIPS/kNN search and the tail is handled by Gumbel sampling; it gives (epsilon, delta) forward guarantees and sub-quadratic estimators for attention gradients via Markov-chain sampling. Experiments are small: random inputs, a character-level nanoGPT on Shakespeare, and GPT-2 XL fine-tuning on one L40.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does kNN attention train a learned selector, or does it use top-k plus sampling only to approximate attention and its gradients?
Only to approximate. The top-k set is obtained by a fixed k-MIPS reduction to kNN search over the keys, not a learned scorer [full text, Section 2.2.1 / Eq 9]; sampling via lazy Gumbel noise estimates the softmax expectation [Section 2.1-2.2, Theorem 2]; backward-pass estimators approximate dV etc. by random-walk simulation [Section 3, Theorem 3]. Its LLM experiments test whether models tolerate approximate attention in training (char-level nanoGPT, GPT-2 XL fine-tuning) [Section 4.3]; nothing about learning which blocks to select or correcting a selector's misses.
