# OasisKV: Scaling In-Decode KV Cache Beyond HBM with Lookahead Sparse Prefetching

## What this paper does
A KV-offload serving system. HBM holds per-request working sets plus Quest-style min/max block summaries, and the full KV sits in host or remote tiers. A speculative draft token gives an approximate next-step query ("lookahead attention"), which ranks blocks and prefetches the next step's top-K during the current step. Evaluated on Qwen3-8B/235B, including an AIME24 reasoning workload, with single- and multi-node serving.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How is the next step's far demand predicted, and how accurate is it?
The draft token is propagated through the normal token's resident sparse KV, and its query scores the block summaries. Its top-K agrees with the exact next-token set 98.28-99.69% across the 36 layers of Qwen3-8B (GSM8K, Eagle-3) [full text, Section 3.1, App D]. Attention is still sparse top-K under the fresh query.
### Goal: What do host-memory capacity and PCIe bandwidth do to offload designs at large batch?
Host DRAM must hold the full context of every live request. The batch multiplier over HBM-only serving approaches the block size, e.g. 12.8x at 128K, so host DRAM is "quickly exhausted" [Section 2.3, Eq 2, Fig 4a]. If step-wide transfer time exceeds model-forward time, decode stalls [Section 2.4]. On AIME24 with Qwen3-8B: 1.51x-2.26x over dense vLLM as K goes 192 → 64, with avg@32 falling 76.67 → 71.67; at K=128 throughput is 2,300 vs 1,235 tok/s [Section 4.2.3].
