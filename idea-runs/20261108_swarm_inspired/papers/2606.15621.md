# Re-feeding Is Not Replaying: Measuring Replay Noise in Counterfactual Token-Credit Estimation

## What this paper does
Measures how replay procedure affects per-token counterfactual credit (cut the rollout at a low-margin pivot token, substitute the best alternative, sample continuations with hashed seeds, compare graded outcomes) on GSM8K with vLLM. It compares continuations resumed from the verified decode-time KV state, a bit-identical replica pass, and a fresh re-feed of the prefix. Re-feed changes credit signs far above the replica floor; batch-invariant kernels remove all disagreement; and the replica floor itself is large.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How reliable are single-sample counterfactual outcome differences under seeded replay, and what makes replay exact?
Credit is A_t = mean_k R(prefix, a, c_k) - mean_k R(prefix, a', c'_k) with seeds hashed from (problem, rollout, pivot, arm, k), so all passes share sampling randomness [full text, Section 3.1]. Re-feeding the prefix flips the credit sign at 14-28 pp above the replica floor; most changes are zero-crossings, and the perturbation is mean-zero [Section 5.1]. Under VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1 all three passes are identical on all 395 pivots [Section 5.4]. Even on stock kernels, two bit-identical request batches with identical seeds "disagree on 9-23% of eligible per-pivot estimates and diverge on 2-14% of greedy probes", so "single-sample estimates at low-margin tokens are uninterpretable under any replay method on stock engines" [Section 5.5]. Batch invariance is therefore achievable in vLLM, but only removes numeric noise, not genuine state differences.
