# SARATHI: Efficient LLM Inference by Piggybacking Decodes with Chunked Prefills

## What this paper does
Observes that prefill saturates GPU compute even at batch 1, while decode is memory-bound at practical batch sizes because KV memory caps the batch. It proposes chunked prefills (split a prompt into equal compute-sized chunks) and decode-maximal batching (one prefill chunk plus as many decodes as fit), fusing the linear layers of chunk and decode tokens into one matmul so decode tokens reuse weights already fetched for the prefill. Attention for the chunk and the decodes is computed separately. Implemented on nanoGPT; up to 10x decode throughput and 1.33x end-to-end throughput for LLaMA-13B on A6000, 1.25x for LLaMA-33B on A100, and 6.29x fewer pipeline bubbles.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: How much compute does decode leave idle, and what is the marginal cost of mixing prefill tokens into a decode batch?
Decode per-token cost is 200x, 100x and 16.7x that of prefill at batch 1, 2 and 18 (LLaMA-13B, A6000, seq 1024); "the incremental cost of linear operators for decode is almost zero as batch size increases", while decode attention "does not benefit from batch size as it is memory-bound" [full text, Section 3.1, Fig 3]. Arithmetic intensity of decode ops is more than two orders of magnitude below prefill; decode becomes compute-intensive only around batch 256, which KV memory makes infeasible [Section 3.1, Fig 4]. Table 2: a decode-only batch of 4 takes 49.96 ms; prefill-only (4x1024) 234.8 ms; a mixed batch (1021 prefill + 3 decodes) 238.4 ms, so per-token decode cost drops from 12.49 to 1.2 ms [Section 4.3.1, Table 2]. Note the framing is decodes riding on a prefill; the paper does not measure the converse (how many prefill tokens a large decode batch absorbs at no latency cost).
### Goal: What mechanism fuses prefill and decode, and how is the chunk size chosen?
Linear operations of the chunk and decodes are fused into one matmul; attention runs separately (batched decode attention, separate chunk attention) [Section 4.3.1]. Chunk size trades prefill efficiency against how many decodes can piggyback (the P:D ratio); the sum of chunk size and decode count should be a multiple of the matmul tile size (128) to avoid tile-quantization waste, e.g. a 1-token overshoot past 256 raised iteration time by 32% [Section 4.4, Fig 7].
