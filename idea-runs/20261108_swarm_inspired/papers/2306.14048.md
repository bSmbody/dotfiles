# H2O: Heavy-Hitter Oracle for Efficient Generative Inference of Large Language Models

## What this paper does
Observes that attention in pretrained LLMs is >95% sparse and that accumulated attention scores follow a power law, so a few "heavy-hitter" tokens dominate. Proposes H2O, a fixed-size KV cache policy that keeps recent tokens plus the tokens with the highest accumulated attention, evicting at most one entry per step. Formulates eviction as a dynamic submodular problem and gives a greedy guarantee under a submodularity assumption. Evaluated on OPT/LLaMA/GPT-NeoX with up to 20% cache budget, with large throughput gains in FlexGen.

## Goal-by-goal answers

### Goal: What signal does it use to decide which KV entries to keep, evict, or recall, and is the decision reversible?
Accumulated (summed) attention that each cached token has received from all previous queries ("local H2" statistic), plus a recency window [full text, Section 4.1, Definition 4.3]. Each step the new token is added and the entry whose removal least reduces the score function is evicted; evicted entries are deleted permanently (the policy maps S_{i-1} to S_i with |S_i|=k and the evicted KV is set to zero) [full text, Section 2.1, Definitions 2.1-2.2].

### Goal: What does it report as its failure mode or limitation for tokens that are needed later (long chain-of-thought or long context)?
The paper notes that Belady-style optimal eviction is not applicable because "once evicting important KVs, it could destroy the performance of LLMs due to the sequential dependency of LLM generation" [full text, Section 3.2]. It relies on the empirical claim that local (past) accumulated attention is "equally effective as taking into account the attention of future tokens" [full text, Section 4.1], shown on short-generation OPT tasks, not long reasoning.

### Goal: What method assumption does its scoring or training rely on (per-token additive utility, per-head independence, teacher-forced or offline training)?
Per-token scalar score (accumulated attention), computed per head; the near-optimality guarantee assumes the attention scheme is submodular [full text, Lemma 3.1, Definition 4.1]. No training. The score is backward-looking: a token's past attention mass is used as a proxy for its future use.
