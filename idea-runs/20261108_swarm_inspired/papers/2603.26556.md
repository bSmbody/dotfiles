# When Perplexity Lies: Generation-Focused Distillation of Hybrid Sequence Models

## What this paper does
GenDistill: a multi-stage pipeline distilling Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B into a Hybrid Kimi-Delta-Attention student (7 of 28 layers kept as frozen attention), ablating objective, masking, data, freezing and mixer type under both log-likelihood and generation-based evaluation. Main finding: perplexity/log-likelihood evaluation hides large generation gaps and can reverse design-choice rankings (e.g., QRWKV6-7B is within 0.2 pp of its teacher under log-likelihood but 20.8 pp behind under generation).

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What mechanism does it use to choose which layers keep attention, and is that signal generation-aware?
Beam Search (Add) from a fully-KDA student, restoring attention layers and keeping configurations with lowest perplexity averaged over synthetic associative recall, C-EVAL and FineWeb-EDU [full text, Section 3.1.1]. The authors explicitly call perplexity a "cheap comparative proxy" for an undertrained scaffold and separate it from the final-evaluation use they criticize [Section 3.1.1; Appendix E]. They note optimal layer selection is task-dependent [Section 3.1.1]. Appendix E describes PostNAS as the generation-aware but expensive end (50B tokens, ~800 H100 GPU-hours) and GA-S2 as ~7-26B tokens.

### Goal: What does it show about perplexity vs generation for hybrid students, and what limitation does it state on long context?
Under perplexity four student configs sit within 3.8 pp of the teacher; under generation they open 4.6-14.7 pp gaps and re-rank (SFT looks fine under PPL but is worst under generation) [Section 5.1, Figure 3]. Freezing attention changes generation by 5.2 pp with <1 pp PPL change [Section 6]. Limitations: distillation at 4K does not transfer; Hybrid-KDA keeps 55% of teacher LongBench and degrades on NIAH beyond 16K [Section 6.1]. It suggests on-policy distillation on student-generated responses as a remedy [Section 6.1].
