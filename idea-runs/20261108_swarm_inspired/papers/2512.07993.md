# SkipKV: Selective Skipping of KV Generation and Storage for Efficient Inference with Large Reasoning Models

## What this paper does
Training-free sentence-level KV management for reasoning models. (1) Evicts whole sentences scored by pairwise sentence-embedding similarity (semantic redundancy) instead of single tokens; (2) adds a SEAL-style steering vector (execution minus non-execution thought hidden states) at newline delimiters, with strength adapted to the count of non-execution thoughts, to suppress unnecessary reflection; (3) groups requests by prefill length to cut padding in multi-batch eviction. Evaluated on R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B and R1-Distill-Llama-8B over MATH-500, AIME24, GSM8K, LCB.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does token eviction lengthen generation, and why?
Yes. With R1-Qwen-7B at batch 10 on MATH-500, R-KV generates longer sequences than FullKV at all budgets [full text, Section 3, Fig. 3 right]. Token-level eviction keeps fragments of numbers or of the final answer (e.g., "(6,9)→(,9)"), prompting repeated re-derivation and self-validation [full text, Section 3, Fig. 4]. Also, in multi-batch decoding padding tokens consume the fixed budget and distort attention, so R-KV/H2O lose accuracy at batch 10 vs batch 1 [full text, Section 3, Fig. 3 left/center].

### Goal: What protocol and results on accuracy and length?
Max generation 8,192 (GSM8K, MATH-500), 10,000 (LCB), 16,384 (AIME24); budget ratio = budget / mean FullKV length per model–dataset (R-KV convention); batch 10 [full text, Section 5.1–5.2]. SkipKV matches FullKV on AIME24 with 6.7× less KV on R1-Qwen-14B, ≥4× on all models; prior eviction methods always generate more tokens than FullKV, while SkipKV is up to 28% shorter than FullKV and 32/39/48% shorter than R-KV on the three models [full text, Section 5.2, Fig. 10]. Part of the length reduction comes from steering, which changes model behaviour, not just compression.

### Goal: How is throughput measured?
GSM8K, R1-Qwen-7B, max 8,192, budget 512, one A100-40GB, FlashAttention-2, batch swept to the memory-bound "transition point": eviction allows up to 2.8× larger batch; R-KV 7.6× and SkipKV 9.6× over FullKV; SkipKV beats R-KV by up to 1.7× at the same batch thanks to shorter outputs [full text, Section 5.2, Table 1]. Measured in samples per minute (close to a per-problem cost), but not per correct answer, and on HF-style batching rather than a paged serving engine.
