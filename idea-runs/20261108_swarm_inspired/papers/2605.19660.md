# OScaR: The Occam's Razor for Extreme KV Cache Quantization in LLMs and Beyond

## What this paper does
Argues the bottleneck of per-channel INT2 key quantization is Token Norm Imbalance (TNI): shared per-channel scales must span tokens with very different L2 norms. Plain token-wise rescaling fails because low-norm tokens become artificial outliers ("Scaling-Induced Outlier Artifact"). OScaR = online Hadamard on Q and K ("Canalized Rotation") then token-wise L2 normalization of K ("Omni-Token Scaling") before per-channel INT2, inverted at dequantization; V gets an offline Hadamard fused into W_O and per-token INT2. Three CUDA kernels; training-free; text, VLM and omni models. Not to be confused with OSCAR (2605.17757), a different 2-bit rotation method.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What is the mechanism and its cost?
Online FHT on Q and K, token-wise norm scaling on K, per-channel quantization with residual length 128, group 32; V offline Hadamard + per-token quantization [full text, Sec 3.2-3.3, Alg 1]. Prefill cost per token ≈ 2d log2 h + 3d + 5d ops [App L].
### Goal: What accuracy is reported, and on what tasks?
Only long-input understanding/retrieval and multimodal tasks: LongBench-E INT2 avg Llama-3.1-8B 41.75 vs 16-bit 41.70 (KIVI 39.84, OTT 40.74, TurboQuant+ 2.5-bit 40.03); Qwen3-8B 48.74 vs 49.56; NIAH 96.5% vs 16-bit 96.0% [Sec 4.2, Table 1]. Baselines KIVI, OTT, QuaRot, RotateKV, TurboQuant+ (community implementation) [Sec 4.1]. No long-CoT generation benchmarks (AIME/MATH) appear in the main text, so its behaviour under decode-time error accumulation is untested.
### Goal: What efficiency is reported?
On one H20 with Qwen3-8B vs BF16 FlashDecoding-v2: up to 3.0× decode speedup at 128K (batch 1); at 4K context, batch 48, 5.3× lower memory and 4.1× throughput [Sec 4.3, Fig 6]. Code released (github ZunhaiSu/OScaR-KV-Quant). Cited by QuantMLA and RAQ-MLA.
