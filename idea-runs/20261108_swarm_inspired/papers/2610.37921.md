# FAD-KV: Adaptive Dictionary KV Caching for Efficient Reasoning Decoding

## What this paper does
Dictionary-based sparse KV compression (each K/V ≈ D·y with ||y||_0 ≤ s from an offline 4096-atom dictionary trained on WikiText-103 activations; Lexico lineage). FAD-KV replaces full OMP with a one-shot top-n candidate screen plus a light correction (greedy-marginal or pooled-ridge) and replaces append-only online dictionary growth with fixed-size replacement of inactive atoms (22.9% of atoms stay inactive after prefill), bounding memory.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Which models and reasoning setting are evaluated — are these long-CoT reasoning models?
No. Models are Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-4B-Instruct (non-thinking) on GSM8K, MATH500, GPQA-D, LongReason, plus RULER and LongBench; three runs each; baselines Lexico and Lexico-ADP [full text, Section 5.1–5.2]. The paper frames the result as a quality–efficiency trade-off, not maximum accuracy, since append-style baselines use more memory [full text, Section 5].

### Goal: What efficiency is reported?
Under a 24 GB memory budget at the largest batch that fits, Llama-3.2-3B: 1.16× at 4K context (324 vs 280 tok/s; 9.3 vs 21.7 GB) and 2.0× at 16K (141 vs 71 tok/s) [full text, Section 5.5]. No generation-length, loop or serving-engine evaluation; not a long-CoT (16K+) thinking-model study.
