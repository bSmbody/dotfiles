# Fast Spatial Memory with Elastic Test-Time Training

## What this paper does
The paper adds an elastic weight consolidation (EWC) style regularizer to Large-Chunk Test-Time Training (LaCT) fast weights. After each chunk's fast-weight update, the weights are pulled back toward an anchor (an EMA of past fast weights) in proportion to a per-parameter Fisher-style importance, which is itself an EMA over chunks. Multi-chunk inference over long 4D video sequences then stays stable. The resulting Fast Spatial Memory (FSM) model is applied to 4D novel-view synthesis, where elasticity narrows the train/test gap.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: Does it protect fast-weight memory from forgetting, and what importance signal does it use (reads or writes)?
Yes. The consolidation step is theta_{c+1} = theta'_c - lambda F_c ⊙ (theta'_c - theta*_c), applied at chunk boundaries [full text, Section 2.3 / Eq 5]. Importance F is an EMA over chunks of phi(S_c), where S_c is the chunk's own update theta'_c - theta_c. For MAS/EWC, phi is |.| or squared [Eq 6]. In SI form the update is multiplied by its drift from the anchor. So importance comes from the magnitude of past *write* updates, elementwise per parameter. The paper uses no read-side or query-side signal and no subspace (key-space) structure. It reads MAS/SI only as ways to estimate update magnitudes.
### Goal: In what setting is it evaluated, and what does it show?
It is evaluated on 4D reconstruction and novel-view synthesis (Stereo4D, 12-layer ablations, about 32B tokens) [Section 4]. Streaming-EMA anchors work best, and consolidation narrows the train/test PSNR gap [Sections 4.1-4.2]. There is no language modelling, no associative recall and no linear-attention/delta-rule state. With a single chunk, consolidation reduces to a negligible second-order term [Section 4.1].
