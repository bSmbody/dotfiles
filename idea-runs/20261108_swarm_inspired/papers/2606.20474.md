# UltraQuant: 4-bit KV Caching for Context-Heavy Agents

## What this paper does
Industry study (AMD, EMNLP 2026 Industry) of 4-bit KV caching for multi-turn agents in vLLM on MI355X. Uses TurboQuant-style Walsh-Hadamard rotation plus codebook (Ultra-TQ, an optimized TQ4/4 whose decode kernel is upstreamed into vLLM) as the quality anchor, then replaces the codebook by hardware FP4 (UltraQuant: FP8 queries, FP4 KV, UE8M0 group scales, native scaled-MFMA on CDNA4) with asymmetric K/V treatment and QJL removed. Evaluated on SWE-bench Lite agent loops, retrieval, GPQA/GSM8K/AIME25, and replayed production Claude Code traces.

## Goal-by-goal answers
### Goal: What accuracy and throughput results are reported against FP8 KV and TurboQuant?
Within 2 points of BF16 on every reasoning model-benchmark pair (except +5.58 on Qwen3-32B GPQA) [full text, Section 6.3]. MiniMax-M2.5 32K/1K, C=64: throughput 1.38x BF16 vs hardware FP8 KV 1.37x, at half the KV bytes; median TPOT 1.40x BF16 vs FP8 1.37x, Ultra-TQ 1.58x and vLLM OSS TurboQuant 5.56x [Section 7]. Adaptive-SLO replay: 2.71x (MiniMax-M2.5) and 4.38x (Qwen3-235B) qualified-request throughput of BF16 [abstract]. All KV-compressed runs keep first and last two layers in BF16 [Section 6].
### Goal: What limitations do the authors state?
One global constant c=0.156 for all models/heads; speed benefit over FP8 "only observed when the context length is long enough, or concurrency high enough, to exceed the resident cache capacity of the FP8 baseline"; other frameworks and non-CDNA4 hardware out of scope; centroid calibration helps only ~10% of layers [Section 9]. UltraQuant kernel itself not yet released ("plan to release") [Appendix A.2].
