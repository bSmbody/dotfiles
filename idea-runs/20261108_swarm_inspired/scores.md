
### C4 verdict
- **Verdict**: `reject(reason=patch object and rebase are published as HYPIC; on GDN, combining several states adds nothing measurable over concatenating attention KV and keeping one state; the commutator signal is degenerate at segment lengths far above d_k)`
- **Evidence**:
  - SOURCE GROUNDING, patch theory [1311.3903]: stated correctly. A pushout exists exactly when the two patches touch disjoint places, and conflicts are represented explicitly. The transfer has no counterpart of "disjoint places", though: a 512-2K-token GDN segment has far more keys than the d_k = 128 key dimensions, so for heads with spread-out keys its erasures cover the whole key space.
  - SOURCE GROUNDING, Trotter error [1912.08854]: the mismatch here is that the bound covers splitting the exponential of a SUM of generators. Its commutator bounds the error against a joint evolution that is well defined. Two sibling branches have no such joint evolution: no token sequence corresponds to "both branches happened". So the commutator measures only how much the order of an arbitrary chaining matters, the same quantity as PICASO's order-sensitivity bound (Prop 4 in [2502.17605]). It says nothing about whether content is lost. A counterexample to "near-zero commutator means a lossless merge": when the transitions A1 and A2 are both close to 0, [A1, A2] is close to 0, yet chaining S0·A1·A2 + B1·A2 + B2 is close to B2, so branch 1 is erased completely. I checked this in a toy run (d_k = 128, isotropic unit keys, beta = 0.5, gate = 1, L = 2048): ||A||_F/sqrt(d) = 0.008, ||[A1, A2]||_F = 9e-4, and only 0.7% of B1 survives A2. Real gates below 1 shrink A further.
  - NOVELTY, the patch object (b): HYPIC [2607.01299] already defines and caches exactly the C4 patch for GDN hybrids, as the tuple (segment-cumulative transition T_C, a dense d_k x d_k matrix; zero-start end state S_{C|0}). It accumulates the tuple during prefill at 5-7% overhead and "rebases" cached segments onto any prefix state in constant time (Eq 6). Earlier still, the same pair composed by associative scan is the basic primitive of parallel linear RNNs (Algorithm 1 in [1709.04057]) and of chunkwise WY DeltaNet/GDN [2406.06484, 2412.06464]. Fixed-size segment operators, length independence and rebase are therefore not new. The C4 text cites only Bole/DeltaLog/Tail-Replay and misses this.
  - NOVELTY, composing states from several contexts (a, e): Mamba already has decay-weighted state mixing that uses the scan's cumulative transition, plus mean "state soup" [2406.08423, a task-arithmetic analogue on states]. PICASO [2502.17605] makes the composition of many contexts order-free by averaging over permutations. It needs commuting (diagonal) transitions, finds the composed states out-of-distribution, and needs fine-tuning to match concatenation. What remains new in C4 is narrow: a three-way merge of *sibling* branches from a common ancestor under *non-commuting* GDN transitions, a commutator conflict flag, and doing this mid-generation. PICASO's efficient permutation averaging does not carry over to non-commuting transitions, which leaves C4's "apply erasures once" step with no defined order (unspecified mapping).
  - NOVELTY (c, d): the searches found no prior paper that merges parallel reasoning branches of a recurrent or hybrid model mid-generation; a WebSearch found none either. Tree-speculation work for GDN keeps factors but materialises only the accepted path and discards siblings: TreeWY [2608.20961] (pseudo-values via one triangular solve), Bole [2608.01651], DeltaLog [2608.15533]. HyLaMAS [2610.37779] already runs latent multi-agent collaboration on Qwen3.5 hybrids by passing KV plus GDN state along a *chain* of agents. That weakens the premise that "hybrids cannot be used", which C4 takes from [2611.08483], although HyLaMAS merges no parallel states.
  - VALUE, direct evidence that the trivial baseline ties: LinearKV [2608.11231] runs exactly the user's baseline (concatenate the attention KV of K chunks, keep ONE chunk's linear state) against exact multi-state composition. On GDN hybrids (OLMo-Hybrid, Qwen) "exactness buys nothing": the gap is at most 0.013 F1 and changes sign, and a random block does as well. On Mamba-2, multi-source composition collapses (46.6% vs 86.8% of full quality) and more recompute does not rescue it.
  - VALUE, what the GDN state carries [2609.04434]: on Qwen3.5-4B, rec-only exact retrieval is 0.00 while kv-only keeps 0.89-0.98. Language and persona survive only through the recurrent state. In a state swap, the answer's *value* comes from the KV side and its *language* from the state side (>=99% of items). Branches of one problem share language, format and persona, so merging their GDN states should transfer almost no content. HyLaMAS's channel ablation agrees: 88/87/84/90% for both/KV/state/none, none significant. The state only changed output length.
  - VALUE, horizon: Qwen hybrids rebuilt from a ZERO state by replaying only the last 128 tokens keep >99% of LongBench/RULER quality [2609.33477]. Tail-Replay keeps 92.8-98.9% with a 5-10% suffix [2608.30310]. On Qwen3-Next about 60% of GDN heads have a static horizon under 512 tokens (2.49x prunable at W_max = 512) [2608.30386]. So after a 512-2K segment, the ancestor term S0·A contributes almost nothing in most heads, rebase is effectively a no-op, and a merge's effect on the GDN layers fades within about a hundred decoded tokens. Caveat: all of these are retrieval-heavy benchmarks, not long CoT; DASC notes that states matter more for aggregation and reasoning.
  - CONFLICT SIGNAL, key-space geometry: in Qwen3-Next, the split of GDN heads into near-zero-rank and saturated (full-rank) heads is the same across data domains [2602.02195]. In saturated heads, any segment longer than d_k touches every key direction, so the write footprints of two branches always overlap. Low-rank heads write the same few directions in every context. Either way, overlap or commutator size is set by the head type, not by whether the branches disagree. Two branches on the same problem also draw keys from almost the same distribution (same vocabulary and format). The key assumption ("branches that differ in substance write nearly orthogonal key directions") is therefore very likely false.
  - DOUBLE-COUNTING / OOD: the merge rule is underspecified. "Shared decay once" is undefined when each branch has its own data-dependent cumulative gate gamma_i. Delta-sum merging S0 + sum_i (S_i - S0) gives the ancestor a coefficient of 1 - sum_i (1 - gamma_i), which goes negative once N(1 - gamma) > 1, as it will in short-horizon heads. Summing the B_i superposes N saturated associative memories into a state no token sequence produces. PICASO [2502.17605] and LinearKV [2608.11231] both report that composed multi-source states are out-of-distribution or fragile.
  - MAPPING / CONTRIBUTION TYPE: it is a method (passes the gate). File = state, patch = (T_C, write) and fork = copy are specified, but the pushout and the conflict-resolution policy are not mapped to a defined operation.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The patch object and rebase are already HYPIC [2607.01299], and in GDN hybrids the recurrent state carries style and recent context rather than retrievable content [2609.04434, 2609.33477], with LinearKV [2608.11231] showing that combining several states ties keeping one; so a GDN-state merge can at most match "concatenate branch KV and keep one branch's state", and its commutator flag measures head type and segment length, not disagreement.

#### Failure modes (ranked)
1. **The GDN merge carries no content; the trivial baseline ties (most likely).** Merging the GDN states of sibling branches transfers mode/style, which the branches already share, and not facts or partial results, which live in attention KV. Evidence: rec-only retrieval 0.00 and value-from-KV / style-from-state in the state swap [2609.04434]; GDN exact composition ties a single state [2608.11231]; the GDN channel has no significant accuracy effect in hybrid latent multi-agent runs [2610.37779]. Consequence: C4 equals "append all branches' attention KV pages + keep one branch's state", which needs no patches at all.
2. **The state forgets the ancestor within one segment.** The ancestor term, rebase and "decay counted once" all become numerically irrelevant once segments are hundreds of tokens long. Evidence: a 128-token replay from zero is enough for Qwen [2609.33477]; about 60% of heads have a horizon under 512 [2608.30386]; the toy run gives ||A|| about 0.008 at L = 2048.
3. **The commutator signal is degenerate.** In the long-segment regime it is close to 0 because A is close to 0 (it reports "no conflict" exactly when chaining wipes a branch). Otherwise it is fixed by head type: saturated vs low-rank heads, data-independent [2602.02195]. Either way it does not track branch disagreement. It also measures the arbitrariness of the order, not content loss (Trotter's ground truth does not exist here).
4. **Merged states are out-of-distribution or the merge rule is ill-defined.** The non-commuting erasures need an arbitrary order or a permutation average, and PICASO's efficient form needs commuting transitions [2502.17605]. Delta-sum merging double-subtracts the decay; summed writes inflate norms. Multi-source composition collapses on Mamba-2 [2608.11231], and PICASO needed fine-tuning.
5. **Upstream of C4: unproven value of mid-flight merging itself.** Even with an exact merge, nothing shows that a long-CoT trunk benefits from reading other branches' partial reasoning more than it would from voting or simply continuing the best branch. The external audit raises the same unmeasured premise for C7 (does foreign KV get attended?). C4's GDN machinery cannot fix this, and its attention half is just SwarmEvolve-style KV concatenation [2611.08483].
6. **Deep-layer conditioning error.** Patches are exact only at layer 0. Deeper layers' keys and gates come from each branch's own context, which gives 8.9% state drift even for same-document composition [2607.01299]. The error compounds when branches diverge.
7. **Novelty exposure.** A reviewer will point to HYPIC, PICASO, State Soup and LinearKV; what is left (sibling three-way merge + commutator flag) is the part failure modes 1-3 make least likely to matter.

#### Cheapest falsification experiment
No kernels and no training, about one GPU-day on Qwen3.5-4B (or Qwen3-Next) in HF:
1. Take about 100 AIME/HMMT problems and sample N = 4 branches each from a shared 1-2K-token prefix, 2K tokens per branch, using the plain recurrent mode; the patches come for free as S_i and the ancestor S0.
2. At the merge point, continue decoding to an answer under five conditions:
   - (a) continue the best single branch;
   - (b) append all 4 branches' attention-KV pages (RoPE re-rotated) and keep branch 1's GDN state;
   - (c) the same as (b) but with a C4-style merged GDN state (S0·gamma_max + sum of B_i), with and without the commutator-chosen winner per head;
   - (d) the same as (b) but with the GDN state of an unrelated problem, a negative control;
   - (e) text re-prefill of all branches, the oracle.
3. Log the per-head ||A_i||, the commutator norms and the B_i overlap, and correlate them with whether the branches' final answers agree.

C4 is falsified if (c) is not better than (b) by more than the noise between seeds, or if (d) is about equal to (b): the state would then be irrelevant to content. It is also falsified if the per-head commutator norms have a rank correlation of |rho| < 0.2 with branch answer disagreement, or are explained by head identity alone (a head fixed effect). If (b) is also not better than (a), the merged-branch premise fails for transformers too, and the lineage should be dropped.
