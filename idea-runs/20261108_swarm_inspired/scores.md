
### C4 verdict
- **Verdict**: `reject(reason=patch object and rebase are published as HYPIC; on GDN, combining several states adds nothing measurable over concatenating attention KV and keeping one state; the commutator signal is degenerate at segment lengths far above d_k)`
- **Evidence**:
  - SOURCE GROUNDING, patch theory [1311.3903]: stated correctly. A pushout exists exactly when the two patches touch disjoint places, and conflicts are represented explicitly. The transfer has no counterpart of "disjoint places", though: a 512-2K-token GDN segment has far more keys than the d_k = 128 key dimensions, so for heads with spread-out keys its erasures cover the whole key space.
  - SOURCE GROUNDING, Trotter error [1912.08854]: the mismatch here is that the bound covers splitting the exponential of a SUM of generators. Its commutator bounds the error against a joint evolution that is well defined. Two sibling branches have no such joint evolution: no token sequence corresponds to "both branches happened". So the commutator measures only how much the order of an arbitrary chaining matters, the same quantity as PICASO's order-sensitivity bound (Prop 4 in [2502.17605]). It says nothing about whether content is lost. A counterexample to "near-zero commutator means a lossless merge": when the transitions A1 and A2 are both close to 0, [A1, A2] is close to 0, yet chaining S0·A1·A2 + B1·A2 + B2 is close to B2, so branch 1 is erased completely. I checked this in a toy run (d_k = 128, isotropic unit keys, beta = 0.5, gate = 1, L = 2048): ||A||_F/sqrt(d) = 0.008, ||[A1, A2]||_F = 9e-4, and only 0.7% of B1 survives A2. Real gates below 1 shrink A further.
  - NOVELTY, the patch object (b): HYPIC [2607.01299] already defines and caches exactly the C4 patch for GDN hybrids, as the tuple (segment-cumulative transition T_C, a dense d_k x d_k matrix; zero-start end state S_{C|0}). It accumulates the tuple during prefill at 5-7% overhead and "rebases" cached segments onto any prefix state in constant time (Eq 6). Earlier still, the same pair composed by associative scan is the basic primitive of parallel linear RNNs (Algorithm 1 in [1709.04057]) and of chunkwise WY DeltaNet/GDN [2406.06484, 2412.06464]. Fixed-size segment operators, length independence and rebase are therefore not new. The C4 text cites only Bole/DeltaLog/Tail-Replay and misses this.
  - NOVELTY, composing states from several contexts (a, e): Mamba already has decay-weighted state mixing that uses the scan's cumulative transition, plus mean "state soup" [2406.08423, a task-arithmetic analogue on states]. PICASO [2502.17605] makes the composition of many contexts order-free by averaging over permutations. It needs commuting (diagonal) transitions, finds the composed states out-of-distribution, and needs fine-tuning to match concatenation. What remains new in C4 is narrow: a three-way merge of *sibling* branches from a common ancestor under *non-commuting* GDN transitions, a commutator conflict flag, and doing this mid-generation. PICASO's efficient permutation averaging does not carry over to non-commuting transitions, which leaves C4's "apply erasures once" step with no defined order (unspecified mapping).
  - NOVELTY (c, d): the searches found no prior paper that merges parallel reasoning branches of a recurrent or hybrid model mid-generation; a WebSearch found none either. Tree-speculation work for GDN keeps factors but materialises only the accepted path and discards siblings: TreeWY [2608.20961] (pseudo-values via one triangular solve), Bole [2608.01651], DeltaLog [2608.15533]. HyLaMAS [2610.37779] already runs latent multi-agent collaboration on Qwen3.5 hybrids by passing KV plus GDN state along a *chain* of agents. That weakens the premise that "hybrids cannot be used", which C4 takes from [2611.08483], although HyLaMAS merges no parallel states.
  - VALUE, direct evidence that the trivial baseline ties: LinearKV [2608.11231] runs exactly the user's baseline (concatenate the attention KV of K chunks, keep ONE chunk's linear state) against exact multi-state composition. On GDN hybrids (OLMo-Hybrid, Qwen) "exactness buys nothing": the gap is at most 0.013 F1 and changes sign, and a random block does as well. On Mamba-2, multi-source composition collapses (46.6% vs 86.8% of full quality) and more recompute does not rescue it.
  - VALUE, what the GDN state carries [2609.04434]: on Qwen3.5-4B, rec-only exact retrieval is 0.00 while kv-only keeps 0.89-0.98. Language and persona survive only through the recurrent state. In a state swap, the answer's *value* comes from the KV side and its *language* from the state side (>=99% of items). Branches of one problem share language, format and persona, so merging their GDN states should transfer almost no content. HyLaMAS's channel ablation agrees: 88/87/84/90% for both/KV/state/none, none significant. The state only changed output length.
  - VALUE, horizon: Qwen hybrids rebuilt from a ZERO state by replaying only the last 128 tokens keep >99% of LongBench/RULER quality [2609.33477]. Tail-Replay keeps 92.8-98.9% with a 5-10% suffix [2608.30310]. On Qwen3-Next about 60% of GDN heads have a static horizon under 512 tokens (2.49x prunable at W_max = 512) [2608.30386]. So after a 512-2K segment, the ancestor term S0·A contributes almost nothing in most heads, rebase is effectively a no-op, and a merge's effect on the GDN layers fades within about a hundred decoded tokens. Caveat: all of these are retrieval-heavy benchmarks, not long CoT; DASC notes that states matter more for aggregation and reasoning.
  - CONFLICT SIGNAL, key-space geometry: in Qwen3-Next, the split of GDN heads into near-zero-rank and saturated (full-rank) heads is the same across data domains [2602.02195]. In saturated heads, any segment longer than d_k touches every key direction, so the write footprints of two branches always overlap. Low-rank heads write the same few directions in every context. Either way, overlap or commutator size is set by the head type, not by whether the branches disagree. Two branches on the same problem also draw keys from almost the same distribution (same vocabulary and format). The key assumption ("branches that differ in substance write nearly orthogonal key directions") is therefore very likely false.
  - DOUBLE-COUNTING / OOD: the merge rule is underspecified. "Shared decay once" is undefined when each branch has its own data-dependent cumulative gate gamma_i. Delta-sum merging S0 + sum_i (S_i - S0) gives the ancestor a coefficient of 1 - sum_i (1 - gamma_i), which goes negative once N(1 - gamma) > 1, as it will in short-horizon heads. Summing the B_i superposes N saturated associative memories into a state no token sequence produces. PICASO [2502.17605] and LinearKV [2608.11231] both report that composed multi-source states are out-of-distribution or fragile.
  - MAPPING / CONTRIBUTION TYPE: it is a method (passes the gate). File = state, patch = (T_C, write) and fork = copy are specified, but the pushout and the conflict-resolution policy are not mapped to a defined operation.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: The patch object and rebase are already HYPIC [2607.01299], and in GDN hybrids the recurrent state carries style and recent context rather than retrievable content [2609.04434, 2609.33477], with LinearKV [2608.11231] showing that combining several states ties keeping one; so a GDN-state merge can at most match "concatenate branch KV and keep one branch's state", and its commutator flag measures head type and segment length, not disagreement.

#### Failure modes (ranked)
1. **The GDN merge carries no content; the trivial baseline ties (most likely).** Merging the GDN states of sibling branches transfers mode/style, which the branches already share, and not facts or partial results, which live in attention KV. Evidence: rec-only retrieval 0.00 and value-from-KV / style-from-state in the state swap [2609.04434]; GDN exact composition ties a single state [2608.11231]; the GDN channel has no significant accuracy effect in hybrid latent multi-agent runs [2610.37779]. Consequence: C4 equals "append all branches' attention KV pages + keep one branch's state", which needs no patches at all.
2. **The state forgets the ancestor within one segment.** The ancestor term, rebase and "decay counted once" all become numerically irrelevant once segments are hundreds of tokens long. Evidence: a 128-token replay from zero is enough for Qwen [2609.33477]; about 60% of heads have a horizon under 512 [2608.30386]; the toy run gives ||A|| about 0.008 at L = 2048.
3. **The commutator signal is degenerate.** In the long-segment regime it is close to 0 because A is close to 0 (it reports "no conflict" exactly when chaining wipes a branch). Otherwise it is fixed by head type: saturated vs low-rank heads, data-independent [2602.02195]. Either way it does not track branch disagreement. It also measures the arbitrariness of the order, not content loss (Trotter's ground truth does not exist here).
4. **Merged states are out-of-distribution or the merge rule is ill-defined.** The non-commuting erasures need an arbitrary order or a permutation average, and PICASO's efficient form needs commuting transitions [2502.17605]. Delta-sum merging double-subtracts the decay; summed writes inflate norms. Multi-source composition collapses on Mamba-2 [2608.11231], and PICASO needed fine-tuning.
5. **Upstream of C4: unproven value of mid-flight merging itself.** Even with an exact merge, nothing shows that a long-CoT trunk benefits from reading other branches' partial reasoning more than it would from voting or simply continuing the best branch. The external audit raises the same unmeasured premise for C7 (does foreign KV get attended?). C4's GDN machinery cannot fix this, and its attention half is just SwarmEvolve-style KV concatenation [2611.08483].
6. **Deep-layer conditioning error.** Patches are exact only at layer 0. Deeper layers' keys and gates come from each branch's own context, which gives 8.9% state drift even for same-document composition [2607.01299]. The error compounds when branches diverge.
7. **Novelty exposure.** A reviewer will point to HYPIC, PICASO, State Soup and LinearKV; what is left (sibling three-way merge + commutator flag) is the part failure modes 1-3 make least likely to matter.

#### Cheapest falsification experiment
No kernels and no training, about one GPU-day on Qwen3.5-4B (or Qwen3-Next) in HF:
1. Take about 100 AIME/HMMT problems and sample N = 4 branches each from a shared 1-2K-token prefix, 2K tokens per branch, using the plain recurrent mode; the patches come for free as S_i and the ancestor S0.
2. At the merge point, continue decoding to an answer under five conditions:
   - (a) continue the best single branch;
   - (b) append all 4 branches' attention-KV pages (RoPE re-rotated) and keep branch 1's GDN state;
   - (c) the same as (b) but with a C4-style merged GDN state (S0·gamma_max + sum of B_i), with and without the commutator-chosen winner per head;
   - (d) the same as (b) but with the GDN state of an unrelated problem, a negative control;
   - (e) text re-prefill of all branches, the oracle.
3. Log the per-head ||A_i||, the commutator norms and the B_i overlap, and correlate them with whether the branches' final answers agree.

C4 is falsified if (c) is not better than (b) by more than the noise between seeds, or if (d) is about equal to (b): the state would then be irrelevant to content. It is also falsified if the per-head commutator norms have a rank correlation of |rho| < 0.2 with branch answer disagreement, or are explained by head identity alone (a head fixed effect). If (b) is also not better than (a), the merged-branch premise fails for transformers too, and the lineage should be dropped.


### C10 verdict
- **Verdict**: `reject(reason=the core operation, a far softmax summary computed under an earlier query and LSE-merged with a fresh near band, is published as MAC-Attention and is training-free with a zero far read on hits; batched exact far reads over several future queries already exist as self-speculative verification; the residual "extreme lag" regime lacks the low drift it needs)`
- **Evidence**:
  - SOURCE GROUNDING, p(l)-CG [1801.04728]: the source is stated correctly (reduction consumed l iterations late, ~5x at l=3, accuracy falls with depth). But its iterates match CG *in exact arithmetic*, because the auxiliary basis recurrences compensate for the lag. C10 has no compensating term, so the lag changes the model; it does not merely reschedule it. The borrowed principle ("lag + compensation = same answer") does not carry over.
  - SOURCE GROUNDING, MTS/RESPA [1312.1284]: the slow force is evaluated *fresh* at each outer step and applied as an impulse. It works only if the slow part has no fast components. The faithful analogue is "fresh far read every B steps, reused in between", which is RefreshKV [2411.05787] / MAC [2604.00235], not a lagged answer. The stated condition is also violated, because far reads in CoT have fast, token-triggered components (see failure mode 3).
  - NOVELTY, closest prior MAC-Attention [2604.00235] (MLSys 2026): it computes o_m ≈ (S^{(p)}_{1:p-r} + S^{(m)}_{p-r+1:m}) / (Z^{(p)}_{1:p-r} + Z^{(m)}_{p-r+1:m}). The far prefix uses an *earlier* query q_p and the recent band/tail uses the fresh q_m, merged by LSE [Sections 3.1, 3.3-3.4]. That is C10's merge, minus the fixed lag. Summaries chain across hits, so the far part can be many steps stale. It is training-free, with a >99% hit rate, 98.6-99% of the prefix skipped and accuracy equal to full attention (Qwen3-30B-A3B 36.6-37.6 vs 37.0), and up to 2.6x end to end [Section 5.5, Table 4; Section 7]. On a hit MAC reads **no** far KV, while C10 still reads it once per B. MAC also gates by similarity and falls back on a miss, which C10 cannot. In-HBM C10 is therefore dominated on both bytes and fidelity.
  - NOVELTY, batched exact far reads over a block of queries: self-speculative decoding with a window/sparse draft verifies gamma draft tokens against the full cache in one multi-query pass. That is "far cache read once per block, exact softmax" with no semantic change. On Qwen3-8B AIME25 it gets 1.70-2.12x (ASPIRE [2609.17943]), 1.25-2.70x (Vegas [2602.07223]) and the MagicDec StreamingLLM draft [2408.11049]. C10 is the "accept every draft" version of this, trading the guarantee for a longer B.
  - NOVELTY, other uses of a lagged far signal: ScoutAttention [2603.27138] computes exact partial attention over offloaded blocks with a *predicted* query one layer ahead and LSE-merges it on the GPU (~2% accuracy cost at an 8% CPU share). AsyncTLS [2604.07815], SparDA [2606.04511] and OasisKV [2608.08097] lag only the *selection* by one step or one layer. Block-Recurrent Transformer [2203.07852] is a trained architecture whose far channel lags by up to one block (compressed state). RetroAttention [2508.09001] applies late contributions to the *original* token and overwrites downstream K/V. KVBuffer [2605.19049] defers only state *writes* and still reads the far state every step, because linear-attention state can be queried exactly. None of these uses an unconditional fixed-lag exact far read batched over B queries, so that combination is the only new piece (>=20 lit.py queries + 2 WebSearch). But each half exists, and MAC beats the in-HBM version.
  - DRIFT, the external audit's condition: (i) top-1/8 selection overlap between queries <=16 steps apart is only ">40%" on Qwen3-32B AIME24 [2510.07486, Section 3.1]; (ii) <15% of important blocks change per step, but importance drifts steadily and needs a refresh every ~8.7 steps [2603.27138, Sections 3.2, 3.4]; (iii) MAC's error grows with the reuse gap at fixed band, and it stays faithful only with r ≈ 256 *recomputed under the fresh query* plus similarity gating [2604.00235, Section 5.6]; (iv) 63-85% of heads have drift events, and the best wake-up predictor in CoT is the just-generated token (F1 0.53-0.62) [2610.02713]. Drift is low for gated, small-gap reuse and unmeasured or unfavourable at B in the hundreds.
  - TRAINING-FREE DELAY, analogue evidence: making 16 upper layers of Llama-3.1-8B consume a block output one step late drops GSM8K from 84.99 to 10.54 zero-shot; 3B tokens of SFT recover 86.81, and 20 layers recover only 76.19 [2501.06589, Table 4]. On copy-heavy long generation, a stale far set recovers only 52%/42% of full quality [2411.05787, Section 4.2].
  - HEAD SPLIT [2410.10819, 2510.08525]: on reasoning, only 40-60% of heads can go sink+local losslessly, which gives 1.56x at s=0.4 (79.6 vs 79.4); s=0.6 gives 2.06x but 73.8. Compressing DuoAttention's retrieval heads causes overlength errors [2510.08525, Section 4.3, Table 3]. The training-free C10 variant keeps those retrieval heads fresh, so its far bandwidth stays where DuoAttention/RLKV already leaves it. Lagging streaming heads saves nothing, since they can be dropped outright.
  - TRAINED CEILING [2606.11634]: pure SWA after SFT+RL comes within 0.4-1 pt of full attention at 8K-12K windows (-6.3/-2.5 at 2K) and stores no far KV. A trained C10 must beat SWA-RL on cost per correct answer, which only seems possible at small windows, and it needs SWARR-scale training (42B SFT tokens + RL).
  - MAPPING / CONTRIBUTION TYPE: roles are specified (far = reduction, near = local work, B = depth) and it is a method, so it passes the gate. Underspecified: batching B queries *and* hiding the pass behind B steps needs a lag of up to 2B-1. A synchronous pass keeps the lag at exactly B, but then it sits on the critical path every B steps.
- **Strongest objection in 1 sentence**: MAC-Attention [2604.00235] already merges a far summary computed under an earlier query with a fresh recent band by LSE, training-free and with no far read on >99% of steps, and self-speculative verification [2609.17943] already batches exact far reads over a block of queries losslessly on Qwen3-8B AIME, so C10's only new part is an unconditional fixed lag that is strictly less faithful than MAC's gating, worth little in HBM (B≈16 captures most of the gain) and dependent on unmeasured low drift at B in the hundreds for its host-streaming payoff.

#### Failure modes (ranked)
1. **Dominated by published reuse and verification (most likely).** In HBM, MAC skips 98.6-99% of the far prefix at full quality, and C10 reads it every B steps without a similarity gate [2604.00235]. Self-spec gets block-batched exact far reads with an output guarantee [2609.17943, 2602.07223, 2408.11049]. A reviewer would ask what C10 adds over MAC + speculation, and in HBM the honest answer is nothing. My roofline estimate for Qwen3-8B on one H200 (144 KiB/token BF16, 16.4 GB weights, 4.8 TB/s): at memory saturation with a 16K mean context, about 48 requests read ~131 GB/step (~27 ms). With C10 and W=1024, B=16 reads ~30 GB (~6.3 ms, 4.3x) and B=256 reads ~24 GB (~5.0 ms, 5.4x), against ~23.6 GB for SWA alone. Far attention FLOPs (~0.46 TFLOP/step) are not binding. B≈16 therefore captures ~80% of the gain, and the long lag pays off only in the host-streaming regime.
2. **The wrong answer is injected, not merely missing.** Training-free, the far summary merged at t is the answer to q_{t-B}'s question, and its LSE weight Z_far is set by how strongly q_{t-B} matched. So a past retrieval spike can outweigh the fresh near output at an unrelated token. "Lagged ≥ nothing" holds only for a trained, gated model. Untrained, lagged may be *worse* than SWA. Evidence: MAC needs gating, a 256-token fresh band and fallback, and still sees outliers when mass falls outside the band [2604.00235, Section 5.6]. Ladder zero-shot GSM8K goes from 85 to 10.5 [2501.06589].
3. **The far reads that matter cannot be issued B tokens early.** In CoT, far demand is triggered by the token just written (best wake-up predictor [2610.02713]). Retrieval and copy heads are needed for CoT [2404.15574]. Intermediate results are re-read "unpredictably and non-monotonically" [2605.09490], and >95% of tokens show importance recurrence [2506.15969]. Chain-of-key-style dependent lookups fail under stale far access [2411.05787]. Induction (match the current token, then copy its successor) is wrong by construction under a lag.
4. **The head-split variant collapses into DuoAttention/RLKV.** Lag-tolerant heads are the ones that tolerate removal, and fresh heads carry the bandwidth. The bound stays at about 1/((1-s) + sW/L), i.e. 1.56x lossless on reasoning [2510.08525]. A "middle" head class (needs far context, but slowly varying) is assumed, not shown.
5. **Host streaming is capped by host DRAM and PCIe, and needs very long lags.** Host DRAM must hold every live request's full KV (~250 GB per GPU at 2 TB per 8 GPUs, so ~100 requests at 16K, ~2x the HBM batch) [2608.08097, Section 2.3]. At ~55 GB/s effective PCIe, streaming ~240 GB per block needs B ≈ 600-700 steps, W ≥ ~1.3K (lag up to 2B), and gives roughly 13K vs 7.6K tok/s for in-HBM C10 at B=16 (my estimate). The extra ~1.8x therefore rests on a lag ~40x longer than any measured drift horizon (<=16 tokens [2510.07486]; ~9-step refresh [2603.27138]).
6. **Compounding through layers and the cache.** Token t's K/V at layers > l is computed from a hidden state that contains the misattributed far answer, and that K/V is written into the cache permanently. RetroAttention shows that late contributions require overwriting downstream K/V to stay consistent [2508.09001]. Multi-hop composition (layer l retrieves X, layer l+1 queries with X) costs one lag per hop, so its effective lag is ≥ 2B.
7. **The trained version has a low ceiling and a high price.** Recovering needs SFT (+RL) at Ladder or SWARR scale [2501.06589, 2606.11634]. Trained SWA is already within 0.4-1 pt at 8-12K windows with no far KV stored, so C10's room is only the small-window gap (2K: -6.3/-2.5 pts). It must close that gap more cheaply than enlarging the window.
8. **Pipeline detail.** Batching plus overlap means a lag of B to 2B-1. A synchronous flush means exact lag B but a latency spike every B steps, like KVBuffer's flush [2605.19049]. This is minor, but it doubles the lag in the host design.

#### Cheapest falsification experiment
Training-free, HF, Qwen3-8B (thinking), about 1 GPU-day on one H200.
1. **Teacher-forced probe (exact for this model).** The lag is within each layer, so one parallel forward over a fixed trace computes exactly what the lagged model would compute at decode time on those tokens. Generate (or reuse) full-attention traces for 30 AIME24/25 problems (~15K tokens each). Implement each layer with FlexAttention or chunked SDPA as near(q_t; sinks + prompt + [t-W, t]) ⊕_LSE far(R_B q̃_{t-B}; [prompt_end, t-W)). Sweep B ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64, 256} and W ∈ {256, 1024}.
   - Baselines: (a) full attention; (b) SWA (far dropped), same W; (c) MAC-style gated reuse (pre-RoPE L2, tau 0.75, r 256); (d) C10 with fresh far reads restricted to the top-5% retrieval heads.
   - Log per token: KL to full, trace NLL and top-1 agreement. Also report them separately on "far-copy" tokens (an n-gram match whose source lies > W back).
   - Log per head: far mass f_t = Z_far/(Z_far + Z_near), the drift |f_t - f_{t-B}|, and cos(o_far(q_t), o_far(q_{t-B})).
2. **Free-running check.** 30 AIME25 × 4 samples with B ∈ {16, 64}, W = 1024, against full attention and SWA: accuracy, mean length, cap-hit rate.
3. **Kill criteria.** Reject the training-free lineage if any of the following holds:
   - at B=16, recovered fraction R = (KL_SWA - KL_lag) / KL_SWA < 0.7;
   - the lagged model is *worse* than SWA on far-copy tokens (failure mode 2 confirmed);
   - free-running accuracy drops > 5 pts or length grows > 15%;
   - MAC-gated (c) matches or beats C10 at a far-read fraction ≤ 1/B, so C10 is dominated.

   Kill the host-streaming claim if median far-mass drift at B=256 exceeds 0.1, or if output cosine falls below 0.8 in heads carrying > 20% far mass.

   Only if C10 beats SWA and MAC at B ≥ 256 is a trained version worth considering, and it must then be benchmarked against SWA-RL at equal training cost [2606.11634].
